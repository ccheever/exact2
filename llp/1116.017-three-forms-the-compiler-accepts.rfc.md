## 0. Summary

Most of what guided builders get wrong first is Exact's vocabulary and structure (LLP 1116.016 §7). r1 proposed
that the compiler accept every form whose meaning it could name, and draw the author's word where it conflicts
with the platform's convention, with a warning. All three reviews refused that rule: being able to name an edit
does not make applying it silently faithful; an accepted spelling is permanent under "Delete; don't deprecate"
while a message can change in a day; and a warning is the worst of both, since agents act on warnings (two
warning ids stopped 19 and 15 of 104 real builds) and people ship them.

What survives is three forms, each the canonical way to write what it means and none an alias, and the guide's
two sentences that invited errors:

| | What changes | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | `h1` to `h6` are headings | language |
| D2 | A `fn`'s body may start on the line after `=` | language |
| D3 | A section's `header` or `footer` that chooses one of several texts by `if`/`else` is one text | language |
| D4 | start-here names the root node for the accent and the heading's form | docs (built) |

Everything else r1 listed stays refused, with LLP 1116.018's messages: role-name blends, quoted numbers,
`empty()` outside a resource, `image src`, a one-value `margin-inline`, a component used without `()`, several
top-level nodes, a native button's sized symbol, a styled button as a grouped list's row, a footer that comes and
goes.

**What it buys.** First-try correctness for the cold builder and consistency with "write the web" (`h1`); no
token claim. Real builds rarely meet D1 to D3; the guess corpus does.

## 1. The evidence

The guess corpus (LLP 1116.014 D3; a cold population) and real builds (rounds 13 to 15 and A/B 3, 104 Exact
builds, 182 error sites), for the forms this RFC accepts:

| Form | Guess corpus, screens of 552 | Real builds |
|---|---|---|
| `heading` or `h1` | 24 (`lower-unknown-tag`) | none |
| a `fn` body on the next line | 20 (`syntax-expected-expression`) | none |
| a header or footer holding an `if`/`else` of texts | part of 37 (`lower-grouped-list`) | part of 9 |

## 2. The tests every accepted form passes

- **T1, one meaning.** A reader of HTML, CSS or Exact expects one meaning, and every host gives it.
- **T2, no alias.** It is no second name for a name Exact invented.
- **T3, nothing inferred.** The compiler supplies nothing the author did not write.
- **T4, no new capability.** LLP 1116.016 D0 is untouched.
- **T5, canonical.** It is the form the guide would teach for what it means, so accepting it adds no second way
  to say one thing.

## D1. `h1` to `h6`

`h1 "Recipes"` is `text "Recipes" role="heading" aria-level=1`, and so on to `h6`: HTML's heading elements, whose
level is in the name. Its look is the platform's heading for that level, as `role="heading"`'s is (LLP 1115:
unsaid presentation is the platform's); the browser's default margins are not brought along, as no other tag
brings them. The guide teaches `h1`; `text … role="heading"` remains for a heading whose level is computed.

## D2. A `fn` body on the next line

```contract
fn moodName(mood: number): string =
  mood >= 4 ? "Good" : "Low"
```

is the same as the body on the `=` line. The body is one expression, indented one level, as a long expression is
today inside `(`; nothing else may follow it at that level.

## D3. A header or footer that chooses its text

A section's `header` or `footer` whose only child is an `if` / `else if` / `else` chain, every arm one `text`
and the chain ending in `else`, is one `text` whose words the same conditions choose, in order: what the
refusal now tells the builder to write by hand (LLP 1116.018). Each arm's attributes must agree (a `testId`
written on one arm only stays refused), since the result is one text. A chain without a final `else`, a footer
under an `if`, and any other content stay refused with LLP 1116.018's messages.

## D4. The guide (built)

start-here put the accent "on the root" and builders wrote it on the `view` line; it said a header holds "a
heading" and builders wrote `heading`. It now names the root node (`main accent-color=…`) and, after D1 lands,
`h1`. +62 bytes.

## 8. Cost

D1 is a table row in the lowerer and a line in the guide; D2 is the parser's `fn`; D3 is a rewrite in the
grouped list before its check. A test each. No source migrates.

## As built

**Built (2026-10-16, `1e64e4b07`).** `h1` to `h6` are desugared in the parser (`heading()` in
`contract/syntax/src/parser/view.rs`, outside `node_element`, whose frame every nested view node costs: inline,
it overflowed the deep-nesting tests); a `fn` body on the next line in `parser.rs`; a header or footer's chain
of texts folded before the grouped list's check (`grouped/messages.rs`). `three_forms.rs` holds each to the plan
of the form it means, byte for byte. start-here teaches `h1` and `h2` and names the root node for the accent;
`contract vocab h1` answers.

**Measured (2026-10-16).** The frozen guesses of `base17a` and `base17b` (319 screens), recompiled on the new
compiler with no model: 1 screen turns clean (a footer chain) and none breaks; the errors fall from 298 to 296,
since an accepted form lets the compiler reach errors after it. Builders had written `heading` 56 times and `h1`
never: start-here's "a heading". A fresh guess run on the new compiler and guide (`acc17a`):

| Run | GPT clean | Muse clean | `heading` | `h1`–`h6` | accent on the `view` line |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `base17a` | 30 | 31 | 24 | 0 | 1 |
| `base17b` | 29 | 37 | 32 | 0 | 5 |
| `acc17a` | 29 | 31 | 0 | 262 | 0 |

The guide and `h1` together move what builders write, and the forms they target are gone; clean screens are within
the spread, since the screens that met them had other errors too. With the messages, the forms add a little in
repair (LLP 1116.018's table: GPT 72 to 75 features kept, Muse 59 to 58). The stop rule, no screen compiled to a
wrong screen, held for the one screen that turned clean (its footer shows each state's words).
