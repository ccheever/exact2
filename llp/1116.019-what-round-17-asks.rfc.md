## 0. Summary

Charlie set the next rounds' aim (2026-10-16): soundly beat UIKit and SwiftUI on an iPhone, in fidelity first,
then tokens and wall clock. Round 17 was the first with UIKit in the comparison and the first whose grader lists
**tells**, each thing someone who knows iOS would notice is not as a hand-built UIKit or SwiftUI app would do it,
with every app checked in dark mode and at the largest text size.

| Round 17 (24 iPhone pairs, Exact over the other) | Preferred | Platform score | Tells an app | Wall clock | Tokens |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| vs SwiftUI (12) | 9-2-1 | 6.8 vs 5.8 | 6.0 vs 6.8 | 0.76x | 1.16x |
| vs UIKit (12) | 9-3-0 | 6.9 vs 5.6 | 5.8 vs 6.5 | 0.63x | 0.98x |

Exact wins, but not soundly: its apps carry six tells each, nearly as many as the native ones. Three
investigations reproduced the 141 tells graders listed for Exact's 24 apps on an iOS 27 simulator beside a
hand-written UIKit app (`scratchpad/inv-{a,b,c}`). They fall into seven causes. Four are Exact's own defaults
and three are what its docs teach:

|---|---|---|---|

| D2 | The builder sees the largest text size: the agent renders Exact's text at the device's size, and `verify` reports what overflows there | about 12 (rows the builder laid out) | tools |

Tokens and wall clock are the next RFC's: round 17's token ratio is mixed by model (GPT 0.89x vs SwiftUI but

## 1. The evidence

The tells graders listed for Exact's apps, by cause, with what the investigations found:

| Cause | Apps | Found |
|---|---|---|
| Rows the builder laid out run off the screen at the largest text size | ~12 | CSS flex keeps a word whole, so a `row` of text and buttons overflows; a SwiftUI `HStack` truncates and a hand-built screen stacks. The builder's choice, but invisible to them: the agent draws Exact's text at 17 pt while UIKit's controls follow the device (`PageFacts.swift:33,110`), and `verify` never tries the largest size. |
| A grouped row's label beside a text, number or date field vanishes or overlaps | 4-5 | Exact's bug: the title gets `min-width: 0` (`contract/lower/src/grouped.rs:1150`) and the field is 20 characters wide at the current font, so at 53 pt the label is squeezed to nothing. A SwiftUI `Form` stacks the label over the field. The shipped `grouped-form` recipe breaks the same way. |
| The segmented control balloons | 4-5 | It is UIKit's own `UISegmentedControl` (pixel-identical at 17 pt), but its box takes the hidden radio buttons' scaled height (`kernel/src/style.rs:1461` treats the measured height as a minimum): 2.5x UIKit's 32 pt. Its labels staying small is UIKit's behaviour too. |
| A checkbox dominates its row; a − / + stays small; `RoundButton` truncates to "L…" | 4 | Exact's defaults: `.large` symbol scale at body size (`ControlsIOS.swift`); a symbol-only button sized from a title font it lacks (`ButtonConfigurationIOS.swift:222`); `RoundButton` is 80 × 80 with a one-line title (`content.contract:208`). |
| Teal, indigo or orange everywhere; teal buttons with black text | 8 | 23 of 24 apps wrote an accent: start-here's example is teal and all five patterns write indigo. UIKit keeps a filled button's white label on every tint; the black is Exact's own rule (LLP 1116.007 D3.10). Apple's Increase Contrast system colours keep white at about 4.5:1. |
| No swipe to delete, no context menu; a delete button in every row | 10 | Both work as UIKit's (driven with real touches), but only 1 of 24 apps used swipe and none a menu: the `list-detail` pattern deletes from the detail screen, start-here never mentions it, a swipe row is ten attributes of scroll-snap, and the `list-rows` recipe's menu does not compile as written. |
| Return says "return"; a lone field says "next"; Send drops the keyboard; a quantity gets a decimal pad; the keyboard stays behind a date popover | 6 | Exact's defaults, except the lone "next", copied from a pattern: an unsaid return key is `.default` though Return always dismisses (LLP 1115.000 D9.3); a button press ends editing (`PointerIOS.swift:444`), where a `UIButton` never resigns the field; `type="number"` is always the decimal pad, though HTML's default step is 1; a date picker's press skips focus handling. |
| A small inline title over a blank band; a large title that never collapses | 6-7 | The `rest` pattern puts a fixed persona switcher between the header and the list, which (correctly) forbids the collapsing large title; builders copied it. |
| An action row in plain label colour; a chevron in the accent | 5 | Exact's: a `button` directly in a section draws in label colour (SwiftUI's `List { Button }` is tinted); a chevron deep in a custom row loses even an authored colour. |
| No haptics; decorative symbols spoken; an ECG for water | 4 | Docs: `haptic()` exists and no app called it; start-here never names it. |

Two graders' tells are UIKit's own behaviour and need nothing: a bare back chevron (iOS 27's default) and no
grabber on a sheet of one height.

## 2. The tests every change must pass

- **T1, the reference.** The comparison is a hand-built UIKit or SwiftUI screen on the same iOS version, checked
  on the simulator beside Exact's, not a grader's word. Where they differ, the hand-built screen wins.
- **T2, author > platform.** A default changes only what the author left unsaid. Nothing an author wrote is
  overridden, and where the change touches something written (D3's label colour), the RFC says so.
- **T3, the web.** A default added for iOS keeps the web's behaviour the web's (LLP 1115).

## D1. Exact's controls at the largest text size

- **A grouped row of a label and a field** wraps: the label keeps its natural width, the field takes `flex: 1 1
  8rem`, so at 17 pt they sit side by side and at the accessibility sizes the field goes under the label, as a
  SwiftUI `Form` does.
- **The segmented control** is its natural height, centred; the hidden radio buttons no longer size it.
- **The checkbox and radio mark** scale as UIKit's 22-point circle does, to a maximum, not at `.large`.
- **A symbol-only button** takes its symbol size from the body text style when it has no title.
- **`RoundButton`** keeps its circle and limits its title's size, as the Clock app's do; **`RingProgress`**'s
  label fits its ring; **`AnswerTile`** survives the largest size. Exact's parts are Exact's to adapt.

## D2. The builder sees the largest text size

- The agent renders Exact's own text at the device's text size, as UIKit's controls already are, so a screenshot
  at the accessibility sizes is what a person sees (`PageFacts.swift`).
- `verify` reports, at a large text size, a box past the screen's edge, text squeezed to zero width and boxes
  that overlap, with the node's test id: on the web host at a large root font size in every run (no iOS build),
  and at the largest size in `verify ios`. It never fails the run, and each finding names its remedy (wrap the
  row; a role font or a `rem` size scales), never "shrink it". The builder's own layout is still theirs
  (LLP 1115.000 D12.1 stands), but they see it.
- A recipe line: a row of text and buttons wraps (`flex-wrap="wrap"`, a `flex-basis` in `rem`), which restacks it.

## D3. The accent

- start-here and the five patterns leave the accent unsaid, so it is the platform's blue; a brand is an `app.tamber`.
- A filled native button keeps UIKit's white label on any accent the author wrote (LLP 1116.007 D3.10's black label
  is removed); a system accent reaches UIKit as its own dynamic colour, so Increase Contrast darkens it exactly as
  it darkens a hand-built button's. An authored label colour still wins. `check` still reports a pale accent's
  low contrast, and its advice is to leave the accent unsaid.
- `RoundButton`'s tinted style stops forcing a label colour, so UIKit draws the hue on its pale fill, as Clock
  does.

## D4. A list's rows

- The `list-detail` pattern's rows delete by swipe (opening the delete confirmation it already has) and open a
  menu on a long press; start-here names both for a list the person deletes from.
- The `list-rows` recipe's menu compiles as written, and `contract vocab contextmenu` says what it does.
- No warning. A swipe row in two lines, and the other names builders lack, are the next RFC's.

## D5. Fields, in the iOS host only

- An unsaid return key on a single-line field is Done (Search on a search field): Return already puts the
  keyboard away there unless the action refocuses (D9.2), so Done is the honest label. This replaces LLP 1115.000
  D9.3's "the system's label"; nothing is written into the document, and the web keeps its own.
- `type="number"` whose `step` and `min` are whole or unsaid gets the number pad; `step="any"`, a fractional step
  or `min` keep the decimal pad; an authored `inputmode` wins.
- Pressing a date or time field ends editing, as opening a sheet does.
- The chat recipe's Send keeps the keyboard (`retainFocus=true` and `focus()`), and the patterns' lone
  `enterkeyhint="next"` goes. General button presses are unchanged.

## D6. The bar and the list's action rows

- The `rest` pattern's persona switch moves into the header (a menu bar item) or the list's first section, so its
  title is a collapsing large title (`aria-level=1`).
- A `button` that is a grouped section's row draws tinted, at the row's inset, as SwiftUI's `List { Button }`.
- A forward chevron in a custom row is the system's grey accessory, and an authored colour on it wins.

## D7. One line each

start-here or the card names `haptic(…)` on a selection and a completion; decorative symbols take `aria-hidden`;
`symbol:activity` is an ECG and `DoneMark` is done/not done; `autocapitalize="words"` for a name; a server's
refusal stands beside the field it is about.

## 7. How it is judged

The grader's noise, measured: four round-17 pairs regraded by the same grader moved each arm's tells by 0 or +1,
its platform score by at most one point and its bugs by about one; one verdict moved (a tie to Exact)
(`~/bakeoff/rounds/r17/regrade-{orig,new}`).

| Measure | What it is | Stop rule |
|---|---|---|
| The reference | each change beside a hand-built UIKit screen on the iOS 27 simulator, at 17 pt and the largest size | no change lands that looks less like UIKit's |
| Replay | round 17's 24 Exact apps rebuilt unchanged on the new main and graded beside the same native apps by the same grader | the tells and bugs D1, D3, D5 and D6 answer are gone from the replayed apps; nothing new appears |
| Round 18 | round 17's pairs, prompts and grader, on the new main | per model, with intervals: tells and bugs an Exact app fall, platform score does not; source counts move (accent written, `swipeTrailing` present); tokens and wall clock as paired per-spec ratios, timeouts excluded |

## 8. Cost

D1 and D5 are host and lowering defaults with tests each, about a day apiece; D2 is the agent's text size and a
pass in `verify`, two to three days; D3, D4, D6 and D7 are docs, a pattern, one warning and two host rules, a
day or two together. Five lanes.
