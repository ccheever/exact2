## 1. Stage 0's report

Every probe ran on an iOS 27 simulator (iPhone 17 Pro) or a watchOS 27
simulator (Apple Watch Ultra 4), Xcode 27.0. The probes are throwaway
branches, never landed: `s1126/archive`, `s1126/session`, `s1126/hermes`
(the Mac mini, `~/lanes/s1126/wt/<lane>`), `s1126/watchboot` (the Mac
Studio), `s1126/device` (this Mac), and Interview's `spike/1126-baseline`.
Each has a `probes/1126/<lane>/REPORT.md` with its commands and evidence.

### 1.1 Q1 — the archive keeps Exact's placement

Fixed frames with offsets, `.position`, a custom `Layout` and
`GeometryReader` all survive WidgetKit's archive in a Home Screen widget.
The archive is a value built in the extension: custom layout code logged the
extension's PID once per rendering mode (full colour, accented, vibrant) and
again on a text-size change. So D4 may place with a custom `Layout`, which
the kernel's frames feed.

Measured: content rectangles (small 128.26 × 128.26 pt and medium
313.59 × 128.26 pt inside 18.04 pt margins on a 402 pt-wide iPhone; accessory
circular 58 × 58 with none); `containerBackground` shown on the Home Screen
and removed on the vibrant Lock Screen; a Dynamic Island compact region of
22.667 pt height that honoured a 40 × 22 fixed frame; expanded regions
probed at zero and infinity before finite widths (leading and trailing 165,
centre 109.33, bottom 338 pt). Open: the expanded Island's pixels did not
capture on the simulator in three tries, though its accessibility tree did.

### 1.2 Q2 — the kernel's lines hold, and platform time ticks

Core Text's lines, each emitted as one single-line `Text`
(`.lineLimit(1).fixedSize()`), were never re-broken or truncated at 15, 17,
23 and 28 pt, with bold runs, CJK and an emoji, in full-colour, accented and
vibrant modes. `Text(date, style: .timer)` and `.relative` ticked in the
archived widget with timeline policy `.never` and stayed in their reserved
widths (70 of 110 pt on an accessory, 110 of 160 on the Home Screen). The
hand-built baseline found the widest `.relative` value is two units ("59
min, 59 sec ago"), which the reserved width must fit. `.accessibilityElement(
children: .combine)` survives as one element whose label joins the lines;
VoiceOver reading a whole paragraph aloud is unconfirmed (it spoke a
64-character summary, and Grouped navigation could not be enabled on the
simulator).

### 1.3 Q5 — a refused view is visible, its absence is not

A `UIViewRepresentable` in an archived widget draws as a red prohibition
mark on yellow, and WidgetKit logs `The body of the Widget entries' view
contains the following unsupported types: …`, which a scoped log stream
caught in 2 of 3 reloads. A positive line names a refused type; silence
proves nothing. The `snapshot` profile (WP2) stays the guard.

### 1.4 Q3 — a session in an extension: fast; memory waits for the device

A headless session (the C ABI, the owner thread, Core Text measurement, no
presenter, built with `-application-extension`) boots a plan, lays it out and
hands SwiftUI its frames and lines inside a real WidgetKit `getTimeline`:

| Plan | Cold boot → tree | Whole timeline request | Peak in the extension | Ceiling D8 needs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Hello | 3–10 ms | 7–15 ms | 13.1 MiB | 26.2 MiB |
| Caltrain, the whole app's plan | 22–53 ms | 126–230 ms | 20.1 MiB | 40.3 MiB |
| Interview, the whole plan, TypeScript under Hermes, 50 posts | 28–30 ms | 195–202 ms | 27.4 MiB | 54.8 MiB |

The timeline rows include a retained workload of 20 entries at two sizes.
Every time is far inside D8's one second. Memory is D8's open question: the
simulator does not enforce an extension's limit, and these plans are whole
apps (281 to 633 nodes), where a widget entry's own plan (WP2) holds only
what it reaches. Hermes's activation added about 1.4 MiB. The device probe
(`s1126/device`, signed for this Mac's team) measures the ceiling with a
widget that allocates 1 MiB every 50 ms until iOS kills it, and reads the
jetsam report; it needs the phone connected and unlocked.

**Decision rule.** If a widget entry's measured peak on the device is under
half the measured ceiling, D8 stands. If only a whole-app plan exceeds it,
D8 stands on WP2's pruning, measured again on the device. If an entry's own
plan exceeds it, WP5 builds D8's render-only fallback instead, and WP1, WP3
and WP4 are unchanged.

### 1.5 Q4 — one framework costs nothing visible

The app and its extension loading one dynamic framework launched within the
noise of the static build (Caltrain 542 against 536 ms, overlapping ranges)
and halved the combined executable size (16.7 against 33.0 MiB unstripped).

### 1.6 The watch

exact2's Rust builds for `aarch64-apple-watchos-sim`, `aarch64-apple-watchos`
(whose minimum the compiler raises to watchOS 26) and `arm64_32-apple-watchos`
with the web's pinned nightly and `-Zbuild-std`, and every Apple bake takes
iOS's policy on the watch (landed: `944b0e02c`, `190be13d7`). A stub watchOS
app built with `swiftc` boots Caltrain's plan through the C ABI with nine of
`ExactKit`'s files, lays it out at 211 × 257 pt and draws it with SwiftUI:
first frame about 0.75–0.93 s after launch on the simulator, boot 40 ms,
peak footprint 34 MiB, a 9.2 MiB executable.

### 1.7 What stage 0 changed

- D4: a custom `Layout` is admitted, not only fixed frames (§1.1).
- D5: a `privacySensitive` modifier makes its subtree ignore placeholder
  redaction, so the driver leaves it off when it renders the placeholder.
- D6: a reserved width fits the platform format's widest value, two units
  for `.relative`.
- Stage 2's push test needs real APNs: `simctl push` refuses a Live Activity
  update (`UNErrorDomain 1401`).

## 2. Stage 1's work packages

Stage 1 ships iPhone widgets for Interview (the job: the person's open
question, with its latest answer) and Caltrain's countdown widget as the
in-tree fixture (LLP 1126 §0, question 7). Each work package lands on its
own, reviewed blind by two families, behind nothing a running app sees until
WP7.

### WP1 — `ExactCore`: a presenter-free Apple core (D8)

A target of `host/apple/Package.swift` that `ExactKit` depends on and that
builds for macOS, iOS, tvOS, watchOS and visionOS, extension-safe: the C
module and the Rust link, the owner thread, the bridge and batch decode, the
text engine's measurement half (`Text.swift` split: measurement, shaping,
line breaking and paragraph geometry in the core; painting, native-control
caches and Markdown in `ExactKit`), fonts, and a headless session that boots
a plan at a size, answers measurement, keeps a node model (frames, styles,
text lines) and lays out again on a store or size change. No presenter's
behaviour changes. Done when every Apple build, the XCTests, the iOS and
macOS smokes and the six checks match origin/main, the core builds alone for
the watch simulator with `-application-extension`, and one XCTest asserts a
laid-out tree with no view. *In progress (lane `extract`).*

### WP2 — Entries, the `snapshot` profile, and a plan per entry (D2)

- `app.json` takes top-level `widgets` (and, for stage 2, `live_activities`):
  each entry names its kind, families, root component, props shape, refresh
  function and preview props (`scripts/app.schema.json`,
  `contract/cli/src/manifest.rs`).
- A `snapshot` column on `kernel/tables/schema.json`'s rows, read by the
  compiler alone, as `terminal` is (`contract/cli/src/terminal.rs` is the
  model): rows the driver draws are admitted, the rest refused by absence,
  with an edit. The profile's other refusals: a read of the clock outside
  `time` and `progress` (D6), a handler that navigates or takes input (D7),
  a native module, a timer, a request.
- The bake compiles each entry's root into its own plan, with a header that
  names it a snapshot entry (every running host refuses it, and the snapshot
  driver refuses any other), in the app's bundle beside the app's plan and
  under its compatibility id: the envelope's `plans` map (LLP 1031 D1).
- Done when Caltrain's countdown entry bakes to a plan of its own that holds
  only what it reaches, the app's plan is unchanged, and a refused row, a
  clock read and a navigating handler each fail the bake with an edit
  (tests in `contract/cli`).

### WP3 — `time` and `progress` on every host (D6)

- `time` becomes a built-in tag with HTML's meaning (`datetime`, an instant),
  and a named presentation the platform formats: a relative time, a timer, a
  countdown, a date in a style. `progress` takes an interval (a start and an
  end instant) as well as a value. Running hosts format and advance them on
  the runner's clock; the snapshot driver hands them to `Text(date, style:)`
  and `ProgressView(timerInterval:)`.
- The kernel reserves the width of the format's widest value at the
  element's font (two units for a relative time), so the archived text never
  re-flows its line.
- Done when the web, iOS, macOS, Android, Linux and the terminal show the
  same `time` advancing (a smoke step with `clock +60000`), and the kernel's
  reservation has a test. Contract's docs gain the two (`contract vocab`).

### WP4 — The snapshot driver (D4, D5)

A SwiftUI renderer on `ExactCore`'s node model (its own target, linked by an
app with entries):

- boxes as shapes at the kernel's frames (a custom `Layout` fed by the
  frames), borders, radii, gradients, clips, images from the bundle or the
  app group (never fetched in the render);
- text as one single-line `Text` per kernel line, colours as roles;
- `time` and `progress` as the platform's live views;
- the platform's presentation (D5): the root's background as the
  `containerBackground`, the system's margins (never applied twice), the
  rendering modes by role (`widgetAccentable` from the node's role, images
  desaturated unless the author says so), accessibility (labels, roles,
  paragraphs combined), `privacySensitive` on account content except while
  rendering the placeholder;
- links as `widgetURL` and `Link` with the route's URL; a button or toggle as
  the generic intent (D7) in stage 2.

Done when the driver renders Caltrain's and Interview's entries in-process
at every family, mode, appearance and two text sizes, and the result is
compared, image by image, with the hand-built baseline
(`spike/1126-baseline`), with every difference either fixed or listed.

### WP5 — The widget extension (D8)

`host/apple/build.mjs` builds, for an app with `widgets`, a WidgetKit
extension beside the app: `ExactCore` and the driver in one shared framework
the app and the extension load (§1.5); a generated `WidgetBundle` of the
entries' kinds and families; a `TimelineProvider` that runs the entry's
refresh function in a headless session (the data module, under Hermes for a
TypeScript app) and returns its timeline; the placeholder from the entry's
preview props. The app's store and update store move to the app group's
container (the app owns updates and publishes immutable generations; the
extension reads the current one, pinned for the invocation); secrets come
from a shared Keychain access group, read only in a refresh. Done when
Caltrain's widget is on a simulator's Home Screen through `build.mjs --ios
--run`, refreshes from its data, and the app still passes its smoke. If §1.4's
rule calls for the fallback, this package builds the render-only extension
instead.

### WP6 — Development and the agent (D14)

A development build's gallery of the app's entries (each family, mode,
appearance and text size, rendered by the driver in the app's process), and
the agent's addressing of it: `bun scripts/agent.mjs ios widget <entry>
--family systemMedium [--mode accented] tree screenshot state`. An async-lane
test adds a widget through SpringBoard (the archive probe's kit) and compares
the archived capture with the gallery's. Done when the agent screenshots
Caltrain's entry in every family and the async test passes on a simulator.

### WP7 — The consumers

Caltrain's countdown widget (in tree), and Interview's open-question widget
(Interview's repository, by `EXACT_APP_DIR`), each an entry under `widgets`
with its refresh function. Done when both are on a simulator's Home Screen
and on Charlie's iPhone, judged against the hand-built baseline.
