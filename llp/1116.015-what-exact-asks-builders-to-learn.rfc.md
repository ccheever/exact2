## 0. Summary

An Exact2 build costs more total tokens than a React or SwiftUI build because the builder must learn Exact
before writing it, and every step re-sends what it read: docs are about two thirds of what an Exact2 build
re-sends (LLP 1116.013). Reading less up front did not help; builders fetched the same knowledge later.
The knowledge has to go, not move.

r1 asked whether Exact could need less of it by taking the builder's guess. The census (§2), its control,
and three reviews give a narrower answer:

- **Most of the knowledge is Exact's protocol, not its spelling.** Expressions, functions and the view's
  control words are 0.20 MB-steps a GPT build and 0.31 a Muse build, about 3% and 2% of tokens. Data, kept
  values and time are 0.49 and 1.21, and most of that (failure, grants, the clock, `pending`) stays whatever
  it is called.
- **Models write what they are shown.** With today's start-here they write Contract as taught. Unguided,
  both write `if` and `for` in a view, a typed `state` and `.length`; beyond that intersection they split,
  and they bring TypeScript's semantics with the spelling (sequential reads, `try`, effects, navigation by
  state).

So r2 proposes three decisions, each small, each judged on its own:

| | What changes | Census (of 80) | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | A view chooses with `if` and repeats with `for` (`key` still required) | 71 and 59 / 0 | sugar |
| D2 | Calls on a value for the roster that exists (`s.trim()`, `xs.length`); `state x: T = …`; `T[]` | 31, 40; 45; 32 | sugar |
| D3 | `app.ts` exports plain functions; the build writes its `Sources` map and `answer` | 75 / 0 | build; gated on §7's measure |

D1 and D2 are a few percent of tokens at most, inside one comparison's noise: they are kept or not as
language changes, by what the census and real builds show about walls and repairs. D3 is the only one that
could move tokens, and it is measured before it is built.
§8 lists what left the RFC and what each would need to come back.

## 1. The question

Each thing a builder must know about Exact and cannot guess costs three ways: the bytes that teach it,
re-sent on every later step; the steps spent looking it up; and the build that fails, or worse compiles
wrong, when the builder guesses anyway. The question for each construct is whether its difference from what
models write buys something: a guarantee the Lean semantics proves, a platform answer LLP 1115 requires, a
determinism the test clock needs. Where it does, the difference stays and the message that meets a wrong
guess names the edit (LLP 1116.014). Where it does not, the difference is a tax.

children; one counter; tags are HTML's names; data in `app.ts`. Each gets one feature of a bake-off app and

## 4. The tests every change must pass

**T1. The semantics that carry guarantees stay.** Contract's expressions are total and pure; conditions are
booleans (no truthiness); "nothing" is an option, so a value that is not an option is never empty; an action
is one transaction that reads the state it began with; the data module sees no clock or randomness. The
Lean semantics proves well-typed programs reach no wrong state under these rules (which permits refusal; it
does not prove an app does what its author meant), and the test clock replays a build because of them.

**T2. Sugar is a rewrite into the program the semantics already has.** The parser or checker rewrites the
spelling into the abstract syntax `contract lean` already emits (`Expr.call`, `typed`, the view's existing
conditional and repeat). The plan, the runner and the proofs do not change. difftest cannot catch a wrong
rewrite, since the runner and the Lean semantics both consume the rewritten program, so each rewrite has
expected-behaviour tests of its own: a program in the new spelling and the same program in the old, compiled
to the same plan.

**T3. One spelling, unless the census splits.** LLP 1081 and RULES ("Delete; don't deprecate") hold: the
familiar spelling replaces the old one, and the old one is refused with the edit. The exception is a
construct the census splits: `and`/`or`/`not` (35 features) against `&&`/`||`/`!` (26), which Contract
accepts both of today and keeps.

**T4. No spelling that compiles with another meaning.** The closer Contract looks to TypeScript, the more of
TypeScript a builder assumes. A refused assumption costs a repair; an accepted one with another meaning
costs a wrong app. A spelling is taken only when its meaning in Contract is the meaning a builder writing it
expects (`xs.length` is; `xs[i]` as `at`, which counts negative indices from the end, is not; a sequential
read after a write is not). The gate is per model: refused TypeScript forms (`try`, camelCase style,
truthiness, effects, `String`, object literals, assignment to a resource) and the read-after-assign warning
must not rise.

**T5. Write the web, for what LLP 1115 covers.** LLP 1115 settles names and layout (CSS's) and leaves
presentation to the platform. It does not make JavaScript's expression semantics Contract's (LLP 1081
excludes expression functions from its naming rule), and it keeps CSS's `font-size` over the camelCase the
census also shows (34 features). Familiar method syntax is taken because both models write it, not because
LLP 1115 asks.

**T6. The people, and the migration.** Charlie and James read and write Contract; before a decision lands,
they read representative whole apps before and after it. The rewrite touches 449 sources (33k lines),
Contract embedded in tests, about 500 KB of docs, `contract vocab`'s answers and `exact new`'s files,
the apps outside the repo that consume exact2 by path (RULES gives them no deprecation window), and open
branches. Each decision is one mechanical commit by a rewriter that runs once and is deleted after the open
branches have taken it.

## D1. A view chooses with `if` and repeats with `for`

A view's `when cond` is written `if cond`, with `else if` and `else` as an action's; `each x in xs key=…` is
written `for x in xs key=…`. The parser maps each to the node it maps today's word to, so the plan is
identical (T2's test is the corpus compiled both ways). `when` and `each` in a view are refused with the edit.
A task's `when` gate keeps its word: it is not a view's branch. That leaves `when` in the language with
one meaning fewer, and loses the word that told a reader "this is the view", which is why D1 is Charlie's
call and not the census's alone.

`key` stays required: it is the row's identity, and an `id` field is not one (LLP 1116.014). Muse omits it
in 27 of 40 features, so the missing-key message names the item's fields that could key it, as it does now.

Both models wrote these in nearly every feature (71 and 59 of 80) and neither wrote `when` or `each` unguided.
The token effect is small; the case is that every builder's first view meets this wall, and the old words buy
no guarantee.

## D2. Calls on a value, and types as written

- **Calls on a value, for the roster that exists.** A roster function whose first argument is its subject
  is called on it: `s.trim()`, `s.toLowerCase()`, `xs.filter(x => …)`, `xs.map(…)`, `xs.includes(x)`,
  `xs.join(", ")`, `xs.isEmpty()`, `xs.length`, `s.length`. The parser rewrites `e.f(args)` to `f(e, args)`
  for a roster name whose result is the JavaScript method's of that name; `.length` on a list or text is the
  checker's rewrite, since a record may have a field of that name. A roster name whose Contract meaning
  differs from the JavaScript method's keeps its free spelling and the method form is refused with the edit.
  No function joins the roster here; `find` (11 features, the only addition with evidence) is its own
  semantic change, with the runner, the Lean roster and difftest's generator.
- **Types as written.** `state x: T = …` declares the type (`Expr.typed`); inference stays, so the
  ascription is optional. `T[]` is `list<T>`. This reverses LLP 1116.014's "drop the type" diagnostic, which
  moves with it. Muse's `[T]`, `String` and `-> T` stay refused with the edit: one model, and `[T]` is not
  TypeScript's.

## D3. `app.ts` exports plain functions

Both models wrote `app.ts` as exported functions (75 of 80), and none wrote a `Sources` map or an `answer`.
Today the data module is a `Sources` map from a resource or mutation name to its implementation, and an
`answer` export.

- `app.ts` exports `async function loadItems(): Promise<Item[]>` and `async function addItem(title:
  string): Promise<Reply>`, one per resource and mutation the contract declares, by name. The build writes
  the `Sources` map and `answer`; a declared name with no export, or a type that disagrees with the contract's
  shape, fails the build with the edit.
- The handles the functions use (storage, the network) come from an import (`import { storage } from
  "exact:app"`), not parameters. `grants` stays the boundary; the import's scope is per call, as the
  parameters' is today, and nothing in `app.ts` runs before first pixel that does not today.
- The contract does not change: `resource`, `mutation`, `send`, `then`, `pending` and `failure` stay, and
  its calls stay implicit (no `import`; `main1` writes none once taught).

It is gated on §7's measure of how much of the data topic the `Sources`/`answer` shape is. If that share is
small, D3 is a tidier boundary and judged as one; if it is large, it is the one decision here that can move
total tokens, and a graded comparison decides it.

## 7. How it is judged

- **Walls on what exists.** The census's errors by diagnostic id, first error and all errors, per
  construct, on `priors1`, `priors2` and `main1` as they stand; no row is cited for a decision without its
  wall count.
- **Cheap first, on what exists.** For each decision, cut from start-here and the card only the text the
  decision deletes, and recompute MB-steps on the round 13–15 and A-arm logs. For D3, the same for the
  `Sources`/`answer` text alone, against the data row's remainder (grants, failure, SQL, the clock).
- **The census of the intersection.** The one-kilobyte guide, then the guide rewritten for the decision,
  on the 40 features and a held-out set from apps outside the 24; both models, and a third family where one
  is available. Walls counted by diagnostic id per model, with a parser that reports more than its first
  error. A decision passes when its own walls go and T4's refused forms do not rise in either model.
- **Behaviour.** Each rewrite's expected-behaviour tests (T2).
- **Real builds, one decision per comparison.** The graded A/B of LLP 1116.013 D4, with the smallest effect
  that would count named in advance per builder. D1 and D2 are predicted below that effect, so they are kept
  on repair steps, fresh tokens and the grade, not total tokens. D3 is kept on total tokens if §7's first
  measure predicts an effect the comparison can see.
- **Order.** D1, D2, D3, each after the last is judged.

## 8. Not in this RFC

- **`await` in an action, `try`/`catch`, `state x = await …`.** Two transactions in one authored action.
  It would need per-invocation captures (a `then` takes no parameters today), a failure continuation, a rule
  for queued sends and `refreshes`, and a difftest corpus pinning one reply, a failed reply whose prefix
  stays committed, two sequential replies, a send from another action between them, and a refresh the
  continuation reads, before any surface.
- **Persisted records.** A storage design, judged on data loss: atomic replacement, explicit defaults or
  versioned migration (never a silent `""` for a missing `id`), the stored original kept when a decode
  fails, unchanged slots not re-encoded on every commit (today's runner and web host encode each persisted
  slot after every commit), an aggregate budget measured against the 100 ms cold start on the slowest device,
  and the same key isolation from grants (`exact.state.*`). First, the cheap question: with one line of
  `persist` in the guide, do the keep-on-device features stop inventing `localStorage`?
- **Navigation.** Routes stay the one model, since system Back, links and the split view are what the grader
  credits (LLP 1115). A shorter route block, the expander writing the scaffold and the `navigate=` handler,
  is judged by the bytes it deletes. `dialog open=<bool>` is a separate trial that needs a rule for who writes
  the bool when the host dismisses the sheet.
- **Spelling with another meaning or no evidence:** `null`, `??`, `?.`, narrowing, `xs[i]`, `const`,
  `return`, block `fn`s, `Math.*`, the larger roster, a `timer` declaration, `Date.now()`, HTML text
  elements, `import`, ungranted `localStorage`. Each should meet a diagnostic that names the edit; the ones
the census shows that do not yet join LLP 1116.014's fixture.
- **Tooling and tests** (0.60 MB-steps a GPT build, the largest topic): its own RFC.
- **The look** (0.30): LLP 1095 D4 (the web's look) is Charlie's open decision.
- **A JavaScript UI tier**: refused by `rules/DEFERRED.md`.

## 9. For Charlie

1. **Accept D1 and D2 as language changes** (they are a few percent of tokens at most), with each kept or
   reverted by its census and real-build measure?
2. **D3** changes `app.ts`'s shape for every app with data; measure first (§7), then decide?
3. **The program's lever.** This RFC does not close the gap. Fresh tokens are already at parity (LLP
   1116.012); what multiplies the re-sent context is steps. What remains is the data protocol's ceremony
   (D3's measure) and tooling and tests, where a step saved (a one-command verify and drive, fewer repair
   loops) also saves everything re-sent after it; the next RFC is whichever measures larger.

**D2 built.** The roster functions that are the web's methods (`isEmpty`, `toString`, `includes`,
