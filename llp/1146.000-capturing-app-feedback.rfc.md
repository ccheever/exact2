## 1. Recommendation

When an authored test's assertion fails, keep what the agent would otherwise rerun the
test to see: the failing target's tree and layout, the journal since the last read, one
picture of the scene as it is, and the identity of the build and run. That is D1, and it
is worth building now as a small extension of the failure path that already exists. It
needs an implementer.

A real-time recording of an interaction (r1's D2) is deferred. Two diaries asked for one,
but the evidence for a hitch that landed on main on 2026-10-28 (`perf frames health`,
`report`, `capture`, `track`: James Ide's #1141) has not yet been tried against either case,
the agent cannot read a movie, and the one host whose recorder exists today films the
whole simulator display. §4 says what exists and the narrowest form that would be worth
admitting later.

Everything else r1 listed (focus observations, app audio, memory and CPU samples, haptics
notes) is dropped: no diary of 2026-10-25 to 10-29 needed it, and what each asked for is
either already a read (`tree`, `tree --ax`, `logs` with its crash report) or a human note
(`evidence.mjs note --device`). One line survives: a note can name the run it is about (§5).

## 2. What exists

The diaries of 2026-10-25 to 10-29 (50 of them) are the evidence. What agents lost time on:

- A picture and the tree of the same moment disagreed, and the pair was the diagnosis:
  "It looked disabled in the screenshot; `tree` showed it enabled" (Excord, polls, a
  button in an inactive Mac window).
- A hitch a person saw was in no measurement: "The jitter at the bottom is not in the
  trace's frames; a screen recording is asked for" (Excord, Mac scrolling, before #1141).
- Motion on real time could not be filmed: "`screenshot over` refuses `--timing platform`,
  so the zoom was seen in `simctl io screenshot`s taken in a loop" (Excord, viewer route).
- The decisive fact was in the journal and not on screen (Excord's gateway refusal), which
  LLP 1146 D4 now reports.

What a failed test already keeps, all as text in the result:

- The failing step's line, what was expected and seen, and `fail`'s reading of `state` and
  the logs at that step: which resources failed and why, what is held or pending, and why
  the clock may not have moved (`agent-test.mjs` `fail`).
- Failures the test did not arrange (LLP 1146 D4) and the last eight console lines of the
  data module (after `atEnd`).
- The newest simulator crash report, in `logs`; a dead app's report, in the error.
- Under `verify`, one picture of each test's end, failed tests included, taken after `clock
  settle` and after the theme and size sweep, into `.exact/verify/`, which the next run
  deletes (`verify.mjs:1333`). `agent.mjs --test` keeps no picture.
- A line in `<app>/.exact/runs.jsonl` per run, with the build's digest computed when the run
  ends (`evidence.mjs` `recordRun`).

What it does not keep, and the agent reruns the test to see: the target's `tree` and
`layout` at the failing step, the journal lines `fail` read, and a picture of that step
before anything settled.

## 3. D1: keep the failing step

**Trigger.** The first failing `expect` of a test, before the next step runs and before
`mutationClock.advance` moves the clock for it. Later failures in the same test point at
this capture. Steps continue as they do today, and the test's result is unchanged by
anything the capture does or fails to do.

**What is kept.** A directory `<app>/.exact/failures/<run>/<test index>/` with:

- `failure.json`: the test's name and file, its digest, the step's line, the assertion's
  expected and observed values and the failure line `fail` composed, as the result already
  has them. No second assertion engine.
- `tree.txt`, `layout.txt`: the failing target's `tree` and `layout` replies, as the
  transcript prints them, with the target's source line where the source map matches.
- `logs.txt`: the journal lines `fail` already read through `peekLogs`, which does not move
  the driver's cursor, and the host's lines since the test began.
- `screen.png`: one picture in the driver's film mode (`tags`, not `picture`): on Chrome a
  transition is left where it is, not held at its end as a `picture` holds it (LLP 1116.003
  D8); on the iOS simulator the app's own drawing, with no display capture and UIKit's own
  motion not landed first (`AgentIOS.swift` `settleMotion`, skipped for a film's frame). It is the
  scene as the assertion saw it, which is the point.
- `run.json`: the run's identity, taken at launch, not at the end: host and target, timing
  and touch modes, viewport, seed, epoch, locale and zone, the commit, and the build
  record's digest as `recordRun` computes it. A run id allocated at launch names the
  directory and the `runs.jsonl` line, so a capture cannot be attached to a build that
  replaced the one it ran on while the drive held a lease on the old generation.

**What is not kept.** No new `state` dump, no new `perf` read, no trace started after the
failure, no `clock` operation of any kind: settling lands the replies and transitions whose
absence may be the failure. A read already in hand is written; a read not yet made is made
once, in the order above, and a read that fails is named in `failure.json` with its error.
A host request that does not answer within the driver's reply budget hangs the session up
today (`REPLY_MS`, 120 s); the capture adds no shorter deadline, because a timeout on that
path is not a partial capture but a lost session, and the picture and the two reads are
the same requests a test step makes.

**Bounds.** The transcript's own: `STATE_CAP` for a reply printed whole, the picture's
existing image wait on the web, the three seconds the failure path already allows the
console tail. No byte budget is set in this document; the first implementation records in
the app's diary what a capture cost on the web and the iOS simulator (time and bytes for a
passing run, a failing run and a run whose app died), and a bound follows from that.

**Verify.** `testEnd` keeps taking its settled end picture; it is a different observation
and stays named as one. `verify --shots` copies the failure directory beside the end
pictures. `.exact/failures/` is kept across runs and pruned to the last ten runs.

**Privacy.** The capture is local, never uploaded by `verify`, `test` or `evidence`, and
may hold private content: a picture of an account, journal lines with names. The report
does not call it sanitized. `recordDrive` already keeps a drive's operation strings, `type`
arguments included, to 300 characters; the capture adds no typed text beyond what the
journal holds.

**Freshness.** LLP 1146 D2's rule is unchanged: a run is current while its build is the
host's build and its sources are unchanged, and restoring the sources makes it current
again (parent §12). The capture's presence or absence changes no standing. A missing
directory is reported as missing when the record names it.

## 4. Recording on real time: deferred

What exists today:

- `screenshot <png|apng> over <ms> every <ms>` films on the agent's clock and refuses
  `--timing platform` ("film is the agent clock's, and --timing platform leaves UIKit's
  motion on its own", `agent.mjs:1248`).
- `xcrun simctl io <udid> recordVideo <file>` records the simulator's display, out of
  process, in HEVC or H.264, with no frame-rate or surface argument; `docs/agent-pitfalls.md`
  already tells an agent to run it for a launch flash.
- Chrome's `Page.startScreencast`, which the web tests use (`host/web/tests/collection.mjs`),
  delivers timestamped frames that must be acknowledged one by one.
- On main since 2026-10-28, `perf frames health`, `report`, `capture` and `track` (#1141):
  bounded text about late frames, with presentation and GPU timing unobserved.

Why r1's D2 is not proposed:

- The agent reads text. A movie needs a person, or frames cut into a contact sheet, which
  `screenshot over` already makes; r1 made the sheet an afterthought.
- A recorder's timestamps are its own. `CADisplayLink`'s are in the process's time base,
  a simctl movie's are from its start, a screencast frame's are optional. Nothing in the
  repository relates any of them to the driver's `clock`, `seq` and `incarnation`, so an
  operation marker cannot name a frame, and r1's companion track would have claimed it.
- `rules/DEFERRED.md` keeps session record/replay out (§Agent API) and, since the design
  tool's admission of 2026-10-14, "captures of running apps" out. A recording with input
  markers is the record half of the first and is the second. Admitting it needs Charlie's
  waiver naming the narrowing, which r1 did not ask for.
- The Mac jitter diary asked for a recording before #1141 landed; nobody has run `perf
  frames report` on that case yet. The viewer-route zoom was seen with a screenshot loop.

The narrowest later form, should a diary show a hitch that `perf frames report` cannot
explain: let `screenshot over` accept `--timing platform` on the iOS simulator by running
`simctl io recordVideo` for the span and cutting the movie into the sheet it already makes,
each frame stamped with the recorder's time. That is a change to an existing form of the
tenth operation, adds no flag and no companion track, and its deciding test is whether the
sheet shows the viewer-route zoom the diary saw by hand. It is a QUEUE line, not a decision
here.

## 5. A note can name its run

`bun scripts/evidence.mjs note` records a person's observation against the host's build
now. With D1's run id it takes `--run <id>`, so "this jumped" names the run and its capture
rather than whatever build the host has when the note is written. Nothing else from r1's
table remains: focus is in `tree` at the failing step; a crash report is in `logs`; audio,
memory and CPU samples, and haptics wait for a diary that needs them.

## 7. How D1 would be verified

- A Caltrain test whose `expect text` fails on purpose, on the web and the iOS simulator:
  the next step still runs, the directory holds the five files, `tree.txt` names the
  target, `screen.png` shows the scene with a transition mid-way, and `runs.jsonl` names
  the directory by run id.
- The same test with the app killed before the assertion: `failure.json` names the
  original failure and the reads that could not be made; the session's crash report is in
  the error as today.
- Two concurrent runs of the same app write two directories and neither reads the other's
  journal cursor.
- Editing `app.contract` after the run makes the run stale and the capture still readable;
  restoring the bytes makes it current (the parent's rule, unchanged).
- A run on `verify` keeps the end picture and the failure picture as two files with
  different names, and `--shots` copies both.

r1's fixture of a refused Excord request is not a case: a refusal the test did not arrange
is a note under LLP 1146 D4 and the test passes, so nothing triggers D1.

## 8. Reviews and revisions

r1 (Codex, 2026-10-30) was reviewed blind on the same day by Astra (GPT-6, xhigh), Grok
4.7 (xhigh) and Claude (Fable 5.1), each in `llp/reviews/rfc-2026-10-30-1146.000-r1.*.md`.
Muse's subscription quota was exhausted and it gave no review. All three said NOT READY.

What r2 takes:

- **D1 narrowed to what the failure path does not keep** (all three). r1 specified
  resource status and pending summaries that `fail` already composes.
- **The capture boundary** (Astra): before `mutationClock.advance`, in film mode, so the
  picture does not hold a transition at its end or settle the simulator's images.
- **Identity at launch** (Astra): a run id and the build digest when the session opens,
  not when it ends; verify's deterministic, deleted-each-run picture paths are not durable
  references.
- **Budgets** (all three): r1's 5 s, 15 s, 20 MiB, 100 MiB, 30 s, 1080p, 30 fps and 100 MiB
  came from no measurement and are replaced by the existing bounds plus a measurement the
  implementation owes.
- **No deadline of its own** (Grok): a capture deadline shorter than `REPLY_MS` cannot
  cancel a host request and would turn a failure into a hangup.
- **D2 deferred** (Astra 90, Grok 84; Claude had proposed the simulator slice now): the
  movie's reader, the clocks, DEFERRED's two entries and #1141's untried evidence. Claude's
  narrow form is kept as §4's QUEUE candidate.
- **§5 deleted** (all three): no diary consumer for focus collection, audio, samples or
  haptics; the annotation anchor survives as one flag.
- **Errors corrected:** the freshness sentence (a stale run does become current again when
  its sources are restored); the claim that the run record holds no inputs (`recordDrive`
  keeps operation strings); the Excord refusal fixture (a note, not a failure); the iOS
  recorder named and its limits stated.

What r2 declines: Grok's "do not admit D1" (its own alternative is D1 without the picture;
the polls diary shows the picture and the tree together were the diagnosis, and film mode
answers its settling objection); Astra's paired diagnostic trial as the deciding
measurement for D1 (worth running after D1 exists, not before a 100-line change).
