## 1. The problem, measured

A curve begun by a commit starts at that commit's engine time. This applies to
a transition's `Running`, a flight's `Layout` curve, a new keyframe play, and an
exit. On iOS the first frame that shows the curve comes after two things: the
rest of the commit's own work (layout, mounting, image decode), and up to one
display interval. Its first visible sample is therefore already well into the
curve.

- **Simulator, unthrottled (Bluesky clone, a 402×874 iPhone, iOS 27):** a
  lightbox open on a 489 ms critical spring. The flight begins at t = 0, and its
  first displayed frame is 24–30 ms later. That frame shows p = 0.15–0.22. The
  engine clock was already ~12 ms past the commit, so the curve had effectively
  started before it.
- **Phone (iPhone, ProMotion), from a recording of the clone's b08:** the
  backdrop's luminance on its first dimmed frame matches its fade curve
  25–45 ms in.

Reanimated starts an animation at the timestamp of the frame in which it is
set: `valueSetter` calls `onStart` with `__frameTimestamp`, then `step` at that
same timestamp. That frame shows the start, and the next shows one interval.
Core Animation starts an implicit animation at its transaction's commit to the
render server, at the end of the run-loop turn, after the main thread's work.
Neither shows the commit's processing time as motion. exact2 does, so every
open, close and press response on a phone jumps on its first frame.

## 2. The rule

> On a host whose display drives the clock, a curve an author's commit begins
> between frames is **pending** until the next presented frame. While pending
> it does not move: every reader sees it at its local time zero. The frame
> starts it: its local time zero becomes that frame's instant, and the frame
> shows it there. The next frame shows one interval.

- **Local time zero, not "progress 0".** The invariant is elapsed local time
  zero at the frame, followed by ordinary evaluation (§3, D8). For an easing or
  spring transition with a delay ≥ 0, a flight, and a new play or exit, the
  first frame shows the curve's start. A negative delay starts partway into the
  curve, as it does today, by the same amount. A step timing, a reversed
  direction or a fill shows whatever that local time shows.
- **A fling keeps its release instant (D6).** A curve that a release with
  a nonzero finger velocity begins is never pending, and its local time zero
  is the input's own time. The first frame after a fling already includes v·Δt.
- **Off under the agent's clock (D7).** The agent's seekable clock, the motion
  fixtures and the existing host tests keep today's timing exactly. The rule
  is a host property, which the native session turns on and turns off at the
  agent's clock takeover.
- **The frame's instant is its target time (D5).** That is the display's
  `targetTimestamp`, the time LLP 1073 already gives frame tasks. It moves
  what the engine samples at, never the input clock.

### Why local time zero at the first frame

- **The reference app does this.** Reanimated's `step(currentTimestamp)` runs
  in the frame where the value is set. Bluesky's lightbox sets it inside a
  `requestAnimationFrame`, so its open is "start, then one interval per frame".
- **It is deterministic.** "One interval since the commit" depends on where
  in the display interval the commit fell and on how long the commit took,
  which are the very things this RFC removes from the curve. With the flag on,
  every sample is a closed-form function of the inputs and the frame instants
  the host supplied. Variable refresh and skipped frames make those instants
  uneven; they are inputs, not something the engine reads (it never reads
  `CACurrentMediaTime`).
- **It is the nearer of the two native behaviours.** A CA animation committed
  at the end of the turn starts at that commit and is first shown at the next
  vsync. Matching CA exactly would need the render server's frame time, which
  the main thread does not have. Reanimated's form is the nearer, measurable
  one. This is a behavioural choice, not an equivalence to CA.

## 3. Decisions

**D1. A pending curve does not move.** While the engine's switch is on (D7),
each curve an author's commit begins between frames is pending. Pending state
belongs to the curve itself, never to a record keyed by node:

- *A transition.* A `Running` that `observe` or `observe_path` begins carries
  `pending: Some(begin)`, where `begin` is the engine's sampling time when it
  began (D5). Every reading of it reads at `begin`, whatever the engine's time
  is: `sample` and `easing_progress`, and so `advance`, `frame`, an
  interruption's current value and velocity, a hold's acquisition and CSS's
  reversal arithmetic. `advance` therefore never moves it, never presents a
  mid-curve value and never retires it, except a zero-length curve with no
  delay, which is done at local time zero as today.
- *A play.* An `AnimationPlay` gains `pending: Option<f64>`, the local time
  it waits at: 0 for a new play or an exit, the held time for a resume. Its
  `local(now)` is that time while pending. `hold` keeps its two meanings, an
  authored pause and a drag timeline's position, and is never used for this.
  A pending play occupies `animating` until it starts, lowered or not
  (`advance_animations` never retires it), so a commit that begins only a
  lowered play still reports `motion`. It counts for `spatial`, and does not
  make a running clock busy (D8). A lowering host emits it held at that local time (D4).
- *What reads its end.* While a curve is pending, its end is reckoned as if
  it started at the engine's sampling time `t`: `end_time + (t − begin)` for
  a `Running`, `t + end − local` for a play. That covers `settle_time`,
  `animations_settle_time`, and the start that `spring_descriptor` and
  `spring_frames` report (web exports with no Apple caller).

Any pending curve keeps the engine non-quiescent.

**D2. The first frame starts them.** `Engine::present_frame(frame)` (D5)
raises `shown` to `frame` and starts every pending curve whose `begin` is at or before `frame`:

- a `Running`'s `start` moves by `frame − begin`, so a delay is kept and a
  negative delay keeps its skip into the curve; then `pending` clears;
- a pending play's `start` becomes `frame − local` and `pending` clears. An
  authored `hold` is untouched (a play paused before it ever ran is not
  pending in the first place);
- then every running curve is sampled, and an ended one retired, at the new
  sampling time, so no value cached by an earlier `advance` is behind `shown`;
- each started node is rescheduled (`schedule_animations`) and its animated
  properties are marked dirty. The host gets the nodes whose plays started,
  so it can re-emit their lowered specs (D4).

A curve whose `begin` is after `frame` stays pending. That happens when a tick
queued behind owner work reaches the engine after a later commit. It never
starts at its commit by a clamped shift. Because a pending curve was read at
`begin`, its sample at the frame after starting equals the sample every
reader already had. Nothing presented changes value at the start.

**D3. Each curve keeps its own clock.**

- *Interruption before the first frame.* A commit that retargets a key whose
  curve is pending reads it at `begin`. For a delay ≥ 0 that is its `from`,
  and the velocity `Running::sample` reports there: a spring's initial
  velocity, or an easing's one-sided difference at local time zero. The new curve starts from
  there and is pending in turn. A curve on another key is independent; two
  commits between frames that begin curves on different keys are started by
  the same frame.
- *Plays.* Pending state follows the matched play. CSS Animations 2 §4.3
  matches names from the end of the list, so reorders and duplicate names
  keep each play's own state.
  - A pending play the next commit keeps stays pending at its local time.
  - One the commit pauses becomes an authored pause at that local time
    (`hold`), not pending.
  - One it drops is gone, and a replacement is a new pending play.
  - A resume is pending at the time it was held at.
  - Binding a node to a drag timeline clears its plays' `pending`, exits
    included, as binding already takes every play's `hold`: the source owns
    their time.
  - Unbinding resumes each unpaused play from where the timeline left it.
    With the switch on, that resume is pending at that local time.
- *Exits.* A leaving view keeps "its exit was appended" (`played`) apart from
  its end, so commits before the frame never append it twice. Its end is read
  from the engine: `Engine::exit_end(node, count)`, the latest `start +
  end_time` over the exit's own `count` plays (the last ones on the node).
  That is `None` while any of them is pending, and then there is no destroy.
  The host takes it again after each start. No per-node shift is applied, so
  an older pending transition and a newer exit on one node each start on
  their own.
- *Flights.* A flight keeps its landing as an offset from its curve's start
  (today's `land_after` for a spring, `None` otherwise). It lands at
  `Engine::curve_start(node, Layout)` plus that offset; the start is `None`
  while pending, and then it does not land. The early-landing criterion is
  unchanged.
- *LLP 1099's additive components* (stage 2, not built). Pending state belongs
  to each component.
  - A component born between frames is pending at its own begin. A further
    retarget before the frame leaves it pending, and the frame starts both.
  - Each component is evaluated at its own clock and composed as LLP 1099
    composes them: summed residuals for the additive properties, the product
    of factors for `scale`, replacement for opacity and paint.
  - A hold's collapse reads each pending component at its begin.
  - An established, started spring promoted into component 0 keeps its start.
  - When the 64-component bound folds two, the survivor is pending only if
    both were.

**D4. Lowered Core Animation specs are held until the start.** A pending
play's lowered spec carries `"h": local`: `svg_lower::specs` writes
`hold.or(pending)`. So `CssAnimations.make` adds it at `speed` 0 and
`timeOffset` local, or adds none before a delay without a backwards fill.
That shows the value the engine reads.

- **No early run.** Core Animation never runs a spec ahead of the engine.
  Every on-screen lowering path writes through `svg_lower::specs`: box
  transforms and colour (LLP 1055.001), SVG scene tracks, macOS's lowered
  opacity.
- **Filtered SVG.** `SvgFilterLive` draws through `CARenderer`, which runs a
  held animation from where it is held. Today it re-seeks held animations
  before each render against one wall read, and renders against a later one.
  It takes one instant, read once per render, for both the re-seek and
  `beginFrame(atTime:)`. So a held (pending) play draws exactly at its local
  time. A pixel test in §5 pins it.
- **The start.** The started node's spec changes: `"h"` becomes `null` and
  `"s"` becomes `frame − local` (the frame instant for a new play). The host adds the started nodes to the
  roots and boxes it emits for. `box_sent` sees a new string, and
  `CssAnimations.apply` replaces the held animation by id.
- **A missed target.** A tick batch that lands after its target (an
  asynchronous tick behind owner work) is a hitch. While a tick is in flight,
  later callbacks send none (`tickInFlight`), so it can span several vsyncs.
  - Core Animation keeps time from the frame instant through it, as any CA
    animation does through a hitch.
  - Sampled motion shows the in-flight batch's values until the next tick
    that is sampled and applied on time. From that tick the two agree again.
    The disagreement lasts as long as the hitch, never the rest of the curve.
  - The start is not re-armed and no timer or frame task is replayed. Moving
    every start to the instant the batch lands would make CA and the engine
    disagree for the rest of the curve.

**D5. Two clocks: the input clock and the frame instant.** The engine keeps
`now`, the input clock, and `shown`, the latest frame instant it presented.

- **What moves `now`.** Inputs, holds, releases and timer receipts move it,
  each at its own time, and never past the wall. Velocity tracking and
  `ClockWentBackwards` read `now` alone. So `begin_hold`, `update_hold`,
  `end_hold`, the transform holds, a transform-drag packet's floor and a
  height drag's equality check compare against `now`, as today.
- **A frame task does not move it.** `Host::frame(frame, wall)` moves the
  runner and the host's `now_ms` to the target (LLP 1073, unchanged). The
  engine's input clock goes only to `wall`. The curves that the frame's
  receipts begin are pending until the same callback's tick starts them.
- **One sampling time.** Every sample is taken at `S = max(now, shown)`.
  That covers:
  - `advance`, `observe`, `observe_path` and a curve's `begin`;
  - a hold's pre-sample;
  - `frame` and `AnimationPlay::local` for a running play;
  - the hold a pause captures in `set_animations` (`S − start`);
  - a clock join's `boundary`;
  - the exit and flight deadline comparisons (`≤ S`).

  So a commit, pause or join between frames never paints behind what was
  presented. A release's spring still starts at the input's own time, and
  its first frame samples `v·(frame − release)`.
- **The motion tick and its latch.** `Host::tick(now, frame)` takes the wall
  (as today) and the frame instant (`link.targetTimestamp − t0`).
  - It advances `now`, then sets a frame latch.
  - While the latch is set, `Host::present` begins with an idempotent
    `present_frame(frame)`. So every curve born since the last present is
    started before the batch is sealed: by the tick itself, the height-driven
    layout, the arrange-settle path, or a resize handler's commit in `finish`.
  - The latch clears when `tick` returns.
  - Between frames the latch is unset, and a curve born then stays pending.
    No birth site is special-cased.
- **The asynchronous tick** carries both instants.
- **Timers keep their due times** (LLP 1073). A timer's commit is heard at its
  due time and its curves begin pending; a timer fired inside the display
  callback has them started by that callback's tick.
- **The link stays awake.** Pending curves, pending plays and a pending clock
  origin keep the engine non-quiescent, so the display link runs until the
  frame starts them.
- **The takeover floor.** The agent's clock takes over at
  `max(wall, runner, shown)` (D7).

**D6. A fling keeps its release instant.** `end_hold` with
`HoldEnd::Release` and a nonzero velocity never makes a pending curve. It
continues a gesture the user is watching, and a still first frame would drop
the fling's motion. Its local time zero is the release's input time
(`24563562f`).

A cancel, and a release with zero velocity, return to rest from the held
value. They take the same processing-time jump §1 measures, so they are
pending like an authored curve. (`Change::velocity` is not an Apple commit
path; kernel commits pass `None`.) A fling that retargets a key with a
pending curve reads it at `begin` (D3).

**D7. The switch: latched on the bridge, off at the agent's takeover.**

- **The latch.** A new ABI call, `exact_start_on_frame(rt, on)`, sets a latch
  on the bridge (the runtime), which outlives hosts. Every host reads it at
  `Engine::new`, before its first `sync.apply`: `Host::boot`, and the
  prepared host of `prepare_plan` / `prepare_module` that a reload or update
  commits. So boot's initial plays are pending before their first batch is
  emitted. Setting it while a host exists also sets that host's engine, and
  any prepared candidate's (whose stored batch is regenerated, below).
- **On.** `Session` sets it right after making the runtime, before boot, when
  it boots without an agent-owned clock (`clock == nil` and not
  `ExactEnv.agentFreezes`). The latch defaults off, so the Rust suite, the
  motion fixtures and every host test that never sets it keep today's timing
  exactly.
- **Off.** `Session.clock` gains a `didSet`: when it goes from `nil` to a
  value, it calls `exact_start_on_frame(rt, false)` before anything else runs
  on the agent's clock. That covers `take`, `land`, `data` and the stepped
  advance alike, not only `presentFrames(false)`.
  - The call carries the takeover instant, `exact_start_on_frame(rt, false,
    at)`. It runs `present_frame(at)`: both executors start what is pending
    at that one instant, sampled there, with no runner timer fired. It
    returns a batch, which Swift applies, so the held specs are replaced even
    by a bare `take`, which emits nothing else.
  - A prepared host's stored batch is regenerated when the latch changes, so
    a later commit of it installs started specs, not `"h":0`.
  - Already-started curves keep their starts.
  - With no host yet (`agentFreezes` sets the clock before boot), the call
    only clears the latch, which was off anyway.
- **macOS** shares `Session` and the rule. Its link is the viewport's and has
  a `targetTimestamp`. Its press feedback is `PressClock`, not the engine.

**D8. What is never pending.**

- *A play held by a drag timeline* (LLP 1057.003): its time is the source's.
- *A play joining a clock timeline that is already running* (LLP 1055.002):
  its phase is the clock's. This is decided per play by its actual join: an
  exit appended on a clock-associated node does not join, and is pending.
- *An idle clock's new origin is pending, explicitly, and sticky.*
  - When a join finds the clock idle, it sets the origin pending at the
    sampling time. Until the frame, the origin does not change.
  - Every further join to that clock enrolls in it and is pending, in any
    commit, including a play that resumes after a pause.
  - A pause counts as a busy member only once the origin has started.
  - A resume keeps its clock membership: it is counted before it becomes
    pending, so a lone paused member's resume does not make its clock look
    idle.
  - A pending origin keeps the engine non-quiescent, even when only paused
    plays joined it, so the frame comes.
  - The frame shifts the origin and every member still pending by the same
    amount, so they keep one phase.
  - A member that leaves before the frame (unbound, removed, re-bound to a
    drag timeline) is no longer shifted.
  - Unbinding a drag timeline resumes a play pending at the drag's local time
    only when `join_clock_node` does not claim it. A running clock wins, and
    a pending origin wins over the drag's local time.
- *A played transition* (`play_transition`, LLP 1076): its host plays it. On
  Apple none exist.
- *A change that begins no curve.* A first observation, and an
  `observe_settled` of an idle property. An `observe_settled` of a running or
  held property is an ordinary `observe`, and may begin a pending curve.
  Layout's first-seen rule is unchanged: a gained row's seeded layout
  animates its next move, and that curve is an ordinary pending `Running`.
- *Native UIKit motion the engine does not own:* `PressFeedback`, UIKit's zoom
  transition (LLP 1035.001), sheet presentation and keyboard animation.
  Nothing is started twice. Engine curves that accompany them now start at the
  next frame, not at the commit. That phase difference is at most the
  commit's processing time plus one interval, and §5's recordings measure it.

## 4. Not in scope

- **The web host.** The browser starts CSS transitions and WAAPI animations at
  the frame that applies them; the wasm host's lowered springs are WAAPI.
- **Linux and Windows.** Their frame loops differ. Queued if they show the
  same jump.
- **Reduced motion.** No change: a curve that does not run is not pending.

## 5. Tests and measurements

**Engine (`motion`), with the switch on unless stated:**

- **Transitions.**
  - Begun at t₀ and started at t₁: `from` at t₁, one interval's progress at
    t₁ + h.
  - A delay is kept, and a negative delay keeps its skip into the curve.
  - `observe_path`'s interruption and reversal read at `begin`.
- **Commits between frames.**
  - A commit on another key between t₀ and t₁ moves `now`, but nothing is
    presented mid-curve.
  - A 5 ms curve is not retired before t₁.
  - An interruption starts from `from` with the old curve's initial velocity.
- **Releases.**
  - A fling (nonzero velocity) is not pending, even beside a pending curve on
    the same node.
  - Its first sample includes `v·(t₁ − release)` when `shown` is already past
    the release.
  - A cancel and a zero-velocity release are pending.
- **The two clocks.**
  - `begin_hold`, `update_hold`, `end_hold` and the transform holds at an
    input time behind `shown` are accepted.
  - A commit at that time samples at `shown`.
- **Plays.**
  - A new play, a resume, a kept play, a pause before the frame, a drop and
    replace, duplicate names and a reorder each keep their own state.
  - A play paused at birth is not pending and stays paused after a start.
  - Drag bind and unbind behave as D3 says.
- **Exits.**
  - A sampled exit's values after the start, not only its end.
  - An exit beside an older pending transition: each starts on its own, and
    the exit's end is its plays' end.
  - Several commits before the frame append the exit once.
- **Clocks.** A busy-clock join is not pending. Two idle-clock joins in two
  commits before the frame both shift with the origin.
- **Quiescence and settle.**
  - `quiescent()` is false while only a lowered play is pending, and true
    after the start.
  - `settle_time` follows D1's formulas.
  - A frame earlier than a curve's `begin` leaves it pending.
- **Round 3's cases.**
  - A frame task followed by input at a wall time before its target is
    accepted.
  - After `present_frame`, an existing curve's presented value is its
    sample at the frame, not the earlier `advance`'s.
  - A pause, a path interruption and a clock join between frames never paint
    behind `shown`.
  - A lone paused clock member's resume, and a paused rebind, keep the
    clock's origin. An idle clock joined only by paused plays still gets its
    frame.
- **Switch off.** Identical to today: the existing suite, plus one equality
  test over a mixed sequence. Turning it off starts what is pending at the
  sampling time.

**Apple host (`host/apple`):**

- **Boot and reload.** With the latch on, boot's first batch holds a lowered
  play (`"h":0`) and reports `motion`; with it off, it is unchanged. The same
  for a prepared host that a reload commits.
- **Ticks.**
  - `tick` after a commit presents the start, then one interval.
  - A curve begun in a tick (height layout, a resize handler's commit, arrange
    settle) is started at that frame and presented once at its start.
- **Lowered specs.** Two commits, then one tick, leave one held spec, then one
  started spec at the frame instant.
- **Flights and exits.** A spring flight lands at its curve's start plus
  `land_after`. An exit's destroy comes after its own end.
- **Input behind the frame.** A transform drag and a height drag at a wall
  time behind the last frame instant are accepted, and their velocity is
  unchanged.
- **One instant.** A frame task and a motion tick in one callback use one
  instant.
- **The agent.** `advance` starts nothing. The takeover turns the latch off
  and returns the batch that starts what was pending. Its clock is at least
  `shown`.

**Swift (XCTest):**

- A tick in flight across two dropped callbacks, carrying sampled and lowered
  motion. The two agree from the next tick that is applied on time (D4's
  missed target).
- A filtered SVG's held play draws the same pixels at two renders some
  milliseconds apart (D4).
- A bare `take` after a long gap starts both executors at the takeover instant.
- Prepare with the latch on, take over, then commit: the committed batch has
  started specs.

**Recordings:**

- **Bluesky clone.** A simulator recording of the lightbox open, before and
  after, frame-matched against Reanimated's curve. The first displayed frame
  goes from p ≈ 0.15–0.22 to 0, then one interval per frame.
- **Signal clone** (`tools/motion-trace.mjs --trigger`). Trigger-aligned
  recordings of whole sequences, before and after, including native motion
  (the keyboard, a sheet) where a sequence has it.
  - The trigger-aligned onset moves by the measured δ: the commit's
    processing plus the wait for the frame.
  - Curve shapes match within their existing tolerances.
  - The one choreography that pairs a timer with an exit is checked
    explicitly: Signal's `runMenuAction`, 400 ms after a dismissal whose menu
    exit is also 400 ms. Under this RFC the timer still fires at its logical
    time, and the exit ends δ later. The recording reports by how many frames
    the action's motion overlaps the exit's last frames. A visible overlap
    would be a reason to revisit D5's timer rule, not to hide it.

## 6. Implementation notes

- `host/apple/src/host.rs` is at the 1,500-line cap. The host's part (the
  tick's start, the exit and flight reads, the latch) goes in a module of its
  own; `tick` gains one call.
- The engine's part lives in `motion/src/engine` beside `animate.rs` and
  `hold.rs`. `Running` gains `pending`, `AnimationPlay` gains `pending`, and
  the engine gains `shown`.
- The ABI's tick and frame gain the second instant (the frame's for a tick,
  the wall's for a frame). `exact_start_on_frame` takes the takeover instant
  and returns a batch.

## 7. Questions for review

- **Q1:** Is any reader still sampling at `now` alone (D5's list)?
- **Q2:** Is the tick latch (D5) enough to start every curve born inside a
  tick, including a nested `present`?
- **Q3:** D8's sticky pending origin: is every join, pause, resume and
  rebind path covered?
