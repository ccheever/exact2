# LLP 1003.001: Motion starts at the first frame that shows it

**Type:** RFC
**Status:** Draft r1. Round 1: Astra NEEDS REWORK, Grok NEEDS REWORK (`llp/reviews/llp-1003.001-first-frame.{astra,grok}.md`), not yet folded; handed to x2-perf for r2 on 2026-10-07. Approved in principle by the lead on 2026-10-06 ("the first-presented-frame clock fix in exact2 is the right fix"), design round requested before code.
**Systems:** Motion (`exact-motion`: the engine's transitions and plays), Apple host (`host/apple`: tick, flights, exits, lowered box animations, the ABI), Apple Swift (`Session`, `Agent`)
**Author:** Claude (Opus 5.5), for the Bluesky clone's lightbox
**Date:** 2026-10-06
**Related:** LLP 1003 §5 (transition semantics; `Engine::advance` is a seek), LLP 1055 D5/D7 (plays; sampled and lowered executors), LLP 1055.002 (clock timelines: synced phases), LLP 1057.003 (drag timelines: held plays), LLP 1063 (exits, `layout-transition`), LLP 1013.000 D4 (flights on one engine curve), LLP 1073 D4 (the display drives frame tasks), LLP 1099 (UIKit's springs: engine curves, not Core Animation's)

## 1. The problem, measured

A curve begun by a commit starts at that commit's engine time. This applies to
a transition's `Running`, a flight's `Layout` curve, a new keyframe play, and an
exit. On iOS the first frame that shows the curve comes after two things: the
rest of the commit's own work (layout, mounting, image decode), and up to one
display interval. Its first visible sample is therefore already well into the
curve.

- **Simulator, unthrottled (Bluesky clone, a 402×874 iPhone, iOS 27):** a
  lightbox open on a 489 ms critical spring. The flight begins at t = 0, and its
  first displayed frame is 24–30 ms later. That frame shows p = 0.15–0.22. The
  engine clock was already ~12 ms past the commit, so the curve had effectively
  started before it.
- **Phone (iPhone, ProMotion), from a recording of the clone's b08:** the
  backdrop's luminance on its first dimmed frame matches its fade curve
  25–45 ms in.

Reanimated starts an animation at the timestamp of the frame in which it is
set: `valueSetter` calls `onStart` with `__frameTimestamp`, then `step` at that
same timestamp. That frame shows the start, and the next shows one interval.
Core Animation starts an implicit animation at its transaction's commit to the
render server, at the end of the run-loop turn, after the main thread's work.
Neither shows the commit's processing time as motion. exact2 does, so every
open, close and press response on a phone jumps on its first frame.

## 2. The rule

> On a host whose display drives the clock, a curve begun between frames
> starts at the first presented frame after it. That frame shows its start
> (progress 0); the next shows one interval.

- **One rule for every curve kind:** transitions (springs and easings, delays
  included), flights, new keyframe plays and exits. Their timing functions,
  durations, delays and ends are unchanged; only their start moves.
- **A commit made inside a frame's tick** (a timer due at it, a frame task)
  begins its curves at that frame's time. The shift is zero.
- **Off under the agent.** The agent's seekable clock, the motion fixtures and
  every existing host test keep today's timing exactly. The rule is a host
  property, enabled by the native session (D5).

### Why progress 0 at the first frame (Q3)

The first presented frame shows the curve's start, not one interval past the
commit.

- **The reference app does this.** Reanimated's `step(currentTimestamp)` runs
  in the frame where the value is set. Bluesky's lightbox sets it inside a
  `requestAnimationFrame`, so its open is "start, then one interval per frame".
- **It is deterministic.** "One interval since the commit" depends on where
  in the display interval the commit fell and on how long the commit took,
  which are the very things this RFC removes from the curve. Starting at the
  frame's own time makes the first presented sample 0 and every later one a
  whole number of intervals.
- **It is Core Animation's in effect.** A CA animation committed at the end of
  the turn gets `beginTime ≈ CACurrentMediaTime()` at commit, and the render
  server's first frame is the next vsync: under one interval, usually close to
  zero, never the commit's processing time. Matching CA exactly would need the
  render server's frame time, which the main thread does not have. Reanimated's
  form is the nearer, measurable one.

## 3. Decisions

**D1. The engine records what it began.** An opt-in flag,
`Engine::set_start_on_frame(on)`, is off by default. While it is on, the
engine records every curve it begins at its current time (`self.now`):

- each `Running` that `observe` or `end_hold` creates, keyed by
  `(node, property)`. A later begin on the same key replaces the record.
- each `AnimationPlay` that `set_animations` adds as new (not a matched one,
  which keeps its start), and each one `play_exit` adds, recorded as
  `(node, start)`.

Turning the flag off clears the records.

**D2. The first frame starts them.** `Engine::start_pending(now)`, before
`advance(now)`, shifts every recorded curve whose begin is before `now` by
`now − begin`:

- a `Running`'s `start` moves by the shift (the start includes any delay, so
  the delay is kept);
- a play's `start` moves by the shift if it is still the same play (same node,
  `start == begin`, not held).

It returns the shift per node, `Vec<(u64, f64)>`, and clears the records. A
record whose curve has ended, been replaced or been removed is dropped. The
shift is never negative: a begin at or after `now` stays.

**D3. What is never shifted.**

- *A play held by a drag timeline* (LLP 1057.003): its time is the source's
  position, not the clock's.
- *A play joined to a clock timeline* (LLP 1055.002): its phase is the
  clock's; shifting it would break the sync it exists for.
- *A played transition* (`play_transition`, LLP 1076): its host plays it. On
  Apple none exist today.
- *Native UIKit motion the engine does not own:* `PressFeedback`'s press
  scale, UIKit's zoom transition (`navigationSource`, LLP 1035.001), sheet
  presentation and keyboard animation. Core Animation or UIKit starts these at
  their own commit. They are not engine curves and are not touched. Nothing
  is shifted twice (Q1).

**D4. The host follows the shifts.** `Host::tick(now)` calls `start_pending`
before its `advance`. For each shifted node:

- a leaving view's exit end (`presence.leaving[*].end`) moves by the shift, so
  its destroy waits for the shifted exit;
- a spring flight's `lands_at` moves by the shift, so it lands where the curve
  does;
- a node whose box animations are lowered to Core Animation (`svg.rs`,
  `box_motion`) is marked for re-emission. Its specs carry the play's start, so
  the new spec moves Core Animation's `beginTime` (`SvgScene.swift`:
  `beginTime = t0 + start + delay`). A spec Core Animation already started
  (committed in the earlier batch) is replaced by the shifted one before its
  first displayed frame.

`Host::advance`, `Host::frame` and `dispatch_at` never call `start_pending`.
Only the display link's motion tick does.

**D5. The switch.** A new ABI call, `exact_start_on_frame(rt, on)`, maps to
`Host::start_on_frame(on)` and then to the engine's flag.

- `Session` turns it on when it boots without an agent clock (`clock == nil`,
  not `ExactEnv.agentMode`).
- `Agent.swift` turns it off where it already calls `presentFrames(false)`,
  when the agent's clock takes over.
- macOS shares `Session`, so it gets the same rule.

**D6. A pending curve interrupted before its first frame (Q2).** A commit
between frames that retargets a key whose curve is still pending samples it at
its begin time, not at `now`. The user never saw it move: it was at its `from`
value, at rest, and the new curve starts there. A pending play that the new
commit's `set_animations` keeps (matched by name) keeps its start and its
record; one it drops is dropped. A new curve on another property of the same
node is independent and recorded separately. Two commits between frames that
begin curves on different keys are both started by the same first frame, with
different shifts, so they start together.

## 4. Not in scope

- **The web host.** The browser starts CSS transitions and WAAPI animations at
  the frame that applies them; the wasm host's lowered springs are WAAPI.
- **Linux and Windows.** Their frame loops differ. Queued if they show the
  same jump.
- **Reduced motion.** No change: a curve that does not run has nothing to
  shift.

## 5. Tests

**Engine (`motion`):**

- With the flag on, a transition begun at t₀ and started at t₁ samples its
  `from` at t₁ and one interval's progress at t₁ + h.
- The same for an easing with a delay: the delay is kept.
- A new play and an exit; their end times move by the shift.
- An interruption between t₀ and t₁ starts the new curve from `from` (D6).
- A drag-timeline-bound play and a clock-joined play are not shifted.
- The flag off: identical to today (the existing suite, plus one equality
  test over a mixed sequence).

**Apple host (`host/apple`):**

- `tick` after a commit presents the start, then one interval.
- A spring flight lands later by the shift.
- An exit's destroy comes later by the shift.
- A lowered box animation's spec is re-emitted with the shifted start.
- `advance` (the agent's path) does not shift.

**Recordings:**

- The clone's lightbox open on the simulator, frame-matched against
  Reanimated's curve (`motion-trace.mjs`), before and after. The first
  displayed frame goes from p ≈ 0.15–0.22 to 0, then one interval.
- The Signal clone's frame-matched motion (its `tools/motion-trace.mjs`
  references) measured before and after on the same simulator. Onset-aligned
  curves should match within their existing tolerances; only the onset moves,
  by under a frame.

## 6. Questions for review

- **Q1:** Is anything on Apple already started at Core Animation's commit and
  also an engine curve, so it would be shifted twice? D3 claims not: LLP
  1099's springs are engine curves sampled per frame, and Zoom and press
  feedback are UIKit's.
- **Q2:** Is D6 right for a commit that lands between frames while an earlier
  pending curve on the same node is unpresented? What about the CA spec of a
  lowered play re-emitted twice before a frame?
- **Q3:** Is p = 0 at the first presented frame (Reanimated) the right single
  choice over one interval since the commit (CA-like)?
- **Q4:** Is a per-node shift right for exits and flights, which the host
  times? Or should the engine expose the shifted end times instead?
