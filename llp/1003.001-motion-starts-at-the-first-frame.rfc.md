# LLP 1003.001: Motion starts at the first frame that shows it

**Type:** RFC
**Status:** Draft r2, for design review round 2. Approved in principle by the lead on 2026-10-06 ("the first-presented-frame clock fix in exact2 is the right fix"); design round requested before code.
- r1 (`c166d914d`, bsky3) was reviewed blind. Astra (`gpt-6-astra`, reasoning effort max): NEEDS REWORK, `llp/reviews/llp-1003.001-first-frame.astra.md`. Grok 4.7 (xhigh): NEEDS REWORK, `llp/reviews/llp-1003.001-first-frame.grok.md`.
- r2 (x2-perf2, 2026-10-07) takes every round-1 finding (§8). The design changes from "record what began, shift it later" to a pending state that every curve carries until the first frame starts it.
**Systems:** Motion (`exact-motion`: transitions, path transitions, plays, exits, clock timelines), Apple host (`host/apple`: tick, commits, flights, exits, lowered specs, the ABI), Apple Swift (`Frames`, `Session`, `Agent`)

**Date:** 2026-10-06 (r1), 2026-10-07 (r2)
**Related:** LLP 1003 §5 (transition semantics; `Engine::advance` is a seek), LLP 1055 D5/D7 (plays; sampled and lowered executors), LLP 1055.002 (clock timelines), LLP 1057.003 (drag timelines), LLP 1063 (exits, `layout-transition`), LLP 1013.000 D4 (flights on one engine curve), LLP 1073 D2/D4 (presented frames; frame tasks at the target time), LLP 1099 D5 (additive spring components), LLP 1080.000 §12 (the agent's clock takeover)

## 1. The problem, measured

A curve begun by a commit starts at that commit's engine time. This applies to
a transition's `Running`, a flight's `Layout` curve, a new keyframe play, and an
exit. On iOS the first frame that shows the curve comes after two things: the
rest of the commit's own work (layout, mounting, image decode), and up to one
display interval. Its first visible sample is therefore already well into the
curve.

- **Simulator, unthrottled (Bluesky clone, a 402×874 iPhone, iOS 27):** a
  lightbox open on a 489 ms critical spring. The flight begins at t = 0, and its
  first displayed frame is 24–30 ms later. That frame shows p = 0.15–0.22. The
  engine clock was already ~12 ms past the commit, so the curve had effectively
  started before it.
- **Phone (iPhone, ProMotion), from a recording of the clone's b08:** the
  backdrop's luminance on its first dimmed frame matches its fade curve
  25–45 ms in.

Reanimated starts an animation at the timestamp of the frame in which it is
set: `valueSetter` calls `onStart` with `__frameTimestamp`, then `step` at that
same timestamp. That frame shows the start, and the next shows one interval.
Core Animation starts an implicit animation at its transaction's commit to the
render server, at the end of the run-loop turn, after the main thread's work.
Neither shows the commit's processing time as motion. exact2 does, so every
open, close and press response on a phone jumps on its first frame.

## 2. The rule

> On a host whose display drives the clock, a curve an author's commit begins
> between frames is **pending** until the next presented frame. While pending
> it does not move: every reader sees it at its local time zero. The frame
> starts it: its local time zero becomes that frame's instant, and the frame
> shows it there. The next frame shows one interval.

- **Local time zero, not "progress 0".** The invariant is elapsed local time
  zero at the frame, followed by ordinary evaluation (§3, D8). For an easing or
  spring transition with a delay ≥ 0, a flight, and a new play or exit, the
  first frame shows the curve's start. A negative delay starts partway into the
  curve, as it does today, by the same amount. A step timing, a reversed
  direction or a fill shows whatever that local time shows.
- **Release starts keep the release instant (D6).** A curve that continues a
  gesture (`end_hold`, or a change carrying a release velocity) is never
  pending. The first frame after a fling already includes v·Δt.
- **Off under the agent's clock (D7).** The agent's seekable clock, the motion
  fixtures and the existing host tests keep today's timing exactly. The rule
  is a host property, which the native session turns on and turns off at the
  agent's clock takeover.
- **The frame's instant is its target time (D5).** That is the display's
  `targetTimestamp`, the time LLP 1073 already gives frame tasks.

### Why local time zero at the first frame

- **The reference app does this.** Reanimated's `step(currentTimestamp)` runs
  in the frame where the value is set. Bluesky's lightbox sets it inside a
  `requestAnimationFrame`, so its open is "start, then one interval per frame".
- **It is deterministic.** "One interval since the commit" depends on where
  in the display interval the commit fell and on how long the commit took,
  which are the very things this RFC removes from the curve. With the flag on,
  every sample is a closed-form function of the inputs and the frame instants
  the host supplied. Variable refresh and skipped frames make those instants
  uneven; they are inputs, not something the engine reads (it never reads
  `CACurrentMediaTime`).
- **It is the nearer of the two native behaviours.** A CA animation committed
  at the end of the turn starts at that commit and is first shown at the next
  vsync. Matching CA exactly would need the render server's frame time, which
  the main thread does not have. Reanimated's form is the nearer, measurable
  one. This is a behavioural choice, not an equivalence to CA.

## 3. Decisions

**D1. A pending curve does not move.** While `Engine::set_start_on_frame(true)`
holds, each curve an author's commit begins is pending. Pending state belongs
to the curve itself, not to a record keyed by node:

- *A transition.* A `Running` that `observe` or `observe_path` begins carries
  `pending: Some(begin)`, where `begin` is the engine time it began. Every
  reading of it reads at `begin`, whatever `now` is: `sample`,
  `easing_progress`, and so `advance`, `frame`, an interruption's current
  value and velocity, a hold's acquisition, and CSS's reversal arithmetic.
  `advance` therefore never moves it, never presents a mid-curve value, and
  never retires it. The one exception is a zero-length curve with no delay,
  which is done at local time zero as it is today.
- *A play.* An `AnimationPlay` that `set_animations` adds or resumes, or that
  `play_exit` appends, is pending. It is held at its local time, `hold:
  Some(local)` with `pending: true`. `local` is 0 for a new play or exit and
  the held time for a resume. A sampling host samples it there. A lowering
  host emits it held (D4), so Core Animation shows it at that local time with
  `speed` 0.

Any pending curve keeps the engine non-quiescent (`quiescent()` is false while
one exists), and `settle_time` counts a pending curve as starting at the
engine's current time.

**D2. The first frame starts them.** `Engine::start_pending(frame)` starts
every pending curve at the frame's instant:

- a `Running`'s `start` moves by `frame − begin`, so a delay is kept and a
  negative delay keeps its skip into the curve; then `pending` clears;
- a pending play's `start` becomes `frame − local`, its `hold` becomes `None`
  and `pending` clears.

The shift is never negative. A begin at the frame's own instant is shifted by
0. Because a pending curve was read at `begin`, its sample at the frame after
starting equals the sample every reader already had. The frame needs no
resampling, and nothing presented changes value at the start.

`start_pending` returns the nodes whose plays started, so the host can
re-emit their lowered specs (D4). Exits and flights read their own end times
back from the engine (D3); they are not told a per-node shift.

Turning the flag off starts every pending curve at the engine's current time,
then stops making new ones.

**D3. Each curve keeps its own clock.**

- *Interruption before the first frame.* A commit that retargets a key whose
  curve is pending reads it at `begin`. For delay ≥ 0 that is its `from`, with
  its initial velocity (zero for an easing, the declared or inherited velocity
  for a spring), so the user, who never saw it move, sees the new curve start
  where the old one stood. The new curve is pending in turn. A new curve on
  another property, or on another node, is independent; two commits between
  frames that begin curves on different keys are both started by the same
  frame.
- *Plays through `set_animations`.* Pending state follows the matched play,
  using CSS Animations 2 §4.3's name matching from the end of the list, so
  reorders and duplicate names keep each play's own state. A pending play
  that the next commit keeps stays pending with its local time. One that the
  commit pauses becomes an authored pause at that local time (not pending).
  One that the commit drops is gone, and its replacement is a new pending
  play. A resumed play is pending at the local time it was held at, so it
  continues from there at the first frame instead of losing the commit's
  processing time.
- *Exits.* `play_exit` appends its plays after the node's existing ones,
  which keep their own starts and ages. A leaving view's end is read from the
  engine: the latest `start + end_time` over that leaving view's exit plays.
  While any of them is pending it has no end, so its destroy waits. Once
  started, the host takes the end again (`Engine::exit_end(node, count)`,
  `None` while pending). No per-node shift is applied, so a node with an
  older pending transition and a newer exit gets each its own start.
- *Flights.* A flight keeps its landing as an offset from its curve's start:
  today's `land_after` for a spring, `None` otherwise. It lands at the
  `Layout` curve's own start plus that offset. The start is read with
  `Engine::curve_start(node, property)`, which is `None` while pending, and
  then a flight does not land. The early-landing criterion (UIKit's settling,
  not the engine's rest) is unchanged.
- *LLP 1099's additive components.* When D5's component list lands, pending
  state belongs to each component. A component born in a commit between
  frames is pending at its own begin. A pending component that a further
  retarget leaves in the list stays pending, and both are started by the same
  frame (each at local time zero, both offsets summed at their own clocks). An
  established, started spring promoted into component 0 keeps its start and
  age. A component is never pending because a sibling is. Until stage 2
  lands, one `Running` per key is the only component.

**D4. Lowered Core Animation specs are held until the start.** A pending
play's lowered spec is emitted with `"h": local` (`svg_lower::specs` already
writes `hold`), so `CssAnimations.make` adds it at `speed` 0 and `timeOffset`
local, or adds none before a delay without a backwards fill. That shows the
same value the engine reads.

- **No early run.** Core Animation never runs a spec ahead of the engine.
  Nothing has to replace it before the first vsync, so an asynchronous tick
  (`asyncFills`) that lands a turn later is safe.
- **The start.** At `start_pending` the started node's spec changes: `"h"`
  becomes `null`, and `"s"` becomes the shifted start. `box_sent` sees the
  new string, and `CssAnimations.apply` replaces the held animation by id
  with one whose `beginTime` is the frame instant. If that batch lands later
  than its frame, the CA animation and the engine-sampled curves are late
  together, by the same amount.
- **Every lowering path.** This covers box transforms and background colour
  (LLP 1055.001), SVG scene tracks on iOS and macOS, and macOS's lowered
  opacity, because all of them write their specs through `svg_lower::specs`
  from the play's `hold` and `start`.

**D5. The frame instant, and the tick's order.** The display link's motion
tick takes `link.targetTimestamp − t0` as its instant: the frame's
presentation time, the same instant the frame tasks of LLP 1073 already
receive (`Frames.tick`'s `frameNow`).

- **One callback, one instant.** The timers due by that instant, the frame
  tasks, and the motion tick all run in the same callback, and all use it.
- **The tick's order** (`Host::tick`):
  1. seek the engine (pending curves stay put);
  2. run the work that can begin curves in a tick (height-driven layout);
  3. call `start_pending(frame)`;
  4. present.

  A curve that step 2 begins starts in the same frame with a shift of 0. It is
  never shown at its start twice.
- **Timers keep their logical due times** (LLP 1073: a timer's due time is not
  the frame's). A timer's commit is still heard at its due time. The curves
  it begins between frames are pending, and the frame starts them.
- **The engine may run ahead of the runner.** The engine's clock may now be up
  to one interval ahead of the runner's (wall-clock input times are unchanged,
  LLP 1096 D3). Every engine seek on Apple takes the larger of its time and
  the engine's: `commit_tree`'s receipt seeks already do, and its final seek
  and `end_hold`'s seek do too. `Host::tick` moves only the engine to the
  target. The host's `now_ms` is not moved by a motion tick, so an event's
  `now()` is not moved into the future by one. (A frame task's
  `Host::frame` already moves it to the target, LLP 1073; that is unchanged.)
- **No tick, no start.** `Host::advance`, `Host::frame` and `dispatch_at` never
  call `start_pending`; only the motion tick does.
- **The link stays awake.** Because pending curves keep the engine
  non-quiescent (D1), a commit that begins only lowered plays reports
  `motion`, so the display link runs and its tick starts them. After the
  start, a lowered-only play leaves `animating` again, and the link stops as
  it does today.

**D6. Release starts keep the release instant.** `end_hold` (release or
cancel) never makes a pending curve, nor does an `observe` whose change
carries a release velocity (`Change::velocity`). These continue a gesture
the user is watching: the presented value was the held one, so there is no
processing-time jump to remove. A still first frame would also drop the
fling's motion. Their local time zero is the release's own instant (the
input's time, `24563562f`), so the first frame shows v·Δt. A release that
retargets a key with a pending curve reads it at `begin` (D3).

**D7. The switch, and the agent's takeover.** A new ABI call,
`exact_start_on_frame(rt, on)`, sets `Host::start_on_frame(on)`, which sets
the engine's flag.

- **On.** `Session` turns it on right after making the runtime and before
  boot's first commit, when it boots without an agent-owned clock (`clock ==
  nil` and not `ExactEnv.agentFreezes`). So the initial plays are pending
  before their first batch is emitted. A development reload makes a new
  runtime, which takes the same call.
- **Off.** It turns off at the agent's clock takeover: wherever
  `Session.clock` goes from `nil` to a value, a `didSet` calls
  `exact_start_on_frame(rt, false)` before anything else runs on the agent's
  clock. That covers `take`, `land`, `data` and the stepped advance alike,
  not only `presentFrames(false)`. Turning it off starts every pending curve
  at the engine's time (D2), so nothing is left waiting for a frame the agent
  never presents. Already-started curves keep their starts.
- **macOS** shares `Session` and gets the same rule. Its link is the
  viewport's and has a `targetTimestamp` too. Its press feedback is
  `PressClock`, not the engine, and is not touched.

**D8. What is never pending.**

- *A play held by a drag timeline* (LLP 1057.003): its time is the source's
  position, not the clock's.
- *A play joined to a busy clock timeline* (LLP 1055.002): its phase is the
  clock's. This is decided per play by its actual join, not per node: an exit
  that `play_exit` appends on a clock-associated node is an ordinary
  time-based play, and is pending.
- *An idle clock's new origin.* When a join sets a clock's origin because the
  clock was idle, the origin is pending with the plays that joined it, so the
  cycle's phase zero is the first frame. Shifting the origin moves exactly
  those plays, the clock's only live members, by the same amount.
- *A played transition* (`play_transition`, LLP 1076): its host plays it. On
  Apple none exist today.
- *A first observation and an unauthored change.* A property's first value,
  and `observe_settled`, begin no curve. Layout's first-seen rule is
  unchanged: a gained row's seeded layout (`seed_layout`) animates its next
  move, and that curve is an ordinary pending `Running`.
- *Native UIKit motion the engine does not own:* `PressFeedback`, UIKit's
  zoom transition (LLP 1035.001), sheet presentation and keyboard animation.
  Core Animation or UIKit starts these at their own commit. They are not
  engine curves, so nothing is started twice. Engine curves that accompany
  them now start at the next frame, not at the commit. That phase difference
  is at most the commit's processing time plus one interval, and the
  recordings in §5 measure it.

## 4. Not in scope

- **The web host.** The browser starts CSS transitions and WAAPI animations at
  the frame that applies them; the wasm host's lowered springs are WAAPI.
- **Linux and Windows.** Their frame loops differ. Queued if they show the
  same jump.
- **Reduced motion.** No change: a curve that does not run is not pending.

## 5. Tests and measurements

**Engine (`motion`), all with the flag on unless stated:**

- A transition begun at t₀ and started at t₁ samples its `from` at t₁ and one
  interval's progress at t₁ + h. An easing with a delay keeps the delay. A
  negative delay keeps its skip into the curve.
- A second commit on another key between t₀ and t₁ advances the engine, but
  leaves the pending curve at its start: nothing is presented mid-curve, and
  a 5 ms curve is not retired before t₁.
- An interruption of a pending curve starts the new one from `from` with the
  old curve's initial velocity. A pending spring with authored velocity hands
  that velocity on.
- `end_hold` with a release velocity is not pending: its sample at t₁ includes
  v·(t₁ − release). The same when another curve on that node is pending.
- Plays: a new play, a resume, a matched kept play, a pause before the frame,
  a drop and replace, duplicate names and a reorder each keep their own state.
- An exit with an older pending transition on the same node: each starts at
  its own frame; the exit's end is the exit plays' end, not a per-node shift.
- A drag-bound play and a busy-clock join are not pending. An idle clock's
  origin starts at the first frame.
- `quiescent()` is false while only a lowered play is pending, and true after
  it starts.
- The flag off: identical to today. That covers the existing suite, plus one
  equality test over a mixed sequence. Turning it off starts pending curves
  at the engine's time.

**Apple host (`host/apple`):**

- `tick` after a commit presents the start, then one interval.
- Height-driven layout that begins a curve inside `tick` presents its start
  once.
- A lowered-only play's first batch has `"h":0` and `motion` true. The tick's
  batch replaces it with `"h":null` and the frame's start. Two commits, then
  one tick, leave a single held spec, then a single started one.
- A spring flight lands at its curve's start plus `land_after`. An exit's
  destroy comes after its own end.
- A frame task and a motion tick in one callback use one instant.
- `advance` (the agent's path) does not start anything. The takeover `didSet`
  turns the flag off before the first agent-clock batch, and starts what was
  pending.

**Recordings:**

- **Bluesky clone.** A simulator recording of the lightbox open, before and
  after, frame-matched against Reanimated's curve. The first displayed frame
  goes from p ≈ 0.15–0.22 to 0, then one interval per frame.
- **Signal clone** (`tools/motion-trace.mjs --trigger`). A trigger-aligned
  recording of whole sequences, before and after, including native motion
  (the keyboard, a sheet) where a sequence has it. The trigger-aligned onset
  moves by the measured δ (processing plus the wait for the frame). The
  curve shapes match within their existing tolerances. The one choreography
  that pairs a timer with an exit is checked explicitly. That is Signal's
  `runMenuAction`, 400 ms after a dismissal whose menu exit is also 400 ms:
  under this RFC the timer still fires at its logical time and the exit ends
  δ later. The recording reports by how many frames the action's motion
  overlaps the exit's last frames. A visible overlap would be a reason to
  revisit D5's timer rule, not to hide it.

## 6. Implementation notes

- `host/apple/src/host.rs` is at the 1,500-line cap. The host's part (the
  start step, exit and flight reads, the switch) goes in a module of its own,
  and `tick` gains one call.
- The engine's part lives in `motion/src/engine` beside `animate.rs` and
  `hold.rs`. `Running` gains one field; `AnimationPlay` gains `pending`.

## 7. Questions for review

- **Q1:** Is a curve's own pending state (D1), read at `begin` by every
  reader, enough to close round 1's "pending curves advance before the start"
  class? Is any engine path still reading a `Running` at `now` without going
  through `sample` or `easing_progress`?
- **Q2:** D6 exempts every `end_hold` and any change carrying velocity. Should
  a cancel (zero velocity) be pending instead, as an author-like return?
- **Q3:** D5 moves the engine, but not the runner, to the frame's target time.
  Does any Apple path compare the engine's time with the host's `now_ms` and
  break when the engine is ahead by under one interval?
- **Q4:** D8 makes an idle clock's origin pending. Is "the plays that joined
  an idle clock are its only live members" true for every join path?
