## Summary

Ink is now measured against egui, GPUI and the native hosts on the same screen, the same
input and the same probe, on the Mac and on Windows. It starts fastest on Windows, matches the
field on cadence, sits within GPUI's range on fling CPU on the Mac, and costs more than egui
during a fling on both platforms. The traces say where the cost is and most of it is plumbing
around the renderer. This document orders the work: first the numbers the program will be
judged by, published and reproducible, with the counters that turn its leads into facts;
beside them the dated obligation LLP 1149 already carries, stated as LLP 1149 wrote it, with
the one part this document asks to renegotiate put as a question; then the fixes that need
no design change, each with its contract and prediction; then the design changes, as
amendments to LLP 1149 with their contracts written; then the platform layers. It says how
each stage is measured and what it will not do.

Off the main thread G4 has 42 ms/s more process CPU that no trace attributes: ImageIO decodes
at the drawn size (14 ms/s in S1.4's normal run, the same code as egui's arm), the present
worker, the Metal driver's threads; the staging ring's waits and the completion wait are
blocked time, not CPU. On the GPU, per frame in the control trace: the scene's command buffer
0.23 ms; the pass that samples the compose target into the drawable 1.0 ms (S1.4's normal
run: 1.46). A one-quad pass costing four times the scene is not proportional to its work and
no format conversion explains it (both targets are unorm); Stage 0 explains it (the GPU's
clock state, or a wait inside the measured interval) before M3's saving stands.

Three leads the reviews found in the code, each a hypothesis until Stage 0's counter says:

- **The viewport-target pool** (`host/ink/src/layer.rs`) reuses a target as soon as its last
  GPU use completes and evicts a target unused for 120 calls of `begin()`, so idle time does
  not age it; a lagging worker therefore makes a frame allocate fresh targets that nothing
  frees for 120 frames. The targets are shared-storage textures, about 10 MiB at 1600×1600;
  335 MiB is 34 of them; G6b found 1,201 targets on Windows during the repaint churn, which
  G6c removed. Against it: S1.4's tracked textures total 61 MiB in the normal run, the
  footprint falls to 246 MiB by the end of the fling phase, and egui shows a 320 MiB transient
  with no such pool. So a second cause stays open; Stage 0 reads `layer_targets` and
  `layer_bytes` as timestamped gauges beside a `vmmap` of the same run.
- **A kept row is reused only at whole-device-pixel offsets** (`Ink::reusable`,
  `host/linux/src/ink/rows.rs`): rows are recorded in row space relative to their unrounded
  recording origin and replayed by a per-instance translate, but each glyph was rasterized at a
  quarter-pixel horizontal phase and a truncated y chosen at record time, so a replay whose
  shift from the recording is fractional would place the wrong raster. AppKit's momentum
  deltas are fractional. If most frames fail the test, rows re-record every frame, which would
  look exactly like the painter walking every node; the kernel's change receipts already reach
  the painter, which derives a row's stale and suspect state from them, and a valid kept row is
  not walked today.
- **On Windows, clip masks were rebuilt per paint** (G6b's largest named main-thread item);
  G6c cut the paints but no trace of the G6c build exists yet.

### Stage 1: fixes within the design

These amend one table row of LLP 1149 (M5, D7's pacing row) and one policy sentence (M4a,
D2.5's pool); the RFC records their contracts and predictions.

- **M1, coalesce the polling; service pending work independently.** Input delivery stays
  per event: a wheel or pointer event applies its input and advances the clock when it
  arrives, as now. What changes is the polling: the runner's command queue, hatches and image
  reports are pumped once per display period while the display link runs, and that pass is
  `draw`'s own; the pass `about_to_wait` runs on every loop turn is skipped while the link
  runs. Pending work is serviced when it arrives, in order: a due timer on its wake, a hatch
  or executor reply on its wake, an image report on its wake; a wake whose cause the period's
  pass already served does nothing. A timer's latency is therefore unchanged; what is removed
  is the redundant pumping of empty queues. The headless agent is untouched (requests are
  serviced synchronously; `clock` and `settle` step the runner directly), and so is the
  live-mode agent request, which keeps its synchronous service. Predicted, if Stage 0 confirms
  the 12.6 ms/s as near-empty passes: about 1.2 ms/s of passes remain (348 draws' own
  passes, which the draw row may already contain, so the saving may be the whole 12.6), the
  Mac main thread 44 → ~33 ms/s; the necessary timer, reply and image turns that remain are
  what Stage 0 counts.
- **M2, nothing wakes at rest** except a due timer, a reply or an image. Predicted after the
  rest trace names the wakeups: Mac ≤ 0.4 ms/s; Windows no tick in ten seconds.
  M2b (2026-10-30, `41b339e758`): sampled steps wake at their deadlines; G5m caret falls
  from 119.74 to 2.00 presents/s, 154.320 to 27.148 CPU ms/s and 0.718 to 0.019 W
  whole-machine GPU power. The predicted CPU below 5 ms/s missed; bar remains near
  120 presents/s. The caret workload has five app timer firings per second, so it does
  not qualify M2's rest gate. Its service and display-link costs still need the rest
  trace. Flat spans of `linear()` conservatively demand every frame; only steps and
  equal-valued keyframe spans sleep. See
  [G5m, after M2b](../bench/heavy-list/gpui/RESULTS.md#g5m-after-m2b).
- **M4a, the pool's policy**, if and only if Stage 0's counters show idle targets, not
  targets held by incomplete GPU work: retain for the frames in flight plus a short grace.
  This amends D2.5's sentence that the pool grows to the high-water mark and is trimmed on
  warning and background (§7 A6). Predicted: the Mac peak footprint down by the idle targets'
  bytes; CPU unknown, since trimming adds allocation. **M4b, one copy per upload** into a
  per-frame staging arena fenced on that frame's completion. Predicted: at most the 6.5 ms per
  ten seconds of upload preparation now on the main thread; a small number, honestly.
- **M5, the display link.** During a phased gesture the Mac carrier keeps the link running

### Stage 2: design changes, as amendments to LLP 1149

- **M3, render into the drawable where the frame allows.** Today every frame renders into a
  compose target and a second full-viewport pass samples it into the drawable; D2.5 needs
  that target for frames with a backdrop filter or a blend mode (a swap-chain image cannot be
  sampled) and D7 for readback. Amendment A1:
  - *The choice is made before the first flush.* The predicate is every node for which the
    painter would call `destination()`: a backdrop filter, a background material (which the
    painter turns into a backdrop blur), a blend mode on a node or on an image, and an SVG
    island whose mode samples. Two counts with two owners: the kernel keeps a count,
    maintained on commit, of present nodes with any of the first three, a kernel change; the
    painter keeps the count of sampling SVG islands, reported by the SVG scene builder. `begin()` reads both in constant
    time and allocates the compose target only when either is nonzero, or when D10's overlay
    readback (the UI below a full-window canvas read back as pixels) is in force. Present,
    not visible: the counts are conservative, and an SVG island whose scene is not yet
    resolved at `begin()` (resolution happens inside the walk today) counts as sampling
    until it is known. A miss is a wrong frame, not a slow one, so it has a fallback: if a
    walk asks for a destination the counts did not predict, a debug build asserts and a
    release build abandons the direct target and re-renders that frame into the compose
    target from the passes already flushed, which the retained scene holds, then continues,
    counting the event. The kernel's paint order already evaluates all three node tests in
    one place and an SVG item's blend is its element node's own style, so the builder may
    make the SVG count the kernel's as well; a painter-side count would otherwise force the
    compose path on every frame whose kept rows hold an island, since replayed rows are
    not walked. A frame with none renders into the drawable; a
    frame with one keeps today's path into the parked drawable.
  - *Acquisition stays off the main thread, as D7 has it, on a thread of its own.* A second
    worker, the acquirer, does nothing but call `nextDrawable` and park the result, so an
    outstanding acquisition never delays an upload, a capture, a commit or a synchronous
    resize queued on the render worker, and the render worker never waits for the acquirer:
    independent progress by construction, not by ordering. The acquirer acquires one
    drawable when the layer attaches, and after each present the render worker asks it for
    the next; the acquisition uses the layer's timeout. Requests are coalesced (one
    outstanding acquisition at a time) and a result that arrives after a resize, a scale
    change, shutdown or the transition to rest is discarded, never parked; the live-resize
    wait covers any older acquisition still running. A park carries the layer's size and
    scale generation; a resize or scale change discards it. The carrier checks for a park
    before the walk, where the Windows carrier calls `prepare_window_frame`; with none
    parked, no frame is painted, the presenter keeps its damage, and the carrier retries at
    the next display period; an acquisition that returned nothing is requested again at that
    period, independent of any present. Both paths take the park: a frame that keeps the
    compose target samples it into the parked drawable rather than acquiring a second one.
    When the display link stops, the park is released, so rest holds no drawable and the
    end-of-rest footprint row is unchanged; the acquisition for the next gesture is requested
    where the link is started, on the event that made the presenter dirty, so the first frame
    finds its park at the first tick (the worker's `nextDrawable` is 0.2 ms at p99). The one
    case where the main thread waits is the live-resize frame: it discards the park, asks the
    acquirer synchronously for a drawable of the new generation before encoding, then
    encodes, commits, waits until scheduled and presents inside the transaction, as S1.6 made
    it; that wait is bounded by at most two of the layer's timeouts (an older acquisition
    still running is awaited and discarded first) and stated here as D7's exception.
    Encoding on the host thread, commit and present on the render worker, are D11's division
    unchanged.
  - *Formats.* Stage 0b prepares BGRA replacement only. Direct drawable rendering also
    needs BGRA blending: A1 must prepare and archive that variant, keeping a miss off
    first pixel, and measure its added startup cost on the same instrument.
  - *Readback.* A `screenshot`, the agent's pixels or an oracle comparison takes the frame's
    pixels in one of two ways. A frame that rendered into the compose target is read from it,
    as today's `finish()` does: no re-render, every pass of the frame included. A frame that
    rendered into the drawable is a single pass and is re-rendered from the retained scene:
    the last encoded frame's clear, size, batches and instance buffer, with the pool targets
    and atlas pages it references pinned until the next frame's encode replaces them, since
    a live request arrives on an arbitrary turn: one frame's targets and entries are held one
    frame longer than today, a cost stated; the re-render happens on the request's turn and
    before the next `begin()`. In live mode that frame is the last
    encoded one; a mid-animation capture replays its sampled state without advancing or
    settling time. Headless screenshots are unchanged and, since today's live screenshot is a
    walk, a render and a readback of a new target, the re-render is cheaper than today.
  - *The parity gate (D9).* In test builds the layer is created with `framebufferOnly` false
    and, after the scene pass on the worker's commit, a blit copies the drawable into a
    readback texture tagged with the frame's sequence number, compared after channel
    normalization (the drawable is BGRA, the readback RGBA); the snapshot of that frame
    survives until its comparison completes, even when later frames have been encoded. The
    gate compares that copy with the same frame's readback within the band, so the frame
    compared is the frame presented (not proven shown: S1.5 records presented drawables the
    compositor never showed, which the presented handler reports as a zero time and the gate
    tags), and it qualifies the test configuration of the layer; the shipped configuration
    keeps `framebufferOnly` true and is qualified by the window captures of the series. The
    offscreen path keeps its comparison against tiny-skia. The copy's cost is kept out of
    ordinary performance samples.
  Predicted: GPU per frame 1.2 → ~0.3 ms on frames without destination sampling, once Stage 0
  has explained the 1.0 ms pass; the present pass's encode gone; no CPU saving claimed.
- **M6, rows reusable across fractional scroll offsets.** Stage 0's counter decides. If kept
  rows re-record because momentum deltas are fractional, amendment A2, a moving-content
  policy under D3 and D9, not a D4 contingency: the snap is per scroller, not per row, and it
  is defined in the coordinates glyphs are rasterized in. The backend receives the scroller's
  offset in points at `group_begin`; with `p` that offset times the device scale, the rows of
  that scroller are recorded and replayed where a walk at the offset `round(p)` device pixels
  (`round(p) / scale` points) would place them. The shift of the content between a row's recording and its
  replay is then `round(p_rec) − round(p_new)` device pixels (content moves against the
  offset), whole for every row, so every
  recorded glyph phase and coverage mask is the one a fresh walk at that position would
  choose; rows keep their mutual positions, so there is no seam between rows whose layout
  heights are fractional (the kernel does not round frames); the whole list sits at most half
  a device pixel from its layout position while `p` is fractional, which is what an AppKit
  clip view's pixel-aligned bounds origin does. The fast path is taken only where that
  equivalence holds: rows whose transform is a pure translation (no scale, no skew; the row
  path admits axis-aligned scale today and such rows record fresh) and whose glyphs do not
  cross zero in device coordinates under the shift, nor lie in the unit interval below zero
  before or after it (truncation is not translation-invariant there; a row with such a glyph
  records fresh, so the mismatch today's whole-pixel
  reuse accepts at the viewport's edge is not extended). When the scroller's offset stops
  changing, the carrier requests one final redraw and the rows re-record at the exact offset,
  so the settled frame is pixel-exact with a fresh walk; when motion resumes they re-record
  once at the snapped position. The fractional-scroll test asserts equality with a fresh walk
  at the snapped offset during motion and exact equality at rest, with the fallback rows
  recorded fresh in both. Predicted: the walk's
  5.7 ms/s becomes new rows' recording, kept rows' box copies and validity checks; a static
  screen with one animating element records that element and replays the rest.
- **M7, persistent instance ranges.** A replayed row still re-emits every instance through
  the clip stack. Kept rows keep ranges in a persistent instance buffer under rectangular
  outer clips (a scissor), with the copy as the fallback for every other outer clip, rounded
  or coverage. Lifetime: a range is immutable while any submitted frame or pinned snapshot
  references it; a changed row takes a new range and the old one is reclaimed after those
  references retire. D4's contingency. Predicted: encode 2.9 → under 1 ms/s, less the added
  draw calls, measured after M6.
- **M8, a plain pipeline.** The shader guards its rounded and coverage clips by flags, so
  the general path's cost per pixel is a branch and the general vertex load; a second
  pipeline for clip-free rectangles and glyphs with solid or image brushes is worth its
  second shader on four device layers only if G5's GPU rows, after M3, show the GPU above
  about 15% of the frame budget on either platform. Otherwise it is dropped. D6.1's portable
  buffer and shader constraints hold through M7 and M8.
