## 1. Decisions

### D1 — iOS in seconds, and no background builds

1. **A contract or `app.ts` edit reaches iOS without a native rebuild.** When the build is stale
   only because plan inputs changed, `test ios` and `agent ios` compile the plan and launch the
   existing binary with it (`EXACT_PLAN`; the host already loads a plan from outside its bundle,
   `ExactIOS/main.swift` `watchPlan`, `PlanURL`). One stderr line says so. Rust, Swift, `app.json`,
   assets or the checkout's host sources changed: it rebuilds, as now. An override never outlives a
   newer real build. This turns D4's backfire into the fast path: iterating on iOS costs seconds.
   - What counts is what the native app depends on. A builder's own backend (a `server/` of
     `.ts` files, reached over HTTP) is neither a native input nor a plan input; only the bake's
     capture from `app.ts` is a plan input. Round 5's book-club rebuilt iOS for `server/index.ts`.
   - The fast path is held to the same app as a real build for everything a drive or a test
     observes. A builder's last check is one drive of a packaged build, and `ios-driving` says so.
2. **No build in the background by hand.** Codex's shell kills a backgrounded process when the
   command returns (seven Exact2 diaries this round, three comparison ones), and start-here tells
   builders to start `bun exact.mjs ios` in the background. `bun exact.mjs ios --prebuild` starts
   the first build detached from the shell (its log in `.exact/ios-build.log`) and returns; `test`
   and `agent` already wait for a running build. Start-here says that instead.
3. **The driver's taps land** (built during the round, `bb0c9f6e2`). A target scrolled away or
   under a floating bar is scrolled into the unobscured viewport first; `--touch platform` aims
   where UIKit draws bar items and segments; a tap waits for the keyboard to settle; a scroll drag
   works without `--touch platform`. A real cover (an open sheet) still refuses, naming it.

### D2 — iOS forms avoid the keyboard; a header's bar is a stated rule

1. **The focused field, and the control after it in its row or section, scrolls above the
   keyboard**, as a UIKit table or a SwiftUI `Form` does: the nearest scroller is inset by the
   keyboard's height and scrolls the focused field into view, on focus and when the keyboard's frame
   changes. Return with `enterkeyhint="next"` moves on and scrolls likewise. An XCTest covers an
   inline number field with its Add button below it in a tab layout.
2. **When a route's `header` becomes the navigation bar is one stated rule**, in `contract vocab
   header` and the navigation recipe, with a warning when a header holds something that keeps it
   from being a bar (workout-log spent fifteen minutes on a title that vanished on one route).

### D3 — colour in both schemes, second pass

(Built during review: D3.1 and D3.2, `3b3b42169` and `2813ea44f`. The web draws black ink only
when white would fall below 3:1 on the accent. WCAG's own crossover would put black on system blue,
which someone who knows iOS would notice. UIKit keeps white on a pale tint, so iOS does too.
**Amended by LLP 1116.007 D3.10 (2026-10-12):** `check` finds that white at 1.4:1 to 2.3:1 on yellow,
orange, green, mint, teal and cyan, two round-9 builds lost a minute to it, and iOS now draws the same
rule as the web: black ink on a `filled` or `bordered-prominent` button once white would fall under 3:1 on
the accent as the scheme resolves it (relative luminance above 0.3). The system blue, red, pink, purple,
indigo and brown keep UIKit's white. One commit, `host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/IOS/ButtonConfigurationIOS.swift`.)

1. **The ink on an accent fill is chosen for contrast in each scheme.** A prominent button, a
   selected segment or a filled tag on the accent draws white or black ink, whichever is legible,
   when the author names no `color`. A pale dark-scheme accent (`light-dark(#6652c5, #b5a4ff)`) no
   longer gets white text on lavender (event-rsvp, recipe-finder). Author > platform: an authored
   `color` still wins. The same holds for state fills: the green and red of an answer tile, and
   every `exact:content` part that fills, choose legible ink in each scheme.
2. **`AccentColor` under an authored `light-dark()` accent is that accent on the web** for symbols
   and text (event-rsvp's icons and tip-splitter's header icon drew system blue).
3. **The docs name surfaces in pairs.** A card on a plain page is `-exact-secondary-background`;
   on a `-exact-grouped-background` page it is `-exact-secondary-grouped-background`; a surface
   inside a card is the tertiary role. Start-here's example named the grouped card for any page,
   and two apps' cards were white on white in light mode (polls, expense-tracker).
4. **The web progress track is the platform's fill** (built during the round, `bf50e3d53`).

### D4 — design the content, leave the chrome

LLP 1115's precedence already says what the author writes wins. The docs now say where to write,
and the parts make it safe:

- **Chrome is the platform's.** Bars, sheets, alerts, tabs, and the controls in forms stay unsaid,
  and so look native and survive dark mode. Rows, grouping, where actions sit, and the layout of
  the content stay the author's.
- **The screen's purpose has a clear focal point.** The readout, the board, the timer's buttons,
  the answer tiles, the hero card get large type, the accent or a system colour as a fill, a shape
  and room. On the web, a wide window gets a layout (two columns, cards), not a centred phone column.
- **Emphasis is a part, not paint on a control.** `exact:content` gains host-drawn parts for what
  recurs: a round button (Clock's Start and Lap), an answer tile that turns green or red, beside
  `SummaryCard` and `RingProgress`. They keep the control's semantics, its target size, adaptive
  type and legible ink in both schemes (D3). The `one-screen` and `dashboard` patterns use them.
- **One recipe replaces `content-design.md`**, with three screens that use those parts: a timer, a
  quiz and a dashboard hero. It is looked up, not added to the default read.
- **Start-here's first pitfall is amended:** paint on a platform control still makes it your own
  box, and the way to emphasise is a part.
- The colour warning stays: designed content uses roles, system colours and the accent, not hex.

### D5 — the docs floor of a small web app

- **The recipes a web build reads are cut to the idiom each answers.** `web-look`,
  `responsive-columns`, `tests` and `files-and-grants` recur in every GPT web build. Each is cut to
  at most about 2.5 KB. A long example moves into the pattern that already holds it: web-look's
  70-line list-and-detail split is `list-detail`'s.
- **The `--help`s of `web`, `test` and `agent` are short:** what a first build needs, with the rest
  behind `--help all`.
- The target is a first read of at most about 25 KB for a web app (start-here, AGENTS.md and the
  recipes the app needs), from 35–69 KB. That is an intermediate target. Round 6 judges D5 by the
  React token ratio and total steps on the same specs, and cuts carried bytes again if the ratio
  barely moves. The starters replace overlapping recipes in practice; essentials stay in the short
  `--help`.

### D6 — a warning for a read after an assignment

A state read after the same action assigned that state sees the value before the action
(contract-for-agents, "State and action semantics"). Five builds were surprised by it in rounds 4
and 5; focus-timer's phases changed 250 ms late in both. A compiler warning names the slot and the
repair (`let next = …; count = next`), as `analyze-call-stale-read` already does for helpers.

### D7 — fixes from the grades

- **iOS: a grouped list's newest rows blank after a relaunch** until scrolled (password-generator):
  the launch path of LLP 1116.002 D2's invalidation.
- **iOS: `line-clamp` in a grouped row** is honoured (chat-rooms' previews grew to 13 lines).
- **iOS: a footer's or a standard cell's authored `color`** is kept (queued in round 5; unit-converter's
  red error was grey).
- **`DoneMark`'s visible text is `caption`, not `label`**; its accessible name is its own
  (water-tracker passed accessibility text and it showed).
- **A signed decimal field** (unit-converter: `inputmode="text"` opened a letter keyboard because the
  decimal pad has no minus key). An `input inputmode="decimal"` whose `min` is below zero gets a ±
  key on iOS, in the keyboard's accessory bar, as a hand-built converter adds one. `number-field`
  shows it, with how the keyboard goes away.
- **Web: the browser's Back pops a router keyed by name** (built during the round, `ecf39106b`;
  every app made from `list-detail` or `rest` was affected).

### D8 — checking without a screenshot

A screenshot read back stays in every later step. Muse's Exact2 iOS builds read 1.5 to 2.5 times
the screenshots its SwiftUI builds did, and workout-log read 25.

1. **`agent <host> check`** reports, in text, what is wrong with the presented screen:
   - text clipped or truncated against its box
   - controls overlapping, or covered by a bar, the keyboard or a sheet
   - a target smaller than the platform's minimum
   - horizontal overflow at the drive's width
   - text and background below a contrast floor, in the current scheme and with
     `prefer prefers-color-scheme dark`
   It reads the host's presented layout and resolved colours: observations, not a second model,
   with each finding naming its node by `testId` and its box. `ios-driving` and the tests recipe
   say to run it before a screenshot, and to keep screenshots for milestones.
2. **Settled screens, relaunches and outside updates have one way each.** A screenshot after a
   transition waits for it to settle (polls spent five minutes on a stale segmented control). A
   relaunch is a second drive on the same `--storage` (done in D1.3). An update from outside (a
   second user's write) is verified by writing through the server and waiting on the clock: one
   documented recipe, not six minutes of invention (team-kanban).
