# LLP 1141.003: Images and menus in the bar

**Type:** RFC
**Status:** Draft r1
**Systems:** the header rule (`kernel/src/bars.rs`, `contract/lower/src/warnings.rs`); image sources; the iOS and tvOS bar (`host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/IOS/NavigationBarIOS.swift`, `BarFaceIOS.swift`, `NavigationTitleIOS.swift`, `MenusIOS.swift`); the Mac toolbar; the web and Android bars; the agent driver and docs
**Author:** Claude (Opus 5.5) for Charlie Cheever
**Date:** 2026-10-23
**Related:** LLP 1141.001 D9 (elaborated here); LLP 1115; LLP 1075.003 §9.8, §9.10, §9.15 (segmented title, richer title, readout); LLP 1115.001 D11; LLP 1115.002 D2; LLP 1021 D3; LLP 1011, 1011.001; LLP 1069.011.000 D2; `~/ports/react-conf/ATTRIBUTION-port1.md` #8, `~/ports/hackers/ATTRIBUTION-port1.md` #2

## Summary

On iOS a route's `header` is its navigation bar. The pilot's ports lost closeness to three
things a hand-built bar has and Exact's cannot show:

1. A picture in a bar item: React Conf's mark.
2. Something other than the title in the title's place: React Conf's "Currently live" banner.
3. A title that opens a menu: Hackers' feed picker.

| | Decision | What the author writes |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | An `image` in the header is a bar item drawn as its picture, and so is a button's image face. `light-dark()` picks an image source by colour scheme on every host. | `image "light-dark(url(a.png), url(b.png))"` |
| D2 | One element marked `principal` is the bar's title view, drawn as written and live. The heading stays the title. | `toolbarPlacement="principal"` |
| D3 | A button around the heading whose popover is a menu makes the title a menu: UIKit's `titleMenuProvider`. | `button popovertarget=…` holding `text role="heading"` |
| D4 | Until D1 lands, the compiler warns wherever the bar drops a picture or flattens a column. | — |

There is no new tag. D2 adds a value to a word Exact already has, and D1 takes CSS's own
`light-dark()`.

## 1. Evidence

**React Conf** (finding 8, confidence 90). In the original (Expo Router,
`(calendar)/_layout.tsx`), `headerLeft` is an image, `conf.png` or `conf-dark.png` by colour
scheme, 72×20 points with no glass. `headerTitle` is the `CurrentlyLive` pressable, a red dot
and "CURRENTLY LIVE" over the session's title, beside the large title "Calendar". The Opus
port wrote the mark as a `role="status"` readout of an atom symbol and two stacked texts,
which iOS drew as one line, "REACT CONF25", in a glass capsule
(`sheets/port1-01-calendar.png`, middle), and moved the banner into the content. The SwiftUI
port has the banner as a `.principal` item above the large title (right). Finding 2b, a tap
on the inline title that did not scroll to the top, was the journey's; in the original that
place holds the banner, which a tap presses.

**Hackers** (finding 2, confidence 85). The original's title, "Top ⌄", is SwiftUI's
`ToolbarTitleMenu`: eight feeds, with a checkmark on the current one (`sheet-h03.png`, left
and right). The port wrote the heading and, beside it, `button popovertarget="feeds"` with a
chevron symbol, which iOS drew as a separate glass button (middle).

**The code** (this checkout, near `origin/main` on 2026-10-23):

- A drawn bar-item face (`ChipFace.Part`, `BarFaceIOS.swift:19-28`) is dots, symbols and
  texts in one row (`draw`, :163). An `image` that is not a symbol falls through `visit`
  (:120) and is dropped without a word; a column of texts joins the row.
- `barItemMade` (`NavigationBarIOS.swift:1211`) hides the shared glass only for a badge face
  (:1245).
- The title is UIKit's or a drawn `HeaderTitleView` (`NavigationTitleIOS.swift:227`), whose
  tap (:381) is refused for a node with no `press` (`NavigationBarIOS.swift:1286`). Nothing
  in `host/apple` sets `titleMenuProvider`.
- The shared rule takes a pressable around the heading as the title only when it hears
  `press` (`kernel/src/bars.rs:269`). A `button popovertarget` around the heading becomes a
  bar item instead, and the heading inside it is never counted, so the header is no bar. The
  compiler agrees (`warnings.rs:296`, :483) and says `lower-header-no-bar`, "holds no
  heading", which misleads.
- An `image` directly in the header is `lower-header-unplaced` (`warnings.rs:509`). An image
  inside a button is never looked at (`Part::Pressable => {}`, :483).
- The Mac draws a raster in a toolbar item (`ToolbarMac.swift:620-636`) but keeps the whole
  header in the page for a picture or a pressable title (`HeaderMac.swift:91-136`). The web
  paints the authored header. Android's bar takes symbol or text items only
  (`presenter/bars.rs:228-245`) and no title tap.

**UIKit on iOS 27.0.** A 90-line UIKit probe ran on an iOS 27.0 simulator on 2026-10-23,
built in a scratch directory and not kept:

- **Title view.** A `titleView` shows in the bar's top row above an expanded large title. It
  stays when the large title collapses, and the heading's words are then not in the bar.
  LLP 1141.001 D9's premise, that UIKit shows a title view only inline, does not hold on iOS
  27. The SwiftUI port's `.principal` item behaves the same.
- **Glass.** `UIBarButtonItem(customView:)` with `hidesSharedBackground = true` draws its
  image with no glass, as the original's mark has; `UIBarButtonItem(image:)` sits in the
  shared capsule.
- **Title menu.** With `titleMenuProvider` set, the inline title draws UIKit's chevron and
  carries a context-menu interaction (`_UINavigationBarTitleControl`). An expanded large
  title draws no chevron and has no interaction of its own.

**Apple's documentation.**
[`UINavigationItem.titleView`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uinavigationitem/titleview)
is "displayed instead of the title", centred, and "may be resized to fit".
[`titleMenuProvider`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uinavigationitem/titlemenuprovider)
(iOS 16, not tvOS) makes "a context menu that appears when a person taps the title".
[`UIBarButtonItem(customView:)`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uibarbuttonitem/init(customview:)),
[`hidesSharedBackground`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uibarbuttonitem/hidessharedbackground)
and [`sharesBackground`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uibarbuttonitem/sharesbackground)
(iOS 26) make an item of a view and leave out its glass. SwiftUI's
[`.principal`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/toolbaritemplacement/principal)
sits "in the center of the navigation bar" and "takes precedent over a title specified
through navigationTitle";
[`toolbarTitleMenu(content:)`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/toolbartitlemenu(content:))
is iOS 16 and macOS 13.

## 2. Decisions

### D1. A picture in the bar

**Where.** An `image` directly in the header of a route whose header is its bar, outside any
button, is a **mark**: a bar item that nothing presses, placed on its side of the heading
as a readout is (LLP 1075.003 §9.15). A button whose face is one `image` has that picture as
its item's image. In a face that says more, a picture is one more part of the row
(`ChipFace.Part.image`).

**What iOS draws.**

| Case | UIKit object | Glass |
|---|---|---|
| A mark | `UIBarButtonItem(customView:)` holding an image view, not interactive. VoiceOver reads its `alt` as an image, and `alt=""` hides it. | none (`hidesSharedBackground`), as the original's `headerLeft` has |
| A button's picture | `UIBarButtonItem(image:)` | shared, as a symbol item's is |
| A button's picture with its own shape (`border-radius`, a round photo) | the same | none, as a filled box's face has today (`BadgeFace`) |

tvOS shows no mark, as it shows no readout (`NavigationBarIOS.swift:246-250`).

**Sources.** Any image source works: a raster, an SVG file (LLP 1011.001, built on Apple),
`http(s)`, `app:/` or `data:`. The header is laid out as `display: none` on iOS, so the item
asks the raster pipeline for its source at the item's size times the screen's scale, as an
image node does, and an SVG is rasterized at the size the bar draws it. The image node's
`load` and `error` fire from that request.

**Size.**

- The author's `width` and `height`, with `object-fit` inside them. A header node has no
  frame, so they are read as a face's sizes are (`kept_geometry`).
- With none written, the natural size, scaled down to fit an item's height and never up.
  Stage 2 measures that height on iOS 26 and 27; drawn faces use 36 points today.

**Light and dark.** A one-colour mark is a template, `tint-color="light-dark(#000, #fff)"`
(LLP 1011 §1). A mark of several colours, such as React Conf's teal atom beside black or white
words, names two sources with CSS's `light-dark()`, which the CSS Working Group resolved in
March 2026 to extend to images
([csswg-drafts #12513](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/12513)); Firefox 150 ships
it and Chromium intends to.

```text
image "light-dark(url(assets/conf.png), url(assets/conf-dark.png))" alt="React Conf 2025" width=72 height=20
```

- **Resolution.** It resolves by the node's used colour scheme, as a colour's `light-dark()`
  does, so under `color-scheme="dark"` a sheet shows the dark mark. `none` in either place
  shows nothing in that scheme.
- **Scope.** It is a value of any `image`, not only one in the bar. The compiler checks both
  `url()`s as it checks one source, and refuses a malformed pair, naming the form to write.
- **On iOS.** The item is drawn for the scheme the page is in, and drawn again when that
  changes, as `ChipFace` is. It is not a two-variant `UIImageAsset`: `BarFaceIOS.swift:38-41`
  records UIKit keeping one variant's size for the other in a bar on iOS 27.

`<picture>` is not chosen: Contract has no `picture` (`tags.rs:1052` says to use `image`),
it takes two more tags to make one choice, and `media="(prefers-color-scheme: dark)"`
follows the page's preference, not the element's `color-scheme`.

**A bar item stays one row.** A column of texts in an item's face, such as the port's "REACT"
over "CONF25", is not laid out as a column. UIKit's bar items are a title, an image or a
custom view. A hand-built app sets a mark in stacked type as a picture, and the original
did. Stacked words in the title's place belong to D2. D4 warns about such a column.

### D2. A title view: `toolbarPlacement="principal"`

One element of the header with `toolbarPlacement="principal"` is the bar's title view
(`navigationItem.titleView`). `toolbarPlacement` is Exact's word for where a toolbar item goes
(`window`, `navigation`, `keyboard`: LLP 1001, LLP 1069.011.000 D2), and `principal` is
SwiftUI's name for this place. HTML has no word for the middle of a bar, and an element's
side of the heading cannot say it, since a button after the heading is a trailing item.
React Conf's header becomes:

```text
header
  image "light-dark(url(assets/conf.png), url(assets/conf-dark.png))" alt="React Conf 2025" width=72 height=20
  text "Calendar" role="heading" aria-level=1
  if live.length > 0
    button press=toLive(live[0].id) toolbarPlacement="principal" aria-label=`Currently live: ${live[0].title}`
      row align-items="center" gap=4
        view width=4 height=4 border-radius=2 background-color="#FF0000"
        text "Currently Live" font-size=10 text-transform="uppercase"
      text live[0].title font-size=12 line-clamp=1
  button popovertarget="tz-menu" aria-label="Time zone"
    text zone
```

- **It is drawn as written, live.** Where the rest of the header is laid out as `display:
  none`, the kernel lays the principal's subtree out as a root of its own, at its max-content
  size and at most the width UIKit offers, as it lays out an open popover in the top layer,
  and iOS hosts those views in the title view. So it presses, updates in place, clamps its
  lines and shows its pictures as in the page. Its unsaid colours and type are the bar's
  (`label`, the body style), as a readout's are.
- **The heading stays the title:** the large title at `aria-level=1`, the next route's back
  title, the window's title on the Mac, `document.title` on the web, and the screen's name to
  VoiceOver. UIKit shows the title view above an expanded large title and keeps it when that
  collapses (§1); Exact adds no collapsing of its own.
- **There is one title view.** The principal takes precedence over a drawn title (§9.10's
  avatar, subtitle or press) and over a header tablist's segments (§9.8). Either one beside a
  principal is `lower-header-unplaced`, and so is a second principal.
- **The agent.** `tree` gives the principal's nodes their frames in the bar, with
  `native: "title-view"`, and `unshownByBar` stops marking them. Under `--chrome platform`,
  `tap` and `expect` reach them through UIKit's hit test; under the painted chrome the
  authored header paints, as now.
- **tvOS** has `titleView` and does the same.

### D3. A title menu

The title opens a menu when the pressable around the heading is a button whose
`popovertarget` names a menu-shaped popover (`MenuShape.swift:11-27`). Two rules Exact has
compose: a pressable around the heading is the title (§9.10), and a menu button's menu is
its pull-down (LLP 1021 D3). Hackers' feed becomes:

```text
header
  button popovertarget="feeds" testId="feed-title"
    text feedTitle(category) role="heading" aria-level=2
  button press=openSettings aria-label="Settings"
    image "symbol:settings"
column id="feeds" popover="auto" role="menu"
  for f in feeds key=f
    button press=choose(f) popovertarget="feeds" popovertargetaction="hide" role="menuitemradio" aria-checked=(category == f)
      image (feedSymbol(f))
      text feedTitle(f)
```

- **The rule.** The walk in `bars.rs` takes a pressable around the heading as the title when
  it hears `press` or opens a popover (`fact::MENU`). The shape records whether the title
  opens a menu. The compiler's walk (`warnings.rs:296`) agrees, so such a header is a bar and
  no longer `lower-header-no-bar`.
- **iOS.**
  - `navigationItem.titleMenuProvider` returns the button's pull-down,
    `MenuHost.pullDown(of:titled:inBar:)` (`MenusIOS.swift:387`). Its rows are read when it
    opens, with checkmarks, symbols, separators and submenus, as a bar item's menu has them.
  - A `press` the button also has runs when the menu opens, as the Mac runs a toolbar menu
    button's (`MenusMac.swift:709-740`).
  - A drawn title (`HeaderTitleView`) shows the same menu as its primary action
    (`UIControl.showsMenuAsPrimaryAction` and its `contextMenuInteraction`, iOS 14).
- **Large titles.** With a large title expanded, UIKit puts the menu on the inline title only
  (§1). A title menu that is to be reachable at rest is written `aria-level=2`, as Hackers'
  original is. The docs say so, and level 1 is not refused.
- **The chevron is the platform's.** UIKit draws it, and on the web the neutral sheet draws
  one after a title-menu button's heading (LLP 1116's designed web default). The author
  writes none. A chevron written inside the button is drawn as written, next to UIKit's,
  because the author wins; the docs say not to write one.
- **tvOS** has no `titleMenuProvider`. There the button is a bar item showing the heading's
  words and opening its menu, beside a plain title.
- **The agent.** Under `--chrome platform`, `tap feed-title` presses UIKit's title control
  (`native: "navigation-title"`) and UIKit's menu opens; its rows are tapped as a bar menu's
  are (`nativeRow`, `shownRow`). Under the painted chrome the button opens the painted
  popover, as a bar item's menu does (`MenusBarIOS.swift:27-45`). The bar's activation
  (`NavigationBarIOS.swift:597`) and `act` (:1286) accept a menu opener as a press.

### D4. Until D1 lands, the bar says what it drops

The walk behind `lower-header-unplaced` looks inside the face of each bar item, a button or
a readout, and warns at the node in two cases:

- **An `image` whose source is not a literal `symbol:`.** The message: "this image is not
  drawn in the bar on iOS: a bar item draws symbols, words and filled boxes; use `image
  "symbol:…"`, or move the picture below the `header`". A computed source is not warned.
- **A column of two or more texts.** The message: "drawn as one row in the bar on iOS".

The iOS host logs each drop once per item (`bar item #id: an image is not drawn`), so a drive
that missed the build's warning sees it in `logs`. When D1 lands, the image warning goes and
the column warning stays.

## Open questions

1. **Does a tap on an expanded large title open the title menu on iOS 27?** The probe found
   no interaction on the large title view, but a tap is the test. If the tap opens it, D3's
   advice to use level 2 goes.
2. **Is a top-layer root the right way to lay out the principal?** It is new for a node that
   is no popover. The alternative, the kernel laying out the whole header for the bar, would
   also retire `ChipFace`'s reading of styles without frames, and D1's one-row rule with it.
3. **What should the web do with the heading inside a title-menu button?** ARIA makes a
   button's children presentational, so there that heading is no heading to a screen reader;
   the ARIA Authoring Practices put the button inside the heading. Keep the composed form (the
   title tap Exact has), also accept a button inside the heading, or have the web host emit
   the heading around the button?
4. **What should the web use where a browser lacks `light-dark()` for images?** A `<picture>`
   on `prefers-color-scheme` is right except under an authored `color-scheme` that differs
   from the page's.
5. **Which is the platform's title menu on macOS 27?** The candidates are SwiftUI's
   `toolbarTitleMenu` and AppKit's document title menu (`representedURL`). Stage 4 probes
   both against a hand-built app.
6. **Does a round photo in a bar button go without glass on iOS 26?** D1 extends today's
   badge rule to it; a hand-built avatar button is the check.
