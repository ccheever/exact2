## Summary

On iOS a route's `header` is its navigation bar. The pilot's ports lost closeness to three
things a hand-built bar has and Exact's cannot show:

1. A picture in a bar item: React Conf's mark.
2. Something other than the title in the title's place: React Conf's "Currently live" banner.
3. A title that opens a menu: Hackers' feed picker.

| | Decision | What the author writes |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | An `image` in the header is a bar item drawn as its picture, and so is a button's image face. `light-dark()` picks an image source by colour scheme on every host. | `image "light-dark(url(a.png), url(b.png))"` |
| D2 | One element marked `principal` is the bar's title view, drawn as written and live. The heading stays the title. | `toolbarPlacement="principal"` |
| D3 | A heading holding a button whose popover is a menu makes the title a menu: UIKit's `titleMenuProvider`. | `text role="heading"` › `button popovertarget=…` › the words |
| D4 | Until D1 lands, the compiler warns wherever the bar drops a picture or flattens a column. | — |

There is no new tag. D2 adds a value to a word Exact already has, and D1 takes CSS's own
`light-dark()`.

## 1. Evidence

**React Conf** (finding 8, confidence 90). In the original (Expo Router,
`(calendar)/_layout.tsx`), `headerLeft` is an image, `conf.png` or `conf-dark.png` by colour
scheme, 72×20 points with no glass. `headerTitle` is the `CurrentlyLive` pressable, a red dot
and "CURRENTLY LIVE" over the session's title, beside the large title "Calendar". The Opus
port wrote the mark as a `role="status"` readout of an atom symbol and two stacked texts,
which iOS drew as one line, "REACT CONF25", in a glass capsule
(`sheets/port1-01-calendar.png`, middle), and moved the banner into the content. A second
port chose the mark's file by `media.prefersColorScheme` in a button, and the bar dropped it.
The SwiftUI port has the banner as a `.principal` item above the large title (right).

**Hackers** (finding 2, confidence 85). The original's title, "Top ⌄", is SwiftUI's
`ToolbarTitleMenu`: eight feeds, with a checkmark on the current one (`sheet-h03.png`, left
and right). The port wrote the heading and, beside it, `button popovertarget="feeds"` with a
chevron symbol, which iOS drew as a separate glass button (middle).

**The code** (this checkout, near `origin/main` on 2026-10-23):

- A drawn bar-item face (`ChipFace.Part`, `BarFaceIOS.swift:19-28`) is dots, symbols and
  texts in one row (`draw`, :163). A non-symbol `image` falls through `visit` (:120) and is
  dropped without a word; a column of texts joins the row. Only a badge face loses the
  shared glass (`NavigationBarIOS.swift:1245`).
- The title is UIKit's or a drawn `HeaderTitleView` (`NavigationTitleIOS.swift:227`, a
  `UIControl`); any title with a press is drawn (`HeaderTitle.drawn`, :166), and its tap goes
  through `act` (`NavigationBarIOS.swift:1286`), which refuses a node with no `press`.
  Nothing sets `titleMenuProvider`.
- The shared rule takes a pressable around the heading as the title only when it hears
  `press` (`kernel/src/bars.rs:265-275`). A `button popovertarget` around the heading becomes
  a bar item, the heading inside is never counted, and the compiler says
  `lower-header-no-bar`, "holds no heading" (`warnings.rs:296`, :483), which misleads. A
  heading cannot hold a button: `lower-leaf-children` refuses one in a `text`.
- An `image` directly in the header is `lower-header-unplaced` (`warnings.rs:509`); one inside
  a button is never looked at (:483).
- The Mac draws a raster in a toolbar item (`ToolbarMac.swift:620-636`) but keeps the whole
  header in the page for a picture or a pressable title (`HeaderMac.swift:91-136`). The web
  paints the authored header. Android's top bar (`host/linux/src/presenter/bars.rs`) has a
  plain title and items of a symbol or a title (`bar_button`, :228-245; `read_bars`, :250),
  and no title activation.

**UIKit, observed on iOS 27 in an unkept probe; stage 0 confirms on 26 and 27.** A 90-line
UIKit app on an iOS 27.0 simulator; nothing was tapped.

- A `titleView` showed above an expanded large title and stayed when it collapsed, so LLP
  1141.001 D9's premise, a title view only inline, did not hold. SwiftUI's `.principal` agrees.
- A custom-view item with `hidesSharedBackground` had no glass; `UIBarButtonItem(image:)` sat
  in the shared capsule.
- With `titleMenuProvider` set, the inline title drew UIKit's chevron and had a context-menu
  interaction; an expanded large title had neither.

**Apple's documentation.**
[`titleView`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uinavigationitem/titleview) is
"displayed instead of the title" and "may be resized to fit";
[`titleMenuProvider`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uinavigationitem/titlemenuprovider)
(iOS 16, not tvOS) makes "a context menu that appears when a person taps the title";
[`hidesSharedBackground`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uibarbuttonitem/hidessharedbackground)
(iOS 26) leaves out an item's glass. SwiftUI's
[`.principal`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/toolbaritemplacement/principal)
"takes precedent over a title specified through navigationTitle", and
[`toolbarTitleMenu(content:)`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/toolbartitlemenu(content:))
is iOS 16 and macOS 13.

## 2. Decisions

### D1. A picture in the bar

**Where.** An `image` directly in a header that is its route's bar, outside any button, is a
**mark**: a bar item nothing presses, placed on its side of the heading as a readout is (LLP
1075.003 §9.15) and mirrored under `direction: rtl`. A button whose face is one `image` has
that picture as its face; in a face that says more, a picture is one more part of the row
(`ChipFace.Part.image`).

**What iOS draws.**

| Case | UIKit object | Glass |
|---|---|---|
| A `symbol:` in a button | `UIBarButtonItem(image:)`, a template | shared, as today |
| A mark | `UIBarButtonItem(customView:)` holding an image view, not interactive | none (`hidesSharedBackground`), as the original's `headerLeft` |
| A raster or SVG picture in a button | `UIBarButtonItem(customView:)` holding a button that draws it | shared |
| The same with its own shape (`border-radius`, a round photo) | the same | none, as a badge face today; provisional until stage 0 compares a hand-built avatar button |

A raster or SVG picture draws with
[`.alwaysOriginal`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiimage/renderingmode)
rendering, so a multicolour mark or a photo keeps its colours; only a `symbol:` source or a
written `tint-color` (LLP 1011 §1) makes a template. To VoiceOver a mark is one element with
the image trait, not a button, labelled by its `alt`; `alt=""` takes it out of the tree.

**Sources.** Any image source: raster, SVG (LLP 1011.001), `http(s)`, `app:/` or `data:`. The
header is `display: none` on iOS, so the item asks the raster pipeline for its source at the
item's size times the screen's scale, and the node's `load` and `error` fire from that
request. A completion for a source the node no longer has, or a route that has gone, is
dropped.

**Size**, as CSS sizes a replaced element
([CSS Images 3](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-images-3/#default-sizing)), with `width` and
`height` read as a face's are (`kept_geometry`). Both written: that box, `object-fit` inside
it. One: the other from the natural ratio. Neither: the natural size, scaled down to an
item's height and never up (stage 0 measures it; drawn faces use 36 points). With no natural
size or ratio (`none`) and none written, the item is left out.

- **Late loads.** An item of written size shows at once and its picture fills in. One sized
  by its picture is added when the picture has loaded, never drawn empty and grown; a later
  picture of another natural size lays the bar out once, as adding an item does.
- **Failure.** `error` fires; a mark is left out, and a button whose face was only that
  picture shows its accessible name as a text item, so it stays reachable.

**Light and dark.** A one-colour mark is a template: `tint-color="light-dark(#000, #fff)"`.
A mark of several colours, such as React Conf's, names two sources with `light-dark()`, which
[CSS Color 5](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-color-5/#light-dark) extends to images (resolved
March 2026, [csswg-drafts #12513](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/12513)) and
[Firefox 150](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Mozilla/Firefox/Releases/150) ships
for CSS image values.

```text
image "light-dark(url(assets/conf.png), url(assets/conf-dark.png))" alt="React Conf 2025" width=72 height=20
```

- **Grammar.** Exactly `light-dark(A, B)`, each a `url()` or `none`, as a value of any
  `image`. The compiler checks each `url()` as one source and refuses any other form, naming
  this one.
- **Resolution.** The kernel resolves it by the image node's used `color-scheme`, as it
  resolves a colour's, so under `color-scheme="dark"` a sheet shows the dark mark whatever
  the page's scheme; hosts receive the chosen source. `none` is CSS's transparent image with
  no natural size, not the removal of the node.
- **Packaging.** Both `url()`s enter the plan's asset list, and `asset_islands`
  (`bake/src/receipt.rs`, LLP 1141.001 D1 as built) scans both, so a masked SVG in either
  scheme brings the SVG module into a release build.
- **iOS** draws the item for the scheme in use, again when it changes, as `ChipFace` is; not
  as a two-variant `UIImageAsset`, whose sizes UIKit mixes in a bar on iOS 27
  (`BarFaceIOS.swift:38-41`).
- **The web** sets the resolved URL as the `img`'s `src`, again when the used scheme changes,
  so `alt`, sizing, `object-fit`, `load` and `error` stay the image's own; `src` takes a URL,
  never a CSS function
  ([HTML, `img`](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/embedded-content.html#the-img-element)).
  The docs call `image "light-dark(…)"` an Exact form ahead of browsers.

Not `<picture>`: Contract has none (`tags.rs:1052`), and its
`media="(prefers-color-scheme: dark)"` follows the page's preference, not the element's
`color-scheme`.

**A bar item's face is one row, for now.** A column of texts in a face, such as the port's
"REACT" over "CONF25", is drawn as one row because `ChipFace` lays out one row: a limitation
of the face, not of the platform, which takes a custom view of any layout. D4 warns where it
bites. Stacked content in the title's place belongs in the title view (D2).

### D2. A title view: `toolbarPlacement="principal"`

One element of a route's `header` with `toolbarPlacement="principal"` is the bar's title view
(`navigationItem.titleView`). `toolbarPlacement` is Exact's word for which toolbar an item
goes to (`window`, `navigation`, `keyboard`: LLP 1001, LLP 1069.011.000 D2), and `principal`
is SwiftUI's name for the middle of a bar. HTML has no word for that place, and a side of the
heading cannot say it: a button after the heading is a trailing item. `principal` applies to
a child of a route `header` other than the heading (a button, a readout or a box), never asks
for a window toolbar, and is warned anywhere else. React Conf's header becomes:

```text
header
  image "light-dark(url(assets/conf.png), url(assets/conf-dark.png))" alt="React Conf 2025" width=72 height=20
  text "Calendar" role="heading" aria-level=1
  if live.length > 0
    button press=toLive(live[0].id) toolbarPlacement="principal" aria-label=`Currently live: ${live[0].title}`
      row align-items="center" gap=4
        view width=4 height=4 border-radius=2 background-color="#FF0000"
        text "Currently Live" font-size=10 text-transform="uppercase"
      text live[0].title font-size=12 line-clamp=1
  button popovertarget="tz-menu" aria-label="Time zone"
    text zone
```

- **Drawn as written, live.** The kernel lays the principal's subtree out as a **title-view
  root**: max-content, capped to the width the bar offers, and laid out again when that width
  changes. It is no popover: no light dismiss, inert page or stacking. It keeps its place in
  the authored tree, so inheritance, events and node identity are as written, and it does
  not also paint in the page. iOS hosts its views in the title view, so it presses, updates,
  clamps lines and shows pictures as in the page. Unsaid type is the drawn title's (headline,
  footnote for lines under it), not body; unsaid colours are `label` and `secondaryLabel`.
- **The heading stays the title:** the large title at `aria-level=1`, the next route's back
  title, the Mac window's title, `document.title`, and the screen's name to VoiceOver. Where
  UIKit shows a title view beside an expanded large title (observed on iOS 27; stage 0
  confirms on 26 and 27), so does Exact; where only inline, the principal replaces only the
  inline title. Exact adds no collapsing of its own.
- **Search.** While a header search is active UIKit hides the inline title and the title view
  with it (stage 0 confirms); `tree` marks the principal unshown until the search ends.
- **One title view.** The principal takes precedence over a drawn title (§9.10's avatar,
  subtitle or press) and a header tablist's segments (§9.8); either beside it is
  `lower-header-unplaced`, as is a second principal. A principal beside a title menu (D3) is
  refused: the bar has one title place, and either the menu or the title view would vanish.
- **The agent.** `tree` gives the principal's nodes their frames in the bar, with
  `native: "title-view"`, and `unshownByBar` stops marking them; under `--chrome platform`,
  `tap` and `expect` reach them through UIKit's hit test.
- **tvOS** does the same, the title view in the focus order between the items.
- **The spike.** Stage 3 starts with one route, and the docs describe the mechanism once it
  meets four exit criteria: the width cap on iPad, in landscape and at AX5; which line
  truncates first; tvOS focus order; and a popover or search field inside the principal.

### D3. A title menu

The title opens a menu when the heading holds a button whose `popovertarget` names a
menu-shaped popover. This is the interactive heading of the
[ARIA Authoring Practices](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/accordion/), correct on
the web as written, and HTML allows it: a heading holds phrasing content, `button` included
([HTML, `h1`–`h6`](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/sections.html#the-h1,-h2,-h3,-h4,-h5,-and-h6-elements)).
Hackers' feed becomes:

```text
header
  text role="heading" aria-level=2
    button popovertarget="feeds" testId="feed-title"
      text feedTitle(category)
  button press=openSettings aria-label="Settings"
    image "symbol:settings"
column id="feeds" popover="auto" role="menu"
  for f in feeds key=f
    button press=choose(f) popovertarget="feeds" popovertargetaction="hide" role="menuitemradio" aria-checked=(category == f)
      image (feedSymbol(f))
      text feedTitle(f)
```

- **Two forms, one menu.** A menu button around the heading (the form of today's title
  press) makes the same title menu on every host. Its heading is no heading to a screen
  reader on the web, so the compiler gives an accessibility warning naming the
  heading-around-button form. The web DOM is never rewritten.
- **The rule.** `lower-leaf-children` admits one button in a heading; one that only presses
  is the title's press, as it is around the heading. The walk in `bars.rs`
  and the compiler's (`warnings.rs:296`) take the title with a menu from either form, counting
  only a menu-shaped popover by the rule of `host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/MenuShape.swift:11-27`
  (LLP 1021 D3: button rows and `hr` separators), moved into the shared rule so every host
  agrees; the compiler reads a `for` or `if` row by its body. Any other popover around or in
  the heading keeps today's behaviour until it is specified.
- **iOS, a plain title.** A title with only a menu stays UIKit's (`HeaderTitle.drawn` stops
  counting a menu opener). `navigationItem.titleMenuProvider` returns the button's pull-down,
  `MenuHost.pullDown(of:titled:inBar:)` (`MenusIOS.swift:387`). It leaves out UIKit's
  `suggestedActions` (document commands) and reads its rows when it opens, as a bar item's
  menu does.
- **iOS, a drawn title** (an avatar, a glyph line, or a subtitle before iOS 26).
  `HeaderTitleView` stays a `UIControl` and shows the same pull-down as its primary action:
  `isContextMenuInteractionEnabled` and
  [`showsMenuAsPrimaryAction`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uicontrol/showsmenuasprimaryaction)
  set, and the menu given by overriding
  `contextMenuInteraction(_:configurationForMenuAtLocation:)` ("Implementing UIControl
  Menus", `UIControl.h`). It does not become a `UIButton`, whose configuration would lay out
  the avatar and lines the view draws itself.
- **A press as well** runs once when the menu opens, as the Mac runs a toolbar menu button's
  (`MenusMac.swift:709-740`).
- **Large titles.** With a large title expanded, UIKit puts the menu on the inline title only
  (observed; stage 0 taps it). The current convention for a title menu reachable at rest is
  `aria-level=2`, an inline title, as Hackers' original has: a presentation convention, not
  accessibility advice. Level 1 is not refused (open question 1).
- **The chevron is the platform's.** UIKit draws it; on the web the neutral sheet draws one
  after a title-menu button's words (LLP 1116). A chevron symbol written in a title-menu
  button is drawn as written, because the author wins, and the compiler warns.
- **tvOS** has no `titleMenuProvider`. The title menu is the title view, one focusable control
  showing the heading's words once, whose primary action opens the menu
  (`showsMenuAsPrimaryAction`, tvOS 17).
- **The agent.** UIKit's title menu opens only to a real touch, as a bar pull-down's does
  (`NavigationBarIOS.swift:594`, LLP 1021 D4). Under `--touch platform`, `tap feed-title`
  touches the title control, found by its frame and accessibility element, never a private
  class name, and rows are tapped as a bar menu's (`nativeRow`, `shownRow`). Under the
  painted chrome the button opens the painted popover. The same stage extends `act` (:1286)
  and the bar's activation (:597) to a menu opener.

### D4. Until D1 lands, the bar says what it drops

The walk behind `lower-header-unplaced` looks inside the face of each bar item, a button or
a readout, and warns at the node in two cases:

- **An `image` whose source is not `symbol:`**: a literal, or an expression that can yield a
  string literal that is not one (the second React Conf port's). An expression with no
  literal is not warned. The message: "this picture is not drawn in the iOS bar yet (LLP
  1141.003 D1); a bar item draws symbols, words and filled boxes".
- **A column of two or more texts**: "drawn as one row in the iOS bar"; with D2 it names the
  title view.

The iOS host logs each drop once, naming item and node (`bar item #12: image #14 is not
drawn`), so a drive that missed the warning sees it in `logs`. When D1 lands the image
warning goes; the column warning stays while the face is one row. With D3,
`lower-header-no-bar` gains a repair for a heading inside a menu button: "the heading is
inside button #n, which opens a menu: put the button inside the heading to make the title a
menu".

**Reach.** None of the 52 apps under `apps/` (13 with a `header`) would get a new warning:
none has a non-symbol image or a column of texts in a bar item, a heading inside a menu
button, or a chevron in a title-menu button. Of the pilot's ports, React Conf's first gets
the column warning once and its second the image warning once; Hackers' gets none.

## 3. Other hosts

| Host | D1, a picture | D2, the principal | D3, the title menu |
|---|---|---|---|
| Web | the `img`, the host setting a `light-dark()` source's resolved URL | painted where written | the authored heading and button open the popover; the neutral sheet draws the chevron |
| macOS (stage 4) | a mark is an unbordered `NSToolbarItem`; a button's raster already draws | a centred item (`NSToolbar.centeredItemIdentifiers`) | as SwiftUI's `toolbarTitleMenu`, screenshotted against a hand-built sample |

| Linux desktop, Windows | painted as written (`host/linux/src/bars.rs:20-23`) | painted as written | painted as written |
| tvOS | no mark | the title view | the title view, a focusable control |

- **The Mac** keeps such a header in the page until stage 4. Its title menu is not AppKit's
  [`representedURL`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nswindow/representedurl),
  a document's path menu, where a feed list would be a tell.
- **Android until stage 4.** Each stage that changes the shared header rule lands with
  Android's fallback, so no control is lost: a header whose title opens a menu is no top bar
  and paints in the page, as today; a principal paints in the page under the bar, as its
  search field does; a button whose picture the bar cannot draw shows its accessible name; a
  mark is left out, as a readout is.
- **tvOS has no mark**, as it has no readout (`NavigationBarIOS.swift:246-250`). A mark that
  carries information has to say it in the page too.
