## 1. Summary

Four diaries arrived in three submissions after the diary instructions landed on
2026-10-04. Authors praised the web loop, diagnostics, authored tests, shared
module/storage paths and offscreen rendering. The recurring cost was reaching
and understanding those paths: finding documentation, preparing generated build
files, repairing locks after the SDK moved, and accounting for native presentation.

The reports contain 13 Rough entries and 11 Lean in entries. Their command logs
contain 48 invocations, 16 unsuccessful. These are counts of recorded entries and
attempts, not independent defects or a product failure rate.

This is an evidence snapshot, under the existing authoring RFC rather than another
general authoring design. It preserves what was reported even where later work has
addressed it. The child RFC separates remaining proposals from existing work.

## 5. What to preserve

**A useful web loop (R1/R2).** R1 recorded a 2.9 s build and a 5.6 s authored test
after building. Its first authored test passed. R2 praises its web tests even
though the attached log includes one initial failed web test. Read praise as the
author's assessment, not proof every invocation succeeded.

**Diagnostics that teach (R1/R2).** Exact ranges and one array containing every
problem were helpful. R2's copied-app lock error gave a usable recovery command.
The Contract guide's repair table and action snapshot rule answered questions.
Style blocks were an effective response to long attribute lists.

**Tests and inspection with a small vocabulary (R1/R2).** `tap`, `clock settle`,
text/state expectations, tree inspection and screenshots were enough to check the
small app. iOS screenshots with `--chrome platform` exposed the real native bars
and helped review layout.

**Existing cross-host seams (R2).** A native module, `later`, and granted
`storage.fs` worked across all three tested hosts. Photos could be read through
`app:/` images. The expensive camera bridge did not require replacing that seam.

**Existing asset and rendering paths (R3/R4).** glTF import, dependency discovery
and content-named texture sharing worked end to end. A single-unit native render
took about five seconds. The contact-sheet workflow rendered 300 frames in about
15 seconds on Metal without a window; the example ran on macOS despite a
Windows-specific comment.

## 6. Interpretation and confidence

**High confidence in the recorded encounters:** the bodies and attached logs
support the inventory and counts. **Moderate confidence in priorities:** related
setup friction appears in all three submissions, and lock repair repeats in both
art diaries. **Low confidence in prevalence across authors or platforms:** the
sample is self-selected, from one model family, mostly tiny workflows, and has no
Linux, Windows, Android, deploy or broad performance coverage.

The strongest inference is to make existing paths easier to enter before adding
capabilities: preparation and recovery commands, discoverable examples, and
clear presentation preconditions. The camera bridge is the largest individually
estimated cost, but comes from one app. Text recognition was skipped by request;
the diary records a missing capability, not an implementation mandate.

On drafting, the checkout was `51c1ba61f`. LLP 1086 records same-day implementation
of its authoring work; generated instructions now contain guide links and an
app-form command table. `game/README.md` now explicitly says material overrides
are written from `Game::present`, and the texture guide distinguishes device
fetches from stored variants. These are document/source observations, not a fresh
reproduction proving each reported problem closed. No app was built or driven
for this research pass.

Future evidence should retain the receipt, SDK revision, exercised surfaces and
final outcome, and separate an earlier complaint from a later successful attempt.
Subsequent submissions can extend this report; a new collector or survey is not
needed to interpret these four.
