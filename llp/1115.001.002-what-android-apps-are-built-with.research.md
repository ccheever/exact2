## 1. Question

LLP 1115's test is whether someone who knows the platform would notice, judged against a hand-built screen. On
Android, which screen is that: Compose Material 3's default theme, Material 3 Expressive, or something else? The
answer decides what Exact draws where an author says nothing, and whether Expressive belongs in the reference.

## 2. Findings

**Compose is how popular apps are built; the long tail is still Views.** Google reports more than 68 % of the top
1,000 apps using Compose in production ([Android Developers Blog, July 2026](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/07/five-years-of-jetpack-compose.html)),
without saying how it counts. AppBrain's crowd-sourced figures agree for popular apps and show the tail: Compose is in
63 % of top apps and 72 % of installs, but 9 % of all apps
([AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/stats/libraries/details/jetpack_compose/jetpack-compose)). Google said at I/O
2026 that its UI work is Compose-first; the Views Material Components library is maintained, not developed.

**Material 3's default theme is the base a hand-built Compose app starts from.** No source found gives a share for
`MaterialTheme` against the alternatives. It is what Compose's templates and documentation use, and until 1.5 the
Expressive theme was reachable only through experimental API.

**Expressive is becoming a stable choice, not the default.** Read in the sources jars on Google Maven
(`material3-android`):

| | 1.4.0 (stable) | 1.5.0-beta01 (2026-10-07) |
|---|---|---|
| `MaterialExpressiveTheme` | `internal` | `public`, no experimental annotation |
| `MaterialTheme`'s motion | `MotionScheme.standard()` | `MotionScheme.standard()` |
| still `@ExperimentalMaterial3ExpressiveApi` | | the loading indicator, `MaterialShapes`, menus, pull to refresh, the slider |

The two themes are built to coexist: a composition local (`LocalUsingExpressiveTheme`) tells a component which it is
under, and only `MaterialExpressiveTheme` substitutes the expressive colour scheme and motion. Google ships Expressive
in its own apps and asks developers to follow
([Lifehacker, via Yahoo](https://tech.yahoo.com/apps/articles/google-just-unveiled-look-android-170000417.html);
[Material Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Material_Design)); third-party adoption is anecdotal
([Android Authority on Backdrops](https://www.androidauthority.com/backdrops-material-3-expressive-redesign-hands-on-future-android-3579229);
[F-Droid's discussion](https://gitlab.com/fdroid/fdroidclient/-/issues/3096)), and no figures were found. Android 16
does not restyle an app: a developer opts in.

**The most-used consumer apps mostly draw their own design systems.** From knowledge, not measured here: Instagram,
TikTok, Spotify, Uber and Airbnb ship design systems of their own, many on Compose and on Material's foundations,
that do not look like Material. What still reads as Android in them is what the platform owns: text editing,
scrolling and overscroll, Back, the system bars, dialogs, haptics and accessibility.

## 3. What follows for Exact

- Where the author says nothing, Compose Material 3's default theme is the reference (1115.001.001 D1). It is what a
  hand-built app gets, and Expressive's adoption does not argue for a different default.
- An app with a brand of its own is the "something else" case, and Exact already serves it: the author writes CSS and
  the author's word wins, while what the platform owns stays the platform's (1115.001 D1.2).
- Expressive is a candidate for an opt-in for each app once 1.5.0 is stable: from then it is an ordinary, supported
  choice of an Android developer, made by stable API. That is an RFC of its own, after S2 has matched the default.

## 4. Confidence

High that Compose dominates popular apps and that 1.5 makes Expressive a stable opt-in rather than the default (both
read from first-party sources). Low on shares within Compose apps (default theme against custom design systems): no
measurement was found, and the paragraph on large apps is from knowledge. To redo: the Compose figures from Google's
blog and AppBrain; Expressive's status from the newest `material3-android` sources jar (`MaterialTheme.kt`,
`grep -r ExperimentalMaterial3ExpressiveApi`).
