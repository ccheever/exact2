## 1. Decisions

### D1 — colour that survives dark mode

1. **The core's accent sentence is reversed.** The one authored colour is the accent: a
   system role (`accent-color="-exact-system-teal"`) or a single `light-dark()` pair on
   `accent-color`.
   - Every other colour is left unsaid or named as a role (`-exact-label`,
     `-exact-secondary-grouped-background`).
   - A bare hex on text, a page or a card stays the same in dark mode while the platform's
     text and controls change, and that is what broke four apps.
2. **A compiler warning, `lower-colour-one-scheme`.** It names a literal colour (hex, `rgb()`,
   `hsl()`, a named colour) on a colour property (`color`, `background-color`, the `border-*`
   colours, `fill`, `stroke`, `accent-color`, `outline-color`) that is not inside
   `light-dark()`.
   - Its repair names the role or the pair.
   - `transparent`, `currentColor` and the system roles are fine.
   - It still fires when the app forces one scheme.
   - Artwork's `fill` and `stroke` are exempt.
   - Round 4's five hex-heavy apps are the test cases.
3. **`no-tells` reports the same,** so the web-look and content recipes stay clean.
4. **One branded example.** The `one-screen` pattern sets an accent and uses role surfaces,
   and its test or screenshot checks both schemes.

### D2 — a new row shows at once

A row appended to a list on iOS (a grouped list, and a plain list if it shows the same) is
drawn on the commit that adds it, not after the screen is left or the app relaunched. The same
goes for a sibling view that the commit inserts (countdowns' "next up" card). The fix is the
host's invalidation, not one list.
Reproduce it with round 4's countdowns app, and with round 1's workout-log app (a third
exercise's add-set row was blank and untappable). There is an XCTest.

### D3 — patterns that carry their weight

The patterns stay and get shorter:

- `list-detail` and `rest` go to about 120 lines of Contract each.
- The adaptive wide layout stays, and the optional extras go: the persisted sort-order setting
  and a second sheet.
- Each pattern's header comment says what to delete for a smaller app.
- The patterns keep list, pushed detail, add sheet, delete alert and the wide split.
- Round 5 measures them on steps and tokens for the same specs against round 4 (polls,
  contacts, household-list, water-tracker), not by line count.
- Recipes and patterns show errors clearly: validation and refusals in `-exact-system-red`,
  beside the field or where the person acts.
- The small starters keep routes, so the browser's Back and deep links keep working.

### D4 — fewer iOS steps

The main lever is the workflow:

- **Edit and check on the web host.** It rebuilds in about a second.
- **Drive iOS at milestones,** several checks per native build: one drive in dark mode, one
  after a write.
- **`ios-driving.md` and the core say so.**
- **`bun exact.mjs test ios` and `agent ios` build the iOS app first when it is stale,** as
  `test web` and `agent web` do. They say so in one line, so a milestone drive is never
  refused and needs no separate `bun exact.mjs ios` step.
