## D1. The doc budget: a small app reads 20 KB

The goal is a build that reads start-here and the one or two recipes its features need, when it writes
them, and nothing else up front.

- **start-here's index is by feature, not by name.** "Look it up" becomes a table: *the app has* a
  timer, a number field, money, a date, a second screen, a list with delete, a sheet, a server, a
  persisted setting, randomness, a wide window → the one recipe or `pattern` that covers it. Its first
  line: read a recipe when you write that part, not before.
- **Every recipe is at most 3 KB**, enforced by `caps.mjs` (today 2–11 KB; 117 KB in all). A recipe is
  its snippet whole, its test, and the pitfalls that cost builders time; prose a snippet already shows
  goes. `navigation-stack` (10.9 KB) and `rest-backend` (9.2 KB) may run to 5 KB to keep their example
  whole; the rest moves to `pattern list-detail` and `pattern rest`, where a list or server app reads it
  anyway, or to `contract-for-agents.md` as lookup.
- **What every build opens is in start-here instead**: every test step on one line (replacing "Every
  step: tests"), the web look in two lines, the parts by name with their props (one line each, from
  content-design's table), each replacing the sentence that sent builders to the recipe. start-here
  keeps its 10 KB cap.
- **`contract vocab` answers the grammar's words** (`task`, `every`, `after`, `send`, `match`, `each`,
  `when`, `let`, `fn`, `shape`, `persist`, `props`), each in under 1 KB. Chat-rooms got 404s on four;
  trivia grepped 11 KB of `contract-grammar.md` for `each`.
- **No `--help` tour.** `AGENTS.md` today says to read `bun exact.mjs <verb> --help` "not exact2's
  scripts", and GPT reads four or five verbs' help (5–20 KB) before writing. start-here's command table
  is the workflow; `--help` is for a flag in question.

Measure: GPT's median docs, help and patterns read per build, 62 KB → at most 25 KB; no recipe over
3 KB (two at 5 KB).

## D2. Orientation in fewer steps

- **`AGENTS.md` is at most 1.5 KB** (today about 3 KB, read at step one and carried to the end): what
  to read (start-here), `feedback status`, and one line pointing at the rest. Mac, Linux, Android,
  hatches, `update`, Snapback and the generated-file list move to a "more" line or to start-here's
  lookup.
- **No dev server to check the app.** `verify`, `test` and `agent` build and serve their own copy; the
  dev loop is for a person watching. start-here's table and `AGENTS.md` say it in one line. focus-timer
  spent six steps on a backgrounded `web` that its shell killed; tip-splitter two.
- **One lifecycle for the app's own server.** `verify` starts and owns it (LLP 1116.006 D3); plain
  `test` does too now, so `test`, `api-test` and `verify` agree. polls' plain `test ios` saw an empty
  list until its builder found `api-test ios`.
- **`agent --json` prints one JSON document** (or one object per line, stated in its help); trivia spent
  five steps parsing a pretty-printed stream.

Measure: commands per build (r9: GPT median 36, Muse 59).

## D3. The fix rounds the diaries repeat

Each is a step or two at 30–60k tokens of context, in many builds.

1. **Symbol names by any of their spellings.** A role also answers to its SF Symbols name and its
   Material name, dotted or dashed (`gear` and `gearshape` → `settings`, `trash` → `delete`, `plus` →
   `add`, `xmark` → `close`, `chevron.right` → `forward-chevron`, `checkmark.circle.fill`,
   `square.and.arrow.up` → `share`). The table holds the aliases. An alias builds, with a warning that
   names the role; an alias is only one that keeps the roles' distinctions (filled or outlined, enclosed
   or bare). An unknown name is still refused with the list, and `symbol:sf/<name>` is unchanged.
2. **Font roles with a hyphenated number** (`-exact-title-1`, `-exact-caption-2`) are the same roles.
3. **System accents under white text.** `RoundButton` with a light system tint (green, mint, teal,
   cyan, yellow, orange) takes dark ink, as `AnswerTile` already does; start-here names the accents that
   carry white text on a filled control (blue, indigo, purple, pink, red, brown). Every snippet in the
   recipes and start-here passes `check` in light and dark, tested where the snippets are compiled
   (`contract/cli/tests/it/docs.rs`, or a script test that runs `check` on them).
4. **`verify`'s relaunch keeps a test's pinned date.** mood-journal and expense-tracker were told
   persisted data was lost when the relaunch moved to the real month.
5. **`agent`'s `prefer color-scheme dark`** is accepted for `prefers-color-scheme` (three builds).
6. **On the web, `screenshot` after `resize` captures the new size** (tip-splitter, in both its builds).
7. **A decorative chevron does not make an automatic split.** event-rsvp ("major"): a `>` symbol in
   a row read as navigation.
8. **Content under a translucent bar is covered**, judged as clipped content is (book-club, mood-journal:
   false contrast findings on rows behind the bar).
9. **`agent state <path>`'s help says what the path addresses** (three builds read it as a resource
   path).
10. **The platform's defaults and `exact:content`'s parts pass `check`** on a plain and a grouped page,
    in light and dark, with each system accent; what fails is fixed in the part or the default, never by
    loosening the check. unit-converter's SummaryCard had the grouped page's own grey; countdowns' indigo
    failed in dark; nine builds repainted a system accent.
11. **Content under the keyboard is covered too** (unit-converter: cards the keyboard hid reported
    invisible), and a child overflowing its row at 320 px is a finding (tip-splitter: a segmented option
    the screenshot showed cut off, which `check` passed).

Measure: framework, tooling and docs issues in the diaries (r9: 41).

## D4. The platform's stepper for a number input

`input type="number"` is a numeric field with the platform's stepping control, as the web draws its
spin buttons: on iOS a `UIStepper` at the field's trailing edge, on the Mac an `NSStepper` beside it
(the Mac's own convention). The field stays editable; `step` (default 1), `min` and `max` bound both. A
step is an `input` and a `change` with the new text, as HTML's stepping is. `appearance="textfield"`,
CSS's way to drop a number input's spin buttons, leaves the bare field on every host; hosts without a
stepping control draw the field. Nothing is inferred from the bounds: a field written with `inputmode`
and no `type="number"` (the number-field recipe's amounts) is unchanged. `contract vocab stepper`
answers with it, and the grouped-form recipe's quantity (1–99) uses it.

On the Mac, Up/Down and the field's accessibility increment/decrement step through the same
path, including with `appearance="textfield"`; the field exposes AppKit's incrementor role and
bounds. On Apple hosts, typed and pasted number text keeps signs, fractions and exponents and
refuses nonnumeric insertions; marked text stays with the input method until commit, including
when the committed candidate is rejected. Like Chrome's editor, it keeps incomplete `12-` and
`--1`, but permits an exponent sign only immediately after `e`/`E`. An empty or incomplete
number steps from zero into the bounds in either direction, as Chrome's spin buttons do.

grocery-list lost to SwiftUI's `Stepper` in rounds 6, 7 and 9 ("a free-text numeric field rather than
a Stepper"); its builder looked up `contract vocab stepper` and got nothing.

## D5. A grouped screen is grouped to the bar

When a route's scroll is an inset-grouped list, the route's unsaid background is the grouped
background role, so content between the header and the list (a segmented persona switcher, a summary)
sits on the list's grey, as in a hand-built `UITableViewController`. The lowering supplies the role and
each host resolves it by its own conventions; an authored background wins, on the route and on the
content between the header and the list. polls (r9): "a white header block over the
grouped-grey list background".

## D6. Docs slips that cost a grade

- **Round buttons at the row's ends**: the content-design example puts its `row` in a column that
  centers its children, so `space-between` has nothing to spread (stopwatch, "bunched in the centre").
  The example stretches the row, and says why in one line.
- **A readout pinned above a scrolling list** (stopwatch: the elapsed time scrolled off under the
  bar): the timers recipe shows the fixed readout over a `list` that is the scroll.
- **A persona switcher is a radio group, kept with `persist`** (`pattern rest`, `rest-backend`):
  chat-rooms drew the current persona as a disabled button, and both chat-rooms and event-rsvp lost
  it on relaunch.
- **A quiz shuffles its answers too** (`random-and-shuffle`): trivia's correct answer was the third
  option 14 times of 24.

## Not in scope

The prompt and grader stay fixed across rounds.

## As built (2026-10-12)

Six lanes, merged on `bake/1116.007-integration` with origin/main; lane log `~/bakeoff/lanes-1116.007.md`.

- **D1.** start-here indexes by feature ("open a recipe when you write that part: one per feature"),
  carries every test step on one line, the parts with their props, the accents that carry white text
  (computed from the schema's colours) and the web look; it stays at the 10,240-byte cap (two pitfalls
  moved into the `verify` row or dropped). The recipes went from 117 KB to 75.6 KB: every one at most
  3,072 bytes, `navigation-stack` and `rest-backend` at most 5,120, held by `caps.mjs` (`cap:recipe`).
  `contract vocab` answers `task` (and `every`, `after`), `mutation` (`send`), `resource`, `match`,
  `each`, `when`, `let`, `fn`, `shape`, `props`, `derive`, `action` and `stepper`, each under 1 KB.
- **D2.** The `AGENTS.md` `exact new` writes is 1,144 bytes (2,610): start-here, `feedback status`,
  and `help` with a verb's `--help` only for a flag in question. start-here says `verify`, `test` and
  `agent` serve their own copy. `test` runs the app's own server as `verify` does (`withAppServer`, an
  empty database); `pattern rest` no longer writes `test.mjs` or an `api-test` command, and an older
  app's `api-test` still works. `agent --json` already printed one compact object per reply; its help
  now says so.
- **D3.** 1: a `symbolAliases` table and every role's SF name (dotted, dashed or underscored) resolve
  to the role in the lowering, with the warning `lower-symbol-alias`; spellings that would cross a
  distinction (`lock.fill`, `check-circle`) stay refused. 2: `-exact-title-1` and the like. 3:
  `RoundButton` takes black ink on a light hue, and the iOS host draws black on any filled or prominent
  button whose white would be under 3:1, as the web does (this replaces LLP 1116.003 D3.1's note that
  UIKit keeps white). 4: `verify`'s relaunch carries the test's launch facts (epoch, zone, locale,
  seed) and compares against a fresh launch under the same facts. 5: `prefer color-scheme dark`. 6:
  the reply's size follows `resize` (the PNG already did). 7: the lowering implies
  `navigationSplit="none"` for a route whose chevron rows don't push. 8 and 11: the iOS check cuts each
  box at the bars and the keyboard; the web check finds a flex item past its row's side. 9: `state
  <name>` reads a state, derive or resource. 10: `SummaryCard` fills with the tertiary fill (it vanished
  on a grouped page), an accent `Badge` is a tint under the label, the web's unsaid and bordered buttons
  draw `ButtonText` ink, the iOS check judges a button's title in a grouped row as its label, and
  `AccentColorText` is judged as the platform's ink (3:1). Still failing, and left as the platform's
  answer: a pale accent as text on white (the default link, `color="AccentColor"`), and the default
  link's 41×21-point target.
- **D4.** `appearance="textfield"` joins the schema's enum. `input type="number"` draws `UIStepper` at
  the field's trailing edge on iOS (hidden when under 28 points of text would remain, about a 130-point

  a step is an `input` and a `change`. `layout <id>` reports the stepper's halves and `tap <id> at x y`
  presses one. The web host hides Chrome's spin buttons for `textfield`.
- **D5.** The lowering supplies `-exact-grouped-background` as an implied background to a route whose
  scroll is a grouped or inset-grouped list (`NavPlace::grouped_page`); an authored background wins.
  iOS and the web measured the band, the list and the area under the bar at the same pixels.
- **D6.** The Stopwatch's row stretches (`align-self="stretch"`); `timers` pins its readout above the
  list the route scrolls; `rest-backend` and `pattern rest` sign in through a `radiogroup` kept with
  `persist`; `random-and-shuffle` deals a quiz with each question's answers shuffled, the right one
  kept by identity; `grouped-form`'s quantity is a number input with `min`, `max` and `step`.
