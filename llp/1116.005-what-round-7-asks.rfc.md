## 1. Decisions

### D1 — a second screen is a route

1. **A warning when an app swaps the page by state.** A `when`/`else` whose arms each replace the
   page's content (each holding its own heading, as a screen does, with or without a `header`), in an
   app that declares no `routes`, warns: the browser's Back leaves the app, reload loses the place,
   and nothing can be linked. Tabs, empty states, error and loading views, and disclosure are ordinary
   state and don't warn. The repair is `routes` and `push`.
2. **`contract vocab` answers `screen`, `navigate`, `back` and `detail`** with the whole two-screen
   idiom: routes, a list that pushes its detail, the browser's Back, deep links and the wide split.
3. **Start-here's structure line says it in one sentence:** a second screen is a route, which is what
   gives the web its Back and links and a wide window its split.

### D2 — a field looks like a field on every platform

An unsaid `input`, `textarea` or `select` in a grouped row is drawn as each platform draws a field
there: borderless in its cell on iOS (the cell is the field), and as a visible field (its line,
corners and height) on the web and the Mac. The docs, start-here's first example, the pitfall that
recommends it, and the `list-detail` pattern drop `appearance="none"` from fields in rows.
`appearance="none"` stays the author's bare box, as written.

### D3 — each route keeps its own scroll

The web keeps its shell, a fixed app bar over the route's own scroller, as iOS does. A pushed route
opens at its top, and Back returns to the list where it was left (LLP 1038 §7: mounted rows keep their
scroll), on the web and on iOS, through a split too. event-rsvp's "detail opens scrolled partway down"
was one scroller shared by state-swapped screens, which D1 addresses; D3 checks that routes don't do the
same.

### D4 — iOS repaints what changed, and keeps its large title

- **Text that changes or leaves is repainted** on the commit that changes it (stopwatch). An XCTest
  covers a text whose content changes, and a text a `when` removes, inside a stack that scrolls.
- **The large title survives a content swap** under the bar (chat-rooms: an error view replacing the
  list), as LLP 1116.003 D2.2 fixed for a `scroll` swapped for a list.
- **Content scrolled under the status bar gets the platform's edge treatment** (stopwatch: with no
  navigation bar, the display and buttons slid under the status bar with no backdrop), as a hand-built
  scroll view on the same iOS version draws it.

### D5 — a seed arithmetic can use

The launch seed (`exactTime().seed`) is a whole number below 2^32 on every host, safe for `%` and
`>>> 0`. Ordinary arithmetic on it is not exact (`seed * 2654435761` is past 2^53), so the
`random-and-shuffle` recipe shows one mix of a round into the seed in 32-bit integer steps
(`Math.imul`, `>>> 0`) in the data source, and stops saying the round argument is unused.

### D6 — warnings, second set

- **`lower-title-twice`:** a route's first content heading saying what its header's heading says, when
  the header is the bar, shows the title twice: once large in the iOS bar and once in the page, and on
  the web just under the header (trivia in rounds 6 and 7).

### D7 — `verify` covers the journeys

Where Exact2's GPT web builds match React's command count, tokens are at or under parity (tip-splitter
32 vs 34 commands, event-rsvp 28 vs 32). The 1.27× sits in small apps that run 24–34 commands to
React's 14–23, spent after the app works: a green `verify`, then a hand-written Playwright script, more
drives and another screenshot pass. React's one browser script is its tests and its screenshots.

- **`verify` takes in the authored journeys:** each `app.test.contract` test's end screen is checked
  (`check` in both schemes) and captured, so the secondary screens and populated states are in the one
  report, not only the first screen.
- **It relaunches once on the same store** and checks the first screen holds what the tests wrote, in
  real time, bounded.
- **Start-here says one thing:** a clean `verify` is done, with a look at its screenshots at milestones.
  The line telling builders to keep driving goes.

### D8 — form chrome on the web

focus-timer lost partly on its form. On the web:

- a disabled primary button looks disabled, as the platform's does;
- a field with `aria-invalid` is outlined in the system red;
- Enter in a field with a `submit` handler submits.

Each is what a well-made web form does, and what the author leaves unsaid.
