## Summary

Charlie (2026-09-27): *"can you come up with some ideas for examples of
things that need or implement complex gestures that we can test with? i'm
also pretty interested in gestures that stay synced with animations, which
seems like it makes a big difference on feel. a specific thing that seems
hard for RN but can be a big deal is something syncing with keyboard
animating in/out. let's dig into this instead of deferring since it seems
fundamental."*

This document is the dig. It catalogs thirteen interactions that need
composed or complex gestures (§1). It works through the keyboard on every
platform (§2). It states the principles that make a gesture feel attached to
the thing it moves (§3). It checks what exact2 does today against the code
(§4). It proposes a fixture suite with measurable pass criteria (§5). It
names what these cases press on, without deciding it (§6).

The main findings:

- **Two questions are tangled, and they separate cleanly.**
  - *Composition* is which gesture wins and when: pinch with pan, long press
    then drag, a row swipe inside a pager. LLP 1057.000 is about this.
  - *Staying in step* is whether what moves stays attached to the finger,
    and to other moving things: a backdrop fading with a drag, a composer
    riding the keyboard, a sheet handing off to its list at the top edge.
  - The keyboard is entirely the second question. No recognizer is involved;
    the host follows an animation the system owns.
- **exact2's iOS host already follows the keyboard in the right way, by
  construction.** Show and hide lay out inside an animation with the
  keyboard's own duration and curve, so Core Animation moves both in one
  transaction (PresenterIOS.swift:196-221). During interactive dismissal, a
  hidden view constrained to `keyboardLayoutGuide` makes UIKit lay the view
  out on every frame of the drag (ExactViewIOS.swift:61-76, :146-160). Nobody
  has measured it frame by frame yet.
- **The web can't follow the keyboard, in principle.**
  - No browser exposes the keyboard's position per frame, or its curve and
    duration.
  - Chrome has `interactive-widget` (108+) and the Chromium-only
    VirtualKeyboard API. Safari has only `visualViewport` events.
  - No browser has interactive keyboard dismissal.
  - exact2's web host already does what the platform allows (glue.js:483-492).
    Parity here can only be of end states, a declared deviation.
- **React Native's trouble is timing, not API.** Its JavaScript learns of the
  keyboard after the fact. On Android, `keyboardDidShow` fires only after the
  animation. `KeyboardAvoidingView` starts its own animation late, and
  interactive dismissal has no per-frame signal at all.
  - The community fix, react-native-keyboard-controller, reads per-frame
    insets on Android and re-derives motion on iOS, then drives UI-thread
    values.
  - Reanimated's own `useAnimatedKeyboard` is now deprecated in its favour,
    citing iOS 26 bugs.
  - exact2 lays out in the host's own UI transaction, so it has no gap of
    this kind. Its risk is the cost of laying out on every frame of an
    interactive drag.
- **What exact2 can't express today is *derived presentation*: one moving
  value driving another property.**
  - The cases: a backdrop's opacity by dismiss distance, a card's rotation by
    its translation, a header's collapse by scroll offset, a lift by press
    progress.
  - Holds follow one property with one value. LLP 1057.000's `follows <row>`
    maps a gesture to one row by identity.
  - The only route today is `pan=`: an action per frame and a runner commit
    per frame (LLP 1043.000 D8), with no release velocity. That is Jordan's
    "code on the main thread", paid for as a transaction.
  - The web already has a standard shape for the scroll-linked half: CSS
    scroll-driven animations (`animation-timeline: scroll()` / `view()`).
    exact2 doesn't implement it.
- **Handoffs are not expressible either.**
  - A sheet that drags when its list is at the top: the gallery's sheet
    drags only by its handle.
  - A photo that dismisses on a downward drag at fit scale but pans when
    zoomed.
- **The first fixtures should be the keyboard ones.**
  - They test the part exact2 already claims to do right, and would show it
    against native Messages.
  - They need no rule change.
  - They settle how "in step" is measured, before the composition work
    starts.

## 1. The catalog

Each case is a candidate fixture. "In step with" names what must move
together. "Checks" are objective where they can be; some things only a
person with a real finger can judge, and those are marked. Hosts are where
the interaction is native or expected: **i** iOS, **m** macOS, **w** web,
**L** Linux. "Home" is where an exact2 app could hold it.

### C1. Photo viewer: pinch, pan, double-tap zoom, swipe down to dismiss

- **Gestures:**
  - Pinch and pan together, anchored at the focal point.
  - Double tap toggles fit/2× about the tap.
  - At fit scale, a mostly vertical drag becomes dismissal; when zoomed, the
    same drag pans.
  - A second finger landing mid-pan joins as pinch.
- **In step with:**
  - The image's translate/scale follows the fingers each frame.
  - The backdrop's opacity is a function of dismiss distance, typically
    `1 − |dy| / 300`, clamped.
  - On release, dismissal carries the fling into the exit, or a spring
    returns with the release velocity.
  - Grabbing a settling image catches it where it is.
- **Checks:**
  - Focal-point error: the image point under the fingers stays under them,
    ≤ 1 pt during a two-finger pinch.
  - Backdrop opacity equals the function of the displayed `dy` on every
    frame (0 frames of lag).
  - Velocity continuity at release (§5).
  - Position continuity when grabbed mid-spring (≤ 0.5 pt jump).
  - Winner sequence: at zoom 1, a 70° drag dismisses; at zoom 2, it pans.
  - *Real fingers:* whether the focal anchoring feels glued.
- **Hosts:** i, w (touch), m (trackpad magnify and scroll), L (no pinch).
- **Home:** `apps/interaction-gallery` (the viewer at app.contract:326), and
  LLP 1041 §8.5's photo campaign.

### C2. Bottom sheet with a nested scrolling list

- **Gestures:** a vertical drag on the sheet, and a vertical scroll on its
  list, arbitrated by position:
  - Dragging up on the list scrolls it until the sheet is at its top detent.
  - Dragging down on the list at scroll offset 0 moves the sheet.
  - A reversal within one contact hands control back.
- **In step with:**
  - The list's scrollport height follows the sheet's height each frame, and
    virtualization follows the scrollport.
  - Detents are chosen at release by projected position:
    `y + v · τ` (UIKit uses a deceleration-rate projection).
  - Handing off between list and sheet preserves position and velocity.
- **Checks:**
  - One contact, scripted: up (list scrolls, sheet fixed), down to offset 0,
    continue down (sheet moves), reverse (sheet up, then list scrolls). Each
    control change keeps the finger-to-content offset within 0.5 pt.
  - Release velocity is carried across the last handoff.
  - Catching an unfinished detent spring.
  - *Real fingers:* whether the handoff hitches.
- **Hosts:** i (UIKit's `UISheetPresentationController` does this natively),
  w, m.
- **Home:** `apps/interaction-gallery`'s reading sheet (app.contract:187-199),
  which today drags only by its handle, and LLP 1041 §8.5 item 4.

### C3. Swipe actions on a row, in a vertical list, in a horizontal pager

- **Gestures:** three recognizers on one contact's path:
  - the row's horizontal swipe;
  - the list's vertical scroll;
  - the pager's horizontal page.

  The system back swipe claims the leading edge.
- **In step with:** the row's actions reveal under the swipe with resistance
  past the knee; the pager's pages follow its drag; the list doesn't move
  during a horizontal gesture.
- **Checks:**
  - Winner by start point and direction: an edge start gives back; a
    horizontal start on a row with actions gives the row; a horizontal start
    on the list's padding gives the pager; a vertical start gives the list.
  - A row fully swiped then dragged the other way hands off to the pager, or
    doesn't, as the rule says (question 2).
  - *Real fingers:* diagonal starts.
- **Hosts:** i, w, m (trackpad horizontal scroll), L.
- **Home:** `apps/messages`' inbox rows (swipe actions, app.contract:514) in a
  new horizontal container, or a new fixture.

### C4. Long press, then drag to reorder, with edge auto-scroll

- **Gestures:** a long press lifts; the same contact then drags without
  lifting (sequenced). Near an edge the list scrolls on its own.
- **In step with:**
  - The lift animation (scale, shadow) follows press progress during the
    long-press wait. iOS shows this as a slow scale-down before the lift.
  - Neighbours spring aside as the dragged item crosses their midpoints.
  - Auto-scroll speed grows with depth into the edge zone, and the dragged
    item stays under the finger while the content scrolls.
- **Checks:**
  - The dragged item's displayed position minus the finger's is constant
    during auto-scroll (≤ 0.5 pt drift per second).
  - Auto-scroll speed as a function of depth.
  - Drop position is the logical gap under the finger.
- **Hosts:** i, w, m.
- **Home:** `apps/interaction-gallery`'s Arrange list (app.contract:352).
  Reorder and edge auto-scroll exist (§4). The long-press lift progress does
  not.

### C5. Interactive edge swipe back, interruptible

- **Gestures:** a leading-edge drag drives the navigation transition. Release
  completes or cancels by position and velocity. A second touch during the
  settling animation catches it.
- **In step with:**
  - The outgoing and incoming screens and the navigation bar's title
    cross-fade.
  - Dimming follows progress.
  - The keyboard, if up, follows the outgoing screen sideways (LLP 1035.001
    D5's freeze).
- **Checks:**
  - Progress equals the finger's displacement over the width on every frame.
  - Completion threshold by position (50%) or velocity.
  - Catching mid-settle keeps progress continuous.
  - The draft is kept on cancel and saved on complete (LLP 1035.001 D2).
- **Hosts:** i (UIKit's `interactivePopGestureRecognizer`), w (no standard;
  some browsers have a history swipe the page can't drive), m (trackpad
  history swipe).
- **Home:** `apps/messages` (already native on iOS, NavigationIOS.swift:139-140).

### C6. Card fling with rotation bound to translation

- **Gestures:** a free drag on a card; release past a distance or velocity
  throws it off, otherwise it springs back.
- **In step with:**
  - Rotation is `dx · k` (commonly ±15° at the edge) about a point below the
    finger.
  - The card underneath scales from 0.95 to 1 with the top card's progress.
  - The throw continues the finger's velocity; the snap-back spring starts
    with it.
- **Checks:**
  - Rotation equals `k · dx_displayed` every frame.
  - The release spring's initial velocity equals the finger's (§5, ≤ 10%).
  - The next card's scale equals the function of the top card's progress
    every frame.
  - *Real fingers:* the throw's weight.
- **Hosts:** i, w, m, L.
- **Home:** a new fixture; LLP 1057.000 §4.2 sketches it as `releaseCard`.

### C7. Pull to refresh with rubber banding

- **Gestures:** overscrolling at the top of a scroll view.
- **In step with:**
  - The spinner's reveal and rotation follow the overscroll distance.
  - Resistance grows with distance; iOS's curve is roughly
    `(1 − 1/(x·c/d + 1)) · d`.
  - The refresh triggers at a threshold, and holds the list open while
    refreshing.
  - The list settles back when the refresh ends.
- **Checks:** spinner progress equals the function of the displayed overscroll
  on every frame; the threshold fires once; the release doesn't jump.
- **Hosts:** i (`UIRefreshControl`, NodeViewIOS.swift:1099), w (overscroll is
  the browser's; Chrome Android's own pull-to-refresh competes), m (elastic
  scrolling).
- **Home:** any feed; the Bluesky consumer asked for it (LLP 1054).

### C8. Scroll-linked collapsing header

- **Gestures:** a plain scroll.
- **In step with:** the header's height, title scale and position,
  background opacity and the large-to-small title swap follow the content
  offset each frame. Snapping at rest is optional.
- **Checks:** the header's displayed geometry equals the function of the
  displayed offset every frame, with 0 frames of lag. Scroll-linked effects
  on the web that update from `scroll` events lag a frame, which is why CSS
  scroll-driven animations exist.
- **Hosts:** i (UIKit's large title does this natively; LLP 1035.001 D9 hands
  it to UIKit), w, m, L.
- **Home:** a new fixture, or Bluesky's profile header.

### C9. Context menu long press, with the lift driven by press progress

- **Gestures:** a press and hold.
- **In step with:** the target scales down slightly as the hold progresses,
  then lifts into the menu preview at recognition. Release before recognition
  springs it back from wherever the progress had it.
- **Checks:**
  - Scale is the function of hold time every frame.
  - Recognition at the platform's duration.
  - The release spring starts at the displayed scale.
- **Hosts:** i (`UIContextMenuInteraction` does all of this; exact2 uses a
  `UILongPressGestureRecognizer`, NodeViewIOS.swift:139-166), m (right click,
  no hold), w (long press on touch; `contextmenu` on desktop).
- **Home:** any list row; `apps/messages`' Tapback double tap is a sibling.

### C10. Messages: swipe to reveal timestamps, swipe to reply

- **Gestures:**
  - A leftward drag anywhere on the transcript reveals timestamps.
  - A rightward drag on one bubble pulls it toward reply.
  - The leading edge belongs to back.
- **In step with:**
  - Timestamps and every bubble move together with resistance, capped at
    58 pt (app.contract:41-42).
  - The reply bubble follows with the knee.
  - Both springs retarget if grabbed.
- **Checks:**
  - The timestamp column's displacement is `min(0.4 · offset, 58)` every
    frame.
  - The release curve matches native. QUEUE's Messages-parity entry reports
    a linked Exact-engine spring at 1.14 pt RMS against 7.70 for scroll snap.
  - Winner by direction and start point: back, reply, timestamps.
- **Hosts:** i, w.
- **Home:** `apps/messages`.
  - The timestamps are a horizontal `scroll` whose offset drives state
    (app.contract:639, `timestampsScrolled`).
  - QUEUE records that this scroll "still consumes rightward gestures over
    the transcript", blocking gutter Back.
  - This is an arbitration bug in a shipping consumer today.

### C11. Keyboard: the composer rides the keyboard in and out

Covered in depth in §2.

- **Gestures:** none. Focus and blur start a system animation.
- **In step with:**
  - The composer's bottom edge follows the keyboard's top edge on every
    displayed frame.
  - The transcript's visible end stays put: the last message stays just
    above the composer.
- **Checks:** §5 F1.
- **Hosts:** i (iPadOS docked, undocked and floating), w (Chrome Android,
  Safari iOS).
- **Home:** `apps/messages` (`interactive-widget` at app.contract:498).

### C12. Keyboard: interactive dismissal

- **Gestures:** dragging the transcript down past the composer pulls the
  keyboard down with the finger. Release either completes the hide or
  cancels back up, with the system's own animation.
- **In step with:**
  - The keyboard, the composer and the transcript's bottom inset move with
    the finger during the drag, then with the system's animation after
    release.
- **Checks:** §5 F2.
- **Hosts:** i only. The web has no interactive dismissal (§2).
- **Home:** `apps/messages` (`keyboardDismissMode="interactive"` at
  app.contract:638).

### C13. Keyboard edge cases

- **Rotation with the keyboard up:** the composer keeps its gap in both
  orientations. LLP 1035.001 D5's rotation repair measured a 4 pt
  guide-versus-notification difference.
- **A hardware keyboard attached:** only the shortcut bar shows, and the
  composer sits above it.
- **iPad undocked and floating keyboards:** the composer should *not* follow a
  floating keyboard, which is how `keyboardLayoutGuide.followsUndockedKeyboard`
  defaults. Reanimated reports height 0 for it.
- **Focus moving between fields:** keyboard types change height (335 → 308 →
  335 on the simulator, PresenterIOS.swift:160-168).
- **A sheet presented over an editor:** the sheet's own guide (LLP 1035.001
  D5).

## 2. The keyboard, in depth

### 2.1 Two ways to follow a system-owned animation

A host can stay in step with an animation it doesn't own in two ways.

- **Adopt its parameters.** The system says, before it starts, where it will
  end, how long it takes and on which curve. The host starts its own
  animation with the same parameters in the same frame, and the two move
  together without ever being compared.
  - It is exact when the host can join the system's transaction; iOS's
    Core Animation allows that.
  - It fails when the animation is interrupted in a way the system doesn't
    announce, or when the host's layout isn't a pure function of the end
    state.
- **Track its presentation per frame.** The system reports where the thing is
  on each frame, and the host lays out or paints to that value in the same
  frame.
  - It works for interactive motion, which has no curve.
  - It costs a layout or paint per frame, and has to land in the same frame
    or it lags by one.

The keyboard needs both on iOS: adopt for show and hide, track for
interactive dismissal. On Android, WindowInsetsAnimation gives the second for
everything. On the web, neither is available.

**One iOS alternative is worth naming:** `inputAccessoryView`. A view
installed as the keyboard's accessory is part of the keyboard's window, so the
system moves it with the keyboard for free, including during interactive

existed. It hands the composer to UIKit, like `UIRefreshControl` and the swipe
actions table do (LLP 1057 §1.4).

### 2.3 Why it's hard in React Native

- **The keyboard's events reach JavaScript after the native animation has
  started.**
  - With the old bridge the gap was an asynchronous hop. With JSI it is
    smaller but still a turn of the JS thread.
  - `KeyboardAvoidingView` answers `keyboardWillShow` on iOS by starting its
    own animation with the event's duration and easing. That is "adopt the
    parameters", but from a thread that may be a frame or more late, and
    through a relayout of the React tree.
- **Android has no `keyboardWillShow`.** `keyboardDidShow` fires after the
  animation, so a composer jumps. Since Android 15 forces edge to edge, the
  window no longer resizes for the keyboard at all (Margelo's write-up).
- **Interactive dismissal has no per-frame signal in JavaScript,** so the
  composer either lags the keyboard or jumps at release.
- **The fixes move the work to the UI thread:**
  - **react-native-keyboard-controller** exposes a per-frame keyboard value
    to Reanimated worklets (`useKeyboardHandler` `onMove`, `onInteractive`)
    and ships a reworked `KeyboardAvoidingView`, `KeyboardStickyView` and
    gesture areas for interactive dismissal.
    - On Android it uses `WindowInsetsAnimationCallback`.
    - On iOS its documentation says the platform "doesn't give an API to
      track each keyboard frame", only the start and end. A secondary summary
      says its `onMove` uses a `CADisplayLink`.
    - How it reads the keyboard's position per frame on iOS isn't documented
      on the pages I read.
  - **Reanimated's `useAnimatedKeyboard`** returned per-frame `height` and
    `state` shared values. Its documentation now says it is deprecated in
    favour of react-native-keyboard-controller, citing "persistent bugs on
    iOS 26". It reports height 0 for the iPad floating keyboard.
- **What exact2 avoids, and what it doesn't:**
  - It has no JS thread between the keyboard and layout. The iOS host lays
    out in the keyboard's transaction or on the guide's layout pass (§4.3).
  - It does lay out the whole tree when the viewport resizes. Under
    `resizes-content` an interactive drag resizes it every frame, so layout
    cost per frame is the number to watch, not latency.

### 2.4 What "the last message stays put" requires

When the keyboard rises, a chat's visible end should rise with it: the last
message stays just above the composer.

- **Under `resizes-content`** the scrollport shrinks, and a list that follows
  its end (`scrollFollowEnd`) keeps its end in view.
- **Under `resizes-visual`** the host insets the scroll view by the overlap
  (PresenterIOS.swift:224-232) and `reveal` moves the focused field into view
  (:238-261), inside the same animation block.
- **During interactive dismissal** the finger owns the transcript's offset
  while the keyboard follows the finger. The composer rides the guide and the
  content inset changes each frame, so the content under the finger must not
  move.

  UIKit's own Messages does this. Whether exact2's per-frame relayout keeps
  the finger's content fixed is unmeasured.

## 3. What makes a gesture feel attached

1. **Same-frame application.** A touch sample reaches the screen in the frame
   it arrived for, and whatever depends on it (the backdrop, the rotation, the
   composer) lands in that same frame. A one-frame lag between two things
   that should move together is visible as shear at 120 Hz.
2. **Velocity handoff at release.** The spring that takes over starts with the
   finger's velocity, measured over a short window (exact2's is 100 ms,
   velocity.rs:13). A zero-velocity start feels like the object was caught
   and dropped.
3. **Interruptible and retargetable.** Grabbing a moving object catches it
   where it is displayed, not where its model says, and a new target mid-flight
   starts from the current position *and* velocity. exact2's springs take their
   initial velocity from the value's motion at start (spring.rs:16-17).
4. **Presentation versus model.** During a gesture or an animation the
   displayed value leads or lags the committed value. Hit-testing, geometry
   reads and dependent values should use the presented one (LLP 1041 §8.5:
   "Hit testing must follow what is drawn").
5. **Resistance, not walls.** Past a boundary, displacement continues at a
   fraction (exact2's swipe knee: 64 pt at 0.2, `exact_motion::gesture`,
   gesture.rs:8-10). Release springs back from the resisted position.
6. **Following a system-owned animation** by adopting its parameters or
   tracking its presentation per frame (§2.1). The keyboard, a system sheet,
   a navigation transition and a scroll view's deceleration are all
   system-owned on iOS.
7. **Derived presentation.** Many feels are a *function* of one moving value
   applied to another property: fade by distance, rotate by translation,
   collapse by offset, lift by progress. Doing it per frame in app code
   costs a transaction per frame. Doing it declaratively needs a mapping from
   a moving value to a property: a timeline, in CSS's terms.

### 4.2 Gestures

- **Precedence:** the table in LLP 1057.001 §1, one drag binding per node
  (rule 4) and one built-in simultaneity, pinch with pan on a transform
  binding (rule 6).
- **The bindings:**
  - `swiperight`;
  - `heightDragFor` (a `UIPanGestureRecognizer` on iOS,
    HeightDragIOS.swift:6-40);
  - `transformDragFor`;
  - `reorderFor` (a long press and a pan on iOS, ReorderIOS.swift:13-15).
- **Edge auto-scroll** in reorder exists: iOS writes `contentOffset`
  (ReorderIOS.swift:68-70), and the web runs a frame loop at 720 px/s
  (motion-glue.js:823-832).
- **The web applies drag samples in the pointer event** (motion-glue.js:540-554):
  the held value is written before the next frame.
- **`pan=` dispatches to an app action once per animation frame**
  (input-glue.js:131, `requestAnimationFrame(flush)`) and commits through the
  runner (event.rs:769).
  - It is the only path where app code runs per frame during a gesture: the
    reflow dragon and the textflow orbs use it, and LLP 1043.000 D8 blesses
    it for layout-affecting motion.
  - It has no release velocity.
- **The sheet in `apps/interaction-gallery`:**
  - It drags by its header only (`heightDragFor="reading-sheet"`,
    app.contract:189).
  - Its list is a separate virtualized scroll with `overscroll-behavior:
    contain` (:196).
  - Release picks a detent in app code from height plus `velocity · 0.15`

  - There is no list-to-sheet handoff.

### 4.4 The agent

- **One contact per session,** in `down`, `move`, `hold`, `up` and `cancel`
  phases (LLP 1035.003 D1).
  - Delivered as real input on the web (CDP) and on macOS (NSEvents).
  - On the iOS Simulator, through the desktop pointer (the carrier sets
    `pointer: true`, scripts/agent.mjs:696).
  - Not on a physical iPhone, which has no touch synthesis.
- **`tap … pinch`** is `platform` on the web and macOS, and `recognized` on
  iOS (LLP 1057.001 §5).
- **The seekable clock drives springs and holds.** It can't drive UIKit's own
  animations (the keyboard, sheets, navigation) except by applying their end
  state at once, which is the frozen default.
- **Nothing samples presentation per displayed frame.** `layout` reads the
  current frame once.

### 5.1 Measuring "in step"

All of these need a sampler that reads presentation on every displayed frame,
which exact2 doesn't have (§4.4).

- **iOS:** a test-only `CADisplayLink` probe in agent mode, reading each
  watched view's `layer.presentation()` frame and the keyboard guide's
  `layoutFrame` at each tick. The guide is the model value, exact during
  interactive drags; during notification-driven show and hide it jumps to the
  end state.

  So F1 checks the adopted parameters instead:
  - the composer's position animation has the notification's duration and
    curve-7 timing;
  - it begins in the same transaction as the keyboard's.

  Comparing against the keyboard's own presentation needs its window's layer,
  which is private hierarchy. That's acceptable only in a test probe, and not
  verified to be reachable.
- **Web:** a `requestAnimationFrame` sampler reading `visualViewport.height`,
  `offsetTop` and the watched elements' `getBoundingClientRect()`. It can
  measure jumps against the viewport; it can't measure against the keyboard,
  which no API exposes per frame.

The metrics:

| Metric | Definition | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| Edge gap | max over frames of \|composer.bottom − keyboard.top\| (guide or visual viewport) | ≤ 0.5 pt on iOS; on the web, no frame where the composer is covered or more than 1 px above the viewport's bottom after the first resize |
| Lag | frames of shift that best aligns two sampled series (cross-correlation) | 0 |
| Velocity continuity | \|v_spring(0⁺) − v_finger(release)\| / \|v_finger\| | ≤ 10% |
| Catch continuity | displayed position jump when a moving object is grabbed | ≤ 0.5 pt |
| Derived value | max \|shown − f(driver shown)\| per frame | ≤ 0.5 pt, 0.5°, or 0.01 opacity |
| Anchor drift | finger-to-content offset change during a handoff or auto-scroll | ≤ 0.5 pt |

### 5.2 What only real fingers show

- **Anything with UIKit's own recognizers or transitions on a phone:**
  - pinch focal anchoring;
  - the edge swipe;
  - interactive dismissal;
  - diagonal starts.
- **The weight of a throw, and whether a handoff hitches.**

The iPhone is reachable over the network from this Mac (LLP 1052's run). A
real-finger session needs a person and a written script of what to try, with
the sampler logging.

## 6. Implications

### 6.1 What these cases press on (DEFERRED §Motion)

| Line | Pressed by |
|---|---|
| "A gesture arena, claims, leases, compositions, or an interactive-navigation model" | C1 (dismiss vs pan by state), C3 (handoff after a full swipe), C4 (sequenced long press and drag, today a fixed binding), C5 on the web (no platform model) |
| "A second value graph. No shared-value plane, derived values, bindings" | C1 backdrop, C6 rotation and next-card scale, C8 header, C9 lift: all derived presentation |
| "Decay and sequence drivers" | C1: a zoomed photo that coasts after a pan (UIScrollView decelerates; a spring doesn't coast). C6's throw exits on a spring today, by design. |
| "General layout transitions … laying out per frame" | C12: interactive keyboard dismissal under `resizes-content` resizes the viewport every frame. It's a viewport change, not an animated layout property, but it is layout per frame. |
| "No per-frame app code" (LLP 1057.000 §4.2's "no `on change`") | Every derived case, if done in app code; `pan=` already does it (LLP 1043.000 D8) |

The keyboard presses on none of these on iOS. It is host work that already
exists, and measuring it needs no rule change.

### 6.2 Against LLP 1057.000's designs

- **A (the table, built)** covers C10's arbitration once its bug is fixed,
  C5 natively, and the existing bindings.
- **B and C (the algebra)** would add C1's conditional composition (with a
  state-dependent `exclusively`), C3's handoff and C4's sequencing.
  - Their `follows <row>` maps one gesture value onto one row, so none of the
    derived cases (C1 backdrop, C6, C8, C9) is covered.
  - Handoffs between a scroll view and a drag (C2) are covered by neither.
- **The keyboard (C11–C13) is orthogonal to A, B and C.** It's about
  following a system animation, not recognizing gestures. So is any
  system-owned sheet or transition.

So Charlie's two interests may call for different mechanisms:
- the algebra for composition;
- something for derived presentation and handoffs;
- host work, plus a measurement, for system animations.

### 6.3 Directions, not decisions

1. **Timelines for derived presentation.**
   - CSS already has the web-standard shape for scroll-linked effects:
     scroll-driven animations (`animation-timeline: scroll()` / `view()`,
     with `animation-range`). Keyframes are laid over a scroll position
     instead of time, and the browser runs them on the compositor.
   - exact2 could implement that standard. The Contract already has
     `@keyframes` and `animation` rows (DEFERRED's 2026-09-26 SVG and CSS
     animations expansion).
   - For gestures, the same shape with a gesture's displacement as the
     timeline would be a declared deviation (no CSS feature exists):
     "the backdrop's opacity animates from 1 to 0 over the dismiss drag's
     0–300 pt".
   - That is derived presentation without a value graph: a keyframe list
     evaluated against one moving value, which the engine already evaluates
     against time.
2. **Per-frame app code as a first-class path (Jordan's preference).**
   - `pan=` exists. It would need phases (`begin`, `end` with velocity) and a
     guarantee that its commit lands in the frame of its sample.
   - Its cost is a runner transaction per frame; it's unmeasured at 120 Hz
     (LLP 1043.000 §6 leaves the frame-cadence source open).
   - It expresses everything, including handoffs, in app code.
3. **Handoffs as host behaviour of a binding.** A height binding that yields
   to its nested scroll at offset 0 and takes over at the top, as UIKit's
   sheet does, is one rule rather than an authorable arena.
4. **The keyboard: measure first.** Build F1 and F2 with the sampler before
   any design work. If iOS already holds 0 frames of lag, as the code
   suggests, it is a demonstrable advantage over React Native's usual result.
   On the web, adopting the VirtualKeyboard API on Chromium is a small,
   separable question (Q4).

### 6.8 F1 measured: the composer against the keyboard, on an iPhone (2026-09-27)

**Method.**
- **Setup:** Messages, built at `90135298` for an iPhone 17 Pro Max (iOS
  26.6.1), was driven through the agent in `timing: 'platform'`, so the
  keyboard animated at its natural timing.
- **The sampler** was a local-only probe, never committed. On every displayed
  frame (a 120 Hz display link) it logged two edges in the root view's
  points:
  - the keyboard's presented top edge: a zero-size view pinned to the
    keyboard layout guide, which UIKit moves inside the keyboard's
    animation;
  - the composer bar's presented bottom edge: its presentation layer.
- **The drive:** the probe toggled the composer's focus six times, two
  seconds apart.

| Transition (×3 each) | Frames of motion | Keyboard | Composer bar | Largest per-frame difference in progress |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Show | 47 (393–397 ms) | −311 pt | −345 pt | 0.00% (0.01 pt) |
| Hide | 47 (417–425 ms) | +311 pt | +345 pt | 0.00% (0.01 pt) |

- **In step: yes.**
  - Both edges start moving on the same frame.
  - On every displayed frame the bar is at the same fraction of its travel
    as the keyboard.
  - The bar travels 34 pt further, the home indicator's inset, so the gap
    between them closes along the keyboard's own curve.
  - This is §2's claim measured: the host lays out inside the keyboard's
    animation, and nothing trails it by a frame.
- **Each transition starts late, though.**
  - The frame on which focus changes takes 29–45 ms instead of 8.3, and the
    first show took 100 ms then 28 ms.
  - The next frame then takes up to 45 ms more, before the 47 smooth frames.
  - That is 2–5 frames of main-thread work at the focus change: UIKit's
    keyboard session, the focus action, and the relayout that
    `resizes-content` triggers.
  - Not yet split. The next step is a Time Profiler trace of the same drive,
    against Apple's Messages as the reference.
- **Still to measure:** F2, interactive dismissal, needs real fingers.
