## Summary

When, in one commit, a glass element with `glassId="send"` leaves a glass group and another with the same `glassId` enters it, a **ghost** — a paint-only glass shape in the group's container, holding no content — springs from the leaving glass's box and corner radius to the entering glass's, while the entering element's own glass is held hidden; the container unions the ghost with its neighbours every frame; when the spring rests, the ghost is removed and the entering glass shown, in the same place and shape. SwiftUI's `glassEffectID` does this; the platforms' own appear/disappear animations do not (LLP 1053.000.000.000 r1 §2: iOS cross-fades two separate shapes, macOS ignores a glass view's alpha and jumps an animated frame), while glass moving through glass in a container unions on both.

The ghost is a node of Exact's motion engine, so `clock +N` and `clock settle` move it, the display link runs while it moves, and a screenshot at any clock is reproducible. Its "from" box is captured in the Rust host when the leaving node is destroyed, before anything forgets it; pairing is per commit, not per presentation batch.

## 1. What exists (the reviews' findings, and the map)

- **Commits.** `commit_tree` (`host/apple/src/host.rs:1157-1304`) folds several `Timed` receipts into one Swift batch: per receipt it begins exits, removes each destroyed key from `keys` and the host's `Mirror` (`host.rs:1182-1194`, emitting `destroy` unless an exit holds it), creates and updates; children and **layout run once, after all receipts** (`host.rs:1215-1224`, `:1280`), so the entering node's box exists only then. The engine is advanced to each receipt's `at_ms` in a second loop (`host.rs:1239-1272`) and finally to `now_ms`.
- **What survives a destroy.** The kernel frees the slot during apply; the host's `Mirror` (`host.rs:95-103`) holds the node's props (`glassGroup`, `backgroundMaterial`), its last style JSON (the `border_radius*` keys) and its parent-relative frame until the destroy loop removes it.
- **The engine** never references the kernel: nodes are numbers the host chooses, kernel nodes are `generation << 32 | index` and generation 0 is never minted (`kernel/src/arena.rs:706`), so numbers below 2³² are free for the host. `Property::Layout` is a non-wire four-component box that springs as physics (`motion/src/property.rs:84-88`, `:249-259`), governed by `set_layout_transition` (`engine.rs:314-326`); any running curve counts in `quiescent`, `spatial` and `settle_time` (`engine.rs:610-640`), which drive the batch's `motion` (`host.rs:1141-1151`), the display link (`Session.swift:897`, `:1336-1368`) and the agent's `settle` (`host.rs:820-826`, `Agent.swift:282-286`, `:366`, `:399`).
- **Presentation.** `present()` (`host/apple/src/presence.rs:200-271`) maps each changed engine value through `keys` or `leaving` and drops what it cannot map; Swift's `present` case drops an unknown id (`PresenterIOS.swift:772`, `PresenterMac.swift:931-941`).
- **Glass bodies** are the content's container (a material's children sit in its effect view, `NodeViewIOS.swift:206`, `NodeViewMac.swift:554`) or the content itself (a native glass button's `UIButton`/`NSButton`), and their frames are owned by autoresizing or by `ControlHost`. Neither can be moved for a morph.

## 2. Design

**D1. `glassId`.** A host-policy string prop beside `glassGroup` (a name, a custom-ident: letters, digits, `-`, `_`), on an element with glass: a `backgroundMaterial` glass. Contract refuses it elsewhere, and on a native glass button in this version (its body is its content; §4). Inert on the web (the attribute is written), Linux and Android, before iOS 26 / macOS 26, and in an app that keeps the earlier design (`LinkedDesign.liquidGlass` false). It is not `view-transition-name` (LLP 1013's snapshot cross-fade of a whole element on a flagged action is a different effect), and it does not start one.

**D2. Pairing, per commit.** In the Rust host's per-receipt tree loop:
- *Leaving*: a node destroyed (or beginning an exit) in this receipt whose mirror has `glassId` and glass, whose nearest ancestor with `glassGroup` is still mounted after the receipt. Its group, its `glassId`, its absolute box (the mirror's frame chained through its ancestors' mirrors, which still hold the previous layout) and its corner radii (the mirror's style) are captured then, before the mirror is removed.
- *Entering*: a node created in the same receipt with glass and `glassId` under a `glassGroup`.
- A pair is one leaving and one entering with the same `glassId` under the same group in the same receipt. Two candidates on either side, or a leave and an enter in different receipts of one batch, are no pair: the swap is immediate, as today — so stepping a clock through the commits one by one and seeking across them in one jump give the same result.
- A pair begins after the batch's layout (`host.rs:1280`), when the entering node's box exists; its start time is its receipt's `at_ms`, so a morph whose commit is earlier in a batch is already part-way when it is first drawn, as an engine animation started at that receipt is.

**D3. The ghost on the engine.** Each pair is an engine node numbered from a host counter below 2³². The host sets its layout transition to `all spring(322, 35.9, 1)` (critically damped, a 0.35 s response; UIKit's own glass motion is a smooth spring), observes `Layout` at the leaving box (no motion), then at the entering box (the spring starts), and the same for a new non-wire property `Radius` (four corner radii, sprung alike; added to `Property` beside `Layout`, with `name`, `components`, `springs`, `identity`, `Engine::remove` and the `lowered` table). From then on the engine owns it: `settle_time`, `quiescent` and `spatial` include it, so `clock settle` lands on its rest, the display link runs while it moves (spatially, at full rate), and an agent's tick samples it.

**D4. Ops.** Three new batch ops: `ghost` (create: ghost id, group view id, material kind, box in the group's coordinates, radii; and the entering node's id), `present` for a ghost id with `property: "ghost"` (box and radii as they move), and `ghost-end` (remove). `present()` (`presence.rs`) handles ghost nodes before its key lookup, and emits `ghost-end` and removes the engine node when both properties have stopped running (`Engine::is_active`).

**D5. Swift.** `ghost` creates a paint-only glass (`UIVisualEffectView` with the material's `UIGlassEffect`; `NSGlassEffectView` with its style) in the group's container (`glassGroupView`'s content), above no content, not hit-testable, hidden from accessibility, and hides the entering node's own glass body (`materialView.isHidden = true`) while its content draws as authored; `present` sets the ghost's frame and corner configuration; `ghost-end` removes the ghost and shows the body. The ghost draws in the group's container for its whole life, so it unions with the group's other glass every frame.

**D6. Isolation wins.** At every batch end `glassGroups.reconcile` checks the entering node's path as LLP 1053.000.000 D4 does (opacity, mask, clip). If the entering node is isolated at any frame of the morph, the ghost is removed and its body shown, isolated as it would be without a morph; the engine node is left to finish unseen (its `ghost-end` then finds nothing). The leaving node is gone by then; its isolation at the moment it left is not read (its box and radii are).

**D7. Lifetime and interruption.** The ghost belongs to the group, not to either node: the group's destruction, the entering node's destruction or its loss of glass or `glassId`, and a pool retire of the entering row remove the ghost (and the engine node) in the same batch. A new pair whose leaving node is a ghost's entering node (A → B, then B → C before B's ghost rests) starts from the ghost's current presented box and radii, not B's laid-out box, and replaces the old ghost.

**D8. Reduced motion** is the producer's (LLP 1002 §9): `glassId=(viewport.prefersReducedMotion ? none : "send")` turns it off, and the docs say so.

**D9. Tests.** Contract: `glassId` on a glass material accepted; elsewhere and on a native button refused. Rust host (`host/apple` tests with a kernel and a plan): a paired swap in one commit makes one ghost whose sampled box at 0, 175 and 350 ms equals the spring's (closed form against `motion`'s own `Spring::sample`), and whose end is `settle_time`; two candidates, two groups, a leave and an enter in two receipts of one batch, and a stepped versus a jump seek; an interruption A → B → C starting from the presented box. XCTests (both platforms): the ghost in the group's container, the body hidden and shown, the ghost removed on isolation and on the group's destruction. The fixture `scripts/fixtures/glass-group.contract` gains a compose button that becomes a send button (`glassId="compose"` on both), filmed with `screenshot … over 500 every 40 window` on iOS and macOS, and the agent's `clock settle` ending on the rest.

## 3. Build

1. Schema (`glassId`), Contract and tests.
2. `motion`: `Property::Radius`. Rust host: a `ghost.rs` beside `presence.rs` (capture in the tree loop, pairing, the engine node, the ops; `host.rs` is at its line cap, so it gains only the calls); its tests.
3. Swift: the three ops in `Batch.swift`/`BatchShapes.swift`, a `GlassGhost.swift` (both platforms), the isolation check in `reconcile`; XCTests; the fixture, filmed.
4. Docs: LLP 1053.000.000 §5, LLP 1001's glass paragraph.

## 4. Open questions

1. **Native glass buttons.** A `UIButton.Configuration.glass()` draws its glass and its title together; hiding the body hides the title. A ghost could instead stand in for the whole button (the button hidden and a glass ghost drawn under a snapshot of its title), or the button's configuration could draw no background during the morph. Out of this version; which, if any?
2. **Timing authoring.** The spring is fixed. A `transition` on `glassId` (`transition="glass-id 300ms ease"`) would be Exact's own syntax for something CSS spells `::view-transition-group(name)`; wait for an app that asks?
3. **A lone appear or leave in a group** stays immediate (no unseekable platform materialize).
