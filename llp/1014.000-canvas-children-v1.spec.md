**Related:** LLP 1014 (the decisions; §2 names what this leaves undone), LLP 1009 (the canvas), LLP 1012 (the smoke and the agent API this is verified through), LLP 1008 §1 (one `NSView` per node), LLP 1007 §1 (the web host's elements)

## Summary

A `canvas` holds children. The kernel lays them out in its box (LLP 1014
D1); the web host composites them over the surface inside a wrapper element
(D2); the macOS presenter, when the surface asks, paints them into a texture
the surface samples and composites the children themselves at alpha 0 (D2,
D3), capturing again on four sources of change (D4) — with hit-testing and
accessibility still the kernel's boxes (D5). The Caltrain aurora is the first
use: three texts refracted through it. Where this document and the code
disagree, the code and its checks are the authority.

## 1. What landed

- **Kernel** — `NodeType::can_hold_children` includes `Canvas`
  (`kernel/src/node.rs`); nothing else: a `Canvas` is `display: block`, never
  measured, sized by its rows, and Taffy lays out what it holds.
  `kernel/tests/canvas.rs` holds the three facts: block children fill the
  canvas's width at its origin; a taller child overflows and never sizes it;
  a percentage child resolves against it.
- **Contract** — the lowering's `lower-leaf-children` refusal lifts with the
  predicate (no corpus reject named `canvas`). `contract/corpus/canvas.contract`
  gains children under `map` and a third canvas, `sky`, whose surface samples
  its children and which holds a button and an input — the smoke's fixture.
- **Web host** — the create op for a `Canvas` still carries `tag: "canvas"`;
  the glue makes a `<div>` for the node and puts a `<canvas data-surface>`
  inside it (`position:absolute; inset:0; z-index:-1`), the kernel's children
  after it in flow; the `children` op skips `data-surface`; the GPU glue
  resolves a canvas node's surface element through the wrapper. The host
  appends `position:relative;` (unless the author positioned the canvas) and
  `isolation:isolate;` to a canvas's CSS — the containing block and the
  stacking context that keep the surface above the canvas's background and
  below its children (`host/web/src/host.rs::host_css`;
  `host/web/tests/canvas.rs`).
- **GPU module** — `Surface::wants_children(&self) -> bool` (default
  `false`) and `Surface::children(&mut self, Option<&TextureView>)` (default
  no-op); `Module::wants_children`, `Module::texture` (an `Rgba8Unorm`
  texture, `TEXTURE_BINDING | COPY_DST`, created or replaced at a new size,
  `write_texture` in place, the canvas marked dirty); the C ABI gains
  `gpu_wants_children(id)` and `gpu_texture(id, w, h, bytes, len)`. The web
  ABI is unchanged.
- **The aurora** — `wants_children` is `true`; a texture and a sampler join
  its bind group (a 1×1 transparent texture when there are no children); the
  fragment samples the children at the fragment's uv, refracted by the warp
  field, split per channel, glowing under bright ribbons, composed
  premultiplied over the sky.
- **Apple presenter** — a canvas node's children live in a flipped overlay
  view above the `MetalView` (`NodeView.overlay`, the `container`). When the
  module creates a canvas's surface, it asks `gpu_wants_children`; a surface
  that wants them is captured (`Capture.bitmap`: `cacheDisplay` into
  premultiplied RGBA at the window's scale, the overlay painted opaque
  regardless of its alpha, `Capture.capturing` set for the duration),
  uploaded with `gpu_texture`, and the overlay composites at alpha 0 from
  then on. It captures again when: (a) a batch touched a node under it
  (`Presenter.touched`, every op but `roots` and `create`; a `children` op on
  the canvas itself) — at the end of `apply`; (b) a node view under it draws
  outside a batch (`NodeView.draw` → `repaintThrough`); (c) a scroll node
  under it scrolls (`boundsDidChangeNotification` on the clip view); (d)
  every display-link frame while the first responder is under it
  (`Canvases.editing(under:)`, which also keeps the display link running).
  (b) and (c) coalesce per run-loop turn (`scheduleCapture`) and are
  ignored on the turn a capture already ran (`paintedThisTurn`), so a batch
  never captures twice. An overlay with no subviews is never captured unless
  a texture was uploaded before (then once more, to clear it). In agent mode
  each capture writes `canvas <id>: captured WxH in N ms, uploaded K KiB in
  N ms` to stderr, which `logs` carries.
- **Two presenter rules the fixture found** — nothing is focused at launch
  (`initialFirstResponder` is the viewport, no key-view loop): the web's rule,
  where AppKit would have made the first field the first responder and a
  canvas holding an input showed a blinking caret from its first frame. And
  the GPU module loads from the **first draw** (`NodeView.draw` schedules
  `loadIfNeeded` on the next turn): a batch's own attempt runs before the
  display pass and found no first draw, and an app with no later batch — no
  image, no timer, no motion — never loaded the module at all (the Caltrain
  app masked this: its image load brings a second batch). LLP 1009 D4's rule
  holds; its trigger was wrong.
- **The readback fixture (LLP 1014 §2 step 3, built).** Native half:
  `apps/caltrain/gpu/tests/children.rs` hands the aurora a known children
  bitmap — an opaque white block on a transparent ground, made as
  `Module::texture` makes one — renders it into the module-owned texture
  (`exact_gpu::fixture`) and reads back: a transparent texture changes no
  pixel; white ink is white; ten pixels from the block the sky is exactly the
  sky; the white area is the block's within the refracted edge; `None`
  restores the sky. Host half: smoke step 10 crops the fixture's `sky` canvas
  from the host's screenshot by its `layout` box (a window shot's title bar
  subtracted) at clock 0, before any tap or edit, and holds it against
  `scripts/fixtures/canvas-sky.<host>.png` within a band (≤ 1% of pixels
  differing by > 8/255); `--record-canvas` rewrites a host's reference after a
  deliberate change. `scripts/png.mjs` is the decoder, encoder, crop, and
  diff the scripts need (no dependency).
- **Build** — `host/apple/build.mjs` replaces the GPU dylib instead of
  overwriting it: rewriting a mapped, ad-hoc-signed file in place poisons the
  kernel's cached signature for that inode, and every later `dlopen` died with
  `SIGKILL (Code Signature Invalid)` (found landing this, three crash reports
  deep).
- **The app** — the aurora canvas holds a column of three texts
  (`aurora-title` is the station's name). **The line map's stations are its
  children** (the second use): a column with 16 points of padding and
  14-point rows spread `space-between`, each row two equal flex spacers
  around a dot — 14 points red for the selected station, 7 gray otherwise —
  and the station's name in the right spacer. The map surface draws only the
  line and the moving train (left of the line now); its `top`/`bottom` are
  the rows' first and last centres, 23 points in, so the train interpolates
  between the same points the kernel put the dots at. The two agree by
  construction, and the map has names it could not have had from a shader.
  Composited *over* the surface on both hosts (the map does not sample its
  children): D2's representation, identical on the web and on macOS.
- **The presenter renders no canvas for a window that cannot be seen**
  (`Canvases.visible`, `windowDidChangeOcclusionState`): an occluded
  window's Metal layer hands out no drawables and asking blocks the main
  thread up to a second for nothing (§3). The frame source itself keeps
  running for motion — pausing it too made an agent-driven app behind the
  terminal wait out a `settle` (185 s smoke), since motion is applied on it.

### 1a. The app inside the sky (2026-08-30, the night's first demo)

- **The root is a canvas.** `main` is `height="100%"` (the kernel resolves a
  root's percentage height against the viewport offer; the web page gives
  `html`, `body`, and `#exact-root` `height: 100%` so a bare div does the
  same) and holds one `canvas surface=glass(material, selectedId)` filling
  it; the page's `scroll` is the canvas's child, so the whole interface is
  captured and painted through the surface — every tap, keystroke, and
  scroll still the kernel's. Panels are translucent white (`#ffffffd9`, an
  alpha byte on any colour) so the sky shows through them.
- **`glass`**, the surface (`apps/caltrain/gpu/src/glass.rs`,
  `shaders/glass.wgsl`): two passes — the sky (the aurora's field) into a
  quarter-resolution texture, then the children composed over it at full
  resolution — in one of three materials from state: **glass** (frosted
  panels over the blurred sky, a slow sheen, the sky's field refracting the
  ink uniformly by 0.4%), **ink** (paper with fibres, the interface as sepia
  ink that bleeds a hair), **crt** (dark ink as amber phosphor, scanlines,
  bloom, a flicker from the clock, a two-pixel barrel). Three buttons switch
  it; the "Next stop" block switches its text colours with it.
- **A change of children crossfades.** `Frame::children_generation` counts
  uploads; a surface that answers `wants_previous_children` gets a second
  texture the module copies the children into before each write (the copy
  submitted ahead of the enqueued write, which is the order that keeps it
  "before"); glass crossfades over 320 ms of the frame clock, paper dissolves
  grain by grain, the crt cuts — after a quiet spell; a change within 100 ms
  of the last is motion and shows at once, or an animation under the glass
  would trail its own previous frame. Under the agent's clock the fade
  advances only when the clock does — a screenshot after `clock +1000` is
  settled.
- **A canvas nested under a canvas painted through its surface** (the line
  map, inside the sky) **is never presented**: its Metal layer is under an
  alpha-0 overlay and is never composited, and presenting to it blocks
  `nextDrawable` a full second per frame — the app froze this way once. It is
  read back instead: `gpu_readback` renders the surface again into a
  module-owned texture (`Module::readback`, the fixture's path; a readback
  clears its dirty flag), and `NodeView.draw` paints that picture during the
  ancestor's capture. A change to its inputs asks the ancestor to capture
  again. A nested surface that wants every frame is not supported (it would
  recapture the ancestor every frame); the map wants none.
- **A full-window Metal layer must stay composited** (`isOpaque = false`):
  an opaque layer covering the window is given a direct-to-display path whose
  drawables return only at vsync, so a canvas rendering on the display link
  blocked ~8 ms in `nextDrawable` per frame, back to back, and the main
  thread never idled — ops starved. Composited, a drawable returns as soon as
  the compositor has read it.
- **Costs, measured:** the whole window (840×1720 px) captures in **5–8 ms**
  (15 ms cold) and uploads (5.6 MB) in **1 ms**; the glass render is **p50
  0.23 ms, p90 0.38, max 2 ms** at the display link; the app idles at 4% of a
  core and 103 MB. The first launch after a build pays macOS's assessment of
  the new dylib (seconds, once per build; the smoke's step-9 poll allows 10 s).

### 1b. Transforms and per-child textures — D5, built (2026-08-30, the second demo)

- **The trait:** `Surface::wants_children_each` (default `false`) asks for
  every direct child as its own texture; `Surface::child(index, texture,
  frame)` hands one over with its kernel frame in the canvas's points (the
  texture created or resized, its contents updated in place; `None` when
  the child is gone); `children_count(n)`; and `Surface::placement(index)
  -> Option<Placement>` — a 3×3 homography, row major, from the child's own
  points to the canvas's, plus a depth, larger nearer the eye. `None` is the
  kernel's frame. This is the browser's `updateElementGeometry` with the
  arrow reversed: the surface tells the host where it put the child.
- **The module and ABI:** `gpu_wants_children_each`, `gpu_child(id, index,
  x, y, w, h, wpx, hpx, bytes, len)`, `gpu_children_count`, `gpu_placement
  (id, index, out[10])`; `Module::child/children_count/placement`.
- **The presenter:** a canvas whose surface wants each child captures the
  overlay's direct children one by one (`captureEach`; 48 cards in
  19–25 ms) and, after every frame the surface renders — presented or read
  back — reads each child's placement onto its `NodeView.placement`.
  **Hit-testing goes through the placements** (`NodeView.hitTest` on the
  canvas): the point in canvas space is mapped through each placed child's
  inverse homography, nearest child first — what is seen on top is what a
  tap reaches, the browser's hit-test-order rule — straight from the canvas
  to the child, skipping the box AppKit would test; a press's inside-check
  (`NodeView.local`) maps the window point the same way, so a press begun
  on a fanned card ends on it. **Accessibility** reports the mapped bounding
  box (`accessibilityFrame`) and posts a layout change when a placement
  moves. The **agent's `layout` and `tap`** use the placed box (`Agent.box`),
  so a tap lands where the card is seen.
- **`stack`**, the surface (`stack.rs`, `stack.wgsl`): the northbound
  departures as cards — each a direct child of a `canvas`, a `button` the
  kernel laid out — placed in depth with a little perspective: a closed
  deck (cards behind one another, twelve points down and forty-six back
  each), an open fan (the soonest train nearest and lowest, later ones
  stepping up and away, tilted, seven at most), the focused card forward
  and the rest dimmed, departed trains faded and moved off the canvas
  (nothing hits a card that is not there). Poses move on critically damped
  springs integrated in 1/120 s steps over the frame clock — a clock that
  jumps, the agent's, settles the same way a display link would — and the
  surface wants frames until they settle. Every placement is the model
  matrix's homography (z dropped: a card is flat) with the card's depth.
  The pipeline blends premultiplied; clip z is a constant, since a receded
  or tilted card would otherwise be clipped away. A hermetic test
  (`tests/stack.rs`) renders the fan over a moving clock and checks the
  placements, the depth order, the settle, and the departed card.
- **The deck in the app:** a `Deck` button toggles `state deck`; the
  northbound board becomes `canvas surface=stack(northBoard, focus, deck,
  nowMs)` holding a card per departure; a tap on a card sets `focus`. The
  canvas is nested under the sky, so it is never presented: its picture is
  read back into the sky's capture, and while its springs run it asks the
  sky to capture again — only when the clock has moved, so a clock that
  stands still never spins it. On the web the cards are a plain column over
  the surface (D2).
- **Two presenter rules this found.** A nested canvas painted through its
  own surface has its overlay hidden during the ancestor's `cacheDisplay`
  (which paints views regardless of alpha) — its picture arrives by
  readback, or its children would appear twice. And **drawable
  starvation**: when a window is covered — every session's agent-driven app
  launches centred, each covering the last — the compositor stops taking
  the layer's drawables and `nextDrawable` blocks a second per render while
  the occlusion notice cannot land on a blocked main thread. A render that
  waited more than 200 ms now stops presenting that canvas until the
  occlusion state changes or a second passes (one lost second, not every
  frame), and agent-mode windows are spread by pid so none is fully hidden.
- **Under the agent's clock**, a change after a quiet spell crossfades over
  the frame clock: a screenshot before the next `clock` shows the previous
  children, one after a `clock +400` or more the new. Not a bug: the frame
  clock is the surface's clock. And never log per tick in agent mode: a
  240-line-per-second stderr stream through the driver stalled every op.

### 1c. The review folds (2026-08-29, r2)

Two code reviews of §1a/§1b (codex and grok, mutually blind, both NOT
READY on the reviewed tree) converged on three defects, folded the same
day:

- **A placed child is only where the surface put it.** `NodeView.hitTest`
  on a canvas with placements fell through to AppKit's own hit-testing on a
  miss, which tests the overlay's subviews at their *kernel* frames — so a
  tap on empty fan space, or on a departed card's column cell, reached a
  card nobody could see. Now a miss through every placement tests only the
  children the surface left in place, then is the canvas itself.
- **A call that moved something settles the canvases before it replies.**
  `clock`, `tap`, and `type` used to return while the frame — and the
  placements read after it — waited for the display link; the next call
  could see stale placements. `Canvases.settle(now:)` paints the pending
  captures and renders every canvas at the landed clock (bounded: a nested
  canvas that wants a frame asks its ancestor to capture again, once more
  here). `clock settle` remains motion's fixed point (LLP 1012): a
  surface's own springs run on the frame clock the agent advances — after
  `tap deck-toggle`, the deck is at its closed poses until a `clock +N`
  carries the springs (a 2 s clamp on one frame's `dt`, so `clock +60000`
  lands settled; `tests/stack.rs`). Pinned by smoke step 11: the deck's
  placements move on a `clock +100` — the nested canvas is read back into
  the sky's capture before the reply. (A Mac whose display is asleep
  occludes the window, and `Canvases.tick` is gated on occlusion: nothing
  renders or reads back until a timer redraws text, and `screencapture -l`
  exits 1 — the smoke wants the display on, LLP 1008 §8.)
- **A new children texture shows at once.** A resize replaces both
  textures and the previous one is blank; a quiet change after a resize
  crossfaded from nothing. `GlassSurface` now treats a fresh texture like
  the first upload. A change *during* a fade restarts it from the latest
  picture (a small jump the `CONTINUOUS_MS` rule already hides for bursts):
  declared, not fixed.

Also folded: the C ABI checks every host pointer for null before it
becomes a slice, byte counts for overflow, and a child index for order
(`gpu_placement` takes `out`'s length and writes unaligned); a refusal is
reported once (`gpu/tests/module.rs`). `accessibilityFrame` maps through
the placed *ancestor*, as the agent's `layout` does, so a card's countdown
text reports the fanned box to VoiceOver. `Canvases.tick` no longer
captures a second time on the turn a keystroke's batch already captured.
`host/web/tests/agent.rs` reads the slots by name (three new ones). Left
from the reviews: the 1 Hz tick's countdown change is a quiet-spell upload
and crossfades the digits over 320 ms — by design, and worth watching;
`children.rs` still drives the aurora, not glass (the fade logic has no
hermetic test).

## 2. Deviations from LLP 1014's text

- D2 named `wants_children` as the trait's question; it is, and the module
  answers it per instance (`gpu_wants_children`) so the presenter captures
  only canvases whose surface samples — the map's overlay is never captured.
- D4's four sources are built as listed; the coalescer and the
  `paintedThisTurn` guard are the transcription of "captures coalesce per
  run-loop turn". Source (b) fires for every `NodeView.draw` under an
  overlay, which today means images finishing their load; nothing else draws
  outside a batch yet.
- The agent-mode stderr line is new: the smoke needs to see captures, and
  `logs` already carries the host's stderr.
- §2 step 3 is built as two halves (§1); its second sentence — Chrome's
  flag as the oracle for children *through* a surface — is not (§5). The
  references are per host, not cross-host: the same fixture composes
  differently by D2 (through on macOS, over on the web).

## 3. Numbers (2026-08-29, this machine, Retina 2×, release)

- The app's aurora (760×440 px): captured in **0.55–0.59 ms**, uploaded
  (1,306 KiB) in **0.25–0.64 ms** — the probe's numbers, in the tree.
- The fixture's sky canvas while its input is edited: 26 captures over the
  smoke's ~150 ms of editing at the display link (D4 d).
- The web host is unchanged in size; the wrapper is two elements per canvas.
- **The render tail (LLP 1014 §4.1), measured per frame on the display
  link, window visible:** the tick is 8.30 ms (120 Hz) throughout; for the
  first ~1.5 s after the window appears every `gpu_render` blocks a frame in
  `get_current_texture` (p50 8.25 ms, spikes of 49 and 143 ms) while
  CoreAnimation and the pipelines warm up; then **p50 0.5 ms, p95 0.7, max
  < 1 ms**, steady, children texture included — 40 wheel taps do not move
  it. The probe's 15–84 ms frames were that startup window. Occluded, the
  layer hands out no drawable (0.01 ms returns) and once blocked 1,008 ms —
  the case the presenter now skips.
- **The readback, run to run:** 0.00% of pixels beyond the band on both hosts
  (the agent's clock holds the sky at t = 0; the GPU is deterministic on one
  machine).
- **Across hosts, as measured** (the macOS picture box-downsampled 2× to the
  web's): mean difference **1.48/255**; 5.2% of pixels differ by more than 8,
  0.5% by more than 32 — the same WGSL on Metal and on Chrome's WebGPU, plus
  the text region where one host refracts and the other composites over. A
  first number for LLP 1009 §4.1's band; not asserted.

## 4. Checks that hold this

- `cargo test`: `kernel/tests/canvas.rs` (layout), `host/web/tests/canvas.rs`
  (the wrapper's CSS; both surfaces of the fixture publish), the corpus
  (`canvas.contract` compiles byte-identically and runs).
- `cargo test -p caltrain-gpu --test children`: the native readback (skips,
  saying so, without an adapter).
- `node scripts/smoke.mjs <web|macos>` — step 6a: the aurora's title is the
  station's name and its box lies inside the aurora's, on both hosts; step 9:
  the canvas fixture — the button's box inside the canvas's; a `tap` on it
  reaches it (on macOS through the alpha-0 overlay, after the first capture
  is seen in `logs`); `type` reaches the input; on macOS at least three
  captures (the first, the tap's batch, the edit); step 10: the canvas's
  picture against this host's reference.
- The five checks, green on the tree this landed in.

## 5. Not in v1 (and where each is declared)

Text selection inside a canvas (1014 "not decided"); Chrome's flag as the
readback oracle for children through a surface (1014 §2 step 3, second
half); the web host painting children *through* a surface (1014 D2: the
browser's, when a browser ships it); the accessibility role of children
under a canvas (1014 §4); zero-copy capture (1014 D3); a nested surface
that wants every frame (§1a: it would recapture its ancestor every frame);
a hit-test callback into the surface for warps that are not projective
(1014 D5's last step — placements are homographies; the crt's barrel is
tolerated at two pixels); a hermetic test of the glass crossfade (§1c).
Transforms, per-child textures, and nested canvases *are* in v1 (§1a,
§1b), by readback and by placement.
