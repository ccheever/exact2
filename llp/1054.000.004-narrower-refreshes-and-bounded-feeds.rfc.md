# LLP 1054.000.004: Narrower refreshes and bounded feeds

**Type:** RFC
**Status:** Withdrawn (r1 reviewed 2026-09-26 by Claude and Astra, both NOT READY; Grok's run was cancelled twice and is not received; Grok reviews the revision in LLP 1058's panel). D1 (`touching`) is withdrawn in favor of LLP 1058 P5 (refreshes by kind of write). D2's C5 recommendation became LLP 1054.000.006. D2's feed design and D3's measurement moved to LLP 1058 P4 and §5.
**Systems:** Contract language (`touching` on a `send`), plan format (the send's touch), runner (the declared-refresh sets at send and reply; a typed walk over committed values); for adoption only: LLP 1027.004 bounded answers
**Author:** Claude (Opus 5.5) for Charlie Cheever
**Date:** 2026-09-26
**Related:** LLP 1054 D2, D4, D8 (the findings); LLP 1054.000 §Deferred (the transport constraints both reviews set) and C5; LLP 1054.000.000 (declared refreshes; amended here); LLP 1054.000.003 (dates in the roster, which takes the clock out of list arguments); LLP 1027 (TypeScript sources) and 1027.003 (value transfer, measured); LLP 1027.004 (bounded resource answers); LLP 1016 D3–D5

## Why withdrawn

Both reviews found D1's premise broken by the writes it named: "the write
changes only matching records, in place; it adds or removes no rows".

- **A like adds rows.** A like adds the post to the viewer's Likes tab
  (`authorFeeds` with the `likes` filter, Bluesky `data/src/reads.rs:323`)
  and adds the viewer to the post's liked-by list (`getLikes`,
  `reads.rs:694`). Neither contains the post beforehand.
- **A favorite adds rows.** A RealWorld favorite can add an absent article
  to the Favorites feed (`favorited=username`, `apps/realworld/app.ts:88`).
- **A delete's rollback loses its refresh.** The optimistic flag filters the
  post out at the send (Bluesky `writes.rs:159`, `reads.rs:72`). If the
  write fails, the reply-time walk no longer finds the post and can skip
  restoring it (`writes.rs:220`). This one is Astra's.

A walk over committed values sees only where a record already is, never
membership or aggregates, and a wrong assertion fails as silent staleness.
Splitting `mutation change` by kind of write narrows most of the same asks,
using existing syntax, and can't go stale (LLP 1058 P5).

The reviews also corrected the motivation. F5's 3.75 ms figure predates
`f4e7a671` and `51fb550e`. The current cost is 1.40 + 0.72 ms per 1,000
synthetic rows (LLP 1027.003 F5). And `Send`'s decoder holds three operands
(`runner/src/vm.rs:323`), so D1's plan change was larger than costed.

The r1 text follows, unchanged, for the record.

## Summary

The Bluesky data layer is moving from Rust to TypeScript, on LLP 1027's paved
path. Every re-ask of a list resource then crosses the seam as JSON and is
shape-checked on the way in (LLP 1027 D2). Three things decide how often
lists are re-asked and how big they are:

1. **The clock.** Every list takes a minute-granular clock, so every list is
   re-asked once a minute. That is LLP 1054.000.003's (dates in the roster)
   and is not repeated here.
2. **Declared refreshes are per resource, not per record.**
   `mutation change … refreshes homeFeeds, profiles, authorFeeds, threads,
   notes, results, people, convos, chats` re-reads all nine when a like is
   sent and forces all nine when its reply lands (LLP 1054.000.000 D1). Most
   of them do not show the liked post.
3. **Lists grow with scroll.** A feed is one resource whose answer is every
   page loaded so far.

This RFC proposes one change and one adoption:

- **D1 — `touching`.** A send may name the record it edits:
  `send change = act("like", uri, cid, now()) touching Post.uri == uri`. The
  runner then narrows that send's declared refreshes to the resources whose
  committed value contains a `Post` with that `uri`, found by a typed walk it
  already has the tables for. **Nothing crosses the data seam.** That is the
  constraint that sank a source-side `changed()` in LLP 1054.000's reviews,
  and the reason 1054.000.000 chose a declaration.
- **D2 — feeds adopt LLP 1027.004.** `reachstart`/`reachend` and a window in
  the resource's arguments are implemented (slices 1–2). The Bluesky feeds
  become their first virtualized-feed consumer. What that needs from exact2
  is LLP 1054.000 C5 (a bound argument on `reachend`), which this RFC
  recommends deciding with this consumer in view.

It measures nothing new. §D3 says what to measure after both land, and what
would reopen the representation question that LLP 1027.003 closed.

## Motivation

### What a like costs today

The Bluesky port's `like` sends `act("like", uri, cid, now())` to
`mutation change`, which declares nine refreshes (`app.contract:58`).

- **At the send**, the runner re-reads each declared resource whose
  arguments did not change (`runner/src/runner/commit.rs:420-424`,
  `settlement.rs:382-386`). The source answers now from its caches with the
  optimistic overlay, so all nine answers are rebuilt, shape-checked and
  compared.
- **At the reply**, all nine are forced (`commit.rs:737-748`) and answered
  again.

The post usually appears in one or two of them: the feed it was liked in,
and perhaps an open thread. On a phone most of the nine are empty or small,
because their route isn't showing. On a wide window, the home feed, a profile
feed, a thread and notifications are mounted at once.

For a Rust source that is cheap value construction. The runner keeps an
equivalent answer's old object (`settlement.rs:557-563`), and `Conformed`
re-checks only list items that are not the same object (`runner/src/conform.rs`).
For a TypeScript source each answer is also `JSON.stringify` in the engine
and a full decode in Rust (`js/src/prelude.js:220-253`, `js/src/lib.rs:587-611`).
The decode builds new objects, so a later equality check pays too.
LLP 1027.003 F5 measured the synthetic Conversation shape at 3.75 ms to
stringify 1,000 rows with the shipped capture replacer, on a Mac. Bluesky's
`Post` is heavier, with 38 fields and nested pictures, cards and runs.

### Why the existing tools don't narrow it

- `refresh r` in an action and `refreshes` on a mutation name resources, not
  records.
- A source cannot say what changed. That was LLP 1054 D2, and 1054.000.000
  answered it with a declaration precisely so that no forwarder
  (`Storage`, `Mixed`, `Placed`, `Anonymous`, `Swappable`, the TypeScript
  `Module` natively and on the web, `snapback4`), worker envelope or module ABI
  has to carry a signal.
- Splitting `mutation change` per resource doesn't help. The action cannot
  know which resources show the post; only the committed values do.

## Design

### D1 — `touching` narrows a send's declared refreshes

**Syntax.**

```
send change = act("like", uri, cid, now()) touching Post.uri == uri
send change = favorite(target, on) touching Preview.slug == target, Article.slug == target
```

After a send's call there may be `touching` and one or more predicates,
separated by commas. Each predicate is `SHAPE.FIELD == EXPR`. A resource
matches when its value contains a record matching **any** predicate. More
than one is needed when one entity appears under several shapes: RealWorld's
article is a `Preview` in a feed and an `Article` on its page.

**Type check.**

- `SHAPE` is a declared shape. `FIELD` is one of its fields, of type
  `string`, `number` or `bool`: the types `key_text` already accepts for an
  `each` key (`runner/src/instance.rs:1209-1219`). `EXPR` has the field's
  type.
- The send's mutation must declare `refreshes`
  (`type-touching-without-refreshes`).
- Each named resource's shape must be able to contain `SHAPE`. If one can't,
  the compiler warns (`lint-touching-unreachable`, "`people` can never contain
  a `Post`; this send never refreshes it"). That is the author's declaration
  saying something the types say is impossible.

**What it means.** `touching` asserts that the write changes only records
matching a predicate, in place. It does not add or remove rows anywhere.
Under that assertion, a resource that contains no matching record would
answer exactly what it answers now, so asking it is waste. A write that adds
or removes rows is sent without `touching`, and every declared resource is
refreshed as today. Examples of such writes: publishing a post, following
someone (which changes the Following feed), deleting a post from a list that
filters.

*Delete is in place.* A deleted post is in the lists that show it, so the
walk finds them, and those lists are asked again and drop it. A list that
doesn't show it has nothing to drop.

**Which resources are narrowed.** At both points 1054.000.000 D1 defines, a
declared resource *r* is asked when any of these holds:

1. the send has no `touching`;
2. *r* has no committed value, or has a request in flight (a load that
   started before the write may carry pre-write data; the reply-time refresh
   exists for exactly this);
3. *r*'s committed value contains a record matching a predicate.

Otherwise *r* is left out of that send's re-read and of its reply's forced
set. An action's own `refresh r` statements are not narrowed; they are the
author asking by name.

**The key is evaluated at the send and kept.** The predicate's `EXPR` is
evaluated with the send's arguments, in the same VM step (`vm.rs:661-674`).
The resulting `Touch { type, field, key: Value }` list rides on the send and
is copied onto the pending mutation request, so the reply narrows by the same
key. The pending request is already in the commit checkpoint
(`commit.rs:16-45`), so a refused commit restores it with no new state.

**The walk.** `contains(value, type, touch)` walks a committed `Value`
together with its plan type (`plan/tables/format.json:19-51`, the `types` and
`fields` tables), because records are positional
(`plan/src/value.rs:16-31`). It descends records, lists and options, compares
the field at the touch's index when it reaches a record of the touch's type,
and stops at the first match.

At `bind`, the runner computes for each plan type whether it can reach each
touched shape (a fixed point over the types table). The walk skips any
subtree that cannot, so a `list<Profile>` is never entered for a
`Post.uri` touch. The cost is O(nodes that can hold the shape) per declared
resource, in Rust, with no allocation. It runs twice per send, against at
most the declared resources. With bounded answers (D2) it is O(K).

**Where it sits.**

- Send side: where `reread_next` is built from `outcome.sends`
  (`commit.rs:420-424`).
- Reply side: before `force_refresh(r)` for a mutation that parsed `Now`
  (`commit.rs:743-745`).

Both have the mutation, its touches and `self.resources` in hand.
`settle_pass` does not change. `refresh_next` and `reread_next` stay plain
resource lists, so the other feeders of `refresh_next` (`refresh` statements,
`data_ready`, delivery facts, checkpoint boot) are untouched.

**Plan format.** A `touches` table of rows `{type: idx:types, field: u16}`.
The `Send` op (`format.json:797-802`: mutation, source, argument count) gains
two operands, the first `touches` row and a count (zero for a plain send).
The key expressions are pushed after the source arguments, and the VM splits
them off by that count. `FORMAT_DIGEST` changes, as it did for
`mutation_refreshes`.

**What crosses the seam: nothing.** The feature reads the plan and the
runner's own resource values. No `DataSource` method, forwarder, worker
envelope, `rustAbi` or TypeScript binding changes, and a source cannot tell
whether it was narrowed. This is the property LLP 1054.000's reviews asked
for and 1054.000.000 kept.

**Journal.** `narrow send change: touching Post.uri="at://…/3k2" → homeFeeds,
threads (skipped: profiles, authorFeeds, notes, results, people, convos,
chats)`, so `state` shows why a resource was not asked. A narrowed-away
resource that should have changed is then visible as a wrong assertion in
the app, not as a runner mystery.

**Why the runner, not the source.** The source knows which caches hold the
post, but saying so is a signal across the seam (rejected above). The
runner already holds every committed value and its type, and it is where
1054.000.000 put refreshes.

**Why a predicate on a shape, not a declared key.** `shape Post key uri`
would let a send say `touching Post(uri)`. It would also claim that `uri`
identifies a `Post` everywhere, which the runner cannot check and which
`Quote` (a quoted post, with its own `uri`) muddies. A predicate is local to
the send that asserts it. A shape-level key can be added later, as sugar,
if predicates repeat.

### D2 — Feeds adopt LLP 1027.004; C5 decides the rest

LLP 1027.004 makes a list's window part of its resource's arguments. A
virtualized `list` reports `reachstart`/`reachend` when its realized window
reaches the first or last supplied row, and each answer is at most K rows
plus cursors. Slices 1–2 are implemented: the `EventKind` entries
(`format.json:570-571`), runner dispatch (`runner/src/runner/collection.rs:117-173`,
`event.rs:640-647`) and handler gating. The only app using them is
`apps/messages-stress` (`app.contract:192`).

A Bluesky feed then answers a window, not everything loaded:
`feeds(did, feedId, window)`. A like re-reads at most K rows per matched
resource (D1), and scrolling a long session costs the same per re-ask as the
first page. No exact2 change is needed for that part.

What a feed needs that Messages-stress didn't: **which list reached its
edge.** Bluesky keeps one list per pinned feed mounted (`homeFeeds` is a
`list<Feed>`), and a profile has several filtered feeds. `reachend` takes no
arguments (`contract/analyze/src/lib.rs:438-439`), so the handler cannot tell
them apart. The port reads page counts off every list through an extra
`loaded(homeFeeds, authorFeeds)` resource (LLP 1054 D4). LLP 1054.000 C5
asks for bound arguments on `reachend`, `reachstart` and `refresh`, and is
left for Charlie. **This RFC recommends yes to C5's narrow form:** the
arguments are evaluated in the list's enclosing scope when the event is
dispatched (`reachend=more(f.id)` inside `each f in homeFeeds`), with the
same arity checks as `press`. The Bluesky feeds are the second consumer
after Messages' transcript (QUEUE.md:122).

### D3 — What to measure, and what would reopen the representation

This RFC proposes no executor change. LLP 1027.003 §9 compared shared
views, typed buffers and a shared heap against JSON and selected JSON plus
bounded answers. DEFERRED keeps cross-runtime shared data behind that
consumer (`rules/DEFERRED.md:209-219`).

After 1054.000.003, D1 and D2, the Bluesky TypeScript port measures, on a
physical iPhone, with a long session (20 pages scrolled, four resources
mounted on a wide layout):

- a like, from tap to commit: the send's re-read and the reply's refresh;
- a minute tick (it should ask no source);
- one `reachend` window move.

**Trigger:** if any of these exceeds one frame (16.7 ms), reopen executor-side
identity reuse. That would mean keeping the `Value` built for a JS object the
engine hands back unchanged. It needs the object walk LLP 1027.003 §3A
deferred, and it is a DEFERRED change. Until then, nothing.

One observation for the executor's owners, not a proposal: F5 shows the
capture replacer costing 108 ms against 19.0 ms without it at 25,000 rows,
which is most of the stringify cost at every size measured.

## Costs

- **Contract.** One contextual keyword (`touching`, meaningful only after a
  send's call, like `refreshes` after a mutation), one diagnostic and one
  lint. DEFERRED held new syntax until formatting and navigation landed
  (`rules/DEFERRED.md:312-315`, LLP 1035.005 §1). `contract fmt` and
  `contract symbols` exist, and §1's step 4 admits syntax for friction
  "demonstrated in source". The demonstration is Bluesky's nine-way refresh
  and RealWorld's four-way one.
- **Plan.** One table, one `Send` operand; the format digest changes.
- **Runner.** The reachability table at `bind`, the walk, two filter sites,
  the touch on the pending request, and the journal line. There is no new
  checkpoint state.
- **C5** (D2). If accepted, its own grammar and dispatch change, costed in
  its own LLP.

## Tests

- **Runner.**
  - A send with `touching` re-reads only the declared resources whose value
    holds the record, and its reply forces only those. The test counts
    `answer` calls per resource.
  - A resource with no value, or with a request in flight, is asked anyway.
  - A predicate list matches on any predicate.
  - A send without `touching` refreshes everything, as 1054.000.000 does.
  - A refused commit restores the pending request's touches.
  - The walk skips unreachable types. The test counts visited nodes against
    a resource that holds 10,000 `Profile`s and no `Post`.
- **Compiler.**
  - An unknown shape, an unknown field, a non-scalar field, a mismatched key
    type, or `touching` on a mutation without `refreshes` refuses.
  - The unreachable-resource lint fires.
  - `contract fmt` prints the clause.
- **Consumers.**
  - RealWorld's `favorite` and `deleteComment` sends gain `touching`
    (`apps/realworld/app.contract:160, 185`). `follow` does not, because it
    changes which articles "Your Feed" holds. `publish`, `addComment` and
    `deleteArticle` add or remove rows, so they stay unnarrowed.
  - Driven on the web: a favorite on a feed page asks `feed` and not `post`,
    `thread` or `person`.
  - The Bluesky port's like, repost and delete-from-thread sends narrow;
    publish and follow do not.

## Not proposed

- **A source-side signal** (tags on answers, or `changed()`). Rejected in LLP
  1054.000 §Deferred for the transport it needs, and not needed once the
  runner can walk committed values.
- **A normalized entity store in the runner.** It would be a second
  application-state graph (`rules/DEFERRED.md:70-71, 217`) and a second
  data model beside resources. D1 and D2 remove the multiplier and the size
  it would have removed, at a fraction of the surface.
- **Tagged unions for list rows.** Bluesky reuses `Post` with a `role` field
  for its header, tab, loading and end rows. The Markdown, LLP, Exact Live
  and Markdown-stress apps carry a `kind` string on one flat `Block` record
  (`apps/markdown/app.contract:47-56`, `:187-230`). That is evidence for a
  separate LLP, not this one.
