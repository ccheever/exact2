## Summary

Two rows of the roster's notifications and background area (LLP 1133.000
§3.6), both marked `later` there, in one RFC because the second needs the
first: an Android call keeps running in a foreground service of type
`phoneCall`, as a recording that outlives the screen keeps running in one
of type `microphone`.

**Foreground services** are Android's answer to "keep working while the
person is elsewhere, and show that you are": a `Service` promoted with a
notification the person can see, typed by what it does
([foreground services](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs)).
LLP 1133 D8 names it as its example of a platform module. Here it becomes
first a **host seam**: packages claim a service of a type, the host owns
the services, their notifications and the platform's start rules (D1, D2).
No app-facing name is added until an app runs long work of its own (D1).
The first claim is recording's: LLP 1133.009 D8 left Android's background
recording `later`, naming this module as the way to fill it, and the
recorder on a Pixel today keeps "recording" after the person leaves,
writing what Android lets a background app hear, which is silence
(LLP 1133.020 §4, row 2). With this RFC the recorder writes one line,

```
device.microphone-background purpose.recordingInBackground
```

and on Android a take goes on in a microphone service with an ongoing
"Recording" notification, while on iOS the same line derives the `audio`
background mode, so the take goes on there too (D3). Without the line,
leaving the app pauses the take on both phones, as LLP 1133.009 D8 already
says of iOS.

**Calls** (`@exact/calls`) put an app's voice or video call in the
system's own call screen: CallKit on iOS, Jetpack Core-Telecom on Android,
the Media Session API's call actions on the web. The module owns what the
system owns: the call's record, the system's incoming and in-call UI, the
answer, hang-up, hold and mute the person does there, and the audio
session the system activates for a call. It does not own the call's media
or signalling, which are the app's (§11 names the gap: exact2 has no
real-time media transport). Tier 1 is a declared deviation in the
platforms' shared words, with the web's action names for the in-call
controls:

```
use startCall, reportIncomingCall, answerCall, reportCallState, setCallHeld,
  setCallMuted, endCall, calls, callsStatus, callanswer, callhangup, callhold,
  callmute, Call, CallEvent, CallsStatus from "@exact/calls"

action testCall
  send placed = startCall(id=callId, handle="test-call", displayName=testCallName)
view
  column callhangup=hungUp callhold=held …
```

No app in the repo makes calls, so this RFC proposes the smallest real
call the repo can carry without a transport: Harmony's **test call**,
which records a few seconds and plays them back inside a real system call,
as Microsoft Teams' "Make a test call" does
([Microsoft](https://support.microsoft.com/office/make-a-test-call-6c131ac4-8998-4cb1-9076-1801741d0e59))
(§9.2). Whether to build calls now on that consumer, or to keep them
designed until a calling app arrives, is Charlie's ruling (§13 Q4).
Ringing an app that is not running needs push (PushKit's VoIP push, a
high-priority FCM message), which `rules/DEFERRED.md` refuses; D12
designs it and builds none of it.

## 0. What is new here

1. **The foreground service is the host's, and packages claim it.** A
   service must be declared in the manifest with its types, started
   while the app has a visible activity when its type needs a while-in-use
   permission, promoted within the platform's deadline, and given a
   notification. None of that is a package's to get right alone, and two
   packages claiming the same kind must share one service. So the host
   keeps one service class per kind of claim (a capture, a call),
   declared with its types only when the app's lines derive it, and the package context gains `foregroundService`
   (D2), beside LLP 1133.009 D10's audio-session claim.
2. **One line says "keep capturing in the background" on both phones.**
   iOS asks for a background mode, Android for a typed service with its
   permissions and a Play Console declaration. The author's statement is
   the same, so it is one grant row (LLP 1069.008 D4) whose derivation
   differs per target (D3).
3. **A person can now kill a take mid-recording.** Android 13's Task
   Manager stops an app with a foreground service "regardless of that
   app's target SDK version", removes it from memory, and sends "any
   callbacks" none ([user-stopped](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/handle-user-stopping)).
   Android's `MediaMuxer` writes an MP4's index at `stop()`, so an
   unfinished take there is unreadable (LLP 1133.009 D6). A take recorded
   under a background claim must survive the kill: D5 amends recording.
4. **The system's call actions are a person's acts, and only the host
   hears them.** CallKit wants "only one instance of `CXProvider`", made
   early ([CXProvider](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/callkit/cxprovider);
   [PushKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/pushkit/responding-to-voip-notifications-from-pushkit)),
   and its actions (answer from the lock screen, end from the Dynamic
   Island) are the person's input on system UI. So the provider is
   ExactKit's, as the notification delegate is (LLP 1133.004 D7), and
   its actions reach the app as activation events with a person's origin
   (D9).
5. **The system, not the app, activates a call's audio.** CallKit's
   session "is one app are generally unable to activate for themselves",
   and an app that calls `setActive` first gets a plain play-and-record
   session that "prevents the phone session from activating" (Apple DTS,
   [forum 749202](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/749202)). The
   audio-session owner, which today activates at a capture's claim
   (`AudioSession.claimCapture`), gains a call claim that configures and
   waits for the system (D10).
6. **A call may open the microphone in the background.** LLP 1133.009 D8
   refuses a capture start while the app is in the background. A call
   answered on the lock screen is exactly that, and both platforms allow
   it on iOS and Android. Admission gains one fact: the app holds a
   connected call (D10).
8. **The host does its part of a system action before telling the app.**
   CallKit activates a call's audio only after the answer is fulfilled,
   and Telecom tears a call down if a callback takes five seconds. So the
   host never waits for app code: it configures the audio, or stops the
   call's capture for a hold or an end, then fulfils, then raises the
   event (D9).
7. **Ringing a closed app makes a wake present.** iOS terminates an app
   that receives a VoIP push and does not report a call to CallKit
   ([PushKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/pushkit/pkpushregistrydelegate/pushregistry(_:didreceiveincomingpushwith:for:completion:))),
   which LLP 1133.017 D9's "a wake never presents" cannot meet from app
   code that boots after the push. D12 designs the host reporting from
   the payload before any app code runs.

## 2. Foreground services

### D1 — A seam first, and no app-facing name until an app runs work of its own

LLP 1133 D8 makes a capability only one platform has a platform module,
"its whole surface tier 2 under that platform's key". A foreground service
is not a capability an app wants for itself, though: it is how Android
lets some other capability (recording, a call, playback, navigation) keep
running, and Play reviews each one by what it does, not by the service
([Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821)).
So the first users are packages, through the context (D2), and the
author's only word is the grant line that says which capability may run
on (D3).

An app-facing `@exact/foreground-service/android` (start a service of a
type around the app's own work, with its notification) is what an app
needs when its own code is the long work: a `dataSync` export, a
`mediaProcessing` transcode, `specialUse`. No app here does that, and
Android steers each of those to other tools: user-initiated data transfer
jobs for transfers, which LLP 1133.017 already uses
([Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821)).
It stays out until a consumer asks (§11; Q1).

**Ceiling.** The roster's row asks tier 2 to reach "service types". The
seam reaches every type; the app reaches the two this RFC's consumers
need, `microphone` and `phoneCall`, through their lines. Each further type
comes with its capability's module (a `mediaPlayback` service with
Android's sound and media session, `location` with background location),
each adding its line.

### D2 — The host's services, and claims on them

**The services.** The reader library carries one service class per
kind of claim, `dev.exact.canvas.ForegroundService.Capture` and `.Call`
here, each `android:exported="false"`. Each is declared with every type
it will pass to `startForeground`, since a type absent from the manifest
throws `IllegalArgumentException`
([launch](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/launch)):
`Capture` as `microphone`, `Call` as `phoneCall|microphone`. The build
writes a kind's `<service>` element into the app's manifest only when a
granted line derives it (D3, D11), with `FOREGROUND_SERVICE` and each
type's own permission, as the grant table's Android column already writes
`<uses-permission>` lines. One class per kind, so that each claim's
notification is its own service's and ending a capture never demotes a
call.

**A claim.** The package context gains:

```kotlin
context.foregroundService.claim(kind = "capture", types = setOf("microphone"), origin = origin,
    notification = { builder -> … }) : ForegroundClaim?   // null, with the journal's reason, if refused
claim.types(setOf("phoneCall", "microphone"))             // promote again with these types
claim.update { builder -> … }                              // the notification changed
claim.release()
```

- **One service per kind, many claims.** The first claim of a kind
  starts its service (`startForegroundService`), and the service promotes
  itself with `ServiceCompat.startForeground(…, types)` at once in its
  `onCreate`, with the union of its claims' types; later claims of that
  kind join it, and a change of types calls `startForeground` again, which
  Android allows to add types ([launch](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/launch)).
  The service stops (`stopForeground(STOP_FOREGROUND_REMOVE)`, `stopSelf`)
  when its last claim is released. Its notification is the claim's that
  promoted it, until that claim ends; a call that rings while another is
  up posts a notification of its own (D11). It is not sticky: an `onCreate` with no claim (a process
  the system made again) promotes and stops at once, as Android requires
  of a service started as foreground.
- **When a claim may start.** Android 12 refuses a service started in the
  background, with exceptions, by
  `ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException`; Android 14 refuses to
  create a `camera`, `location` or `microphone` service in the background
  "even if the app falls into one of the exemptions", and requires
  `startForegroundService` "while your app has a visible activity", apart
  from a few starts such as one "by interacting with a notification"
  ([background starts](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start)).
  So a claim is made where the capability starts, in the foreground, never
  at the moment the app leaves it. The context refuses a claim that would
  start a service while the reader is not resumed, unless the caller
  passes the platform event that permits it (a notification's action, D11;
  a high-priority push, D12); a claim joining a service already in the
  foreground needs none. It returns `null` for any refusal the platform
  throws (`SecurityException` for a missing type permission or runtime
  grant, `ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException`), naming it in the
  journal.
- **A refused claim degrades, never fails the capability.** The capture
  records on in the foreground and pauses on leaving it, as it would
  without the line (D5).
- **Origin.** A claim carries the origin of the call that made it. A
  claim is only made by a capability that is already admitted, and the
  ones here (a capture start, a call) refuse a tool and a wake (LLP 1133.009
  D9; D16), so no claim is ever a tool's or a wake's.
- **Lifetime.** A claim belongs to its session's package instance; the
  session's end releases it. Android runs one session a process, ended
  with the process, so in practice a claim ends when its capability does
  or the process dies.
- **Under the agent** no service starts and no notification posts: the
  claim is listed in `state.extensions` (`foregroundService: [{type,
  package, since, text}]`) and the journal says `foreground service
  microphone: claimed` and `released`. Production: the real service.

The task the person swipes away from Recents destroys the activity and
leaves the process, which the service keeps; reopening the app makes an
activity that adopts the running host (§1), so the take is still there.

**Process priority.** The service is what keeps the process alive and
the microphone audible while the app is not visible. It does not stop the
person ending it (D4).

### D3 — The line: `device.microphone-background`

`device.microphone-background <purpose key>` says that a microphone
capture may keep running while the app is in the background. It requires
`device.microphone` (`grant-needs`, a new bake refusal naming the line it
lacks), and derives:

| Target | Derived | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| iOS, iPadOS | `audio` in `UIBackgroundModes` ([background modes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/configuring-background-execution-modes)), merged with `host.ios.backgroundModes`, and the bundle's `ExactCapturesInBackground` key | a capture's claim on the audio session is made with `background: true`, so `AudioSession.playsInBackground` keeps the process running (LLP 1133.009 D7) |
| Android | `FOREGROUND_SERVICE`, `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MICROPHONE`, and the `Capture` service's `<service>` (D2) | `@exact/media-capture`'s Android implementation claims a `capture` service of type `microphone` at a microphone lease's start (D5) |
| macOS | nothing: a Mac app keeps its input in the background (LLP 1133.009 D8) | — |
| web | nothing: the browser decides (LLP 1133.009 D8) | — |
| tvOS, visionOS | `audio`, when their recording cell is filled (LLP 1133.009 D12: later) | — |

**The key, not the mode, decides.** Today media-capture's iOS
implementation keeps a capture running whenever `audio` is in
`UIBackgroundModes` (`Permission.recordsInBackground`). Another line
derives `audio` too (`device.calls`, D16), and an app's playback may write
it by hand, so the implementation reads `ExactCapturesInBackground`
instead: an app with `audio` and without this line pauses its captures on
leaving, as on Android.

**The purpose key** is the notification's text on Android (D4), and the
name of the notification channel the service posts in; no platform asks
the person about either line, so it is the one place the text appears. iOS
uses no text for it.

**Review.** The line is what the stores review:

- Apple: "Multitasking apps may only use background services for their
  intended purposes: VoIP, audio playback, location, task completion, local
  notifications, etc." ([App Review Guidelines 2.5.4](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).
  A recorder recording is the intended purpose.
- Google Play: an app targeting Android 14 that declares a foreground
  service type completes a declaration in Play Console (App content) for
  each type: a description of the feature, "the user impact if the system
  defers the task or interrupts it", a video link "showing the steps a user
  takes in the app to trigger the feature", and a use case from a list;
  for `microphone` the video must show the "user actively recording"
  ([Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821)).
  The build cannot file that, so `deploy`'s table names each foreground
  service type and each special permission an Android release declares,
  with the declaration Play expects for it (Q8).

**Why a row and not `host.ios.backgroundModes`.** The free list stays for
what has no row yet (playback's `audio`). A row is one line for a
capability on every target, checked at bake, with its Android column; the
free list is iOS's spelling and silently does nothing on Android, which
is the gap the recorder fell into.

### D4 — The notification: unsaid, and what Android does to it

What a hand-built Android recorder shows while recording is an ongoing
notification with the elapsed time, and a tap that returns to the app. The
claim's notification, when the author says nothing:

| Part | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| channel | one per kind, created at the first claim, its name the line's purpose text; for a capture `IMPORTANCE_LOW` (no sound, no peek), for a call `IMPORTANCE_HIGH` (D11), since a channel's importance cannot be raised once made | "The status bar notification must use a priority of `PRIORITY_LOW` or higher" ([launch](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/launch)) |
| small icon | the app's launcher icon's monochrome layer, else the app icon | — |
| title | the purpose text | — |
| content | the elapsed time, by `setUsesChronometer(true)` from the take's start (`setWhen`); "Paused" while every recorder of the lease is paused, the chronometer stopped | — |
| tap | opens the app's activity (`setContentIntent`), as a launch does | — |
| category | `CATEGORY_SERVICE`; `setOngoing(true)` | — |
| actions | none | Q1, §11 |

What the platform does with it, which the app cannot change:

- **Notifications denied.** On Android 13, "if the user denies the
  notification permission, they still see notices related to foreground
  services in the Task Manager but don't see them in the notification
  drawer" ([notification permission](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/notification-permission)).
  The service runs either way; the claim never asks for
  `POST_NOTIFICATIONS`.
- **Dismissed.** On Android 14 the person can swipe an ongoing foreground
  notification away, except while the phone is locked
  ([Android 14](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/behavior-changes-all)).
  The service keeps running and the take goes on; the Task Manager still
  lists it.
- **Stopped.** The Task Manager's **Stop** ends the whole app: "The
  system removes your app from memory", and "doesn't send your app any
  callbacks" ([user-stopped](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/handle-user-stopping)).
  D5 says what this does to a take.

**Tier 3.** The claimant's notification closure is a hatch moment: an
implementation that declares a hatch hands the app's Kotlin the
`Notification.Builder` after the module has set its parts and before the
service posts it, as LLP 1133.004 D4 hands the post's builder. Recording
declares it (`recording.foregroundNotification`), so an app can add
actions or change the text now; the module owns the channel, the small
icon and the content intent.

### D5 — What a capture does with it: recording's Android background

LLP 1133.009 D8's Android cell becomes:

| Target | Recording continues | Otherwise |
|---|---|---|
| Android | with `device.microphone-background`: a `capture` claim of type `microphone` made at the lease's start, released at its end; the take records on while the app is hidden and its notification shows; during the app's call, the call's service carries `microphone` instead (D10) | tracks mute, recorders pause with `reason` `background`, the lease is kept; on return the tracks unmute, as on iOS |

- **The claim follows the microphone lease** (LLP 1133.009 D6), which
  `@exact/media-capture`'s Android implementation holds: it is made when
  the first lease starts (in the foreground, since a capture start is
  `"foreground": true`) and released at the last lease's end. A paused
  recorder keeps its lease, so the service runs through a pause, its
  notification reading "Paused". A level reader alone also holds a lease;
  with the line, its service runs too, as an iOS app metering in the
  background keeps its session.
- **Without the line, Android pauses.** Today leaving the app neither
  pauses nor records sound: since Android 9 an app in the background
  "cannot access the microphone or camera", and the platform's answer is
  "use a foreground service"
  ([Android 9](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/pie/android-9.0-changes-all));
  LLP 1133.020 §4 row 2 records that the take does not pause.
  The implementation reads the context's foreground (LLP 1133.009 D10 item
  3) and pauses at `foreground(false)`, so a take never holds a silent
  stretch the person did not hear.
- **A take survives the process.** Under a claim, Android's implementation
  writes `audio/mp4` as a stream that is readable at every frame: the
  encoder's raw AAC access units, each given the seven-byte ADTS header
  the writer makes from the encoder's `csd-0`, appended to `<path>.part`
  and flushed. At `stop` it strips the headers and writes the frames
  through `MediaMuxer` into `<path>.mp4.tmp`, their times rebuilt from
  1,024 samples a frame; once the muxer's `stop` succeeds it renames that
  to `<path>`, which is atomic, and then deletes the part. So `<path>`
  exists only complete, and a `.part` is always unfinished work: at its
  first call in a later process, the implementation makes the MP4 from
  each `.part` it finds the same way, replacing any `.tmp`, and journals
  `recovered <path>`. So a take the Task Manager
  stopped, or the system killed, is a playable file up to its last frame,
  as Apple's fragmented MP4 is (LLP 1133.009 D6). Without a claim, the
  writer is as built: the process does not outlive the screen there.
  This amends LLP 1133.009 D6 (Q3).
- **The app learns of a recovery** by listing its takes, as the recorder
  does (`takes()` in `app.ts`); a take recovered after the list was read
  appears at the next refresh. A `recovered` field on `mediaRecorderState`
  waits for a consumer that needs it at once.

### D6 — Origins and the foreground fact

Nothing here changes who may start a capture: the line only lets a
capture already admitted keep running. A tool or a wake still cannot start
one (LLP 1133.009 D9), and a capture start while the app is hidden still
fails `NotAllowedError`, except during a call (D10). `resumeMediaRecorder`
stays `"foreground": true`: a resume from the background, such as a
notification's Resume action, waits for that action's consumer.

## 3. Calls

### D7 — Tier 1: the platforms' shared words, a declared deviation

The web has no API for a call the system shows: no incoming-call screen,
no record of calls, no way to keep a page's call going after it closes.
It has the in-call controls: the Media Session API's `hangup`,
`togglemicrophone` and `togglecamera` actions and `setMicrophoneActive`
([Media Session](https://w3c.github.io/mediasession/)), which Chromium
shows in its Picture-in-Picture window
([web.dev](https://web.dev/media-session)). CallKit and Core-Telecom share
the rest: report an incoming call, start an outgoing one, say when it
connects, end it with a reason, hold it, mute it, and hear the person do
each of those on the system's screen. So tier 1 is a declared deviation
in those shared words, and the web implementation maps the Media Session
actions onto the same events (D19).

| Name | Kind | Meaning | CallKit | Core-Telecom |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `startCall(id, handle, handleType, displayName, video)` | send; `"origin": "app"`, `"foreground": true` | an outgoing call the app places | `CXStartCallAction` through `CXCallController` | `CallsManager.addCall` with `DIRECTION_OUTGOING` |
| `reportIncomingCall(id, handle, handleType, displayName, video)` | send; `"origin": "app"`; `"foreground": true` unless the app holds a call (D16) | the app learned of a call to the person (its own connection to its server) | `reportNewIncomingCall(with:update:completion:)` | `addCall` with `DIRECTION_INCOMING`, a `CallStyle.forIncomingCall` notification |
| `answerCall(id)` | send; `"origin": "app"` | the app's own Answer button | `CXAnswerCallAction` | `CallControlScope.answer(callType)` |
| `reportCallState(id, state)` | send; answers nothing | an outgoing call is `connecting`, then `connected` | `reportOutgoingCall(with:startedConnectingAt:)`, `…connectedAt:` | `setActive()` at `connected` |
| `setCallHeld(id, held)` | send | the app's own hold | `CXSetHeldCallAction` | `setInactive()`, `setActive()` |
| `setCallMuted(id, muted)` | send | the app's own mute | `CXSetMutedCallAction` | the app's capture alone; Telecom's `isMuted` is the system's, read (D8) |
| `endCall(id, reason)` | send; answers nothing | the call is over: `local` (the person here hung up, the default), or the other end's outcome | `local`: `CXEndCallAction`; else `reportCall(with:endedAt:reason:)` | `disconnect(DisconnectCause(LOCAL \| REMOTE \| REJECTED \| MISSED \| ERROR))` |
| `calls()` | live source; a fact | the app's calls, `Call[]` | the provider's calls | the `CallControlScope`s held |
| `callsStatus()` | live source; a fact; `sync` | `available`, `systemUI`, platform words | — | `canUseFullScreenIntent()` (D11) |
| `callanswer`, `callhangup`, `callhold`, `callmute` | events on the root, `"raised": "activation"` | the person did it on the system's screen | `provider(_:perform:)` for each action the app did not request | `onAnswer`, `onDisconnect`, `onSetInactive`/`onSetActive`, `isMuted` |

The events' names follow exact2's event style (`notificationclick`,
`backgroundfetchsuccess`) because Media Session's are actions, not events,
and cover only two of the four; on the web `hangup` raises `callhangup`
and `togglemicrophone` raises `callmute` with the toggled state (D19).

**`id` is the app's.** A calling service already has an id for each call
(its server's), and an incoming push carries it (D12). The module maps it
to the platform's own (`UUID` for CallKit, Telecom's `ParcelUuid`); a
second call with a live id is refused `InvalidStateError`. Since the app
names it, a call is not a handle made by the module, and LLP 1133.009
D10 item 10's provisional rule does not apply.

**`handle`** is a string the app chooses, and `handleType` says what it
is: `phoneNumber`, `emailAddress` or `generic` (an account id; the
default). CallKit takes the three as `CXHandle.HandleType`
([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/callkit/cxhandle/handletype))
and matches a number or address against Contacts for the incoming screen
([making and receiving calls](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/callkit/making-and-receiving-voip-calls)).
Telecom's call address is a `Uri` with a scheme
([`CallAttributesCompat`](https://developer.android.com/reference/kotlin/androidx/core/telecom/CallAttributesCompat)),
so the module writes `tel:`, `mailto:`, or for `generic` a scheme of its
own (`exact-call:` with the string percent-encoded), and reads it back.

### D8 — Values

```
shape Call
  id: string
  handle: string
  displayName: string
  direction: enum<incoming|outgoing>
  video: bool
  state: enum<ringing|dialing|connecting|connected|held|ended>
  muted: bool
  audio: bool                        // the call's audio is up (D10): CallKit's didActivate, else at connected
  endedReason: option<enum<local|remote|unanswered|declined|failed|answeredElsewhere|declinedElsewhere>>
  startedAt: number                  // ms since the epoch
  connectedAt: option<number>
shape CallEvent
  muted: option<bool>
  held: option<bool>
shape CallsStatus
  available: bool
  systemUI: bool                     // the system shows the call (CallKit, Telecom), not only the app
  apple: option<AppleCallsStatus>
  android: option<AndroidCallsStatus>
```

| `state` | CallKit | Core-Telecom |
|---|---|---|
| `ringing` | reported incoming, not answered | added incoming, before `onAnswer` |
| `dialing` | `CXStartCallAction` fulfilled | added outgoing ("only `DIALING` or `RINGING`", [Core-Telecom](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/telecom/voip-app/telecom)) |
| `connecting` | `reportOutgoingCall(startedConnectingAt:)` | — (Telecom has no step here; the module keeps it) |
| `connected` | answered, or `reportOutgoingCall(connectedAt:)` | `answer` or `setActive` succeeded |
| `held` | `CXSetHeldCallAction` with `isOnHold` | `setInactive` succeeded or `onSetInactive` |
| `ended` | ended by any path; kept in `calls()` until the commit after its `endedReason` is delivered, then dropped | `disconnect`, or `onDisconnect` |

`endedReason` maps CallKit's `CXCallEndedReason` (`failed`,
`remoteEnded`, `unanswered`, `answeredElsewhere`, `declinedElsewhere`,
[Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/callkit/cxcallendedreason))
and Telecom's `DisconnectCause` (`LOCAL`, `REMOTE`, `REJECTED`, `MISSED`,
`ERROR`, [CallControlScope](https://developer.android.com/reference/kotlin/androidx/core/telecom/CallControlScope))
onto one enum; a reason a platform lacks is sent as its nearest
(`answeredElsewhere` as `REMOTE` on Android).

**Mute** is the system's on iOS (`CXSetMutedCallAction` changes the
system's in-call button, and the person's tap there raises `callmute`).
Core-Telecom's `isMuted` is the system's microphone mute, which the app
reads and does not set
([CallControlScope](https://developer.android.com/reference/kotlin/androidx/core/telecom/CallControlScope)).
So `muted` is the effective mute: the app's own (`setCallMuted`, the
call's mute gate, D9) or the system's, either one; `setCallMuted(muted=false)` clears the app's, and
`android.systemMuted` (D14) says when the system's remains. Where the
pinned Core-Telecom carries the local call silence extension, which lets
an app "silence a call's outgoing audio without physically muting the
device's microphone" (the same guide), the app's mute is reported through
it, so a car or a watch shows it; stage 4 checks that against 1.0.1.

On every target a muted call silences the app's capture: its gate holds
every capture track (D9), and a gated or disabled audio track delivers
silence to its consumers, as Media Capture and Streams requires ("a
muted or disabled `MediaStreamTrack` renders … silence",
[W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/mediacapture-streams/#dom-mediastreamtrack-enabled)).
The native implementations do not do that today: Apple's and Android's
`setTrackEnabled` sets the flag, and the recorders write the samples
regardless (`packages/media-capture/apple/MediaCapture.swift`,
`packages/recording/apple/Recording.swift`, and their Android peers).
Media-capture's fix, the shared input delivering zeros to a disabled or
gated track's consumers, comes before mute (stage 0), with a drive that reads
the recorded level during a mute (§9.3, drive 6).

### D9 — The host keeps the provider; the system's actions are activations

**Apple.** ExactKit makes one `CXProvider` and one `CXCallController` at
launch, in `application(_:didFinishLaunchingWithOptions:)`, when the app
grants `device.calls` (D16), with a configuration from the bundle: the
localized name is the app's display name, `supportsVideo` true only when
some `startCall` or `reportIncomingCall` in the bundle passes `video`
other than the literal `false`, `includesCallsInRecents` the platform's
default, true ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/callkit/cxproviderconfiguration/includescallsinrecents)).
Its delegate is ExactKit's. Why the host's: one provider per app is
Apple's rule, PushKit needs it at launch before any package loads (D12),
and its actions must reach a session that may not have booted, as a
notification's click must (LLP 1133.004 D7). `@exact/calls`' Apple
implementation is the thin layer that admits a call and hands it to
`context.calls`, as `@exact/background-transfer` reaches the host's
carrier (LLP 1133.017 D8).

**Android.** The reader keeps one `CallsManager`, registered with
`registerAppWithTelecom(CAPABILITY_BASELINE or …VIDEO_CALLING)` at the
first call, and runs each call's `addCall` block inside the `Call`
service (D11), as Core-Telecom's guide does ("Host `CallsManager` in a
foreground `Service`", [Core-Telecom](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/telecom/voip-app/telecom)).

**Actions the app requested** (`startCall`, `answerCall`, `setCallHeld`,
`setCallMuted`, `endCall` with `local`) answer their send from the
platform's own result, and raise no event. On Apple the action comes back
through the provider's delegate, which the host matches to its request
and fulfils. On Android each `CallControlScope` operation returns a
`CallControlResult` (`Success` or `Error`), and that is the answer; the
remote callbacks are not involved. **Actions the
app did not request** are the person's on the system's screen: answering
on the lock screen, ending from the Dynamic Island or a headset, holding
to take a cellular call, muting on the in-call screen, or on a watch or in
a car. CallKit says of an answer that it is "allowed by the system and
approved by the user" ([`CXAnswerCallAction`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/callkit/cxanswercallaction)).
The host raises each as an activation (LLP 1133.004 D6) on the session
that made the call, by its label, held if that session's root handler is
not mounted, with a person's input as its origin. So the app's
`callanswer` handler may start a capture (D10), and its commits are the
person's, never a wake's, even in a session with no view.

**Fulfilling: the host enforces, then tells.** A system action never
waits for app code, since the platform's next step does: CallKit
activates a call's audio only after the answer is fulfilled
([WWDC16 session 230](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2016/230/)),
and Telecom's callbacks "must finish within 5 seconds", a timeout counting
"as a transaction failure" that "may tear down the call"
([Core-Telecom](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/telecom/voip-app/telecom)).
So wherever the host hears a person's action, in the delegate's
`perform` (Apple), in a suspend callback, or in the host's own
notification intents (Android, D11), it does the part of the action that
is exact2's, synchronously, then fulfils or returns, then raises the
event. On Android that part includes the Telecom operation itself for the
notification's buttons (`answer(callType)`, `disconnect(…)`), since there
the person's tap reaches the host's intent and not Telecom:

| Action | The host, before fulfilling | The app, after the event |
|---|---|---|
| answer | configures the call's audio (D10); state `connected` | starts its media; a capture waits for `audio` |
| end | stops the call's own capture tracks and pauses its players; state `ended`; the audio claim ends as D10 says, the `call` claim after `addCall`'s block returns on Android | closes its connection |
| hold | the hold gate on every capture track, every player in the call's session paused; state `held` | pauses its connection's media |
| unhold | the gate lifted; state `connected`; players stay paused | resumes its players |
| mute, unmute | the mute gate (D8) | updates its UI |

**The call's media.** At most one call is active (connected, not held).
Neither CallKit nor Telecom holds an app's first call when the app's
second is answered, so the host does: before it fulfils or performs the
second call's answer it holds the first (`CXSetHeldCallAction` in the
same transaction; `setInactive()` on the first call's scope), and the
provider allows one call a group (`maximumCallsPerCallGroup` 1; merging
is §11). A capture track started while a call is connected is that
call's own, and ends with it; one started before any call goes on after
it. A track's gates (hold, mute), apart from its own `enabled`, are its
owning call's; a track no call owns follows the active call's, or is held
while every call is held. A track delivers sound only when it is enabled
and no gate holds it, including one opened under a gate. So holding call
A and answering B keeps A's tracks silent and lets B's sound through.
Players are the active call's, since they play in its session (D10). So a capture or a stream the app kept running after a hold
or an end is silent on its side of the device, whatever its handler does
or fails to do, and Core-Telecom's rule that a hold stops the
microphone and playback before the callback returns is the host's to
keep.

**An app that sends `startCall` or `reportIncomingCall` with no
`callhangup` handler on its root** is refused at bake
(`calls-hangup-unheard`, stage 0), so a hang-up cannot go unheard: a call
the person ended on the system's screen while the app kept sending is the
worst outcome here.

### D10 — The audio a call activates, and the microphone during a call

**The call claim.** The audio-session owner (LLP 1133.009 D7) gains a
claim kind beside captures, speech and players, made by the host's call
provider when a call starts or is answered:

| Claims | Category | Mode | Options | Activated by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a call | `.playAndRecord` | `.voiceChat` (`.videoChat` for a video call) | `.allowBluetoothHFP`, implied by the mode ([`voiceChat`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/mode-swift.struct/voicechat)) | the system: `provider(_:didActivate:)` |
| a call and captures | the call's | the call's | the call's | the system |
| a call and players or speech | the call's | the call's | the call's | the system |

- The owner sets the category and mode in the provider's
  `perform` of `CXStartCallAction` or `CXAnswerCallAction`, before
  fulfilling it, as Apple's guide says ("configure an `AVAudioSession`
  and call the `fulfill()` method", [making and receiving
  calls](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/callkit/making-and-receiving-voip-calls)),
  and never calls `setActive` while a call claim is held: the phone
  session has "a higher interruption priority", and an app that activates
  first blocks it ([forum 749202](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/749202)).
- **No other path activates or restores while a call claim is held.**
  Today a player's hold reconciles with `setActive(true)` and a capture's
  release restores the app's category (`AudioSession.reconcile`,
  `restoreAfterCaptures`); under a call claim each keeps the call's
  category, mode and options and leaves activation to the system, so the
  test call's playback (§9.2) plays in the call's session. The app's own
  category comes back when the call claim ends. The built interruption
  handler, which activates again when an interruption ends with
  `shouldResume`, does neither while a call claim is held: under CallKit
  an interrupted call is held, and resuming it is an unhold, which CallKit
  activates (`didActivate`). Outside a call the handler is as built, the
  one activation Apple DTS leaves to an app (the same thread).
- `didActivate` sets the call's `audio` true and tells every claim;
  `didDeactivate` sets it false. Where no system activates a call's audio,
  `audio` is true from `connected`: on Android when `answer`, `setActive`
  or `onAnswer` succeeds (Telecom routes the call's audio then), on macOS
  and the web at `connected`.
- **A capture claim made during a call** joins without activating: if
  the call's audio is active it proceeds at once; if not, it waits for
  `didActivate` (up to the call's answer or start deadline), where today
  `claimCapture` activates and, failing, returns 0. Its mode and options
  are the call's (the first lease's rule, LLP 1133.009 D7), and a
  development build names a capture's own `apple.mode` that differs
  (`recording-session-conflict`).
- `.voiceChat` without voice processing on the I/O unit "doesn't apply
  voice-specific processing, like echo cancellation" (the same page), so a
  capture made under a call claim enables `setVoiceProcessingEnabled(true)`
  on its engine's input node unless the capture asked `echoCancellation=false`.
- **The claim lasts while any call does,** held ones included; CallKit's
  activation is separate from it. A `didDeactivate` while a call is held
  sets `audio` false and stops the session's I/O, and leaves the category
  alone; the unhold's `didActivate` sets it true again. The claim ends
  when the last call has ended and the session is inactive: at once if
  `audio` was already false, else at the `didDeactivate` that follows the
  end, since restoring earlier would take the session back from the phone
  session. The owner then restores what the app's sound wants, as after a
  capture.
- **Android.** Core-Telecom routes the call's audio; the guide says
  "Don't use `AudioManager#setCommunicationDevice` or
  `AudioManager#startBluetoothSco`" and to play on
  `STREAM_VOICE_CALL` (same page). A capture made during a call uses the
  `VOICE_COMMUNICATION` source and the app's playback the voice usage;
  each is tier 2 on recording today, chosen here by the call claim.

**The microphone during a call.** A capture start is `"foreground":
true` (LLP 1133.009 D8). On iOS and Android it is also admitted while the
app is hidden when the app holds a call that is `connected` (answered or
connected, its audio up or coming), for a person's or the app's own
origin, never a tool's or a wake's; the capture then waits for `audio`
as above. The context's foreground fact (LLP 1133.009 D10 item 3) gains
its companion, `inCall`, which the host sets from the call's state and
admission reads beside `page.hidden`. Both platforms allow it: CallKit
activates the session for a call answered on the lock screen, and on
Android the call's service carries `microphone`, promoted where Android
permits it (D11). The web is not included: a hidden page's `getUserMedia`
is the browser's to refuse, and nothing there makes a call's audio. On
macOS a capture keeps running hidden anyway (LLP 1133.009 D8), so the
foreground rule is unchanged there.

**A capture already running** when the app is hidden during a call keeps
running without `device.microphone-background`: iOS keeps the process for
the call's active session, and on Android the call's service carries
`microphone`. The pause of D5 and LLP 1133.009 D8 skips a lease while
`inCall`.

**The camera is not included.** A video call's camera start stays
`"foreground": true` and its capture stops in the background, as LLP
1133.008 has it: iOS lets a video call keep the camera only through
multitasking camera access with Picture in Picture
([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avkit/adopting-picture-in-picture-for-video-calls)),
and Android only in a service of type `camera`
([types](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types)).
Both wait for a video-calling consumer (§11).

**Background on iOS.** A call keeps the process running in the
background through its active session, which needs `audio` in
`UIBackgroundModes`; `device.calls` derives it (D16), and it does not
make other captures record on hidden (D3).

### D11 — Android: the call's service, its notification, ringing

- **The service.** Each call holds a `call` claim (D2) from before
  `addCall` to its end: the host starts and promotes the `Call` service,
  then adds the call inside it. It is promoted with
  `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_TYPE_PHONE_CALL or FOREGROUND_SERVICE_TYPE_MICROPHONE`,
  since Android 11 requires a service that uses the microphone to declare
  `microphone` ([changes](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/changes)),
  and Android 14 lets a `microphone` type be created only from a visible
  activity or a notification's action, which a call's start always is
  (`startCall`, `reportIncomingCall` in front, an answer from the
  notification). Without the runtime microphone grant it is promoted as
  `phoneCall` alone, and the host adds `microphone` (`claim.types`) at
  each resume of the reader while a `call` claim lacks it and
  `checkSelfPermission(RECORD_AUDIO)` holds, so the resume that follows a
  call's first `getUserMedia` prompt adds it (Android answers the prompt
  before `onResume`), and at an answer from the notification. A service a push
  started (D12) is `phoneCall` alone until that answer.
  `phoneCall`'s prerequisite is the app "has declared the
  `MANAGE_OWN_CALLS` permission" ([types](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types)).
  Core-Telecom's own manifest declares `MANAGE_OWN_CALLS`, its
  `ConnectionService` (used below API 34) and `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT`, a
  runtime permission on Android 12 and later (the library's merged
  manifest, `core-telecom-1.0.1.aar`). Whether Bluetooth endpoints are
  named without it is measured in stage 4 before the recipe says whether
  to ask.
- **The notification.** A `CallStyle` notification "within 5 seconds of
  adding the call" (Core-Telecom's guide): `forIncomingCall(person,
  decline, answer)` while ringing, `forOngoingCall(person, hangUp)` after
  ([`CallStyle`](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/app/Notification.CallStyle)).
  It is the `Call` service's foreground notification, posted in an
  `IMPORTANCE_HIGH` channel named by `device.calls`' purpose, so that it
  rings as a heads-up, `setOngoing(true)`, which on
  Android 14 makes a call notification non-dismissible
  ([call style](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/call-style)).
  A self-managed calling app needs no `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` for it
  ([notification permission](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/notification-permission)).
  Its Answer, Decline and Hang up are the host's `PendingIntent`s, which
  run D9's table, Telecom's `answer` or `disconnect` included, and then
  raise `callanswer` and `callhangup` as activations (LLP 1133.004 D6's
  Android form): Answer an activity intent to the reader's activity,
  Decline and Hang up service intents to the `Call` service, since
  Android 12 blocks a notification that starts an activity through a
  service or receiver ([notification trampolines](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/12/behavior-changes-12)).
- **Ringing on the lock screen** is a full-screen intent. On Android 14 an
  app may use `USE_FULL_SCREEN_INTENT` only if it "provide[s] calling and
  alarms", the person can turn it off, and Play revokes it otherwise; the
  app checks `NotificationManager.canUseFullScreenIntent()`
  ([Android 14](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/behavior-changes-14)).
  `device.calls` declares it; `callsStatus().android.fullScreenIntent`
  reads it; without it the incoming call is a heads-up notification. A
  call that rings while another is up posts its own notification, with its
  own id and the full-screen intent, since the system launches the intent
  when a notification newly alerts, not when one is updated; the `Call`
  service's foreground notification stays the ongoing call's.
  Telecom draws no incoming screen for a self-managed app: a full-screen
  intent displays "an activity when the device is locked", and a heads-up
  while the person uses the device
  ([time-sensitive notifications](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/time-sensitive)).
  So the full-screen intent opens the reader's activity, which shows over
  the lock screen and turns the screen on (`setShowWhenLocked`,
  `setTurnScreenOn`) while a call rings; what it shows is the app's own
  view of the ringing call from `calls()`, as the recipe and the fixture
  draw it. The system draws only the notification.
- **Ringing while hidden.** With no call held the app cannot start the
  `Call` service from the background: Android 12's exemptions name a
  visible activity, a notification's action and a high-priority FCM
  message, and none covers an app's own socket
  ([background starts](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start)).
  So `reportIncomingCall` while hidden is admitted only during a call the
  app holds (its service is already in the foreground; D16), and an
  incoming call to a hidden or stopped app is push's (D12). iOS is the
  same in practice: a suspended app's socket hears nothing, and VoIP push
  is the way in.
- **Answering from the notification** while the app is hidden is the
  start Android permits from "interacting with a notification"; stage 4
  measures it from a locked phone, ringing as a second call during one.

### D12 — Ringing an app that is not running: designed, not built

An incoming call while the app is not running arrives by push. This RFC
designs the path so that `@exact/calls` and push agree; it is built with
push, which `rules/DEFERRED.md` refuses (Q7).

**iOS.** The host makes a `PKPushRegistry` "every time your app
launches, whether in the foreground or in the background"
([`PKPushRegistry`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/pushkit/pkpushregistry)),
desired type `.voIP`, under a line `device.calls-push` (which derives
`voip` in `UIBackgroundModes` and `aps-environment`). The token reaches
the app as LLP 1133.004 §9 designs: a live source the app gives its own
server. When a VoIP push arrives the host, before any app code:

1. reads the payload's `exact-call` object (`id`, `handle`,
   `displayName`, `video`), whose shape the interface declares, and calls
   `reportNewIncomingCall` with it, inside the delegate; a payload without
   it is reported and ended at once as `failed`, since "if you fail to
   report a call to CallKit, the system will terminate your app.
   Repeatedly failing to report calls may cause the system to stop
   delivering any more VoIP push notifications"
   ([PushKit](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/pushkit/pkpushregistrydelegate/pushregistry(_:didreceiveincomingpushwith:for:completion:)));
2. calls the push's completion handler once CallKit has answered;
3. starts a wake (LLP 1133.017 D5) of kind `call`: boots the session
   headless if none runs and raises `callincoming(id, call)`, a wake event
   with origin `wake`, whose handler connects to the app's server;
4. raises the person's `callanswer` or `callhangup`, when they come, as
   activations with a person's origin (D9), held until the root's handler
   is mounted.

This amends LLP 1133.017 D9: **a wake never presents, except the incoming
call a VoIP push requires, which the host reports from the payload before
the wake runs.** The app's code still presents nothing, and the wake's
own commits stay `wake`-origin; the answer is the person's.

It also amends LLP 1133.017 D6, since a wake's session ends when its
budget does and may open no stream until a view attaches, and a call
answered on the lock screen may never attach one: **a session holding a
call the host reported lives until the call ends, with or without a view,
outside the wake's budget, and may open the streams its call needs.** The
call's claims (D2, D10) are what end with it. Its handlers still draw
nothing until a view attaches.

**Android.** A high-priority FCM data message, which may start a
foreground service from the background
([background starts](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start))
and is "deprioritized to normal priority" if it does not lead to a
user-visible notification
([FCM](https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/android/message-priority)).
The push module's host-kept messaging service reads the same `exact-call`
object, starts its `Call` service as `phoneCall` alone (a push exempts
the start, not a `microphone` type, D11) and adds the call, with its
`CallStyle` notification within Core-Telecom's five seconds, then boots
the session headless (LLP 1133.017 D7's Android row, not built) and
raises `callincoming`.

### D13 — Unsaid defaults

## 6. Packaging and callers

### D17 — Packages, and what the seam gains

```
packages/calls/
  package.json      "@exact/calls"; exports ".", "./apple", "./android", "./web"
  exact.json        the interface (D7, D8), the events, the declared list (D21)
  test/             the conformance fixture: a call app with no transport

  android/          exact.json, Calls.kt; maven: androidx.core:core-telecom:1.0.1
  web/              exact.json, index.js (Media Session's call actions)
```

No package is added for foreground services: the seam is the host's (D2),
and `@exact/media-capture`'s Android implementation and `@exact/calls`'
Android implementation are its first claimants.

**The seam additions,** each general and each proved by the out-of-tree
fixture before a package depends on it (LLP 1133.001 D8):

1. **`context.foregroundService`** on Android (D2): claims by kind and
   types, the notification closure, a refusal as `null`; the reader
   library's service classes and their manifest derivation per granted
   line.
2. **`context.calls`** on Apple and Android (D9): the host's provider,
   start, report, answer, state, hold, mute, end, and each call's
   requested actions matched to their sends; the system's other actions
   raised as activations. A third party's call module reaches the same
   provider the same way.
3. **The call claim** on the audio-session owner (D10): configure without
   activating; captures, players and speech joining a call's session with
   no activation or restore of their own; `didActivate` and
   `didDeactivate` told to every claim.
4. **A disabled audio track delivers silence** to its consumers in
   media-capture's Apple and Android implementations (D8), a fix to what
   is built; and **a gate** a host-side owner (the call, D9) sets on every
   audio track, apart from each track's `enabled`, with each track's
   owning call, so a call's end stops only its own.
5. **`inCall`** beside the foreground fact, read by admission for names
   declared `"foreground": true`, and by media-capture's background pause
   (D10).
6. **Grant rows that require another** (`grant-needs`) and rows that
   derive Android `<service>` elements and normal permissions, beside the
   runtime permissions the Android column writes today (D3, D16).
7. **`tap @ref <choice>`** on a declared list entry: the list's `"tap"`
   becomes a map from a choice to the event it raises and the fields it
   sets (D21), where LLP 1133.004 §7 has one event per list.

### D18 — Callers

Contract reads `calls()` and `callsStatus()` and handles the four events
on its root; it sends the rest. Data code calls every send through its
generated binding, in Promise form: an app's connection to its server
lives in data code, and `reportIncomingCall` is called from there when a
call arrives on it. The events reach data code only through a Contract
action (LLP 1133.017 D11). `callsStatus` is `sync` on Apple through
`call`. No name is a permission request.

## 8. The substitute

### D21 — Under the agent

**Foreground services.** No service starts; claims are listed in
`state.extensions` with their type, package, text and time, and the
journal records each claim and release (D2). A capture under the line,
with the page hidden, records on; without it, pauses with `reason`
`background`. A refused claim is driven by `prefer extension
media-capture.foregroundService={"refused":"ForegroundServiceStartNotAllowedException"}`.

**Calls.** The substitute is declared (LLP 1133.002 §6), so every target
drives it, Linux headless included:

- **The list.** `startCall` and `reportIncomingCall` add an entry to
  `state.calls`, keyed by `id`: `{id, handle, displayName, direction,
  video, state, muted, audio, ref}`; `endCall` sets `ended`, and the
  entry goes at the next commit (D8). `calls()` answers the list.
- **The app's sends** take effect at once: `startCall` answers
  `dialing`; `reportCallState` sets its state; `answerCall` sets
  `connected`; hold and mute set their fields and the call's tracks, as
  production does (D9's table).
- **The person's acts** are `tap @<ref> <choice>`, the existing operation
  with a choice, as a permission ticket's (D17 item 7): `answer` sets
  `connected` and raises `callanswer`; `hangup` raises
  `callhangup` and ends it `local`; `hold` and `unhold` raise `callhold`;
  `mute` and `unmute` raise `callmute`. A choice the state does not allow
  (answering an outgoing call) is refused by name.
- **The audio** comes up as production's does, after the answer: `audio`
  turns true in the commit after the call becomes `connected`, so a
  capture the `callanswer` handler starts waits for it, as on a phone
  (D10). `prefer extension calls.audioDelay=<ms>` holds it longer, on the
  driven clock, and `calls.audio=false` keeps it down, to drive a capture
  that never gets it.
- **The target's rules** are the target's: a hidden capture start during
  a call is admitted under the agent on iOS and Android and refused on the
  web (D10).
- **The status** defaults to `{available: true, systemUI: true}`; `prefer
  extension calls.callsStatus={"available":false}` makes every send fail
  `NotSupportedError`.
- **Origins** hold as production (D16): a tool's `startCall` is
  `person-required`.

**Disclosure:** the calls' handles and names, which are the app's own
data. **Production:** none of it.

### 9.1 The recorder records on in the background

string:

```
device.microphone-background purpose.recordingInBackground
```

with `purpose.recordingInBackground` "Recording". Nothing in its
Contract changes: its take's elapsed time comes from
`mediaRecorderState`, which runs on in the background, and its list
refreshes when the take is filed. What it gets:

- **iOS:** the `audio` mode, so a take goes on with the app hidden or the

  recorder off QUEUE.md's iPhone item for leaving the app (a take no

  the writer's failure after that stays recording's open item.
- **Android:** a take goes on in the `Capture` service, with a "Recording"

- **Without the line** (an app that has not written it): leaving the app

### 9.2 A test call in Harmony, and the package's fixture

**Why a test call.** No app here places calls, and none can carry a call's
media between two devices: exact2 has no real-time transport (§1). A test
call is a real feature of calling apps: Teams' "Make a test call" places
a call that "asks you to record a short message, which is then played back
to you" (Microsoft, cited in the Summary). It exercises every part of
this module a two-party call would: the system's call screen, the audio
the system activates, the person ending or holding the call from the
system's controls, a cellular call interrupting, the microphone during a
call with the app hidden, and on Android the call's service and
notification. It does not exercise ringing a closed app (D12) or a second
device. Harmony, the team chat in Slack's and Discord's shape, is where a
call would be.

**Harmony** gains, under You:

- the lines `device.calls purpose.calls` and `device.microphone
  purpose.microphone`, and `@exact/calls`, `@exact/media-capture` and
  `@exact/recording` as dependencies;
- a **Test call** button: `startCall(id=…, handle="test-call",
  displayName=testCallName, apple.includesCallsInRecents=false)`, since
  an echo is no call the person wants in the phone's history; when the
  call is `dialing` the app
  `reportCallState(state="connected")` at once (the echo answers); when
  `audio` is true it opens the microphone with `getUserMedia` and records
  five seconds to `app:/cache/test-call`, showing "Say something" and the
  waveform; then it plays the take back with `audio src=` and, when it
  ends, `endCall(reason="remote")` and deletes the file;
- `callhangup` ends it early, from either screen: the recording stops and
  nothing plays;
- `callhold` pauses the recording (as an interruption does) and resumes
  it at the unhold, when the host has lifted the hold gate, and restarts
  the playback the hold paused; `callmute` shows the mute, which the
  call's gate already applies (D9).

Harmony runs on iOS, macOS and the web. On macOS and the web the same
test call runs as the module's own state, with no system screen (D19).
On the web the module sets Media Session's call handlers; Harmony opens no
Picture-in-Picture window, so whether the browser shows a control is not
part of the drive.

**Android** has no Harmony (LLP 1133.017 §15's ruling on Q9 put
Android's drives in the packages' fixture apps). The package's fixture,
`packages/calls/test/fixture`, an app outside the tree made by `exact new`,
carries the same test call and two ways to ring: **Ring me** calls
`reportIncomingCall` at once, with the app in front, and **Ring me in 10
s**, offered during a test call, calls it ten seconds later, so the person
can lock the phone first and the second call rings over the lock screen
(admitted, since the app holds a call; D16). A first call to a locked
phone is push's (D12). Nothing in `host/android` plays an
`audio` element yet, so on Android the test call records and does not
play back; that waits for Android's `audio`.

## 13. Questions for Charlie

1. **Foreground services as a host seam (D1, D2),** claimed by packages,
   with no app-facing name until an app runs long work of its own.
   *Recommended: yes (75%).* The alternative, a platform module the app
   calls around its work now, has no consumer, and Play reviews a service
   by the capability it serves, which the capability's own module knows.
2. **One line for a capture in the background (D3),**
   `device.microphone-background`, deriving iOS's `audio` mode and
   Android's microphone service, its purpose the notification's text.
   *Recommended: yes (80%).* The alternative, `host.ios.backgroundModes`
   beside a new `host.android.foregroundServiceTypes`, is two spellings of
   one statement, and the first already let the recorder miss Android.
3. **Android takes under a claim are crash-safe (D5),** written as ADTS
   frames and made an MP4 at stop or at the next launch, amending LLP
   1133.009 D6. *Recommended: yes (70%).* The service adds a kill the
   person makes on purpose, with no callback; the alternative is WAV under
   a claim (crash-safe as built, about 5.8 MB a minute at 48 kHz mono),
   or a fragmented MP4 through Media3's muxer, a new dependency in the
   locked graph.
4. **Calls' consumer (§9.2).** (a) Build calls now through the test call
   in Harmony on iOS, macOS and the web and the package's fixture on
   Android, stages 0–4; or (b) design only, building stage 0's substitute
   and nothing native until an app with a transport places real calls.
   *Recommended: (a), 60%.* It is the only way to find out on devices what
   the system's audio activation, a lock-screen answer and Telecom's
   five-second callbacks do to exact2's audio owner and admission, which
   is where this design is most likely wrong (round 1 of review found its
   ordering faults there); (b) is what "nothing speculative" reads most
   strictly, and costs a test call that no person asked for. If Harmony
   goes to the App Store, a CallKit test call with no real calls beside it
   may not pass review as the intended use of the `audio` mode (App Review
   2.5.4), where Teams' sits beside real calls; that is a reason to keep it
   in development builds until a transport lands.
5. **The host keeps the call provider (D9),** and the system's actions
   the app did not request reach it as activations with a person's
   origin. *Recommended: yes (80%):* one provider per app, made at launch
   before any package loads, as LLP 1133.004 D7 keeps the notification
   delegate.
6. **The microphone during a call (D10):** on iOS and Android, a capture
   start admitted while hidden when the app holds a connected call, the
   capture waiting for the call's audio, and a running capture not paused,
   never for a tool or a wake. *Recommended: yes (80%):* without it a call

7. **Ringing a closed app (D12):** designed here, built only with push,
