## 1 Frame health as a key performance metric

Frame health measures whether visible content updates on time, moves evenly and responds promptly to input. It is a runtime quality target for Exact, alongside startup and resource use. FPS describes throughput; pacing and latency explain whether that throughput feels good.

### Targets

| Activity | Target |
|---|---|
| High-impact interaction on flagship phones | 120 FPS |
| Desktop interaction, including macOS | The display's supported rate, including above 120 Hz |
| Static content | Stay idle; do not render extra frames to improve a counter |

Requested rate, operating-system schedule and observed delivery are separate facts. An app asking for 120 FPS cannot pass that target by quietly redefining it as 60 FPS. Intentional lower-rate effects and known power restrictions are reported explicitly.

### The key measurements

| Measurement | What it tells us |
|---|---|
| On-time updates | How often we meet known presentation opportunities |
| Cadence overdue time | How long a frame remains visible beyond its intended replacement interval |
| Content and input latency | Whether evenly delivered frames still show old content or respond late |
| Frame-time tails and worst gaps | Whether occasional stalls hide behind a good average |
| Evidence and coverage | What we actually observed, and where the result is unknown |

Report these per surface and activity. V1 has no blended app-wide score. Presentation feedback, rendering deadlines and callback timing remain distinct evidence levels. When a platform exposes only callback timing, label it as a proxy and leave presentation health unknown. Missing data cannot turn a result green.

### Readouts for people and agents

Agents use the existing `perf frames` operation. The development inspector presents the same snapshots in a human-readable panel. Both show target, observed cadence, timing distributions, evidence, coverage and unresolved observations.

On iOS and iPadOS, the four-finger dev menu adds **Show Frame Health** and **Hide Frame Health**. The card starts hidden and follows the existing menu gate, including in release builds where that menu is enabled. The enabled macOS Develop menu offers the equivalent action.

The card moves by a drag handle and has a close button. Touches on the readout body or outside it pass through to the app. It sits above the app viewport and below sheets and alerts. Moving it changes only its own compositor position; it does not change app layout or app state.

Text refreshes at most twice per active second, pauses during a drag, and stops when hidden or idle. The card reads existing snapshots: showing it cannot create a display link, raise refresh rate, enable full tracing or override disabled collection. Compare its cost while hidden, stationary and moving, including over a full-window GPU canvas.

### Send recent hitches to an agent

On a physical iPhone, show the monitor, reproduce the issue, and choose **Share Frame Health…** in the four-finger menu. Share or Copy returns JSON text capped at **4 KB (4000 bytes)**, including metadata, evidence and the agent carrier newline. Exact writes no report file and performs no automatic upload.

Capture retains the latest 16 events in fixed memory and exports only those at most 120 seconds old. Reports carry callback gaps, scheduled and requested cadence, nearby callback context, the largest observed apply in the interval, matching asynchronous owner/landing boundaries, workload flags, thermal/power state and build identity. They distinguish measured wall time from unobserved CPU/GPU/presentation work. Reload preserves the tail and labels each event's generation. Overwrites, expired events and oldest rows omitted to fit the wire budget are explicit; retained time is not guaranteed during a hitch storm.

An agent uses `perf frames capture start [ms]`, `stop`, `clear`, and `perf frames report`; a lease lasts at most 120 seconds. The HUD and agent lease own capture independently. One report encodes at a time, off main after a bounded copy; agent admission is at most once per 15 seconds. There is no periodic sender, retry queue or idle expiry wakeup. The [wire schema and device workflow](../docs/contract-for-agents.md#compact-frame-health-reports-on-physical-devices) explain all fields and limits.

### Collection overhead

Production collection uses bounded counters, histograms and queues. Exact-owned frame hooks do no allocation, locking, logging, I/O or tree traversal. There are no additional measurement wakeups at idle.

The initial native targets are at most 1 microsecond p99 per record/reduce event, at most 4 KiB per producer, and at most 256 KiB of collector-owned storage per process. Whole-collector work targets at most 0.1% of one CPU core at 120 Hz and 0.2% at 240 Hz. These are proposed budgets to prove on devices, not measured claims. Platform listeners, observation callbacks, bridge calls and UI work all count.

### Delivery

The first Apple slice is implemented: bounded callback counters, an agent readout, and the iOS menu/card. Bounded asynchronous tick-owner and apply evidence is available while the HUD or an agent lease owns capture. Presentation, native-only animation coverage, full deferred-work deadlines, the shared window protocol, other adapters and the inspector remain open. Qualify each adapter before production default-on. The collector may correctly report that an app misses its frame-rate target; that is a useful result, not a reason to weaken the metric.

Read the [measurement contract](#4-measurement-contract), [resource budgets](#5-production-recorder-and-resource-budget), [UI design](#72-app-indicator), [validation](#8-validation-and-overhead-experiments), or [implementation stages](#10-implementation-sequence-and-completion-evidence) for the details.

## 3 Research that shapes the design

FPS is throughput. Pacing is the distribution and ordering of presentation intervals. Uniform 16.667 ms intervals and alternating 8.333/25 ms intervals both average 60 FPS. A game-style timeline, frame-time distribution and worst stall reveal what the average hides. Keep CPU, GPU, waiting and presentation measurements distinct: these stages overlap.

Vsync aligns presentation with display refresh. On a fixed-rate screen, missing a slot can keep the previous picture visible for another interval. Variable refresh changes the available schedule, so one nominal period cannot classify all frames. Queuing can also preserve smooth cadence while increasing response latency. These distinctions follow [Android frame pacing](https://developer.android.com/games/sdk/frame-pacing), [Apple variable refresh guidance](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10147/), [PresentMon's metric definitions](https://github.com/GameTechDev/PresentMon/blob/main/README-ConsoleApplication.md), and [Unreal's stage readouts](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/performance-and-profiling-overview).

For CADisplayLink, `targetTimestamp - timestamp` describes the scheduled interval, and `targetTimestamp - CACurrentMediaTime()` estimates time remaining when the callback runs. Neither acknowledges that Exact's content appeared. `duration` is not a substitute for the actual scheduled interval. Frame-rate preferences are hints, subject to system policy. [CADisplayLink](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/quartzcore/cadisplaylink), [preferredFrameRateRange](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/quartzcore/cadisplaylink/preferredframeraterange).

Metal's `presentedTime` reports a drawable's onscreen presentation time; zero is not a successful presentation. With CAMetalDisplayLink, the submission deadline and predicted presentation time are separate. V1 does not replace the renderer's scheduling API merely to collect metrics. [Drawable presentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/mtldrawable/presentedtime), [Metal display-link deadline](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/quartzcore/cametaldisplaylink/update/targettimestamp).

Android FrameMetrics reports rendering duration and, where supported, deadline and GPU duration. FrameTimeline provides expected versus actual presentation and jank classifications for supported paths. An Android window observation does not automatically cover separately rendered SurfaceViews. [FrameMetrics](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/view/FrameMetrics), [FrameTimeline](https://perfetto.dev/docs/data-sources/frametimeline).

Apple's animation hitch ratio supplies a useful system KPI. Its perceptual adjustments and tracked animation population differ from Exact's raw overdue-time calculation, so store and display it separately. Browser Long Animation Frames entries describe long work, not every missed high-refresh deadline. [MetricKit hitch ratio](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metrickit/mxanimationmetric/hitchtimeratio), [LoAF](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/long-animation-frames).

## 4 Measurement contract

### 4.1 Identity and time

Each metric identifies process incarnation, session incarnation, presentation surface generation, display identity, source and metric-definition version. Schedule epochs annotate observations and slot arithmetic but do not reset cumulative counters/histograms. Identifiers are process-local integers; app content is not copied into the collector. Surface reuse and reload require a fresh baseline. Display moves can preserve deltas, with the crossed displays and epochs reported; source/population changes cannot be silently merged.

Keep timestamps as monotonic integer nanoseconds in a declared clock domain. Convert platform clocks at the adapter boundary and retain calibration uncertainty. Never subtract wall time, the agent's seekable clock, GPU timestamps, and host timestamps without an explicit conversion. JSON encodes nanosecond counters as decimal strings to preserve integers above JavaScript's safe range. FPS and derived ratios may be floating-point values.

An event is a fixed-size record: kind, source/generation, frame token, schedule epoch, timestamp, optional deadline/predicted presentation, and flags. Each source declares which fields are supported. A shared commit sequence can identify submitted content but is not proof of presentation. GPU completion is also not proof of presentation.

### 4.2 Activity and expected work

Track three activity classes: continuous visual demand, one pending visible update, and idle. Demand is declared by existing scroll/transition/animation/frame-task/native-control paths. The collector is not itself a source of demand. An authored animation that intentionally targets a lower rate carries that intent. The HUD cannot keep the measured activity segment open.

For continuous demand, use platform-supplied expected slots or an independently declared schedule. Do not infer a lower target from slowed delivery. Anchor a fixed-rate epoch to each authoritative timestamp/sequence and use rational period arithmetic only to bridge the following gap. Genuine schedule changes close slot arithmetic at the boundary and start another; counters persist. For variable refresh without a complete expected schedule, report per-frame timing and mark slot counts unavailable across ambiguous gaps. An uncertain gap is coverage loss, not an implicit schedule change.

For a pending visible update, start first-response timing at its input or invalidation. Where the platform supplies render deadlines, the response target is the first predicted presentation whose render deadline has not passed at that trigger; otherwise the platform-deadline lateness is unavailable and response duration remains useful. Record the chosen deadline with the token before work starts. Record submission and acknowledged presentation independently. Superseded updates coalesce to the newest eligible content; they do not create a dropped frame for every intermediate commit. Pending-only time contributes to response-duration/lateness distributions, not continuous-motion FPS or overdue-time ratios. The first response after idle is measured even though callback cadence needs a baseline.

Native scrolling, Core Animation transitions, browser compositor animations, video and platform chrome may update outside Exact's own frame clock. V1's proposed native-scroll and browser-motion observation is conditional on S0 proving that an activity-scoped observer neither changes the presented cadence/power policy nor undersamples the target. Reuse an existing link where available. For an extra iOS link, S0 compares range/default choices with observer off/on under the same independent presentation oracle. Asking for the panel maximum can raise the rate; `.default` can undersample it. Neither outcome qualifies. Apply the same test to the macOS fallback and browser rAF observer, including loss of idle suppression. If no nonperturbing source exists, production core does not run that observer and reports native/compositor motion unavailable or reduced-rate proxy coverage, which cannot pass a 120 Hz target. The reference journey stays in the results as unqualified.

Charge all accepted observation callbacks to the adapter benchmark. Start iOS demand on drag, programmatic scroll or transition start and end it on drag without deceleration, deceleration end, animation completion/cancel, visibility loss or teardown. Include interrupted and nested scrolls. Browser animation lifecycle signals open/close observation; activities whose lifecycle cannot be identified remain uncovered. No observer remains running at idle.

Backgrounding, minimization, occlusion and an app's own visibility changes end eligibility at the observed boundary. Do not bridge the gap on resume. Partial occlusion is not inferred from a tree walk. Platform visibility is the declared approximation.

### 4.3 Cadence and latency

Every sample can carry three different rates: requested content rate, the OS schedule, and observed delivery. The hardware maximum is context, not observed FPS. The product policy requests 120 for high-impact interaction on capable mobile hardware, and the supported display rate on desktop, including above 120. Paint-only motion and video may intentionally differ; report their category and policy rather than mixing them into the high-impact target cohort.

Intervals are 8.333 ms at 120 Hz, 6.944 ms at 144 Hz, 4.167 ms at 240 Hz and 2.778 ms at 360 Hz. These are presentation intervals, not a guarantee of that much CPU time after callback arrival. A render duration below one interval does not establish equivalent delivered FPS.

Known low-power, thermal, user-rate and display restrictions are context. Unknown restriction causes remain unknown. Do not declare an OS restriction solely because callbacks became slower, and do not exclude a thermally degraded app from field reporting. Separate restriction cohorts alongside the overall distribution.

Input-to-presentation measures the earliest relevant input included in the acknowledged frame. Without that join, report input-to-submit or input-to-commit under its own name. Software presentation feedback excludes input-device and panel response latency; it is not a physical input-to-photon measurement.

### 4.4 Metrics and denominators

| Metric | Definition and conditions |
|---|---|
| `expected_opportunities`, `missed_opportunities` | Slots during observed eligible continuous demand, with the expected schedule independently known; a missing callback cannot erase a known slot; source/evidence determines whether these are presentation or callback opportunities |
| `on_time_fraction` | `(expected - missed) / expected` over that same observed continuous population; null when the denominator is zero or its schedule/coverage is unknown |
| `cadence_overdue_ns` | Union of excess frame residency during observed continuous demand on one surface: after a valid replacement, the next replacement is due one declared residency interval later; a gap G with fixed residency B contributes max(0, G-B), assigned to the finalized window that completes the gap (§5.3) |
| `cadence_overdue_ms_per_active_second` | `1000 * cadence_overdue_ns / observed_continuous_ns`; Exact's raw cadence measure, separate from content lateness and Apple's perceptual ratio |
| `content_lateness_histogram` | Per acknowledged token, max(0, actual presentation - that token's intended presentation); measures pipeline delay even when cadence is smooth; unmatched content has no such value |
| `presented_updates`, `presented_fps` | Acknowledged replacements during observed continuous demand and count divided by that observed continuous duration; repeated scanouts are not updates; actual content freshness requires a content revision match |
| `callback_*`, `submission_*`, `render_deadline_*` | Separate proxy metrics using their own event populations and denominators; never alias them to presented metrics |
| `interval_histogram`, `lifetime_max_gap_ns`, `window_max_gap_bounds_ns` | Fixed-bin timing distribution; exact maximum since source incarnation; bounds on a requested window's maximum from its histogram delta. Never subtract lifetime maxima to invent a window maximum |
| `input_response_histogram`, `response_lateness_histogram` | Duration for joined pending updates and lateness against their declared response deadline; populations and unmatched/superseded counts are separate from continuous motion |
| `system_animation_hitch_ratio` | Native system value, source, unit, covered animation population and reporting interval; no conversion into Exact's raw metric |

An intended presentation is the platform's prediction for that particular submission, recorded with its token before submission. CAMetalDisplayLink's `targetPresentationTimestamp` is such a candidate, subject to S0's clock/source validation. A plain CADisplayLink callback target, rAF time, command-buffer scheduled callback or FrameMetrics deadline is not automatically a prediction for the drawable/content being submitted. Those paths report content lateness unavailable unless a distinct validated prediction exists. A declared pipeline model may supply an intended target only with an explicit versioned phase/depth constant qualified against the oracle before measurement; report that model as modeled evidence, not a platform prediction. Never fit or subtract a steady offset from the run being evaluated. Otherwise a real queue-latency regression would be normalized away.

For continuous 120 Hz motion, suppose a previous frame presents at 0 and its replacement is due at 8.333 ms but arrives at 25 ms. Cadence overdue time is 16.667 ms, with [8.333, 25) counted once. In the half-open window [0, 25), slots at 8.333 and 16.667 are missed; the slot at 25 belongs to the next window. Integer arithmetic uses the rational schedule rather than repeatedly rounding 8.333 ms. If subsequent frames arrive every 8.333 ms but remain 16.667 ms behind their intended targets, cadence overdue time stops accumulating while every token records 16.667 ms of content lateness. Both facts appear; smooth cadence cannot erase queued latency.

A read without an acknowledgment reports pending-update age and unresolved status, not proof that old content remains onscreen. Presentation counters are finalized only through a source's completeness watermark, where the adapter proves all relevant observations through that time were consumed. Sources without such a watermark finalize a gap only when its next valid acknowledgment arrives. Provisional pending exposure is separate and never added to monotonic cumulative counters; a delayed acknowledgment cannot make a published lifetime total decrease. A source that proves a still-unresolved deadline may report right-censored overdue bounds, outside finalized totals. A dropped or unknown acknowledgment makes the affected span unobserved. Readers mark a stalled publisher stale rather than fabricating current display health.

Only adapters that match intended content revision and target to presentation can produce content lateness or freshness claims. A drawable token alone supports cadence. For a canvas that captures native children into a texture, include that capture's commit revision as well as the drawable revision; otherwise report cadence only. One canvas's health does not establish the health of overlays or another canvas.

### 4.5 Evidence and coverage

Evidence belongs to each metric: `presentation`, `render_deadline`, `callback_proxy`, `submission_proxy`, `system_aggregate`, `virtual`, or `unavailable`. A source may provide several metrics at different levels. Independent validation is metadata, never an evidence-level upgrade.

Coverage includes eligible time, time with the necessary observations, unsupported activity categories/surfaces, lost events, unmatched tokens, clock uncertainty, sampling, capacity exclusions and generation discontinuities. There is no claim of complete frame capture where the platform cannot enumerate expected events. Distinguish `unknown` from `none` for losses.

Coverage is `observed_continuous_ns / eligible_continuous_ns`, with unobserved spans retained in the denominator. FPS and cadence ratios use observed time and are conditional; missing time neither fabricates low FPS nor permits a full pass. Pending-update coverage has its own triggered/matched/unknown counts. Do not average percentages, FPS reciprocals or percentiles. Merge only compatible raw totals/histograms by metric definition, source, activity, device and build, preserving schedule changes and target restrictions. Report per-session/workload distributions alongside totals. V1 presents surfaces independently; it does not sum simultaneous surfaces' active durations into an app-wide user-time score or pretend their independently timed snapshots share a coherent cut.

## 5 Production recorder and resource budget

### 5.1 Architecture

The host owns the collector because the runner does not know when pixels appear. Use one Rust reducer for native hosts and a JS implementation for the web. Swift/Kotlin hooks append fixed numeric records to preallocated native storage through a bounded C/JNI entry; charge the crossing in the hook budget. Drain on the owner's existing frame work, demand transitions and snapshot requests, not only at 2 Hz. A full queue records loss; it cannot synchronously drain on a foreign callback thread. Conformance fixtures compare the two reducers. Avoid a new general observability framework or renderer dependency in non-GPU apps.

Separate three modes:

| Mode | Work |
|---|---|
| Off | Registration/read reports disabled; no recorder callbacks or allocation |
| Core | Scalar counters, fixed histograms, demand/schedule bookkeeping, bounded event handoff and snapshots |
| Detail | Explicit, timed capture into a preallocated ring; additional causal diagnostics remain development-only and carry their own cost |

Core remains independent of plan-site counters, trace export, hatch diagnostics and source-map lookup. A late event takes a bounded record path; it never triggers string construction, a stack walk or an unbounded diagnostic scan. Snapshot reads do not clear counters or change the rendering target. Production hooks never panic, throw or terminate on malformed observations: invalidate that source/span, increment a bounded fault counter and continue the app. Exact's vendor modifications are Exact-owned for the hook rules; pre-existing platform/driver work is measured as adapter cost rather than claimed allocation-free.

Each producer owns its mutable state. Foreign-thread presentation callbacks write to bounded preallocated handoff storage with numeric tokens. No synchronous dispatch to the main thread, busy waiting, or lock acquisition is allowed in a frame hook. Choose an SPSC layout only after proving a single producer; multiple callback threads require independent producer storage or a bounded MPSC design. Every handoff is race-free under the language memory model.

Publish snapshots with preallocated ownership slots and acquire/release publication. Readers cannot access slots while writers mutate them. A full snapshot channel drops a publication, increments its own counter, and preserves metric counters. Reclamation on teardown waits outside the frame hook for registered callbacks, or uses generation-safe storage that cannot be reused until callbacks finish. No raw pointer into a destroyed session escapes.

### 5.2 Budgets to establish before production enablement

All timing numbers below are acceptance targets, not benchmark results.

| Budget | V1 target |
|---|---|
| Native record and reduce work | p99 added wall time ≤1 microsecond per event in calibrated lab measurements, including the boundary crossing; report maximum and cold/cache-contended behavior separately |
| Collector-owned CPU, all core hooks and snapshots | Attributed work target ≤0.1% of one core at 120 Hz and ≤0.2% at 240 Hz for a single continuously active reference window; establish each callback/observer/bridge term rather than attempting to resolve this solely from whole-process noise |
| Critical-path operations | Zero allocations, locks, I/O, logging, app-code execution or tree reads in Exact-owned hooks |
| Core native storage | ≤4 KiB per registered producer, including its event queue and pending-token table; ≤16 producers per process |
| Total native-owned storage | ≤256 KiB per process, including registry, queues, detail ring and local snapshot readers; assert actual layouts |
| Additional measurement wakeups at idle | Zero; externally requested reads are counted separately |
| Snapshot publication | At most 2 Hz per process while activity supplies hooks; readers can request a current snapshot without a periodic idle timer |
| Recent-hitch detail | Latest 16 events within the producer budget; report horizon 120 seconds, agent lease ≤120 seconds, HUD-owned capture lasts while shown. Report overwrite, age expiry and export omission independently |
| Report | ≤4000 UTF-8 bytes including carrier newline; one process-wide encode in flight; agent admission once per 15 seconds; no app-side disk writes or automatic network sender |
| App HUD | Numeric content at most 2 Hz while active; direct drag positioning follows native input without a new frame source; at most one overlay per window; measure stationary and dragging costs separately |

Registration occurs outside hot callbacks. At producer capacity, decline registration deterministically and list the excluded surface/category in the window coverage inventory. Do not steal an existing slot or silently sample only the fastest surface. Counter overflow saturates and invalidates affected deltas; source-incarnation changes require a fresh baseline, while schedule-epoch changes do not. An unmatched/in-flight token table overflow is event loss, not an inferred drop.

Record and disassemble the worst common and late paths: instructions, explicit loads/stores, touched bytes and cache footprint. Limit clock reads and reuse timestamps already supplied by the platform. A bounded instruction count and memory footprint are enforceable design properties; a universal execution-cycle or physical-memory-read ceiling is not, because caches, faults, frequency scaling and scheduling vary. Do not read performance counters or time the recorder on every production event to enforce its own budget. A storm producing more than 16 timing events overwrites the oldest retained events. The 120-second horizon is an age filter, not a guaranteed duration of history.

Measure OS callback registration/allocation and platform listener overhead as part of the whole adapter, even when Exact's reducer allocates nothing. For Android, consume needed scalars before returning because callback data can be reused; include the API's dropped-report count. JS uses preallocated numeric storage, avoids object creation in the recorder, and reports its measured heap/JIT/GC behavior rather than claiming the native storage budget proves browser overhead.

### 5.3 Measurement windows and live snapshots

Keep fixed cumulative counters/histograms by producer/source incarnation, not an unbounded set per schedule epoch. Epoch changes update `epochs_crossed` and rate/restriction summaries without discarding history. Absolute-time bins let deltas cross rate changes; rate-normalized distributions are deferred. The Apple implementation uses two 64-bin histograms per producer: 48 bins of width 500,000 ns covering [0, 24 ms), 15 doubling-width tail bins starting at 24 ms, and one overflow bin. This reduces the originally proposed 128-bin precision to keep counters, distributions and the hitch tail inside the existing 4 KiB producer budget. Quantiles are bin bounds, not invented point estimates. Half-open bin edges and saturation are part of the definition. A producer collects only its declared interval and one latency/lateness population; additional populations need an admitted producer slot. S0 must demonstrate the queue/token/scalar layout still fits 4 KiB; if it cannot, reduce populations or change the budget explicitly before S1, not by hidden allocation.

Subtract coherent cumulative snapshots from the same source incarnation. Each metric's actual delta window is [finalized-through at the first snapshot, finalized-through at the second snapshot), not the read timestamps. A gap that completes after a read is wholly assigned to the next finalized interval; it is not retroactively clipped to that read. Keep read/publication timestamps beside those metric endpoints. Cadence ratios divide by observed continuous time inside the finalized window; any later unresolved tail remains visible separately. Arbitrary wall-time clipping without retained interval records is unsupported. An empty or old finalized window cannot imply current health.

UI readers retain at most eleven snapshots at 2 Hz for a roughly five-second view; actual endpoints are returned. Quantiles and the requested window's maximum are bin bounds from histogram deltas. Exact maxima are lifetime-only unless detail records cover the whole requested window. Do not subtract maxima. Histogram subtraction, formatting and source joins happen in the reader. Core mode has no raw-frame timeline. Snapshot-reader storage, registry and queues remain inside the 256 KiB process bound.

The snapshot includes observation time, finalized-through time and publication time. An outstanding deadline reports unresolved age subject to §4.4's watermark rules. An in-app UI may freeze with the app; devtools mark a snapshot stale after two expected publication intervals while continuous activity is expected, and show observation age. Polling clients distinguish an idle source from a disconnected or blocked publisher.

## 6 Platform implementation

### 6.1 iOS and macOS

Instrument frame-clock entry, outcome application/submission, demand transitions and visibility boundaries. Make an Exact-work deadline proxy mandatory in core: record the monotonic end of Exact-owned apply/commit work and its applicable target from the frame hook, charging the extra clock read. This catches Exact work finishing after its target even with on-time callbacks; it does not observe all render-server or unrelated main-thread work. Mark turns without an applicable target unknown rather than charging them to a future frame.

S0 must prove a nonperturbing scroll/transition-scoped observer under §4.2 before production runs it. A qualified observer is a callback proxy and its cost includes the extra active callbacks. Qualification includes that observer and the end-of-work proxy. If the observer changes cadence, undersamples or exceeds budget, production native-scroll coverage remains explicitly unavailable/unqualified; do not delete the flagship journey to claim completion. The Exact-work proxy still observes supported app-driven work. Inability to observe scrolling efficiently is a reported v1 limitation, not a fabricated pass or a reason to change the app's schedule.

On macOS, elect exactly one observation authority per window/display. Reuse the session Frames link when active, otherwise a suitable pump link, otherwise the scoped observer. Authority handoff keeps the current schedule anchor and deduplicates by the platform timestamp/target pair. Heavy-leaf, hover, follow and press links supply activity/work observations but cannot create additional expected slots for that window. V1 policy predicates are explicit:

1. Idle with no eligible work is `not_applicable`. Virtual, stale, disabled, unknown schedule, an empty population, lost/unmatched observations or incomplete required activity coverage yield `insufficient_evidence` for a full-window pass.
2. An independently known schedule below the requested interaction target yields target-delivery `restricted`, with known or unknown reason. 3. A complete callback/render population with no missed/late reference deadlines can yield only `proxy_pass` for that proxy's declared scope. Presentation target delivery remains insufficient without presentation or the independent certification oracle.
4. Presentation cadence `pass` requires complete observed continuous coverage, independently known requested/scheduled rates that meet the target, no missed opportunities and no cadence-overdue time beyond timestamp uncertainty. Content-latency `pass` additionally requires complete intended-content matches and no late token beyond that uncertainty. Borderline classifications are uncertain, not forgiven as on time.
5. A valid observed failure yields `fail` for the affected assessment even if the rest of its window is missing. Persist both the failure and incomplete coverage. A known restriction is a separate target result and does not erase observed cadence/latency failures.
