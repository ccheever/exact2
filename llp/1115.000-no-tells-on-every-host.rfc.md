## 0. Summary

LLP 1115 changed the top rule on 2026-10-09 from "the web is the standard" to "write the web,
ship the platform": what the author writes is CSS and wins; what the author leaves unsaid is
the platform's for anything a person can see or feel; layout defaults and names stay CSS. Its
two waves and the four bake-off rounds since built most of that on iOS and on the web.

On 2026-10-11 Charlie asked for a second audit: where does the code, or anything else, still
follow the old rule? Six read-only passes (iOS, macOS, Android, the web, the kernel and
compiler, the words) read main at `ccc9afd52` and returned 242 findings, with overlap between
passes. LLP 1116.003 landed the same day; a seventh pass re-read the findings it touched at
`ccd3d3be7`. It had fixed four and part of two. Nothing was built or run by any pass: every
finding is from reading. Appendix A lists them by ID.

This document sorts them three ways:

- **Wave 0** (§3): the words, and fixes with one possible answer under rulings already made.
  It lands now.
- **Fourteen decisions** (§2), each with a proposed answer. They are for review before any of
  it is built.
- **What waits for its own document** (§4): the Mac's structure, Android, the painted hosts.

Charlie has ruled on three questions already (2026-10-11):

## 1. What the audit found

1. **LLP 1115 landed on iOS and the web, and reads as landed everywhere.** System Back
   without an authored control (D5 there) is called only from the iOS presenter on Apple, so a
   pushed route on the Mac has no way back. Press feedback for an undeclared pressable is under
   `#if os(iOS)`. LLP 1115 §6 says "`showModal`/`close` on every host"; the Linux host refuses
   both. Its "Still open" list names three things that have since landed.
2. **Author > platform has two holes in its mechanism** (D2). A host cannot tell what the
   author wrote, so it guesses, and the guess drops authored platform colours on iOS controls
   and cannot separate the compiler's implied rows from the author's.
3. **The compiler writes iOS's look onto every host** (D3). The grouped-list sheet is nine
   literal iOS hex pairs and iOS point sizes, so `list appearance="auto"` on the Mac is an
   iPhone Settings list at 17 points. The web's stylesheet repeats about fifteen of the role
   table's colours as hex, and two have drifted from it.
4. **Native behaviour that was made to match the browser on purpose is now a tell.** On the
   Mac: a click on a button takes the focus, Tab reaches every button whatever the system
   setting, inner scrollers never rubber-band. On iOS: scrolling never dismisses the keyboard,
   a plain Return leaves it up, nested scrollers chain. Each was a ruling under the old rule
   (LLP 1104 D6, LLP 1010 §3), and seventeen tests pin them (D14); five more pin the Linux
   painter's look and are LLP 1115.001's.
5. **The web's designed look has gaps a web developer notices** (D4): a popover is the
   browser's bordered box in the middle of the viewport, custom pressables and links give no
   hover or press response, checkbox and radio are the browser's 13 px ones beside 36 px
   designed fields, a modal route has no surface.
6. **Ten items of the 2026-10-09 audit are in neither of LLP 1115's lists**, done or open:
   keyboard dismissal on scroll, the large title's collapse, nested-scroll chaining, the iPad
   focus halo, `scroll-behavior`, control margins, SVG `fill`, `hr`'s margins, the Mac's route
   header, and Tab under Keyboard Navigation. Each has a decision below or a line in §4.
7. **The words.** `README.md`'s third principle, the root explainer (LLP 1000) and the kernel
   spec's §1 rule (LLP 1001) still say "the web is the standard". About twelve rulings in older
   LLPs decide presentation by that rule and carry no note. Three `QUEUE.md` entries now point
   the wrong way.

### D2 — "Written" is a fact the plan carries

Author > platform needs each host to know what the author wrote on a node. Today hosts guess:

- iOS's `ownColor` counts a colour as written only when it is not a platform colour and
  differs from the parent's. So `color="-exact-system-red"` on a bar button, a spinner, an
  alert's action or a segment's symbol is dropped, and naming a role is what the docs teach
  (IOS-06). A written colour equal to the parent's is dropped as well.
- A row the compiler implies (a tag's fixed rows, a heading's size and weight, a link run's
  colour, the whole grouped sheet) is an ordinary style row. A host cannot tell the sheet's
  `color` from the author's (KRN-07).
- So LLP 1116.003 D7 taught the kernel a third guess: `kernel/src/grouped.rs` keeps a copy
  of the sheet's colours and sizes (`SHEET_COLORS`) and calls a grouped row's text authored
  when its value differs from them. That fixed the footer, and it means the sheet now lives
  in two places that must change together (D3.1).

**Proposed.** One bit per style row: *the author wrote this on this node*. Lowering already
knows it (`Origin::Own` and `Origin::Class` are the author's; `Origin::Tag` and a compiler
sheet are not). The bit travels in the plan, the kernel keeps it beside the row, and the batch
carries it to the presenter. Hosts read it wherever they apply LLP 1115 §2's rule, in place
of `isSystemColor` and the equal-to-parent test. LLP 1115 §2 then reads "written means the
bit is set", which is also true of an inherited value (the bit is not set on the child).

The first consumer, built alone and first, is iOS's `ownColor`. The plan format and the
compatibility id move once (LLP 1030 D3a).

### D3 — One palette, named by role; sizes named by text style

1. **No sheet the compiler or a host writes holds a hex colour.** The grouped sheet
   (`contract/lower/src/grouped.rs`, and the kernel's copy of it until D2 retires that) and
   the web's stylesheet (`host/web/index.html`) name roles. Wave 0 does the grouped sheet's
   colours.
2. **The role table's fallback pairs are the web's palette**, re-measured once on the current
   iOS. They are what the web resolves a role to, which under D4's one look is Apple's. Where
   the grouped sheet's measured value and the table's disagree (separator, accent, red), the
   newer measurement goes into the table.
3. **The grouped sheet's sizes are text styles and em-relative metrics** (`-exact-body`,
   `-exact-footnote`, `-exact-subheadline`), so its rows follow the root font size: 13 on the
   Mac, the reader's setting on the web. Until the Mac has a native list (§4) this is what
   makes `list appearance="auto"` look like the Mac.
4. **An authored `accent-color` reaches every `AccentColor`** on macOS as it does on iOS and
   the web: symbols and rows, not only controls.
5. **A block `link` of text is the tint on native hosts too**, as LLP 1116.000 D4 made it on
   the web. One blue for links on the web: `LinkText`'s web resolution is the accent.
6. Three rows of the role table are corrected. An unset page background is `-exact-background`
   (`windowBackgroundColor` on the Mac), and LLP 1115 §3 says so in place of "`Canvas`";
   `Canvas` stays the document white. `ButtonText` on iOS is the tint, so an authored accent
   reaches it. `-exact-link` and `LinkText` resolve alike on iOS.
7. **Roles are added only as a decision here needs them:** `SelectedItem` and
   `SelectedItemText` (CSS's own names) for a selected row, and a scrim. Shadow colour, metric
   and motion roles are not proposed.
8. **SVG `fill` stays black**, as SVG says: an author's drawing means what it says. The
   compiler's one-scheme colour warning stops skipping `svg`, and names `currentcolor` for a
   shape with no `fill`.

### D4 — The web is one look, finished

Charlie: "one look". The web's designed default is the same in every browser and on every
operating system, and it is the look LLP 1116 D1 built, which is Apple's. LLP 1116 D1 and LLP
1115 §3 record that choice. What is unfinished in it:

1. **Menus and popovers** get a surface (rounded, shadowed, rows that highlight on hover) and
   open anchored to their invoker, or at the pointer for a context menu.
2. **A modal route is a sheet:** the `Canvas` surface, a card over a backdrop on a wide
   window and full-bleed on a narrow one, `role="dialog"` and `aria-modal`, focus moved in and
   returned on close.
3. **Checkbox, radio, range and file** are drawn in the look at the size of the other
   controls, in the accent.
4. **A surface the host paints carries its own ink.** Fields, selects and the app bar take
   `FieldText` or `CanvasText` unless the node's own `color` is written (D2's bit). The
   indeterminate spinner is the secondary label colour, not `currentColor`. This is LLP 1115
   D4 on the web, and it replaces LLP 1104 D4's inherited ink for these surfaces.
5. **Routes are pages.** `document.title` follows the route header's heading. Focus moves to
   the new route's heading on a push and back to the invoking row on Back. The browser's
   Forward, and a same-origin link to a declared route, work with no `navigate` handler, as
   Back does since LLP 1116.003.
6. **A row that only navigates is a link.** The recipes and patterns write `link href=…` for
   it, so ⌘-click, middle click and "Open in New Tab" work. Nothing is inferred in lowering.
7. **The shell carries the resets a careful web app ships:** `text-size-adjust: 100%`,
   `touch-action: manipulation` on controls, and `overscroll-behavior: contain` on a shell that
   scrolls inside itself (so a list's top does not pull the page to refresh).
8. **`forced-colors` and right-to-left are answered:** under forced colours the drawn controls
   revert to the browser's own; the select's chevron and the switch's thumb use logical sides.
9. **A route `header`'s padding, minimum height and stickiness come from lowering**, so every
   host that paints a header lays it out alike. A host that projects the header (iOS) ignores
   them, as now.
10. Small ones, each with one answer: inline links underline on hover and focus; a tablist and
    a menu move by arrow keys; a keyboard press shows the pressed state; the Back button
    matches the other controls and its label is localisable; a segmented track is the width of
    its segments; hairlines are borders, not half-pixel shadows; symbols and images in
    pressables are not draggable; a rendered page keeps the manifest's `lang`.

[WEB-01 to -17, -19 to -24; press and hover feedback (WEB-04) is D6. Of these, `QUEUE.md`
now lists Forward and route links (part of WEB-10) and the range's track (part of WEB-06).]

### D5 — Back is always there, on every host

LLP 1115 D5 landed on iOS and the web. On macOS a pushed route gets all three of the Mac's
ways back, each calling the same `hostBack` iOS calls: **Go ▸ Back, ⌘[**; the **two-finger
swipe**; and a **navigational toolbar item** (a back chevron) while a route is pushed. An
authored Back control is still what they press. The route's heading in the title bar is the
structural work of §4 and is not part of this.

Android's half is LLP 1115.001. The Linux desktop host calls `host_back` for its Back key.

[MAC-01, AND-5]

### D6 — Something pressable responds, on every host

An undeclared pressable (a row, a card, a `plain` button, a link) shows the platform's
response to a press, and the author's own (`-exact-press-scale`, a hover handler) replaces it:

| Host | Unsaid press | Unsaid hover |
|---|---|---|
| iOS | dims (landed) | none |
| macOS | dims while the mouse is down, as a borderless `NSButton` | none |
| Web | dims on `:active` | a fill in `-exact-quaternary-fill` (`hover: hover` only) |
| Android | LLP 1115.001 | |

A disabled custom pressable dims on every host, as a disabled control does. The web keeps the
tap highlight off, since the press state replaces it. On iOS a press keeps tracking in
UIKit's margin around the control before it cancels.

[MAC-20, WEB-04, WEB-28, IOS-16, IOS-37; test `PressFeedbackMacTests`]

### D7 — On the Mac, the Mac's rules for focus, keys and text

LLP 1104 D6 made focus "follow the web". Safari on the Mac does not: it does not focus a
clicked button, and the web conformance run already admits that as a known difference
("Safari's macOS convention", `host/web-js/conformance/known-webkit.json`), with its Tab
order. So the Mac's rule is not a departure from the web a Mac user knows.

1. **A click on a button, or on empty ground, leaves the first responder where it is.**
   `retainFocus` becomes the Mac's default, not an opt-in.
2. **Tab honours Keyboard Navigation** (`NSApp.isFullKeyboardAccessEnabled`): off, it moves
   between text fields and lists; on, it reaches every control. `tabindex` written by the
   author still wins.
3. **Return presses the default button; Space presses the focused control.** A dialog's one
   `filled` button is its default and is drawn so by AppKit; a destructive button never is.
   Return in a field sends `submit` and ends editing, as `NSTextField` does.
4. **A new window puts the caret in its first key view**, unless the author wrote `autofocus`
   elsewhere. A canvas that takes text input keeps today's rule.
5. **A confirmation is `NSAlert`** as a sheet on its window (LLP 1115 D6's Mac half), with
   Cancel on Escape and the default button on Return.
6. **Unsaid `autocorrect` and `spellcheck` are the person's settings.** The host writes only
   what the author wrote.
7. **A release build has no Develop menu.** It follows the development trust the build bakes.
8. Small ones: selection extends with Shift-click and Shift-arrow and by word after a
   double-click; the selected-text menu is AppKit's own; the system focus ring leaves an
   authored border alone; a window opens with AppKit's animation; a one-window app does not
   offer window tabs; the toolbar takes the system's display mode; menu titles are the
   localised standard ones; controls click through an inactive window and content does not.

The open macOS issues filed for browser parity (`20261009-macos-controls-default-tab-order`,
`20261010-macos-tab-from-a-focused-box`, `20261010-macos-hover-during-a-drag`,
`20261009-macos-subtree-hover`) are re-read under this decision and closed or reworded.

### D8 — Scrolling feels like the platform

What scrolls, and how far, is layout and stays CSS. How it feels is the platform's:

1. **`overscroll-behavior: auto` is the platform's edge behaviour.** On iOS and macOS an
   inner scroller rubber-bands at its end; it does not hand the gesture to the scroller
   around it mid-drag. `contain` and `none` mean what CSS says.
2. **A `scroll` node on the Mac is scrolled by AppKit:** its momentum, its wheel step, its
   elasticity. The hand-rolled 40-point tick goes.
3. **Scrollers follow the system's style** (overlay, or always shown). A legacy scroller takes
   room, as it does in Chrome on a Mac, so the kernel learns a scrollbar gutter. That part is
   wave 2 and may slip to §4 if the gutter proves large.
4. **`scroll-behavior: auto` animates a reveal and nothing else.** Bringing a focused field
   or a `scrollIntoView` target into view animates on native hosts; a written `scrollTop`
   still jumps, since restoring a position must not animate.
5. Arrow, Space and Page keys scroll the focused scroller on macOS, by AppKit's own actions.

### D9 — The iOS keyboard goes away as it does in an iOS app

1. **Unsaid `keyboardDismissMode` is `interactive`** on a vertical scroller: the keyboard
   follows a drag that reaches it. An authored value wins. (`on-drag` is what SwiftUI's lists
   do; `interactive` is proposed because it never takes the keyboard from someone who was only
   scrolling a form.)
2. **A plain Return resigns the field.** When the field has a `submit` handler the action
   runs first, and the field keeps the keyboard only if the action focused it again, so an
   "add another" field does not flicker.
3. **Return's label is the system's** unless `enterkeyhint` says otherwise; a `submit` handler
   no longer makes it "Go".
4. A tap on blank ground still ends editing. The stated reason becomes that hand-built iOS
   apps do it, not that a page does.

[IOS-03, -04, -28, -33; KRN-06; test IOS-T1]

### D10 — A control's size and spacing are its own

1. **Lowering stops writing Chrome's margins** onto checkbox, radio and range. On the web
   the browser's own sheet still applies them; natively the control's alignment rect is the
   platform's.
2. **`range` and a determinate `progress` fill the width they are offered** on every host,
   the web's designed sheet included, so layout still agrees across hosts. Their intrinsic
   width (129 and 160) remains the flex basis.
3. **`hr` has no margin of its own** (the reset path of LLP 1104 D4: preflight zeroes it) and
   is a hairline: one device pixel natively, `1px` on the web. The width is a value,
   `-exact-hairline`, that authors may write in place of `0.5`.
4. `audio controls` takes the platform player's size natively.

[KRN-05, -09, -10, -14; IOS-09; MAC-33; DOC-15, -34; tests KRN-23]

### D11 — iOS bars, lists and rows

1. **The bar is decided per route, not per stack.** A stack whose first route has no header
   still shows the bar for a later route that has one.

   LLP 1116.003 D2.2 wrote the rest of the rule down as it stands, with a warning
   (`lower-header-no-bar`): with no single heading a route has no bar, no title and no Back
   button, only the edge swipe. LLP 1115 D5 says a pushed route "still gets the system back
   button". **Proposed, and a change to yesterday's rule:** a pushed route with no header
   and no authored Back control gets a bar that holds the system Back alone; a route that
   declares its own Back control keeps none, which is how an author asks for a full-bleed
   screen. If Charlie prefers D2.2 as it is, only the first sentence is built.
2. **The route's scroller is the bar's scroller with nothing written.** The large title
   collapses and content runs under the bar without `navigationScroll`, which stays as the
   way to name a different scroller.
3. **Header search takes UIKit's placement** and hides on scroll as UIKit does.
4. **A tapped grouped row stays selected through the push** and fades on return. Re-tapping
   the selected tab at its root scrolls to the top. `refresh` on a grouped list is on the list
   that shows.
5. **In a grouped row, a field and a textarea are borderless.** A general `dialog` is a sheet.
6. Small ones: a `link` is a link to VoiceOver; key commands carry titles, so holding ⌘ lists
   them; `user-select="text"` is honoured; a segmented title takes its own width.

[IOS-05, -07, -12, -13, -18 to -20, -26, -27, -30, -31, -36, -40; test IOS-T2]

### D12 — Type is the platform's, all the way

1. **An authored absolute `font-size` is the author's number on every node.** A native
   button's title stops scaling it. `rem` and text styles are what follow Dynamic Type, and
   the docs and `no-tells` already steer authors to them.
2. **A person's accessibility settings reach what they would reach in a hand-built app:**
   Bold Text weights the system font, including under an authored weight; Increase Contrast
   moves roles; Dynamic Type moves `rem` and text styles.
3. **A text style carries its leading and its per-platform weight** when `line-height` and
   `font-weight` are unsaid (the Mac's `headline` is bold). The ramp gains those columns.
4. **Unsaid font metrics are the platform's.** `line-height: normal` and `ui-monospace` on an
   Apple host are CoreText's. The two `QUEUE.md` entries that ask to match Chrome there are
   closed, and cross-host layout fixtures pin a face and a line height (D14).
5. **A clipped single line ends in an ellipsis** when `text-overflow` is unsaid, on every
   host; an authored `clip` wins.
6. `text aria-level=N` without `role="heading"` takes the heading's style.

[IOS-10, -14, -15; KRN-13, -26; DOC-23, -24]

### D13 — States the platform's control does not have

1. **An empty date** shows the author's `placeholder` in the platform's pill, else the
   system formatter's pattern in the person's locale. Never Chrome's English `mm/dd/yyyy`.
2. **A radio on iOS** is the SF Symbols pair at body size with a 44-point target, as the
   checkbox is; in a grouped row it is the row's checkmark accessory.
3. **Accessibility values are the platform's:** a checkbox reads as a toggle with UIKit's
   own value, spoken in the person's language. `tree --ax` joins on what the author wrote
   (D14).
4. An alert-shaped `alertdialog` may take `closedby="none"`, and the diagnostic stops saying
   a native confirmation "is dismissed by a tap outside it".

[IOS-08, -22, -25; MAC-27; KRN-17; DOC-36; tests IOS-T4, -T7]

### D14 — The oracles move with the rule

1. **A test that pins a retired default is rewritten in the commit that changes the default.**
   Appendix A names the seventeen: seven iOS XCTests, six macOS ones, three compiler and
   kernel tests, and one web test.
2. **Cross-host layout comparisons pin their inputs.** LLP 1115 §4 promised a pinned root
   size; it is passed when a host is opened for `conform.mjs --linux` and `parity.mjs`, with a
   pinned face and line height, so a fixture with one unsized text compares layout and not
   13 against 16.
3. **`tree --ax` compares what the author wrote** (`role`, `aria-*`, names), mapped through a
   per-platform table, in place of "disagreeing with the web". `rules/DEFERRED.md`'s sentence
   is reworded to match.
4. **`contract vocab` prints the platform's default** for a presentation row: "the platform's
   body size (17 iOS, 13 macOS, 16 web)" in place of "default 16".
5. **`no-tells` stays a lint of literal colour and type.** It gains two things: it follows a
   colour or size bound by name, as the compiler's warning does, and it runs over what `exact
   new` writes. It stops flagging `-exact-platform-color()`'s required fallback.
6. `exact new --game` makes `start-here.md` the first read, as the app template does.

### D15 — Two defaults that change every app (Charlie's word)

Both are in LLP 1115's "Later, needs an owner". Both are the largest remaining tells on iOS.
They are stated here with a proposed answer and are not in any wave.

1. **A bare `button` on iOS is plain tinted text**, as `UIButton(type: .system)` and SwiftUI's
   `Button` are. `bordered` is what the author asks for. Emphasis is a `buttonStyle` or an
   `exact:content` part (LLP 1116.003 D4), so this fits "design the content, leave the chrome".
2. **Rows of a `list` are cells:** a hairline separator inset to the text and the cell's
   highlight on press, in place of the dim. `appearance="none"` on the list opts out. The rule
   is the element, not a guess about which pressable looks like a row.
