## 0. Summary

LLP 1116.012 found the total-token gap to React is cached context, not fresh tokens, and tried taking fewer
steps: commands fell by a fifth and total tokens did not. This RFC measures what the cached context is.

A builder's every step re-sends everything before it. A command's cost in re-sent context is its bytes (the
command and its output) times the steps after it. Over rounds 13 and 14 (96 builds), steps and that measure
together predict a build's tokens: tokens ≈ a + b × steps + 0.24M × MB-steps (R² 0.60 for GPT-6.1 Sol, 0.92
for Muse), about four bytes a token.

| Per build, rounds 13–14 | Steps | Re-sent MB-steps | of which docs | Tokens | Fresh |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GPT, Exact2 | 39.7 | 3.17 | **2.03** | 1.64M | 91k |
| GPT, comparison | 29.6 | 1.72 | 0.05 | 1.23M | 96k |
| Muse, Exact2 | 57.8 | 4.34 | **3.64** | 3.67M | 184k |
| Muse, comparison | 63.5 | 1.54 | 0.37 | 3.41M | 147k |

Docs are about two thirds of what an Exact2 build re-sends; the comparison's builders re-send almost none
(Muse's 0.37 is its own skills, on both arms): the model already knows React and SwiftUI. By the fit,
re-sending docs costs a GPT build up to 0.50M tokens and a Muse build up to 0.84M (some of the GPT figure is
app source printed in the same command as a doc). Everything else an Exact2 build re-sends is already less
than the comparison's.

Where the docs bytes come from (round 14, GPT builds, per build):

| Read | Times | KB | MB-steps | Read at (0 first step, 1 last) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The card | 1.0 | 15.3 | 0.57 | 0.07 |
| start-here | 1.0 | 11.1 | 0.44 | 0.03 |
| Recipes | 1.6 | 13.7 | 0.44 | 0.16 |
| `contract vocab <name>` | 2.1 | 12.3 | 0.40 | 0.28 |
| Help text | 1.4 | 5.0 | 0.21 | 0.32 |

Muse reads files: the pattern's sources under `docs/patterns/` (9.7 KB a build), start-here (9 of 10
builds), recipes, `docs/card.md` in 4 of 10.

The early reads cost the most, because every later step re-sends them: start-here and the card, 22 KB at step
2 of 35, are re-sent 33 times. LLP 1116.011 D1 grew the card to cut lookups; by this measure it about broke
even.

## D1. A first read of at most 4 KB, and a card that is an index (stage 1)

What every build reads at its start is at most 4 KB in all.

- **start-here** is at most 4 KB: the idea (layout and names are CSS's; say what a thing is; leave colour and
  controls unsaid; the accent on the root), the composition (a row pushes its detail; create and edit are
  sheets; one column on a phone), the syntax that trips builders (indent, quoted units, `when` not `if`, no
  truthiness, a record copy), the three pitfalls, the commands, and where answers are. Its grocery walkthrough
  goes: the pattern the builder starts from is the worked example, and `pattern <name>` writes it into the
  app, where the builder reads it (not again under `docs/patterns/`).
- **`contract vocab` with no name** prints an index: every tag, part, function and test step by name, one line
  per group, and how to ask for one (`contract vocab <name> …`). `docs/card.md` is the same text. The how-to
  section moves behind names (`contract vocab sheet`, `… timer`, `… test`), so a builder pays for the one it
  needs, when it needs it.
- **`AGENTS.md`** stays a pointer: start-here, then a pattern.

## D2. Patterns: one copy, no repeated prose (stage 2)

A pattern keeps its routes, sheet, confirmation, kept value and tests: those are what a builder would
otherwise write, and builders who trimmed them lost (LLP 1116.011 D4). What goes is prose that repeats what
the code says, and the second copy: start-here sends a builder to the app's own files, which `pattern` wrote.

## D3. Lookups that end the question (stage 2)

An answer mid-build is re-sent for the rest of it, and a short answer that earns another lookup or a failed
build costs more. `contract vocab <name>` and a verb's `--help` keep the one example and the rule that prevent
the next command, and drop what repeats; a recipe opens with its snippet. A compile diagnostic names its fix
and the vocab line, so a miss costs one build, not a lookup and a build.

## D4. Judged by a graded, staged comparison

Each stage is compared with the Exact2 before it by `ab.py`, extended: eight of round 14's specs, four React
and four SwiftUI, both builders, each spec twice per arm, interleaved; each B build is graded against its A
build side by side by the round's grader. Reported per builder and platform: paired B ÷ A total tokens with
their spread, fresh tokens, minutes, the grader's preference and feature scores, and re-sent context by class
(`replay.py`, with the injected `AGENTS.md` and harness skills counted apart). A stage stays when total tokens
fall beyond the noise and the grader does not prefer A; the full round after confirms against React and
SwiftUI.

## Not in this RFC

The web's look (LLP 1095 D4, Charlie's decision). Screenshots a builder reads as images are carried too and
are not counted by bytes; the next measurement should count them.

## As built (2026-10-13): stage 1 measured, not adopted

Stage 1 is on branch `bake/r16-firstread` (0f8d3743d): start-here at 4,087 bytes (from 10,235), `contract vocab`
with no name an index of 2,842 bytes (from the 12,268-byte card), the card's how-tos answered by name
(`sheet`, `screen`, `confirm`, `timer`, `persist`, `dated`, `color`, `compose`, `data`, `field`, `test`).
D4's comparison (`~/bakeoff/ab_report.py ab2`): eight of round 14's specs, four web and four iPhone, both
builders, two builds a spec per arm, B graded against A side by side; one B build lost to Codex capacity.

| B ÷ A, paired by spec | Tokens | Fresh | Minutes | Commands |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All (8 specs) | 1.03 (0.96–1.11 at ±1 SE) | 1.05 | 1.08 | 1.06 |

The grader preferred B 7-4-4, with features 0.86 against 0.84 and bugs 3.7 against 3.9: level. The early
bytes went (GPT builds re-sent 0.56 MB-steps of the card and 0.34 of start-here in A, 0.05 and 0.16 in B), and
builders fetched the same knowledge by name: `contract vocab` commands rose from 2.1 to 4.6 a build, six names
each (`test`, `persist`, `color`, `data`, `compose`, `timer`, and the function signatures the index dropped),
re-sending 0.65 MB-steps where A's lookups re-sent 0.20. By D4's rule (total tokens fall beyond the noise) the
stage is not adopted, and stages 2 and 3 do not follow: builders need about the same knowledge however it is
packaged, and a smaller first read moves it into more steps. Repairing compile errors, the other place
knowledge is fetched, is 4% of tokens (LLP 1116.014).
