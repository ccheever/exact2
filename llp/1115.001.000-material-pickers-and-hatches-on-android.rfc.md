## 0. Summary

Compose Material 3 is the reference 1115.001 D1 names, and an Android app's "native widgets" are a library's either
way: the operating system ships no Material 3 control. So the components that are expensive to paint well are
shown by that library, as islands over the painted page, and the rest stay painted. A date or time control is the
first: its press opens Material 3's `DatePickerDialog` or `TimePickerDialog`, and its answer is the control's value.
Compose is linked only into an app whose plan has such a control, so an app without one pays nothing.

Access hatches run on the Canvas host for the first time: the app's Rust ones (`modules/linux`, stage 6's), and
Kotlin ones beside them (`modules/android`), which hear the same moments, act on nodes by number, reach a node's
platform view where it has one (a text field's `EditText`), and set what a date or time control's picker offers, or
replace it.

Material Components needs AppCompat's view inflater: its picker's layouts declare `<Button>` and `TextView` and are

`ColorStateList`'s magenta fallback and its header overlaps its title, until a delegate's view factory is installed
(`AppCompatDelegate.create(…).installViewFactory()`). It is no cheaper, its picker is slower in both compile states
(inflation, not code: its baseline profile is 1.6 KB), and it asks for AppCompat. Compose asks for a lifecycle owner.

## 2. Decisions

### D1 — A date or time control opens the platform's picker (amends 1115.001 D9)

A press on an `input type="date"`, `"time"` or `"datetime-local"` that is not disabled asks the reader for its
picker through 1115.001 D5's channel: request kind 9, with the node, its kind, what it shows now (the person's
choice while the bound value is the one it was made at, else the bound value), and its `min`, `max` and `step`, each
as HTML writes it. The reader's answer is the value chosen, in HTML's form (`2026-10-23`, `09:30`,
`2026-10-23T09:30`; seconds only where `step` asks for them), and it lands as a select's row does: `input`, then
`change`. A dismissal changes nothing and is journaled (`picker: dismissed for <node>`). `datetime-local` asks for
the day, then the time, as Android apps do.

What OK answers is held to HTML's rules for the kind: within `min` and `max` (a time's range may pass midnight,
`min` after `max`), on the `step` from its base (`any` for none; a step that is no number above zero is the kind's
default), and, for a day, one the hatch's `selectable` allows; OK is disabled otherwise, in both pickers. The value is
a normalized string in ASCII digits: seconds only when not zero, and a held value's seconds are kept where its hour
and minute are, since neither picker offers seconds. A read-only control opens no picker, nor does a control whose
picker is up. A picker belongs to its activity: a reader made again over the same activity leaves it up and its
answer lands; an activity that goes closes it, which answers null; a view a commit renews while its picker is up
(a list's row given another item) drops the answer.

The picker is Compose Material 3's (D2), themed by the device's dynamic colours: the reference D1 names. 1115.001 D9
chose the framework's `DatePickerDialog` as "a stated exception to D1.1's library", for its cost; the cost is now
measured (§1) and is small and off the launch path, so the exception is withdrawn. The framework's dialogs remain the
fallback where Compose is not linked.

Both pickers offer Clear while the control holds a value, as Chrome's on Android does; it answers the empty string.
The platform has no string for it and Material 3's are not public, so the reader carries "Clear" in the languages
Android ships (`host/android/canvas/src/main/res/values*/strings.xml`). Material's picker is in the control's
`accent-color` where it or an ancestor sets one, the author's word over the device's: the request carries it, and
the primary roles (`primary`, `onPrimary`, their containers, `inversePrimary`) are its tones, its hue and chroma at
each role's lightness (`ColorStateList.withLStar`), as a scheme made from a source colour has them. The framework's
dialogs keep the theme's.

Under the agent (LLP 1069.007 D4), on every host this presenter runs (Linux, Windows, Android), a press holds the
picker for the agent and opens none: `state` lists it under `pending`, its capability the control's kind, with its
value, `min`, `max` and `step`. `type @date 2026-10-23` chooses, `tap @date clear` empties, `tap @date cancel`
leaves it, and a value not of its kind is refused before the hold is spent. Without the agent a host with no shell
(desktop Linux) opens none; `type` sets a date field on every host.

### D2 — Compose is linked by use

`dates` joins the use set (LLP 1047 D2): a plan with a control whose `type` can be one of the three. The Android
crate's build writes the plan's uses beside it (`contract::native::android_entry`, `uses.txt`);
`agent-android.mjs` reads them from Cargo's build-script output and passes `-Pcompose=1` to the shell's Gradle,
which then applies the Compose compiler, links `androidx.compose.material3` (BOM 2026.09.00, Material 3 1.4.0) and
compiles `app/src/compose` in place of `app/src/nocompose`. Every other host links nothing for it: the web's are the
browser's, Apple's are UIKit's.

The reader's activity stays a plain `Activity`; each picker brings the lifecycle and saved-state owners Compose
looks for, on its own view, resumed while it is up and destroyed as it closes. A GPU app's or a game's bake reads
its uses from the build scripts' output the same way. Compose never runs before a press, so 1115.001 §5 Q3's rule (never on the launch path) holds by
construction.

### D3 — A picker is a hatch's to shape

A date or time control marked `hatch="word"` (a word `app.json` gives `android`) has its Kotlin hatch asked as its
picker opens: `ExactHatches.picker(element, picker)`. The hatch may set the title, open on typing rather than the
calendar or the clock (`input`), hide the mode toggle, refuse days beyond `min` and `max` (`selectable`), choose a
24-hour clock, or replace the picker with its own (`show`, called with what to answer). This is Android's form of
what an iOS hatch does to a `UIDatePicker`; the library's own object is not handed over, since a composable has no
object to hand, and the members are the ones Material's pickers take. The framework's dialogs, where Compose is not
linked, honour the title, `selectable` and the clock, not `input` or `modeToggle`.

### D4 — Kotlin hatches beside the Rust ones (answers 1075.003.000.001 §10 Q4 for the Canvas host)

An app's `modules/android` may hold Kotlin: one `class exact.app.Hatches : ExactHatches()`, compiled into the APK,
made by name the first time a moment is told (after first pixel), and called on the main thread. It hears the
moments a Rust hatch hears, for the same words (`app.json`'s for `android`): `app` (built, and changed when a root
`data-*` word changes), `element` (built, and changed when its `data-*` words or its box's size change),
`elementEnded`. A throw from any of them, or from the class's constructor, is logged and that call lost; the app
goes on. The reader is given each activity made again and lets go of one that ends. Exact sends them as request kind
10 after each turn, from a module beside the app's Rust one
(`hatches::reader`), so the moments, their order and `EXACT_HATCHES=off` are the presenter's, unchanged.

The handle (`ExactElement`) is a painting host's with what the reader has besides:

- `hatch`, `id`, `node`, `frame` (the document's points, as of the node's last moment: a move alone is not told),
  `words` and `data(word)`, `isNew`, `isLive`;
- `click()`, `focus()`, `blur()`, `input(text)`: each crosses to exact2's thread by the node's number and is queued
  and run as a Rust hatch's act is (§2.5 there). A handle whose node ended does nothing, even where a node made
  again has its number. Strings cross JNI as UTF-16;
- `view`: the node's platform view where it is one. Today that is a text field's `EditText` (1115.001 D8). Every
  other node is painted and has none;
- `host { context -> view }`: a view of the app's own in the node's border box, over exact2's paint. The reader
  makes it with the factory for each activity that shows it, so no view outlives its activity. The painter names it
  to the reader where the node is (`NativeKind::Hatch`), and the reader's layer of platform elements (`Natives`) lays
  it out at the box's size and records it into the element's node, so it scrolls, clips and stacks with the page as a
  `video` or a native module's view does; its row is never kept as a recording, and a view given or taken records
  every row again. It is drawn and not touched: exact2 keeps the input, which `observe` hears. Null takes it away,
  and so does the node's end. A node that is a platform element itself (a `video`, an `iframe`, a native view, a text
  field) shows its own;
- `diagnostics`: `log`, `count`, `measure`, `publish`, into the agent's `diagnostics` as a Rust hatch's are; a
  handle whose node ended reports nothing, and `observe` on it observes nothing.

The module (`ExactHatches`) has the clock and the input: `frames(tick)`, each frame exact2 presents with its time
and commit; `after(ms, fired)`; `observe(element, heard)`, a pointer's phase and points or a key, after exact2's own
dispatch, which it cannot claim. Each returns a ticket that `stop()` ends; an observer ends with its node, and every
ticket at a reload, and the reader is told so (scope 8) and drops what it would have called. The clock is exact2's,
so under the agent a hatch's motion follows the agent's clock as the app's does. Kotlin runs after the turn, or the
agent's command, that called it: a frame ticket is called once a turn with its latest frame, and a hatch cannot act
inside a `clock` advance that is still running, as a Rust hatch can. They are a Rust hatch's registrations made on the
reader's behalf (`hatches::reader::act`, whose table names each ask), and what they call back is told on the same
channel as the moments, scopes 5 (a tick), 6 (an `after`) and 7 (input).

`exact hatch <word>` writes `modules/android/Hatches.kt` for an app with an `android/` crate, a word's arm and
functions in it, and adds `android` to the word's platforms.

A Rust file in `modules/android` still overrides `modules/linux` for the Rust side; a directory of Kotlin alone no
longer does.

A Rust hatch's overlay, a canvas recording, has no Kotlin form: a hosted view is Android's way to draw over a
node.

### D5 — The Rust hatches run on the Canvas host

`exact_linux::canvas_jni!(Data, hatches = crate::exact_hatches())` hands the host the app's install, which
`android_entry` writes: the app's Rust hatches with the reader's told beside them, the reader's alone, or none.
They connect as the reader takes its requests after the first displayed frame (until now only a pending data module
did, so an app without one never connected them), run between turns (`hatch_turn` in the frame), and an overlay is
painted as the Canvas host paints a canvas: a picture. The host's clock is the presenter's with the hatches' in it: a
frame ticket asks for frames, an `after` is a deadline, and an act a moment queued asks for another turn.

### D6 — A field is Material's filled text field, and a date shows as the device writes it (builds 1115.001 D9)

On Android a text field, a select and a date or time control are Material 3's filled text field
(<https://m3.material.io/components/text-fields/specs>): a container 56 points tall in `surfaceContainerHighest`,
its top corners 4 points round, its text 16 points in on every side, and an active indicator along its foot, 1
point of `onSurfaceVariant` or, while focused, 2 points of the accent (`primary` unless the node sets
`accent-color`), in place of a focus ring. A field that opens something has a 24-point trailing icon 12 points from
its end, in `onSurfaceVariant`: a select Material's drop-down arrow, a date or `datetime-local` the calendar, a time
the clock. Disabled, the container is `onSurface` at 4 % and the text and indicator at 38 %. Its text is
`onSurface`: an inherited `color` does not tint it, the node's own does. These are the kernel's field chrome on this
host (`paint/material.rs`), so layout knows them; an authored size or padding still wins, and
`appearance="none"` is the author's box.

A field's `placeholder` is its label, as an `EditText`'s hint is in a `TextInputLayout`, and as Apple's date pill
shows it (LLP 1115.000 D13.1): on the middle line in the field's own size while the field is empty and unfocused, and
otherwise floated above its text in Material's body small (12 points on a 16-point line, 8 from the top), the text
below on a 24-point line, 8 above the foot. The kernel asks the chrome knowing whether the field has one
(`FieldChromeRequest::labelled`), so a labelled text field's `EditText` sits under it. The label moves in 150 ms,
at once under Reduce Motion; its colour is `onSurfaceVariant`, the accent while focused, an authored
`::placeholder` colour over either. A hosted field keeps the placeholder as its hint, drawn in no colour, so
TalkBack still names the field. The field's states are layers of `onSurface` over the container: 8 % where a pointer
hovers, 10 % while a field that opens something is pressed.

A date or time control shows its value as the device writes it: the locale's medium date and its clock, 12 or
24-hour as the person set it (`16 Oct 2026`, `09:30`; `Oct 16, 2026`, `9:30 AM` in the United States), and
`datetime-local` the two together as the locale joins them, which is how Material's picker writes its headline. The
reader tells the patterns with the device's settings (`Device.dates`): what `DateFormat.getBestDateTimePattern`
chooses for the skeletons `yMMMd`, `hm` or `Hm` (with `s`), and their joins, ICU's month and weekday names, short
and full (and a month's on its own, for `L`), its eras and day periods, and the zero digit, all of the Gregorian
calendar, which an HTML value is in, whatever the locale's own (a Thai phone's is Buddhist). The presenter fills them in (`paint/dates.rs`), so a change of locale or clock is a
change of the device's settings and re-lays the fields out. The value the app reads stays HTML's. An empty control
shows its label, or nothing, beside its icon, and a value that is not one of its kind shows as it is. The neutral look (desktop
Linux, Windows) is unchanged.

## 4. Not done

- **The press is a state layer, not a ripple.** Material's ripple spreads from the touch; the field shows the
  pressed layer at once. 1115.001 D9's press is owed for every control.
- **A hosted view takes no touches.** exact2 keeps the input that lands in the node's box, as it does for a native
  module's view on this host; `observe` hears it.
- **The framework's dialogs keep the theme's colours** where Compose is not linked, whatever `accent-color` says.
- **A floating label comes only from `placeholder`.** `aria-label` names a field for TalkBack and is not shown: a
  page that writes it beside a visible label would show the words twice.
- **Kotlin cannot act inside an agent's `clock` advance.** Its calls run after the command; a Rust hatch's run at
  each instant of it. Making the advance yield to the main thread is a JNI round trip per instant.
- **One phone.** A Pixel, and a cold start of an app whose first screen has a date control, were not measured.
