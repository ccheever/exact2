## Summary

The roster's device row (LLP 1133.000 §3.3): the battery, the network's
type and the person's data saving, the device's thermal state and its low
power mode. One package, `@exact/device`, answers three live facts:

```
use getBattery, connection, powerState, BatteryManager, NetworkInformation, PowerState from "@exact/device"

resource battery = getBattery() as shape BatteryManager else empty(level=-1)
resource network = connection() as shape NetworkInformation else empty(type="unknown")
resource power = powerState() as shape PowerState else empty()
```

The placeholders say "not known": a `level` of -1 is never an answer, and
`type`'s zero would otherwise be its first enumerator, `bluetooth`. A read
that fails keeps its placeholder (D14).

- **`getBattery()`** is the Battery Status API's `level` and `charging`
  ([W3C Battery Status](https://www.w3.org/TR/battery-status/)), on every
  target with an implementation. A device with no battery answers what the
  specification tells a browser to answer: a full battery, charging (D1).
- **`connection()`** is the Network Information API's `type` and the Save
  Data preference's `saveData`
  ([WICG Network Information](https://wicg.github.io/netinfo/),
  [Save Data](https://wicg.github.io/savedata/)). On Apple `saveData` is
  Low Data Mode, `NWPath.isConstrained`; on Android it is Data Saver (D2).
- **`powerState()`** is a declared deviation, since the web has no name for
  either fact: `lowPowerMode` (Low Power Mode, Battery Saver, Energy
  Saver) and `thermalState` in Apple's four words, which are also the W3C
  Compute Pressure states (D3).

None of the three asks permission or shows anything. They are facts, read
from any origin, announced when they change (D5, D7).

Two things the row lists exist already and stay where they are: whether the
device is online is `exactPage().onLine` (LLP 1069.000), and how capable
the GPU is, `exactViewport().gpuTier` (LLP 1139.003). The module answers
neither (D4). Device and app identity stay out: no app needs them and
`app.json` has no version to report (§11). The screen is LLP 1133.029's.

**Consumers.** Weatherlight does what Apple and Android ask an app to do in
Low Power Mode and under heat: its animated sky pauses while Low Power Mode
is on or the device is seriously hot, unless the person has pressed play,
and its ten-minute forecast refresh stretches to thirty minutes while Low
Power Mode or Low Data Mode is on. Expose, whose status bar draws a phone's
battery as a fixed mark, shows the device's real level, charge and Low
Power Mode's yellow (§10).

**Targets.** Web (Chromium for the battery and the connection; nowhere for
the power state), iOS, iPadOS, macOS, tvOS and Android, all three facts
each except tvOS's battery, which answers the no-battery values. Linux and
Windows have designed cells over the desktop builder LLP 1133.022 proposes;
until it lands they answer `NotSupportedError` (D14, §13 question 1).

## 0. What is new here

1. **A module that is all facts.** Every earlier module has a request or a
   permission. This one has neither: no ticket, no status source, no grant
   line (D7). Its absence is the read failing `NotSupportedError`, as
   `navigator.getBattery` missing is on the web, so `failed(battery)` is
   the feature test (D14); a consumer that draws a battery gives it a
   placeholder no answer can be, and draws a charge only once it is told
   one (§10).
2. **A fact in the web's own fallback.** The Battery Status API tells a
   browser that cannot report the battery to answer a full one, charging.
   Every target takes that rule, so a Mac mini, an Apple TV and the iOS
   simulator answer the same as Chrome on a desktop (D1).
3. **A person's data saving, one word across two platforms.** Apple's Low
   Data Mode and Android's Data Saver are each the person saying "use less
   data", which is what Save Data's `saveData` means (D2). Windows and
   Linux have no such setting, and answer false.
4. **A normal Android permission from an implementation.** Reading the
   default network needs `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE`, an install-time permission
   with no prompt; the implementation declares it in its manifest fragment
   (D8, D12).

## 2. Tier 1

### D1 — `getBattery()`: the Battery Status API's `level` and `charging`

```
shape BatteryManager
  charging: bool      // charging, full, or no battery to report
  level: number       // 0 to 1, in hundredths
```

The names are the specification's `BatteryManager` attributes. As the
roster says, tier 1 takes `level` and `charging`. `chargingTime` and
`dischargingTime` stay out (§11): iOS reports neither, and no consumer asks.

**No battery, or one the platform will not report.** The specification
says: "If the user agent is unable to report the battery status
information, the BatteryManager's internal slots will remain with their
default values, which emulate a fully charged and plugged in battery",
`charging` true and `level` 1.0
([§ The BatteryManager interface](https://www.w3.org/TR/battery-status/#the-batterymanager-interface)).
Every implementation follows it: a Mac with no internal battery (no
`kIOPSInternalBatteryType` source in `IOPSCopyPowerSourcesInfo`), an Apple
TV (`UIDevice`'s battery properties are `API_UNAVAILABLE(tvos)` in the
tvOS 27.0 SDK), the iOS simulator (`UIDeviceBatteryStateUnknown`, level -1,
LLP 1124 §6), and an Android device whose `BatteryManager.EXTRA_PRESENT`
is false. A consumer that draws a battery therefore draws a full one on
those, as a web page does in Chrome on a desktop.

**Precision.** The specification says a browser "SHOULD not expose
high precision readouts of battery status information as that can
introduce a new fingerprinting vector"
([§ Security and privacy considerations](https://www.w3.org/TR/battery-status/#security-and-privacy-considerations)).
Every implementation rounds `level` to hundredths, which is what Android
reports (`BATTERY_PROPERTY_CAPACITY`, a percentage) and what a status bar
shows.

**`charging`** is the specification's: "`false` if the battery is
discharging", and true otherwise, so a full battery on its charger, and one
on its charger that the system is holding at a limit, are `charging` true.
Every platform's mapping is therefore "not discharging". Per platform:

| Target | `level` | `charging` | Changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| web | `navigator.getBattery()`'s | the same | `levelchange`, `chargingchange` |
| iOS, iPadOS | [`UIDevice.batteryLevel`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uidevice/batterylevel), with [`isBatteryMonitoringEnabled`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uidevice/isbatterymonitoringenabled) set | `batteryState` is not `.unplugged` | [`batteryLevelDidChangeNotification`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uidevice/batteryleveldidchangenotification), which UIKit posts at most once a minute, and `batteryStateDidChangeNotification` |
| macOS | the internal battery's `kIOPSCurrentCapacityKey` over `kIOPSMaxCapacityKey` from [`IOPSCopyPowerSourcesInfo`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/iokit/1523839-iopscopypowersourcesinfo) | its `kIOPSPowerSourceStateKey` is `kIOPSACPowerValue` | `IOPSNotificationCreateRunLoopSource` on the main run loop |
| tvOS | 1 | true | none |
| Android | [`BatteryManager.BATTERY_PROPERTY_CAPACITY`](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/os/BatteryManager#BATTERY_PROPERTY_CAPACITY) | `EXTRA_STATUS` is not `BATTERY_STATUS_DISCHARGING` (so `NOT_CHARGING` and `UNKNOWN` are true) | the sticky `ACTION_BATTERY_CHANGED`, registered on the application context ([Monitor the battery level](https://developer.android.com/training/monitoring-device-state/battery-monitoring)) |
| Linux | UPower's display device ([`org.freedesktop.UPower`](https://upower.freedesktop.org/docs/), `GetDisplayDevice`: `Percentage`; `IsPresent` false emulates); without UPower, the system battery in `/sys/class/power_supply/`: a supply of `type` `Battery` whose `scope` is not `Device` (a mouse's or a headset's battery is `Device`), its `capacity` ([sysfs power supply ABI](https://www.kernel.org/doc/Documentation/ABI/testing/sysfs-class-power)) | UPower's `State` is `Charging`, `FullyCharged`, `PendingCharge` or `Unknown` (`Discharging`, `Empty` and `PendingDischarge` are false: a depleted battery, and one discharging down to a charge limit while plugged in); sysfs's `status` is not `Discharging` | UPower's `PropertiesChanged`; sysfs, D5 |
| Windows | [`Windows.System.Power.PowerManager`](https://learn.microsoft.com/uwp/api/windows.system.power.powermanager)'s `RemainingChargePercent`; `BatteryStatus` `NotPresent` emulates | `BatteryStatus` is not `Discharging` | `RemainingChargePercentChanged`, `BatteryStatusChanged` |

`UIDevice.isBatteryMonitoringEnabled` is the process's, not the module's:
the module sets it at its first read and never clears it, since other code
in the process (the app's own Swift) may rely on it, and sets it again at
each `foreground` in case that code cleared it.

**Errors.** Where `navigator.getBattery` does not exist (Safari, Firefox,
[MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Navigator/getBattery))
the read fails `NotSupportedError`; where the page's Permissions Policy
does not allow `battery`, the browser's `NotAllowedError` passes through.
exact2's web host lists only its grant rows' features in its
`Permissions-Policy` header, so `battery` keeps its default allowlist,
`'self'`, and a top-level page may read it.

### D2 — `connection()`: the network's `type` and the person's `saveData`

```
shape NetworkInformation
  type: enum<bluetooth|cellular|ethernet|mixed|none|other|unknown|wifi|wimax>
  saveData: bool
```

`type` is the Network Information API's `ConnectionType`, and `saveData`
is the attribute Save Data adds to `NetworkInformation`, true when the
person has asked the browser to use less data
([Save Data](https://wicg.github.io/savedata/)). The roster's tier 1 is
these two. `effectiveType`, `downlink` and `rtt` stay out (§11).

**`type`** is the interface of the path the system routes the app's
traffic over:

| Target | Read | Mapping |
|---|---|---|
| web | `navigator.connection.type` | as given; `unknown` where the browser gives none ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/NetworkInformation/type)). Stage 1 records what Chrome gives on a desktop |
| Apple | the first of [`NWPath.availableInterfaces`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/network/nwpath/availableinterfaces) that is not a tunnel, from one [`NWPathMonitor`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/network/nwpathmonitor); stage 2 records the order on a Mac with Wi-Fi and Ethernet both | `.wifi` wifi, `.cellular` cellular, `.wiredEthernet` ethernet, `.other` and `.loopback` other; `status` not `.satisfied`, none |
| Android | the default network's [`NetworkCapabilities`](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/net/NetworkCapabilities) from `registerDefaultNetworkCallback` | `TRANSPORT_WIFI` wifi, `TRANSPORT_CELLULAR` cellular, `TRANSPORT_ETHERNET` ethernet, `TRANSPORT_BLUETOOTH` bluetooth, more than one of these mixed, any other transport (USB, Thread, satellite) other; `TRANSPORT_VPN` is skipped when another transport is named beside it; no default network, none |
| Linux | the interface of the route the kernel would choose for a global destination in each family, asked over rtnetlink (`RTM_GETROUTE`, as `ip route get` asks), so policy rules and tables beyond `main` (WireGuard's `wg-quick` table) apply; the IPv4 answer when both exist; then its `/sys/class/net/<if>/` | a tunnel first (`tun_flags` present, or `DEVTYPE=wireguard`), whose underlying link the kernel does not name, other; a `phy80211` link (cfg80211's), wifi; `DEVTYPE=wwan` in its `uevent`, cellular; else type 1 (`ARPHRD_ETHER`), ethernet; else other; no route in either family, none |
| Windows | [`NetworkInformation.GetInternetConnectionProfile()`](https://learn.microsoft.com/uwp/api/windows.networking.connectivity.networkinformation.getinternetconnectionprofile) | `IsWlanConnectionProfile` wifi, `IsWwanConnectionProfile` cellular, `NetworkAdapter.IanaInterfaceType` 6 ethernet, else other; no profile, none |

The rule on every target: `type` is the physical link the traffic leaves
by where the platform names it, and a tunnel whose link it does not name
is other. So a VPN over Wi-Fi is wifi on Apple, whose path lists the Wi-Fi
interface beside the tunnel, and other on Linux and on Android when its
VPN network carries `TRANSPORT_VPN` alone. Until the platform first
answers, `type` is `unknown`.

**`saveData`** is the person's own setting to use less data, on the network
the app is using:

| Target | Read | Source |
|---|---|---|
| web | `navigator.connection.saveData` | the browser's |
| Apple | [`NWPath.isConstrained`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/network/nwpath/isconstrained) | Low Data Mode, which a person turns on per Wi-Fi network or for cellular data: a constrained interface is the network "when the user has specified Low Data Mode" (`allowsConstrainedNetworkAccess`, `NSURLSession.h`, iOS 27.1 SDK) |
| Android | [`ConnectivityManager.getRestrictBackgroundStatus()`](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/net/ConnectivityManager#getRestrictBackgroundStatus()) is `RESTRICT_BACKGROUND_STATUS_ENABLED` or `…_WHITELISTED`, and the default network lacks `NET_CAPABILITY_NOT_METERED` | Data Saver: "When a user enables Data Saver in Settings and the device is on a metered network, the system blocks background data usage and signals apps to use less data in the foreground wherever possible" ([Data Saver](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/network-ops/data-saver)) |
| Linux | false | no desktop has such a setting |
| Windows | false | Windows has no setting asking apps to use less data in the foreground; `ConnectionCost.BackgroundDataUsageRestricted` limits background transfers (roaming, over a data limit, a metered policy) and is tier 2 (D9) |

Android's allowlisted status counts: "The user has enabled Data Saver but
the app is allowed to bypass it. Apps should still make an effort to limit
foreground and background data usage" (the same page).

Whether a network is metered is not tier 1. The web has no word for it,
each platform's word means something slightly different (Apple's
expensive interface is one "the system considers expensive", in practice
cellular or a Personal Hotspot; Android's metered is the carrier's or the
person's flag; Windows has three costs), and no consumer
needs it. Each is tier 2 (D9). The roster's "expensive and constrained
paths": the constrained path is `saveData`, and the expensive one is
`apple.expensive`.

**Errors.** Where `navigator.connection` does not exist (Safari, Firefox)
the read fails `NotSupportedError`.

### D3 — `powerState()`: low power mode and the thermal state, a declared deviation

```
shape PowerState
  lowPowerMode: bool
  thermalState: option<enum<nominal|fair|serious|critical>>
```

No web specification has either. The words are Apple's
[`ProcessInfo.ThermalState`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/processinfo/thermalstate-swift.enum),
which are also W3C Compute Pressure's four `PressureState` values
([Compute Pressure](https://www.w3.org/TR/compute-pressure/)). Compute
Pressure is not the source on the web: its one source is `"cpu"`, a
measure of the CPU's load, which a busy page raises on a cool machine, so
it answers a different question. A web page has no low power fact at all.
So the web is absent (D14).

**`lowPowerMode`** is the platform's battery-saving mode, the one setting
a person turns on to make the battery last:

| Target | Read | Changes |
|---|---|---|
| iOS, iPadOS, macOS 12 and later, tvOS | [`ProcessInfo.isLowPowerModeEnabled`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/processinfo/islowpowermodeenabled) | `NSProcessInfoPowerStateDidChange` |
| Android | [`PowerManager.isPowerSaveMode()`](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/os/PowerManager#isPowerSaveMode()), Battery Saver | `ACTION_POWER_SAVE_MODE_CHANGED`, which is "only sent to registered receivers" |
| Linux | `power-saver-enabled` of the [Power Profile Monitor portal](https://flatpak.github.io/xdg-desktop-portal/docs/doc-org.freedesktop.portal.PowerProfileMonitor.html); without the portal, power-profiles-daemon's `ActiveProfile` is `power-saver` (`org.freedesktop.UPower.PowerProfiles` from its 0.20, `net.hadess.PowerProfiles` before; stage 4 records which a machine has); without either, false. The firmware's `platform_profile` is not this fact: it is a performance profile, not the person's Power Saver | the property's change |
| Windows | where Windows has Energy Saver (Windows 11 24H2 and later), `GUID_ENERGY_SAVER_STATUS` ([Power setting GUIDs](https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/power/power-setting-guids)) is not off, since [Energy Saver](https://learn.microsoft.com/windows-hardware/design/component-guidelines/energy-saver) may stay on while plugged in; before it, [`PowerManager.EnergySaverStatus`](https://learn.microsoft.com/uwp/api/windows.system.power.powermanager.energysaverstatus) is `On` (battery saver, which is off on AC) | `PowerSettingRegisterNotification` with a callback (`DEVICE_NOTIFY_CALLBACK`, no window); `EnergySaverStatusChanged` before it |

Apple TV has no Low Power Mode setting; tvOS's `isLowPowerModeEnabled`
reads false, and the module answers it.

**`thermalState`**: how hot the device is, in the platform's own judgement.
Apple's guidance for each, from `NSProcessInfo.h` (macOS 27.0 SDK):

| Word | Apple | Android's [`getCurrentThermalStatus()`](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/os/PowerManager#getCurrentThermalStatus()) (API 29) |
|---|---|---|
| `nominal` | "within normal limits" | `THERMAL_STATUS_NONE`, "Not under throttling" |
| `fair` | "Reduce or defer background work, like prefetching content over the network or updating database indexes" | `LIGHT`, "UX is not impacted"; `MODERATE`, "UX is not largely impacted" |
| `serious` | "Reduce CPU and GPU usage, reduce the target framerate, and reduce the level of detail in rendered content" | `SEVERE`, "UX is largely impacted" |
| `critical` | "Reduce usage of the CPU, GPU, and I/O to the minimum level required for user interaction" | `CRITICAL`, `EMERGENCY`, `SHUTDOWN` |

Android's seven levels fold into four by where the user experience starts
to suffer, which is where Apple's `serious` starts; the seven are tier 2
(D9). Apple reads [`ProcessInfo.thermalState`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/processinfo/thermalstate-swift.property)
and observes `thermalStateDidChangeNotification`, which "is posted on the
global dispatch queue", and "to receive this notification, you must access
the `thermalState` property prior to registering", so the module reads it
first.
Android registers `addThermalStatusListener`.

Linux and Windows answer `none`: neither gives an app a thermal state
(the kernel's thermal zones are temperatures with platform-specific trip
points, and Windows' power management functions and `Windows.System.Power`
have no thermal member).

### D4 — One fact, one place

- **Online** is `exactPage().onLine`, a page fact every host gives (LLP
  1069.000). `connection().type` answers `none` when the platform has no
  path, as the Network Information API's does, and the two read the
  platform separately: on Apple the host's `NWPathMonitor` and the
  module's are two observers of one path, as `navigator.onLine` and
  `navigator.connection` are two in a browser, and may differ for the
  moment between their callbacks. An app asks `exactPage().onLine`
  whether it is online, and `connection()` what kind of network it has;
  the recipe says so.
- **GPU capability** is `exactViewport().gpuTier`.
- **Low Power Mode as a reason background refresh is off** stays
  `backgroundSyncStatus().apple.lowPowerMode` (LLP 1133.017): it describes
  that status, as `backgroundRefreshStatus` beside it does, and an app that
  shows why its refresh is paused reads one record. It is an iOS snapshot
  taken when that status is read, with no observer of its own
  (`packages/background/apple/Background.swift`, `status()`); the general
  fact, observed and on every Apple target, is `powerState().lowPowerMode`.
- **The screen**, its brightness and keeping it awake, is LLP 1133.029's.

### D5 — Live facts, announced when they change

Each source is `live`, and announces the topic the runner already
observes for a module's source, `extension.source`
(`runner/src/runner/modules.rs`, `module_answer`): `device.getBattery`,
`device.connection`, `device.powerState`. No `topic` is declared. An
implementation:

1. **keeps one observer per source per process,** shared by the module's
   instances in every session (the sample host runs two), each instance
   announcing to its own session's context;
2. **counts readers across sessions:** the first read in any session
   starts the observer; `release(name)`, which the runner sends when a
   session has no reader of the source left (the three take no arguments,
   so `release(name, args)` never comes), or that session's `destroy`,
   removes only that session's readers; the observer stops when no session
   has one;
3. **announces only when an answered field changes,** tier 2 included.
   Android's `ACTION_BATTERY_CHANGED` arrives for voltage and temperature
   changes too; a broadcast that leaves the hundredths of `level` and the
   rest unchanged announces nothing;
4. **reads everything again at `foreground`,** announcing what changed,
   since an iOS app suspended in the background receives none of these
   notifications, and the battery may have charged meanwhile.

On Linux each fact has its own change source, independent of the others:
the network's route by an rtnetlink subscription to route and link
changes (`RTNLGRP_IPV4_ROUTE`, `RTNLGRP_IPV6_ROUTE`, `RTNLGRP_LINK`, and
`RTNLGRP_IPV4_RULE` and `RTNLGRP_IPV6_RULE`, since a policy rule moves the
route `RTM_GETROUTE` answers),
which needs no privilege, with the Network Monitor portal's `changed`
signal for its tier 2 words; the battery by UPower's `PropertiesChanged`,
and without UPower by reading the supply files once a minute while a
reader exists, outside the runner's timer wheel, as polled live sources
are (LLP 1133.009 D10 item 7); low power by the portal's or the daemon's
property change.

### D6 — Unsaid defaults are the platform's

The module shows nothing, so it has no presentation to leave to the
platform. What the platforms ask of an app is behaviour, and that stays the
app's: Apple asks an app in Low Power Mode to "reduce the use of
animations, lower frame rates, stop location updates, disable syncs and
backups" ([Energy Efficiency Guide for iOS Apps: React to Low Power Mode](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/Performance/Conceptual/EnergyGuide-iOS/LowPowerMode.html)),
and Android's `isPowerSaveMode()` says "applications should reduce their
functionality in order to conserve battery as much as possible". The
module reports the facts; the recipe (`docs/recipes/device.md`, stage 0)
quotes the guidance, and Weatherlight follows it (§10). The runner does not
throttle an app on its own, since what to give up is the app's choice.

## 3. Permission

### D7 — None, from any origin

No source asks the person anything or starts anything the person can
perceive: no sensor, radio, indicator or prompt. So the module has no
permission, no ticket and no status source, and its grant table row is
none (`runner/src/device.rs` gains nothing).

**Origins.** Every source may be read from any origin: a person's action,
a tool invocation (LLP 1111) and a wake (LLP 1133.017). LLP 1133.002 D12's
rule that tools and wakes cannot start a device concerns a capture or a
prompt, and a read here starts neither. A wake is where these facts matter
most: a sync that defers a large upload while `saveData` or `lowPowerMode`
holds is what Apple's "disable syncs and backups" asks for.

**What the facts reveal.** Apple's required-reason API categories (file
timestamps, system boot time, disk space, active keyboards, user defaults)
include none of them ([Describing use of required reason API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/describing-use-of-required-reason-api)),
so the app's privacy manifest gains nothing. The one known concern is the
web's: a precise battery reading helps fingerprint a device, which is why
the level is rounded on every target (D1). A tool's result may carry
them.

### D8 — Android: `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE`, a normal permission

`registerDefaultNetworkCallback` and `getNetworkCapabilities` require
[`ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE`](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/Manifest.permission#ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE)
(`@RequiresPermission` in the API 37.2 SDK's annotations), a normal
permission, which the system grants at install with no prompt
([Permissions on Android](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/permissions/overview#normal)).
The host's base manifest declares only `INTERNET`. The Android
implementation declares it in its manifest fragment (D12 item 1), so the
app's manifest has it exactly when the app uses the package. The battery
and power reads need no permission.

The web needs a secure context for `getBattery()` (`[SecureContext]`), which
every exact2 web origin is. Apple needs no Info.plist key.

### D10 — Access hatches: none

Every object the module reads is the process's: `ProcessInfo.processInfo`,
`UIDevice.current`, the IOKit power sources, `PowerManager` and
`ConnectivityManager` from `getSystemService`. An app's own Swift or Kotlin
reaches each of them directly, and its own `NWPathMonitor` sees the same
path as the module's. The module owns nothing an app would need handed
over, so it has no hatch. The one shared setting is
`isBatteryMonitoringEnabled` (D1).

| Use of the platforms' APIs | Tier |

| time to full and to empty | out (§11) |
| link quality and ultra-constrained paths (iOS 26) | out (§11) |
| battery health, cycle count, temperature, voltage | out (§11) |

## 5. Packaging and callers

### D11 — One package, an extension

```
packages/device/   "@exact/device"; exact.json, web/, apple/, android/, linux/, windows/, test/
```

An extension (LLP 1133 D5). It is not exactPage's kind of vocabulary, "a
fact every host gives": no web browser gives the power state and only
Chromium gives the other two, tvOS has no battery, and the macOS
implementation links IOKit, which an app that does not use the module
should not load (LLP 1047.001). One package, not three: the three are one
row of the roster and one recipe, their implementations share one observer
hub, and splitting them would save an app nothing, since the frameworks are
the system's and the module is small.

The interface, abridged:

```json
{
  "kind": "interface", "name": "@exact/device", "version": "1.0.0",
  "shapes": {
    "BatteryManager": { "charging": "bool", "level": "number",
      "apple": "option<AppleBattery>", "android": "option<AndroidBattery>" },
    "NetworkInformation": {
      "type": "enum<bluetooth|cellular|ethernet|mixed|none|other|unknown|wifi|wimax>",
      "saveData": "bool",
      "apple": "option<AppleNetwork>", "android": "option<AndroidNetwork>",
      "linux": "option<LinuxNetwork>", "windows": "option<WindowsNetwork>" },
    "PowerState": { "lowPowerMode": "bool",
      "thermalState": "option<enum<nominal|fair|serious|critical>>",
      "android": "option<AndroidPower>" }
  },
  "names": [
    { "source": "getBattery", "live": true, "answers": "BatteryManager",
      "substitute": { "default": { "charging": true, "level": 1 }, "partial": true,
        "unsupported": ["linux", "windows"] } },
    { "source": "connection", "live": true, "answers": "NetworkInformation",
      "substitute": { "default": { "type": "unknown", "saveData": false }, "partial": true,
        "unsupported": ["linux", "windows"] } },
    { "source": "powerState", "live": true, "answers": "PowerState",
      "substitute": { "default": { "lowPowerMode": false, "thermalState": "nominal" },
        "partial": true, "unsupported": ["web", "linux", "windows"] } }
  ]
}
```

No name declares a permission, a grant or an origin restriction (D7).
`"linux"` and `"windows"` leave each `unsupported` list when that cell lands
(stage 4), and `powerState` then gains `"targets": {"linux":
{"thermalState": null}, "windows": {"thermalState": null}}`, as production
answers there. The Apple implementation targets `ios`, `macos` and `tvos`;
the Android one declares its manifest fragment and no names; neither adds
a Maven library, so the locked Gradle graph is unchanged.

### D12 — Seam additions

1. **`<uses-permission>` in an Android implementation's manifest fragment,**
   for a permission whose protection level is `normal`. LLP 1133.026 D19
   item 3 proposes the fragment for activities, aliases and `<queries>`; this
   adds the one element. The bake refuses any other protection level there,
   since a runtime permission comes from a grant line, which derives its
   prompt's history and policy (LLP 1069.008). The bake's list of normal
   permissions starts with `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE`. If this module is built
   before LLP 1133.026, it builds the fragment with this element alone.
2. **A Rust host's package implementation** is LLP 1133.022 D10 item 5's
   (the `rust-static` builder, the trait, and the desktop loops' dispatch)
   and, on Windows, LLP 1133.025's extension slot. This RFC adds nothing to
   them; its Linux and Windows cells wait for them (D14).

Each is apparatus under `rules/RULES.md` and lands with this ruling.

### D13 — Callers

Contract reads the three sources. Data code calls them through the
generated bindings in Promise form (LLP 1133 D6), which a wake's data code
uses to decide whether to defer work (D7). No name is a request. No name is
`sync`: each platform answers synchronously, but no caller needs a
synchronous read, and the Promise form serves the one data code use.

## 10. The consumers

Two apps in the repository, each gaining what a hand-built app of its kind
does. Together they read all three sources.

### Weatherlight: a sky that rests, and a refresh that waits

Weatherlight (`apps/weatherlight`: web, iOS, macOS, Android and Linux,
and a tvOS build that `apps/weatherlight/apple/build.rs` provides for) draws
an animated sky on the GPU and refreshes its forecast every ten minutes.
It is the app Apple's Low Power Mode guidance describes: continuous
animation and periodic network work.

1. **The sky pauses** while `lowPowerMode` holds or `thermalState` is
   `serious` or `critical`, Apple's "reduce the target framerate" state.
   The pause button keeps working: what the person presses wins over the
   device. `state animated = true` becomes the person's choice, with the
   device's facts beneath it:

   ```
   use powerState, connection, PowerState, NetworkInformation from "@exact/device"

   state skyChoice = ""     // "", "play" or "pause": the person's, once pressed
   resource power = powerState() as shape PowerState else empty()
   resource network = connection() as shape NetworkInformation else empty(type="unknown")
   derive hot = match power.thermalState { case some(t) => t == "serious" or t == "critical", case none => false }
   derive resting = power.lowPowerMode or hot
   derive animated = skyChoice == "play" or (skyChoice == "" and not resting)
   derive saving = power.lowPowerMode or network.saveData

   action toggleMotion
     skyChoice = animated ? "pause" : "play"
   ```

   The button's label stays "Pause sky animation" or "Play sky animation"
   by `animated`, so a paused sky offers play.
2. **The refresh waits:** after the first forecast, `tick` refreshes
   after thirty minutes instead of ten while `saving`, Apple's "disable
   syncs" and Android's "reduce their functionality", and the person's Low
   Data Mode or Data Saver. The first forecast is fetched at the first tick
   whatever the device says (`revision` 0 answers the empty forecast,
   `apps/weatherlight/app.ts`), and Reload (`reloadWeather`) still fetches
   at once.

   ```
   action tick
     let now = performanceNow()
     if revision == 0 or now - refreshedAt >= (saving ? 1800000 : 600000)
       revision = revision + 1
       refreshedAt = now
   ```

Where a source fails (`powerState` on the web, all three on Linux before
stage 4) its resource keeps the `empty()` placeholder, whose `lowPowerMode`
and `saveData` are false and `thermalState` none, so Weatherlight animates
and refreshes as it does today.

**Drives** (each with `bun scripts/agent.mjs <host> --app weatherlight`):

1. Nothing preferred: `tree` shows `pause-sky` labelled "Pause sky
   animation".
2. `prefer extension device.powerState={"lowPowerMode":true}`: the label is
   "Play sky animation", and `screenshot` over 600 every 100 shows a still
   sky. `tap pause-sky`: it animates. `prefer extension
   device.powerState={"lowPowerMode":false}`, then `tap pause-sky`: it
   pauses and stays paused; the person's choice holds.
3. `prefer extension device.powerState={"thermalState":"serious"}`: paused;
   `={"thermalState":"fair"}`: animating;
   `={"lowPowerMode":true,"thermalState":"fair"}`: paused.
4. `clock +600000`, `state`: `revision` one higher, a refresh. `prefer
   extension device.connection={"saveData":true}`, `clock +600000`: no
   change; `clock +1200000`: one higher. `tap refresh-weather`: one higher
   at once.
4a. `prefer extension device.connection={"saveData":true}` before the
   first `clock` step, then `clock +1000`: `revision` 1, the first forecast
   asked at once.
5. On the web, `state.extensions` shows `powerState` failed
   `NotSupportedError`, and drives 1 and 4 hold without the low power
   overlay. On Linux before stage 4 every source fails, and `prefer` does
   not reach a failed read, so only drive 1 and drive 4's first step (a
   ten-minute refresh) run there.

### Expose: the battery a phone shows

Expose (`apps/expose`: web, iOS, macOS, Linux) is a phone operating
system's face, and its README lists "what is real": the clock and the
weather. Its status bar draws the battery as a fixed full mark and its
Settings show "Battery 94%". The battery becomes real, from `getBattery()`
and `powerState()` read as in the summary:

1. **The mark's fill** is `level` of its width (`17.5 * battery.level`,
   rounded to a half point).
2. **A bolt** shows inside the mark while `charging`, as the iPhone's
   status bar does.
3. **Low Power Mode's yellow:** while `lowPowerMode` holds and the level
   is known the fill is yellow, as on an iPhone, where "the battery icon in the status bar turns
   yellow" ([About Low Power Mode on your iPhone](https://support.apple.com/en-us/101604)).
4. **Settings' Battery row** shows the level as a whole percentage.
5. Before the first answer, and where `getBattery` fails (Linux before
   stage 4), the resource holds its placeholder, `level` -1, so Expose
   draws what it draws today: the mark full, no bolt, no yellow, and the
   row with no value. README's list gains "the battery".

   ```
   derive known = battery.level >= 0
   derive fill = known ? round(battery.level * 35) / 2 : 17.5
   StatusBar(time=clock.time, tone="statusInk", wifi=wifi, airplane=airplane,
     fill=fill, charging=known and battery.charging, saving=known and power.lowPowerMode, press=lock)
   ```

`StatusBar` gains three props (`fill`, `charging`, `saving`); the screens
that use it pass them.

**Drives** (each with `bun scripts/agent.mjs <host> --app expose`):

6. Nothing preferred: the full mark with a bolt; Settings shows "100%".
7. `prefer extension device.getBattery={"level":0.42,"charging":false}`:
   Settings shows "42%"; a `screenshot` cropped to the mark shows a fill
   under half its width and no bolt. `clock +60000` and `prefer` again at
   0.41: "41%", the earlier answer replaced.
8. `prefer extension device.powerState={"lowPowerMode":true}`: the fill's
   colour is the yellow (`screenshot` cropped to the mark).
9. On Linux before stage 4: the full mark, the row blank, and `logs` shows
   no error the app did not handle.

## 11. What stays out
