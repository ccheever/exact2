## 1. What this is

Android TV is the Android phone host on a television: the same Canvas reader, the same APK,
the same presenter. It follows tvOS (LLP 1008 §9) where the two can agree:

- The host reports `pointer: none` and `hover: none`. Apps style themselves for a TV from that
  fact (`derive tv = viewport.pointer == "none"`, as Caltrain does), with no shared TV styling.
- Select presses the focused node, and Back goes back.
- The remote sends no pointer events.

They differ in one place. tvOS uses UIKit's focus engine, because each node is a `UIView`.
On Android the page is one `ExactView` that replays a drawing, so Android's focus search
cannot see its nodes. The presenter moves the focus itself, by the rules Android's
`FocusFinder` uses (D4). Focus movement is the platform's (LLP 1115, "What the platform
decides"), so it follows Android's rules, not a web or CSS model.

## 2. Decisions

### D1. One APK for phones and TVs

The phone APK also runs on a TV. The host reads at run time whether the device is a TV. There
is no TV build flag and no second artifact. This is how Android's documentation asks a TV app
to ship ("Declare a TV activity" and "Declare hardware features" in the Android Developers TV
guide).

### D2. A TV is `pointer: none` and `hover: none`

The reader reads `UiModeManager.getCurrentModeType() == UI_MODE_TYPE_TELEVISION` and tells it
with the other device settings (`Native.configure`, LLP 1115.001 D3.1). The presenter maps a
TV to `pointer: none` and `hover: none` before the touch rule. A phone stays `coarse` and
`none` (D3.2).

A TV image has no dynamic colour: its colour roles have resource ids and no colours behind
them. There the reader tells Material's baseline scheme, as it does before Android 12
(LLP 1115.001 D4).

### D3. The remote's keys

- The D-pad arrows reach `key` handlers by their web names: `ArrowUp`, `ArrowDown`,
  `ArrowLeft` and `ArrowRight`. They enter through the presenter's named-key path
  (`hardware_key`, the Linux display's), not a second path. On a phone the same key codes,
  which a hardware keyboard's arrows send, keep Android's own handling.
- The default action of an arrow on a TV moves the focus (D4). It runs only when no handler
  called `preventDefault()` and the focus is not a text field or a grouped grip, which keep
  their arrows. In a radio group the arrows move the focus too, so it can leave the group;
  Select checks a radio.
- DPAD_CENTER and BUTTON_SELECT are Select. A `key` handler hears it as `Enter`, which is what
  Chrome on Android sends for DPAD_CENTER. Its default action presses the focused node, as
  tvOS's Select does: it chooses a bar's target, toggles a checkbox or a switch, checks a radio
  and presses anything else. A held Select presses once, as a held Enter presses a button
  once, in a menu or a dialog too; a held arrow keeps moving the focus. A menu or dialog opens
  with its first row focused, as Android's dialogs take the focus, so Select chooses it.
- KEYCODE_BACK keeps the system Back order of LLP 1115.001 D6, with one step added on a TV: with
  no route to go back from, it presses the first shown, enabled button that answers Escape
  (`aria-keyshortcuts="Escape"`) as a page shortcut finds and presses it, as tvOS's Menu does;
  a bar's target is shown. A remote has no Escape key, so its Back is one. With
  neither, the activity finishes, as tvOS's Menu leaves the app.

### D4. Which node the focus moves to

A node can take the focus from the remote if it is a Tab stop (LLP 1088 D7.3: focusable,
`tabindex` ≥ 0 or none, shown, not inert, with a box), except that every enabled radio of a
group is one: Tab stops once in a group, and the remote must reach each option. A pressable
is one already. The candidates are those nodes, in view or scrolled out of it, and the
targets of the shell's bars (LLP 1115.001 D11): each enabled header button and destination,
by its box in the window. The Up arrow is no candidate; Back does what it does. A bar's
target that has the focus is lit with its bar's state layer, as a held one is, and keeps the
focus through a commit, though its node is inert under the route the bar lifted it from. It is
the shell's control: the remote's keys there are the remote's alone, and its node's `key`
handlers do not hear them.

A container that holds a candidate is no candidate. A node with a `key` handler is focusable
(LLP 1088 D7.3), so a column that listens for keys would otherwise take the focus, and no
control in it lies beyond its edges in any direction. A control that holds another, such as a
pressable row with a switch in it, stays one; the control inside it is not reached yet (§4).

The next node is the one Android's `FocusFinder` would choose
(`frameworks/base/core/java/android/view/FocusFinder.java`, `isBetterCandidate`):

1. A candidate lies in the direction of the move from the focused node's box.
2. A candidate in the focused node's beam (overlapping it across the direction of the move)
   beats one outside it. Moving left or right it always does; moving up or down, only when it
   is nearer than the far edge of the one outside.
3. Otherwise the candidate with the smallest `13 × major² + minor²` wins: `major` is the
   distance along the direction, and `minor` is the distance between the centres across it.

With no focus, an arrow focuses the first candidate in Tab order, else the bars' first target.
From a focused container that holds candidates (a column the app focused) it moves to the
first of them.
From a control a commit disabled under the focus, the arrows still move. With no candidate,
the focus stays: the move does not wrap. The new focus is scrolled into view by the nearest distance, as a field
the keyboard moves to is (`keep_in_view`).

The focus ring is the one a keyboard's focus shows today (LLP 1115.001 D7.6). Its look on a
TV is owed (§4), judged as §5 Q2 rules.

### D5. The launcher

The shell's manifest:

- adds the `android.intent.category.LEANBACK_LAUNCHER` category to `MainActivity`;
- declares `android.hardware.touchscreen` and `android.software.leanback` with
  `android:required="false"`, so one APK installs on both;
- gives the application a banner (`android:banner`), which the TV launcher shows. Every app
  gets the same plain banner for now; a banner per app is owed (§4);
- sets `android:enableOnBackInvokedCallback="true"`. Before Android 16 the platform calls the
  reader's Back callback (LLP 1115.001 D6) only when the app opts in; without it, Back on
  Android 13 to 15, phones included, finishes the activity from any screen.

### D6. A TV's page is 1080 CSS pixels tall

On a TV the reader tells exact2 a scale of the screen's height over 1080 pixels, not the
display's density, so the page is 1920 × 1080 CSS pixels at 16:9, as Apple TV's is 1920 × 1080
points. An app's TV layout is then the same on both TVs. Android's own unit gives a TV half
that: a 1080p TV is density 320, 960 × 540 dp, so the same layout showed text twice as large
as on Apple TV and pushed the page off the screen. Every place the reader turns exact2's
points into pixels reads the one scale (`Device.scale`); Android's own dp, such as its touch
slop and the scroll lead, keep the density.

### D7. A TV's safe area is 5% in from each edge

A TV may overscan its edges, so Android TV's guidelines keep content 5% in from each: 48 dp
at the sides and 27 dp above and below its 960 × 540 dp, which is 96 and 54 CSS pixels on the
page of D6. On a TV the presenter makes that margin the safe area, as the larger of it and any
bar or cutout, so `env(safe-area-inset-*)` answers it, and a page that does not cover the edges
is laid out inside it. The shell's bars are laid out inside it too. Apple TV's safe area keeps content in by about as much (80 points at the
sides, 60 above and below). A phone's safe area is its bars and cutout, as before.

## 3. Stage 1: Caltrain on the TV emulator

Built when Caltrain, installed from the one APK on the `tv_api_34` emulator (Android 14,
arm64-v8a):

- shows in the TV launcher with the banner;
- shows its TV layout (two columns) as Apple TV shows it, with "Change station" on screen;
- moves the focus with the D-pad, presses with Select and goes back with Back;
- still shows its phone layout, with `pointer: coarse`, on a phone emulator.

Built 2026-10-20, except the first item's tile: Android lists the leanback entry and the APK
carries the banner, but the tile on Google TV's Apps row is not yet checked by hand.

## 4. Owed

- The TV's own look: the focus ring, type size and colours a hand-built Compose for TV app has (§5 Q2).
- A banner per app, from the manifest.
- `focusGuide="auto"` (LLP 1008 §9's focus guides).
- The media keys: play and pause.
- Text entry on a TV: a hosted `EditText` and the TV's keyboard.
- A control inside another control, such as a switch in a pressable row: no edge of it lies
  beyond the row's, so no arrow reaches it (HTML allows no interactive content in a button,
  but a pressable row is not one).
- Driving a TV by its real keys from the agent, and a TV smoke. `scripts/agent.mjs`'s `type …
  key ArrowDown` reaches the presenter, not Android's input.
