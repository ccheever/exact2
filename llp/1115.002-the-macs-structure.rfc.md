## 0. Summary

An Exact app on the Mac behaves like a Mac app since LLP 1115.000's wave 1: Back, the
menus, focus, Return, the alert. It still **looks like an iPhone app in a Mac window**. Shelf
shows a tab bar of three buttons along the bottom edge, the page's title drawn in the
content under a title bar that says "Shelf", iOS's 52-point rows in 26-point rounded cards,
a "New Book" form laid over the library with the library showing through its header, and
list rows that neither highlight nor separate.

This document decides what the same Contract becomes on the Mac, with nothing new for an
author to write:

| The author wrote | On the Mac it is | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| a route `header` (a heading, buttons, a search field) | the window's title and toolbar | D2 |
| a root `tablist` | a sidebar | D3 |
| a list whose rows push a detail | a split view on a wide window | D4 |
| a vertical `list` | rows with the Mac's separator and selection | D5 |
| `list appearance="auto"` | the Mac's grouped form: 36-point rows, 10-point cards | D6 |
| a `dialog`, a modal route, a popover | a sheet, a sheet, an `NSPopover` | D7 |

Everything in it was **measured on macOS 27.0.1** with small AppKit and SwiftUI probe apps
(§1), not recalled, except where a line says so. The reference is a hand-built app in
SwiftUI where SwiftUI and AppKit differ, because that is what a new Mac app is written in
today (D1). Two of the six need the kernel to learn something (D6 a table of metrics, D4 and
D7.4 a box for a subtree); the other four are the host's alone.

## 1. What is there today, and what a Mac app shows

Read at `d2f8a309f`. The findings, with file and function names, every number below and 83
pictures, are kept with the audit's records (`~/audits/exact2-no-tells-2026-10-11/mac-structure/`:
`findings.md`, `probes/`, `out/`, `png/`). Audit IDs: MAC-02 to -05, -07, -26, -30.

**How it was measured.** Each probe is an accessory app, never activated, with its windows
far off screen; pictures are the window server's of the probe's own windows. A window
subclass draws its title bar as key without being key. So anything that needs a real key
window, a real pointer, a material with content behind it, or motion was **not** measured:
focus rings, hover, sheet and sidebar animation, a drag, glass tints, and how a row behaves
when the person returns to it. The display is 1x, so 2x hairlines were not seen.

**Title bar and toolbar.** Exact today: `.unifiedCompact`, 40 points, holding only Back,
titled with the app's name. SwiftUI's window: `.unified`, `displayMode` `.iconOnly`, **52
points**; the title 15-point semibold after Back; buttons as 36-point glass circles; a search
item 36 high and 240 to 325 wide; no large title anywhere. With `.default` display the bar
shows labels and is 66 points.

**Sidebar.** A sidebar item's minimum width is 144 (SwiftUI's column: 140, and 144 wide at
every window width tried); rows 32 points in SwiftUI's sidebar, 13-point text, a symbol of
about 15 by 19 points **in the accent**; the selection inset 10 with a 6-point corner,
`#0070f5` with white text when emphasized, grey when the window is not key.

**Lists.** An inset table's selection is inset 10 with a 6-point corner. A SwiftUI `List`
draws a 1-point separator from 16 to 16, and none beside a selected row; a `List` of
navigation links draws **no disclosure chevron**. **On mouse-down a row is selected and
drawn emphasized**, in AppKit and in SwiftUI alike: that is the Mac's press for a row.

**Grouped form.** SwiftUI's grouped `Form` has no AppKit table under it: it is painted.
Rows are **36 points** (Exact: 52), cards have a **10-point** corner (26), inset 20 (16) and
**at most 704 wide, centred**; the card is `#f9f9f9` on the white window (dark `#272727`),
which is why Exact's cards vanish when their surfaces are written as roles: on macOS 27 the
window and control backgrounds are both white. Headers are 13-point bold in the label
colour; a toggle is the small switch; a field is borderless and right-aligned; **a
destructive action is a bordered push button, left-aligned and not red**.

**Sheets and popovers.** A sheet is a separate window centred on its parent on both axes,
with a corner of about 16, the parent dimmed by AppKit. A SwiftUI form sheet sizes to its
content (470 by 226 for two fields), with its title at the top left and **Cancel then Save
at the bottom right**. An `NSPopover`'s window is its content plus 13 points a side.

**What Exact does today.** A `dialog` is a card Exact paints on an 18% backdrop inside the
window; a modal route is un-hidden with no surface at all; a popover is clipped to the
window; `NSPopover` and `beginSheet` are used nowhere but the alert. Covers, the way a host
tells the kernel "this box is the platform's now" on iOS, are not wired on macOS.

## 2. Decisions

### D1 — The reference, and what is used against what is painted

The reference for each piece is what a hand-built Mac app shows on the same macOS, in
SwiftUI where the two frameworks differ. What is the operating system's is **used**: the
toolbar, the split view and its sidebar, a sheet's window, a popover, the alert. What is
drawn inside the page is **painted by Exact in the Mac's measured shape**: list rows and the
grouped form, as SwiftUI itself paints the grouped form. Numbers live in one kernel table
with a column per platform, as the colour roles and text styles do, and are re-measured when
macOS changes.

This is the rule LLP 1115.001 D1 wrote for Android, for the same reason.

### D2 — A route's `header` is the window's title bar and toolbar

A header-shaped route is projected by **the same rule iOS's bar uses** (the header rule,
which a lane is moving into shared code now so that Android can read it too):

- its heading is the window's title. **The root's heading replaces the app's name** (§5 Q2),
  as a SwiftUI window shows its `navigationTitle`. There is no large title on the Mac: a
  level-1 heading is a title like any other;
- its buttons are toolbar items that press the authored buttons; a `filled` one is
  `.prominent`; a button that opens a menu is a menu item;
- its search field is the toolbar's search item; a `tablist` of text tabs in a header is a
  centred segmented group;
- Back stays where wave 1 put it, first.

The bar is `.unified` and `.iconOnly`, 52 points, as SwiftUI's is. **This corrects LLP
1115.000 D7.9**, which said the toolbar takes the system's display mode: on macOS 27
`.default` shows labels and makes the bar 66 points, which no SwiftUI app shows.

The header's box leaves layout, as on iOS, so covers are wired on macOS (D8). A route's
`refresh` is View ▸ Reload, ⌘R (the reference for this one is recalled, not measured).

[MAC-02]

### D3 — The root tab bar is a sidebar

The window's content becomes a split view with a real sidebar, built from the shared rule
that finds the root's `tablist` and what each tab shows (its title, symbol and badge, as
iOS's tab bar reads them): source-list rows, symbols in the accent as the Mac draws a
sidebar's, the selection the system's. It has the toolbar's sidebar button, View ▸ Show
Sidebar, a tracking separator, and its width is remembered. The page beside it simply
narrows: the tablist's box leaves layout and nothing else in layout changes.

**A sidebar, not toolbar segments** (§5 Q1). SwiftUI's unstyled `TabView` on macOS 27 is a
segmented control in the toolbar, so either would pass for hand-built. The sidebar keeps the
symbols, is what LLP 1116.004 D1 already made a wide web window, and is where a Mac app with
three or more places puts them. It stays on a narrow window too; the person collapses it.

[MAC-04]

### D4 — A list and its detail are a split on a wide window

The Mac half of LLP 1116.004 D1.2. The rule that finds a stack whose root is a list of rows
that push (`splitOf`, in the web's JavaScript today) moves into shared code. On a window at
least 900 points wide (the web's threshold, §5 Q6) the runner lays the two routes side by
side, the list 320 points until its divider is dragged; the Mac host draws the divider,
remembers a dragged width, keeps the row that pushed the visible detail selected (D5) and
drops the detail's Back. With two headers in view the window's title is the list's and the
detail's buttons take the toolbar's trailing end; that last rule is a guess to be tried on
Shelf, not a measured fact.

This is one of two places the kernel must learn a size (D8).

### D5 — Rows of a vertical `list` have the Mac's separator and selection

LLP 1115.000 D16's Mac half, built with D16 on every host. Exact's own list draws the row
AppKit and SwiftUI draw:

- a 1-point separator in the separator role from 16 to 16, and none beside a selected row;
- **on mouse-down the row is selected and drawn emphasized**: a fill inset 10 with a
  6-point corner in the selection colour, and the row's unsaid text and symbols in the
  selected-text colour (D2's written bit of LLP 1115.000 says which are unsaid); grey, with
  its text unchanged, in a window that is not key;
- in a split the row whose press pushed the visible detail stays selected. In a stack the
  row stays selected through the push and clears on return, as iOS's does since wave 2;
  what a Mac list does on return was not measured;
- **no disclosure chevron** on a plain list's row (§5 Q4);
- arrow keys move the selection and Return presses the row, and the list is one Tab stop.

`appearance="none"` on the list opts out, and a horizontal list gets none of it, as D16
says. Shelf's library, a `scroll` of hand-built rows with hand-drawn separators today,
becomes a `list`.

**Not an `NSTableView`**, for now: the runner makes and removes rows as they scroll, a table
wants to own that, and the text's inversion is needed either way. Type-select, multiple
selection and row dragging are what that leaves out (§4).

### D6 — `list appearance="auto"` is the Mac's grouped form

The painted sheet stays, because the reference is painted too, **in the Mac's metrics**:
36-point rows, cards with a 10-point corner inset 20 and at most 704 wide and centred,
content and separators inset 10, the card's fill a role whose Mac value is the measured
`#f9f9f9` and `#272727` (which also ends the held change, where a card written as the
control background vanished), headers 13-point bold in the label colour, footers 11-point.
In a row: a toggle is the small switch, a field is borderless and right-aligned, a row that
pushes keeps its chevron, and **a `destructive` row is a bordered push button, left-aligned
and not red** (§5 Q5).

The sheet's numbers become named platform metrics the kernel resolves, in the table D1
names, so the compiler's sheet and the kernel's copy name them and hold none. This
supersedes LLP 1115.000 D3.3's "its row metrics stay as they are", which left them for this
document. The schema's digest moves once.

[MAC-03]

### D7 — A `dialog` and a modal route are sheets; a popover is a popover

1. **Now, as a fix:** a modal route gets an opaque surface. Today the page beneath shows
   through it.
2. **A `dialog` that is not alert-shaped is a sheet**: `beginSheet` with a borderless window
   holding the dialog's own subtree at the size the kernel gives it. Escape, `closedby`, the
   focus's return and the default button are as they are. The alert shape stays `NSAlert`.
3. **A popover that is not a menu is an `NSPopover`** at the subtree's size, transient for
   `popover="auto"`, on the edge its `position-area` names.
4. **A modal route is a sheet**: when the author gives no size, 480 points wide and as tall
   as its content (§5 Q3). Its header is projected into the sheet as SwiftUI lays one out:
   the heading in a title row at the top, and its buttons in a row at the bottom right, the
   cancelling one first and the `filled` one the default.

Items 2 to 4 put Exact's own views in a second window, which the Mac host has not done
before: the driver's coordinates, focus and `tap` learn it, as Android's did for its windows.
Item 4 is the second place the kernel must learn a size (D8).

[MAC-05, MAC-07]

### D8 — What the kernel learns

iOS tells the kernel three things about a box the platform has taken: it is gone (`Whole`),
its edges are covered (`Edges`), or its scroller is inset (`Inset`). Then:

1. **Those are wired on macOS.** A projected header and the tablist are `Whole`. Nothing
   else is needed for D2, D3, D5, or D7's dialog and popover.
2. **One new thing: a box for a subtree.** A route in a split pane, and a modal route in a
   sheet, are laid out in a box that is not the window. iOS's way for a sheet (make the
   sheet the session's whole viewport) is wrong where the window stays visible around it,
   and padding is wrong for an absolutely positioned child. For the split the runner
   writes the two boxes itself (D4); for a sheet the host reports the box. It is designed
   once for the Mac and for the iPad's half of LLP 1116.004 D1, whose containers also own
   a child's size, and the Mac, where both routes live in one view, proves it first.
3. **The platform's metrics are data** (D6), resolved by the kernel like a text style.

The web is unchanged by all of this and stays the layout oracle for everything inside a box.

### D9 — What the window remembers, and what waits

The sidebar's and the split's widths are remembered. Restoring a window's place in the app
through AppKit's restoration is its own item, starting with an experiment: it is off today
for a measured cost while scrolling. The lift of a reordered row is restyled when someone has
measured a real one by hand. Dragging something out of the app needs words an author does
not have and gets its own RFC.

[MAC-26, MAC-30]

## 3. Order of work

Each stage leaves Shelf better on its own. Sizes are S, M, L.

| 1 | The header as title bar and toolbar (D2) | 0 | no | M to L |

| 4 | The grouped form's metrics (D6) | none | a table; the digest moves | M |

Stages 3, 4 and 5 can run beside 1 and 2. **Proposed dates, from the day this is ruled:**
stages 0 to 2 within three working days; 4 and 5 within five; 3 with D16; 6 within ten, after
D8's box has been reviewed on its own; 7 leaves this document for `QUEUE.md` if it has no
date by then.

Each stage is verified as LLP 1115.000's lanes are: the six checks, the macOS and iOS
XCTests and a tvOS build (shared Swift moves), a drive of Shelf with before and after
pictures beside the probe's picture of the reference, and a list of what only a person at a
real Mac can check.

## 4. Not in this document

- **Things an author would have to say something new for** (LLP 1115 D7: authors say
  structure and nothing else): a Settings window, inspectors, sections and counts in a
  sidebar, more than one window, a table's column headers, dragging out, and `label`. Each
  wants its own RFC.
- **Customize Toolbar**, which needs stable item identities and saved configurations.
- **A real `NSTableView`**, with type-select, multiple selection and row dragging, and swipe
  actions on a Mac row (a two-finger swipe scrolls Shelf's swipe scroller today, where a Mac
  user expects Delete or the context menu). Revisit after D5.
- **Content scrolling under the bar** with the system's edge effect, which waits for the
  page's scroller to be AppKit's (LLP 1115.000 D8).
- **The iPad's containers.** D8's box is designed for them; building them is LLP 1116.004's
  queued half.

**What a Mac user would still notice after all of it:** no Customize Toolbar; a sidebar that
holds only the tabs; no type-select in a list; a sheet that does not animate when its content
changes size; no window restoration beyond widths.

## 5. Questions that are Charlie's

Each has a proposed answer above.

1. **Tabs on the Mac: a sidebar, or segments in the toolbar?** Proposed: a sidebar, kept on
   a narrow window too (D3). Both are things real Mac apps do.
2. **The root screen's heading replaces the app's name in the title bar.** Proposed: yes
   (D2). Shelf's window says "Library", then the book's title.
3. **A modal route as a sheet puts Cancel and Save at the bottom right, and is 480 wide and
   content-high when the author gives no size.** Proposed: yes to both (D7.4). It moves the
   buttons from where the author's header drew them.
4. **A plain list's row shows no disclosure chevron on the Mac.** Proposed: yes (D5),
   since SwiftUI's shows none; a grouped form's row keeps its chevron (D6).
5. **A `destructive` row in a grouped form is a plain push button on the Mac, not red
   text.** Proposed: yes (D6), as measured.
6. **The split begins at the web's 900 points.** Proposed: yes (D4).
