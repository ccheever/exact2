## 0. Summary

An Exact app on the Mac behaves like a Mac app since LLP 1115.000's wave 1: Back, the
menus, focus, Return, the alert. It still **looks like an iPhone app in a Mac window**. Shelf
shows a tab bar of three buttons along the bottom edge, the page's title drawn in the
content under a title bar that says "Shelf", iOS's 52-point rows in 26-point rounded cards,
a "New Book" form laid over the library with the library showing through its header, and
list rows that neither highlight nor separate.

This document decides what the same Contract becomes on the Mac, with nothing new for an
author to write:

| The author wrote | On the Mac it is | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| a route `header` (a heading, buttons, a search field) | the window's title and toolbar | D2 |
| a root `tablist` | a sidebar | D3 |
| a list whose rows push a detail | a split view | D4 |
| a vertical `list` | a table, with the Mac's selection | D5 |
| `list appearance="auto"` | the Mac's grouped form: 36-point rows, 10-point cards | D6 |
| a `dialog`, a modal route, a popover | a sheet, a sheet, an `NSPopover` | D7 |

Everything in it was **measured on macOS 27.0.1** with small AppKit and SwiftUI probe apps
(§1), not recalled, except where a line says so. Two of the six need the kernel to learn
something (D6 a table of metrics; D4 and D7.4 a containing block for a subtree, D8); the
others are the host's alone.

## 1. What is there today, and what a Mac app shows

Read at `d2f8a309f`. The findings, with file and function names, every number below and 83
pictures, are kept with the audit's records (`~/audits/exact2-no-tells-2026-10-11/mac-structure/`:
`findings.md`, `probes/`, `out/`, `png/`). Audit IDs: MAC-02 to -05, -07, -26, -30.

**How it was measured.** Each probe is an accessory app, never activated, with its windows
far off screen; pictures are the window server's of the probe's own windows. A window
subclass draws its title bar as key without being key. So anything that needs a real key
window, a real pointer, a material with content behind it, or motion was **not** measured:
focus rings, hover, keys in a list, sheet and sidebar animation, a drag, glass tints, and
how a row behaves when the person returns to it. The display is 1x, so 2x hairlines were
not seen. Only macOS 27 was measured (§5 Q9).

**Title bar and toolbar.** Exact today: `.unifiedCompact`, 40 points, holding only Back,
titled with the app's name. SwiftUI's window: `.unified`, `displayMode` `.iconOnly`, 52
points; the title 15-point semibold after Back; buttons as 36-point glass circles; a search
item 36 high and 240 to 325 wide; no large title anywhere. With `.default` display the bar
shows labels and is 66 points.

**Sidebar.** A sidebar item's minimum width is 144; it runs the window's full height,
under the title bar. Rows are 24 points in AppKit's default and 32 in SwiftUI's sidebar at
the medium size; 13-point text; a symbol of about 15 by 19 points **in the accent**; the
selection inset 10 with a 6-point corner, `#0070f5` with white text when emphasized, grey
when the window is not key.

**Lists.** `NSTableView.style = .automatic` resolves to `.inset`: selection inset 10 with a
6-point corner. A SwiftUI `List` sizes a row to its content and draws a 1-point separator
from 16 to 16, none beside a selected row; a `List` of navigation links draws **no
disclosure chevron**. **On mouse-down a row is selected and drawn emphasized**, in AppKit
and in SwiftUI alike: that is the Mac's press for a row.

**Grouped form.** SwiftUI's grouped `Form` has no AppKit table under it: it is painted.
Rows are 36 points (a picker's 38, a stepper's 40, a button's 44; Exact: 52), cards have a
10-point corner (26), inset 20 (16) and **at most 704 wide, centred**, 10 apart, or 56 with
a header; the card is `#f9f9f9` on the white window (dark `#272727`), which is why Exact's
cards vanish when their surfaces are written as roles: on macOS 27 the window and control
backgrounds are both white. Headers are 13-point bold in the label colour; a toggle is the
small switch; a field is borderless and right-aligned; **a destructive action is a bordered
push button, left-aligned and not red**.

**Sheets and popovers.** A sheet is a separate window centred on its parent on both axes,
with a corner of about 16, the parent dimmed by AppKit. A SwiftUI sheet **sizes to its
content** (100 by 80 for one line of text; 470 by 226 for a two-field form), with its title
at the top left and **Cancel then Save at the bottom right**. An `NSPopover`'s window is its
content plus 13 points a side.

**What Exact does today.** A `dialog` is a card Exact paints on an 18% backdrop inside the
window; a modal route is un-hidden with no surface at all; a popover is clipped to the
window; `NSPopover` and `beginSheet` are used nowhere but the alert. Covers, the way a host
tells the kernel "this box is the platform's now" on iOS, are not wired on macOS.

## 2. Decisions

### D1 — The reference, chosen per presentation; what is used and what is painted

The reference for each piece is what a person sees in a hand-built Mac app on the same
macOS. Where AppKit's default and SwiftUI's differ, the choice is made per piece, by what
Mac apps show, and each decision below says which it took and why: the toolbar's mode and
the grouped form are SwiftUI's (AppKit's defaults there are a labelled 66-point bar and a
column form); tabs are **not** SwiftUI's default (D3). The pieces are chosen to make one
Mac app of one macOS: the toolbar, the sidebar, the table and the sheet are all macOS 27's,
and none is taken from an older look because it is easier to build.

What is the operating system's is **used**: the toolbar, the split view and its sidebar, a
table, a sheet's window, a popover, the alert. **A control Exact uses keeps that control's
own metrics**: nothing here restyles one to match a probe's picture of another framework.
What has no native view under it in a hand-built app is **painted in the Mac's measured
shape**: the grouped form, as SwiftUI itself paints it. Painted numbers are rows of the
kernel's existing schema with a column per platform, as colour roles and text styles are.

This amends one sentence of LLP 1115, which says the oracle on the Mac is AppKit (§5 Q7).

### D2 — A route's `header` is the window's title bar and toolbar

A header-shaped route is projected by the shared header rule (which a lane is moving into
shared code now, so that Android reads it too), with the Mac's branch of it: **there is no
large title on the Mac**, so a level-1 heading is a title like any other.

- **The window's title** is the authored document title when there is one, else the
  route's heading, else the app's name: the order LLP 1115.000 D4.1 gave the web's page
  title (§5 Q2). So Shelf's window says "Library", then the book's title.
- Its buttons are real `NSToolbarItem`s that press the authored buttons (so the glass and
  the search field are AppKit's); a `filled` one is `.prominent`; a button that opens a
  menu is a menu item; its search field is the toolbar's search item; a `tablist` of text
  tabs in a header is a centred segmented group. Back stays first, where wave 1 put it.
- **Precedence.** A toolbar the author declared for the window (`toolbarPlacement="window"`)
  wins over projection. A header holding something the rule cannot project **stays painted,
  whole**, as today's toolbar refuses a shape it does not know; a header is never half
  projected. The header's box leaves layout (D8's cover) only in the same step in which
  projection succeeded.
- The bar is `.unified` and `.iconOnly`, as SwiftUI's is: about 52 points on macOS 27, which
  is what AppKit gives that style and not a number Exact sets. **This corrects LLP 1115.000
  D7.9**, which said the toolbar takes the system's display mode: `.default` shows labels
  and makes the bar 66 points. (A person cannot turn labels on for a toolbar that does not
  offer Customize Toolbar, and this one does not, §4.)

Stage 1 waits for the header rule's move: today the rule is iOS-only Swift.

[MAC-02]

### D3 — The root tab bar is a sidebar

**When the tab bar's placement is unsaid**, as LLP 1116.004 D1.1 has it for the web (an
authored placement is the author's and stays), the window's content becomes a split view
with a real sidebar built from the shared rule that finds the root's `tablist` and what
each tab shows. It is AppKit's source list with the system's row size style, so its rows
follow the person's sidebar size setting; it runs under the title bar; it has the toolbar's
sidebar button, View ▸ Show Sidebar and a tracking separator; its width is remembered; the
selection is the system's. The page beside it narrows: the tablist's box leaves layout and
nothing else in layout changes. What a sidebar does on a narrow window is AppKit's own behaviour (a
split view collapses or overlays a sidebar below a minimum width), but "AppKit's rule" is
not yet a specification: stage 2 measures it on macOS 27 and writes the numbers here before
it lands.

**A sidebar's symbols are the accent**, as AppKit draws them. That is an exception to LLP
1115.000 D3.9 (an unsaid symbol on the Mac is the secondary label colour), for a sidebar
only, and D3.9 gains the sentence.

**A sidebar, not toolbar segments** (§5 Q1). SwiftUI's unstyled `TabView` on macOS 27 is a
segmented control in the toolbar, so either would pass for hand-built. The sidebar keeps the
symbols, leaves the toolbar's centre to a header's own tabs (D2), is what LLP 1116.004 D1
already made a wide web window, and is where a Mac app with several places puts them. Its
cost is plain in Shelf: for three places a sidebar is heavier than three segments.

[MAC-04]

### D4 — A list and its detail are a split view

The Mac half of LLP 1116.004 D1.2. **The rule is that document's, moved into shared code:**
a stack splits when a row of its root route pushes the route shown beside it, and
`navigationSplit` on the root still opts out, names the list's width, or opts in a list the
rule does not find. The web's implementation recognises such a root by a disclosure symbol
in a row; that symbol is paint (D5 hides it on the Mac), so the shared rule reads the push
and not the picture. The web moves to the shared rule afterwards, in a change of its own, once the Mac's
split has proved it.

On the Mac the two routes sit in a **native split view**: its panes hold the routes, its
divider is AppKit's (resizable, reachable by keyboard), and each pane reports its size to
layout (D8). The row whose press pushed the visible detail stays selected (D5); the detail
has no Back; a push from the detail goes deeper inside the detail's pane, with Back there.

**When it splits** (§5 Q6): proposed for now, at a width: the web's 900 points, measured
against what is left after the sidebar. Whether a Mac app of this shape is instead always
split, with a minimum window width (recalled of Mail, Notes and SwiftUI's split view, not
measured, and the more Mac-like answer if true), is settled by a probe in stage 6a; if it
is, this becomes "always".

**Decided before stage 6b is built, not in it:** with two headers in view, whose title the
window shows, where each header's buttons go, and which pane the keyboard's commands belong
to. A split cannot ship without those. They are tried on Shelf beside stage 6a's proof and
brought to Charlie (§5 Q10).

### D5 — A vertical `list` is a table

LLP 1115.000 D16's Mac half, and the owner's "list rows becoming real cells". **The end
state is a real, view-based `NSTableView`** whose rows hold the runner's rows: selection on
mouse-down, drawn emphasized or not by the window's key state, type-select, the keyboard's
own row navigation, row dragging, and what VoiceOver knows of a table come from AppKit and
are not rebuilt. A row is as tall as its content, as SwiftUI's is; separators and the
selection are the table's own (so a hairline is a device pixel without Exact drawing it);
anything Exact paints inside a selected row takes the `SelectedItem` roles (LLP 1115.000
D3.7). **No disclosure chevron** on a row (§5 Q4). `appearance="none"` on the list opts out,
and a horizontal list is not a table, as D16 says. Shelf's library, a `scroll` of hand-built
rows with hand-drawn separators today, becomes a `list`.

**First, a gate.** The runner makes and removes rows as they scroll, and a table
wants to own that. Stage 3a shows whether a table can be fed by the runner: rows arriving
and leaving, heights from layout, ten thousand rows scrolling inside the frame budget,
selection surviving a row's reuse, a control inside a row still taking its own clicks. The
same stage measures what the probes could not: when a press on a row acts (down or up),
what the keys do in a key window, and what selection does when the person returns from a
push. Stage 3b does not start until 3a has passed. If it fails, this decision comes back with what failed; it does not quietly
become paint.

LLP 1115.000 D16's sentence "a hairline separator inset to the text" describes iOS's row.
D16 gains: each host's row is its platform's, and the Mac's is this.

### D6 — `list appearance="auto"` is the Mac's grouped form

The painted sheet stays, because the reference is painted too (both reviewers took this as
the end state, not a shortcut), **in the Mac's metrics**: rows at least 36 points, taller
where a control is (a picker, a stepper, a button); cards with a 10-point corner, inset 20,
at most 704 wide and centred, 10 apart or 56 with a header; content and separators inset 10;
the card's fill a role whose Mac value is the measured `#f9f9f9` and `#272727` (which also
ends the held change, where a card written as the control background vanished); headers
13-point bold in the label colour, footers 11-point. In a row the controls are real ones: a
toggle is the small switch, a field is borderless and right-aligned, a row that pushes
keeps its chevron, and **a `destructive` row is a bordered push button, left-aligned and
not red** (§5 Q5), on the Mac only, with an authored colour still winning.

The sheet's numbers become platform metrics in the kernel's schema, so the compiler's sheet
and the kernel's copy name them and hold none. **iOS's and the web's columns hold exactly
today's values**, so the digest moves once and nothing is laid out differently there. The
resolved metrics are given to the cross-host layout fixtures as inputs, as the root font
size is. How a pressable grouped row shows a press was not measured and is not ruled.

This supersedes LLP 1115.000 D3.3's "its row metrics stay as they are", which left them for
this document.

[MAC-03]

### D7 — A `dialog` and a modal route are sheets; a popover is a popover

1. **Now, as a fix:** a modal route gets an opaque surface. Today the page beneath shows
   through it.
2. **A `dialog` that is not alert-shaped is a sheet**: `beginSheet` with a window holding
   the dialog's own subtree at the size layout gives it. Escape, `closedby`, the focus's
   return and the default button are as they are (exactly one `filled`, non-destructive
   button is the default). The alert shape stays `NSAlert`. The probe's sheet was a titled
   window; whichever kind is used must be shown to take the keyboard.
3. **A popover that is not a menu is an `NSPopover`** at the subtree's size, transient for
   `popover="auto"`, on the edge its `position-area` names.
4. **A modal route is a sheet.** With no authored size it **fits its content on both axes**
   under a maximum (the parent window's content area less the margin AppKit leaves around
   its own largest sheet, measured in stage 6a), as a SwiftUI sheet and
   Exact's own `dialog` do; an authored size wins. Its header is projected into the sheet as
   SwiftUI lays one out: the heading in a title row at the top, and **the two actions it can
   identify, the one that cancels and the `filled` one that confirms, in a row at the bottom
   right**, cancel first, confirm the default (§5 Q3). Anything else in the header stays
   where the author put it. While a sheet is up the menus' commands go to it.

Before stage 6c, an XCTest in a key window shows that the sheet takes the keyboard and that
Escape and the default button work there. Items 2 to 4 put Exact's own views in a second window, which the Mac host has not done
before: the driver's coordinates, focus and `tap` learn it, as Android's did for its windows.

[MAC-05, MAC-07]

### D8 — What the kernel learns

1. **Covers are wired on macOS.** iOS tells the kernel a box is gone (`Whole`), its edges
   are covered (`Edges`), or its scroller is inset (`Inset`). A projected header and the
   tablist are `Whole`, reported in the step in which the projection happened. Nothing else
   is needed for D2, D3, D5, or D7's dialog and popover.
2. **One new fact: a subtree's containing block, given from outside.** A route in a split
   pane and a modal route in a sheet are laid out in a box that is not the window. The
   kernel takes **constraints** for a subtree and lays CSS out inside them; it learns
   nothing about sidebars, splits or sheets. Where the host puts the result, and how it
   clips it, is the host's and is separate.
   - A pane **reports its size** (and a new one when its divider moves).
   - A sheet gives **a maximum**, and layout returns the content's size, which the host
     gives the window. That is the dialog's shrink-to-fit today, with a ceiling.
   - Written down before it is built: nested boxes; a box that goes away; percentages and
     absolutely or fixed positioned children (they resolve against the box); viewport units
     (they stay the window's, as the iOS host's existing rule has it).
3. **A proof before the split is built** (stage 6a), on the Mac alone: one session, two
   resizable native panes and one sheet, with a percentage child, an absolutely positioned
   child, a form whose height depends on its width, and one case that overflows; the same
   constraints given to a browser as the oracle; and a measurement that a subtree whose
   constraints did not change does no work. The iPad's half of LLP 1116.004 D1 takes the
   same seam afterwards.
4. **The platform's metrics are data** (D6), in the existing schema.

Inside a box the web stays the layout oracle. The web's own split moves to the shared rule
later, in its own change (D4).

### D9 — What the window remembers, and what waits

The sidebar's and the split's widths are remembered. Restoring a window's place in the app
through AppKit's restoration is its own item, starting with an experiment: it is off today
for a measured cost while scrolling. Nothing app-owned stands in for it meanwhile, since
that would ignore the person's setting. The lift of a reordered row comes with the table
(D5). Dragging something out of the app needs words an author does not have and gets its
own RFC.

## 4. Not in this document

- **Things an author would have to say something new for** (LLP 1115 D7: authors say
  structure and nothing else): a Settings window, inspectors, sections and counts in a
  sidebar, more than one window, a table's column headers, dragging out, and `label`. Each
  wants its own RFC.
- **A route's `refresh` on the Mac.** View ▸ Reload, ⌘R, is the recalled answer; it was not
  measured and is not ruled.
- **Customize Toolbar**, which needs stable item identities and saved configurations.
- **Multiple selection and swipe actions on a Mac row.** The table makes the first
  possible; what an app hears of it is vocabulary. A two-finger swipe scrolls Shelf's swipe
  scroller today, where a Mac user expects Delete or the context menu.
- **Content scrolling under the bar** with the system's edge effect, which waits for the
  page's scroller to be AppKit's (LLP 1115.000 D8).
- **The iPad's containers.** D8's seam is designed for them; building them is LLP
  1116.004's queued half.

**What a Mac user would still notice after all of it:** no Customize Toolbar; a sidebar that
holds only the tabs; a sheet that does not animate when its content changes size; no window
restoration beyond widths.

## 5. Questions that were Charlie's

**Ruled 2026-10-12: "recs seem ok for the macos questions".** Each proposed answer below
stands as written; question 10 had none and is brought back after its trial. Question 7's
amendment is made in LLP 1115. Each had a proposed answer above. 1 to 6 were r1's; 3 and 6 changed after the reviews.

1. **Tabs on the Mac: a sidebar, or segments in the toolbar?** Proposed: a sidebar, when
   the author did not place the tab bar (D3). Both are things real Mac apps do; for an app
   of three places a sidebar is the heavier of the two, and segments lose the symbols.
2. **The window's title is the screen's.** Proposed: the authored document title, else the
   route's heading, else the app's name (D2).
3. **A modal route's sheet.** Proposed: Cancel and the confirming action move to the bottom
   right; with no authored size the sheet fits its content, under a maximum (D7.4). r1's
   fixed 480 points is withdrawn.
4. **No disclosure chevron on a list's row on the Mac.** Proposed: yes (D5); a grouped
   form's row keeps its chevron (D6).
5. **A `destructive` row in a grouped form is a plain push button on the Mac.** Proposed:
   yes (D6), as measured.
6. **When a list and its detail split.** Proposed for now: at the web's 900 points, measured
   after the sidebar. If a probe shows Mac apps of this shape are always split, with a
   minimum window width, it becomes "always" (D4).
7. **Amend LLP 1115's "on the Mac, AppKit"** to "a hand-built Mac app on the same macOS,
   in AppKit or SwiftUI, chosen per presentation". Proposed: yes (D1).
8. **A real table is the committed end state for a list on the Mac**, built after a proof,
   and brought back if the proof fails. Proposed: yes (D5).
9. **macOS versions.** Everything painted was measured on macOS 27 alone, and the host runs
   on macOS 14 and later. Proposed: painted metrics follow the newest macOS, with no column
   per version; what Exact uses (toolbar, table, sidebar, sheet) is right on each version by
   itself. The alternative is measuring and carrying each supported version.
10. **Two headers in a split** (whose title, whose buttons, whose keys): not proposed yet.
    It is tried on Shelf beside stage 6a and brought back before the split is built.
