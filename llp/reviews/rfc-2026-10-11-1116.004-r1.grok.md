**READY WITH CHANGES.** Round 6 did what LLP 1116.003 aimed at: Expo is won (8/8, 0.53× tokens), and the iOS fast path and the keyboard fix paid. The React ratio did not move (1.61× → 1.59×). D1 is aimed at the right losses and is implementable once it stops overriding the author and stops expecting an opt-in to fire by itself.

### 1. Decisions

**D1 — RIGHT WITH CHANGE.** A root `tablist` drawn as a sidebar on a wide window, with the phone bar unchanged and the router value untouched, matches LLP 1115's tell test and LLP 1038's rule that the rail and the bar are one value. It does not match either document if the host moves a bar the author placed: 1115 says what the author writes wins, and 1038 D12 says choosing a rail is the app's layout decision. Keep the host default only where placement was left unsaid, and let an authored arrangement win. The one-attribute split is the right shape (two columns, one value, router unchanged) and the wrong adoption story. Contacts, the clean loss, is a full-bleed bottom bar with no list beside the detail. The builders who lost already had a 40-line `when wide` in `list-detail` and did not use it. An attribute they must remember will not move those pairs. A list whose push is that row's detail should sit side by side on a wide window with nothing new to write. A pushed form stays a sheet.

**D2 — RIGHT.** Each item is a stock control a person who knows iOS would notice, which is LLP 1115's test, and each one is named in a SwiftUI loss (grocery-list, chat-rooms, unit-converter, polls). This is the quality lever on SwiftUI's 3/8. It does not touch the Expo path.

**D3 — RIGHT.** chat-rooms spent about fifteen minutes and 14.1M tokens, then deleted the sheet. That spike is most of Muse's move from 1.02× to 1.19×. Restoring the screen under a closed sheet is the fix.

**D4 — RIGHT WITH CHANGE.** An unlabelled `button` and `role="dialog"` on a non-`dialog` are warnings for code the builder already wrote, and they are real grade losses (water-tracker's blank +500, flashcards with no Escape and no focus trap). The plural slip is not that kind of fix. "1 cards" and "1 spots" show up in two or three apps every round, including this one, and the builders did not look anything up. An example in start-here and a `contract vocab plural` entry will not fire. The count needs a warning on the text they wrote.

**D5 — RIGHT WITH CHANGE.** Pointing `contract vocab` at the searches that now hit `contract-grammar` (scroll, loading before data, a two-to-four choice, a modal) is the right reply to round 5, where cutting recipes moved the reads into the grammar and total docs fell 4%. The answer has to be the whole repair. A short answer plus a link to a recipe is a second read, which is how the floor survived. This is the smaller half of the React gap, as below.

**D6 — RIGHT.** water-tracker threw under Playwright's fake clock and drew nothing. Small, last in the order, and it does not carry the goal.

### 2. What is missing

D1 is only part of the desktop losses. contacts is the tab-bar-plus-split case. event-rsvp already has a list and a detail inside a narrow column, and the grade cites Full/Cancelled polish and "1 spots," so a split attribute will not obviously flip it. water-tracker is two content columns, which the dashboard pattern already teaches with wrapping flex, and the builder read that pattern and still shipped one column. The blank button is what made feature 2 a partial. habit-tracker has no root tab bar. It lost as plainer than stat cards and a sidebar. flashcards already has a sidebar and lost on the dialog, the plural, and plainer cards. D1.3's check, "narrower than half the viewport and centred," misses contacts (full-bleed) and flags habit-tracker's centred column, which is a legitimate web page. The check should name a bottom tab bar or a pushed-only list at desktop width, and the repair should be the platform's own adaptation.

D4 and D5 are not the larger React token lever. On the six GPT React pairs, Exact2 runs 21–35 commands to React's 17–28. The difference is 5–9 doc lookups and 4–9 drives. React looks nothing up and does not read images. Round 6 already ran `check` 121 times, and screenshots only fell from 109 to 87, so the image still rides the later steps. Another shorter lookup is the lever round 5 spent. The open lever is a web check the builder can stop on, with no screenshot after it.

### 3. Risks

A host sidebar that ignores an authored placement breaks LLP 1115 and LLP 1038 for a layout gain the author did not ask for. An opt-in split plus a centred-column warning spends a round and adds layout edits on pages that are already fine, which raises tokens and can make a clean column worse. D5, if it becomes a fourth place to read, repeats the grammar detour. D2, D3, D4's two real warnings, and D6 do not threaten the Expo wins or the phone tab bar, provided the wide-window rules collapse on a phone.

### 4. Verdict

**READY WITH CHANGES.** Before implementation: the author wins on placement, and a list-detail stack adapts with no new attribute. Retarget the wide-window check at a stretched tab bar and a pushed-only list. Make the plural a warning. Make each vocab answer complete. Treat drives that still end in a screenshot as the React token work D5 does not cover.
