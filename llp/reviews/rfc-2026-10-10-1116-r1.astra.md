**Overall: READY WITH CHANGES.** The proposal targets the main observed costs, but understates misleading verification and overreaches in D1’s layout changes.

1. **D1 — RIGHT WITH CHANGE.** Designed web defaults are strongly supported. Remove the automatic 48-rem route constraint and the substitution of content width for viewport width. Those change layout and observable facts, beyond improving presentation; they could impair desktop layouts such as Contacts’ two-pane design. Put the centred-column pattern in a recipe. Two targets sharing a stylesheet does not establish conformity with CSS layout semantics.

2. **D2 — RIGHT WITH CHANGE.** Safe-area, keyboard, subtitle and identifier fixes are worthwhile. Broaden the presentation repair beyond the bottom strip: [batch B](/Users/ccheever/bakeoff/rounds/r1/analysis/batch-b.md) identifies authored grouped backgrounds disappearing and vertical grouped-row content becoming horizontal. These violate “what the author writes wins” and caused repeated screenshot/fix rounds. Changing the bottom inset alone will not repair white cards on white backgrounds.

3. **D3 — RIGHT WITH CHANGE.** Progressive disclosure and tested recipes attack the largest token cost. A 20-KB mandatory guide remains unnecessarily large; use approximately the analyses’ proposed 150-line core, with task-specific recipes loaded on demand. Start with one screen, adding navigation, sheets and alerts when needed. Otherwise the new skeleton risks replacing copied tab boilerplate with copied stack boilerplate. Measure total reading, including subsequent lookups, rather than just the starter’s size.

4. **D4 — RIGHT.** This is the clearest inexpensive win: broken help directly caused hangs and extensive source reading. Cover nested commands and driver operations sufficiently that builders can discover the actual syntax they need without opening implementation files.

5. **D5 — RIGHT WITH CHANGE.** One-line remembered settings removes repeated plumbing and recurring relaunch defects across nine specs. Keep the initial scope focused on small settings. Arbitrary collections plus automatic writes after every commit broaden this into general persistence, with performance and durability obligations the settings evidence does not establish. Move the bounded version ahead of broad diagnostic analysis.

6. **D6 — RIGHT WITH CHANGE.** Explain unavoidable fallbacks, but first eliminate avoidable ones. Reconcile the missing-`navigationBack` warning with [LLP 1115 D5](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/llp/1115-write-the-web-ship-the-platform.principle.md), which promises system Back without an authored control. Likewise, differing accessible and visible labels should not inherently cost native presentation. Diagnostics should identify losses on each target platform; warnings cannot substitute for actually presenting required content.

7. **D7 — RIGHT.** Stronger semantic heading hierarchy addresses repeated complaints on both hosts, preserves explicit author overrides, and costs builders nothing. Keep this scoped to headings rather than changing every use of a named text style.

8. **D8 — RIGHT.** Formatting, determinate progress, discoverable symbols, randomness and clear clock guidance all address observed failures. Prioritize the clock correction, progress and formatting: these affected required functionality, unlike the favicon. The timer recipe must demonstrate correct elapsed time after relaunch, not merely that the “running” flag persisted.

**Important omissions:**

- **Make verification observe the shipped app.** [Household-list](/Users/ccheever/bakeoff/rounds/r1/grades/household-list/grade.md) lost because its builder’s screenshot showed content absent from the real app. Default native verification to platform chrome and input; distinguish runner-state assertions from assertions that content is presented. Add a bounded real-clock write/refresh/relaunch drive. D2’s annotations and D3’s recipe mention are insufficient for a failure mode that also let Kanban ship broken writes.
- **Isolate backend tests.** [Batch C](/Users/ccheever/bakeoff/rounds/r1/analysis/batch-c.md) attributes roughly 19% of backend diary friction to shared test data. Give the REST recipe isolated per-run data and cleanup. Switching servers does not remove this problem.
- **Permit deliberate app design.** The stopwatch’s undersized readout came from overapplying “no literal font sizes.” Clarify that platform defaults cover omitted choices; authors can still design numeric displays, charts and distinctive content. A blanket styling prohibition can suppress quality even after D1.

**Order of work:** Start with D4 and the minimal D3 core, including backend isolation. Then repair verification alongside D1, D2 and D7. Bring forward D8’s functional fixes and bounded D5; finish targeted D6 warnings afterward. Defer automatic route sizing, general collection persistence and broad clock-flow analysis. Keeping Snapback repairs separate is reasonable, but verification defects belong in this program.

**Risks and measurement:** D1’s viewport substitution threatens CSS expectations; D3 can recreate the reading tax through excessive recipes; noisy warnings can add repair turns. Round 2 also changes backend, prompts and model assignments, so it cannot cleanly attribute improvement to this RFC. Preserve model assignments for paired comparisons and report the backend change separately, ideally with a REST baseline. Treat projected score gains as hypotheses.

**READY WITH CHANGES:** constrain D1 to presentation, strengthen real-app verification, add backend isolation, shrink mandatory onboarding further, preserve intentional styling, and advance bounded persistence plus functional fixes ahead of broad warnings.
