# Blind review: LLP 1151 r1

Reviewer note: I read the RFC whole, the gist whole, the named README sections, both app proofs, `verify.rs`/`explore`, the grammar's "Authored tests", and the rules. Claims below about today's code cite `path:line`; RFC/gist citations are to the documents under review.

The headline counts check out and are not the problem: 59 entries under `apps/`, proof files of 67–159 lines ([semantics/Apps/Proofs/PhotoEditor.lean](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/Apps/Proofs/PhotoEditor.lean), [TypeTour.lean](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/Apps/Proofs/TypeTour.lean), [UpdateLab.lean](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/Apps/Proofs/UpdateLab.lean) at 159 lines) over embeddings of 26–51 lines, and the twelve curated app theorems in [semantics/README.md:344](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/README.md:344). What fails is the mechanism of stage 1 itself.

## 1. Fundamental issues (most serious first)

### Issue 1 — D2's hidden derive destroys the signal it claims to reuse, and its error case is unattributable as described

The RFC says a property that fails to evaluate "refuses the commit it is settled in" and "the harness recognises a refusal whose reason names `@always:`" ([llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:150](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:150)). Today's code gives the harness no such reason to recognise:

- In the semantics, a derive whose body errors throws that error out of settlement entirely ([semantics/Contract/Runtime.lean:264](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/Contract/Runtime.lean:264): `| .error e => throw e`), so the commit refuses for the *body's* reason (a type error, an unbound name), not for a reason naming the derive. Only the wrong-*type* branch names it (`derive \`{d.name}\` of the wrong type`). `items[0]` on an empty list is not that branch.
- On the runner the same failure is a VM trap carrying only a program counter ([runner/src/runner/settlement.rs:346](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/runner/src/runner/settlement.rs:346): `Err(t) => return Err(t.into())`), and the derive-type refusal names the derive only in a Rust error the observation prints as a `#` debug line ([semantics/difftest/src/observe.rs:251](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/difftest/src/observe.rs:251)).
- Both sides put refusal reasons on `#` lines, and the comparison explicitly discards them ([semantics/Contract/Observe.lean:77](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/Contract/Observe.lean:77); [semantics/difftest/src/lib.rs:240](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/difftest/src/lib.rs:240): "`#` lines are commentary (a refusal's reason), never compared").

So a non-total property is indistinguishable, in the compared text, from the app itself refusing — exactly the confusion the report format exists to prevent. Worse, the RFC contradicts itself on the true/false split: it says the harness "reads `false` on either side as a violation" and, one sentence later, that runner-true/Lean-false "is a divergence, reported as one" ([llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:144](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:144)). In today's harness those are different verdicts (`Verdict::Diverge` vs. a property verdict that does not exist), and a derive line that differs *is* a divergence ([semantics/difftest/src/lib.rs:295](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/difftest/src/lib.rs:295)). A heavy property adds a third misclassification: a runner `IterationLimit` trap at the derive is classified `OUTSIDE`, not a violation ([semantics/difftest/src/lib.rs:287](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/difftest/src/lib.rs:287)).

The RFC also misstates the settlement cost it does concede. Lean settlement's pass bound is `p.derives.length + p.resources.length + 2` ([semantics/Contract/Runtime.lean:294](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/Contract/Runtime.lean:294)), so one more derive *does* add a pass of headroom, contra "adds none" ([llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:157](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:157)). And because the derive enters the expanded root, the `verify` compilation and the checked-in embedding compilation must now diverge: `difftest apps` keeps `Apps/*.lean` current against the plain expansion, so stage 1 silently creates two expansions of every verified app, one of which no proof in `Apps/Proofs/` is about.

**Resolution:** do not settle properties inside the program. Either (a) evaluate the property body as a read-only post-commit expression on both sides with its own outcome line (`property @always:<n> ok|false|error <reason>`), so error, violation, and divergence are three distinct lines; or (b) keep the derive but add a dedicated property outcome channel and prove the attribution works on a corpus case whose property traps. Option (a) is the design the RFC rejects at [llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:164](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:164) for needing "an expression evaluator in the harness" — but both evaluators already exist (the runner's VM and Lean's `eval`); what is needed is a *call* to each, not a third evaluator. That objection does not survive contact with `eval`'s signature.

### Issue 2 — `never refuses` cannot be attributed to an action by the mechanism D3 specifies

D3 checks a refusal "at every step whose dispatch reached a handler naming the action (the plan's handler at the observed node, as `observe::site` resolves it)" ([llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:200](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:200)). Three facts in today's code break this:

1. One observed step is up to two commits. `dispatchAt` runs due work first, then the event, and "the outcome is the event's when it failed, else the due work's" ([semantics/Contract/Observe.lean:131](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/Contract/Observe.lean:131)). A timer's or `then`'s refusal inside a tap step therefore wears the tap's step label, and a refused due-work commit followed by a successful tap reports `ok`. The outcome line names no action at all ([semantics/Contract/Observe.lean:79](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/Contract/Observe.lean:79)).
2. `observe::site` does not resolve a handler. It replays the script on a fresh runner and returns a plan *node* index ([semantics/difftest/src/observe.rs:266](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/difftest/src/observe.rs:266)), returns `None` once the replay is poisoned ([semantics/difftest/src/observe.rs:278](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/difftest/src/observe.rs:278)), and says nothing about which action a node's handler names, what its curried arguments evaluated to, or whether delivery even reached a handler (a target with no handler for the event kind falls through to a different delivery path, [semantics/difftest/src/observe.rs:302](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/difftest/src/observe.rs:302)).
3. A tap is more than the action — the gist itself notes the handler's curried arguments are evaluated before the action and either can refuse ([target/review-1148/gist.md:135](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/target/review-1148/gist.md:135)). D3 as written blames the action for an argument refusal and clears it for a due-work refusal.

The RFC concedes the clock half of this ("an action a clock advance runs is not attributed", [llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:209](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:209)) but the same conflation exists *inside* every tap step, not only on clock steps.

**Resolution:** stage 1 needs the runner (and `Observe`) to record, per commit, the action name that ran or `none` (due work / task / `then` / dispatch) — a small observation addition — before any `never refuses` checking ships. Without it, drop `never refuses` from stage 1 rather than ship an attribution that is wrong in both directions.

### Issue 3 — D1 admits persisted states that the gist excluded, and both D3 and D6 then lie about them

The gist's first version reads "only root states that are neither persisted nor a mutation's" ([target/review-1148/gist.md:178](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/target/review-1148/gist.md:178)), for a stated reason: "any stored value of the right type is restored" ([target/review-1148/gist.md:184](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/target/review-1148/gist.md:184)). The RFC widens this to "persisted or not" ([llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:106](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:106)) and defers the consequence to D11. The consequence is immediate, in stage 1's own machinery:

- `SlotOrigin` gives a persisted state a third origin — *any* value conforming to its type ([semantics/Contract/Invariant.lean:540](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/Contract/Invariant.lean:540)). The hand proofs survive this only by case-splitting it away because their slots are not persisted ([semantics/Apps/Proofs/UpdateLab.lean:101](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/Apps/Proofs/UpdateLab.lean:101): "The third origin is a persisted state's stored value: not this state"). For a persisted slot, D6's generated boot obligation is "every conforming stored value satisfies the property," which is false for essentially every property an author would write (`counter >= 1` fails on a stored `0`). D6 will report these "unproved" forever, indistinguishable from non-inductive properties.
- D3's search boots from an empty store (the corpus convention, [semantics/README.md:811](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/README.md:811)), and D11's answer — "the search checks it from an empty store and from the stored cases the corpus has" ([llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:335](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:335)) — means a green stage-1 run on a persisted property has checked the one store the device is *least* interestingly in. The report's "in the semantics' world" line ([llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:217](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:217)) does not say "from an empty store," so this reads as coverage it is not.

**Resolution:** adopt the gist's exclusion in stage 1 — the checker refuses an `always` that reads a persisted state, naming D11 as the stage that admits it. This is one lint, it makes D6's recipe honest, and it costs nothing the RFC's own examples need (neither Type Tour's `screen` nor Update Lab's `counter` is persisted).

### Issue 4 — The walk's alphabet is fixed at boot, so the search cannot reach most of what it claims to cover, at an unstated Lean cost

The walk draws events "over the controls the explore observation showed" ([llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:178](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:178)). Explore's alphabet is the `testId`s visible *at boot*, tapped twice, then two clock advances ([semantics/difftest/src/lib.rs:455](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/difftest/src/lib.rs:455)). Any control that appears only after navigation — the settings screen's controls in the RFC's own Type Tour example, whose five screens are reached by tapping through `lock` → `home` → … — is never in the alphabet, because the walk, like explore, never re-observes. A 40-step walk over boot controls mostly re-taps the lock screen. D4's "names the ones it never ran" ([llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:223](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:223)) will then fire for most actions of most apps, which authors will read as noise, not signal — the opposite of the report discipline D4 wants.

The cost is also hidden. Each walk is a `Case` through `check`, which runs Lean per batch ([semantics/difftest/src/lib.rs:130](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/difftest/src/lib.rs:130)); today's verify batches 16 cases ([semantics/difftest/src/verify.rs:102](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/difftest/src/verify.rs:102)) and builds Lean before anything runs ([semantics/difftest/src/verify.rs:49](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/difftest/src/verify.rs:49)). Twenty walks of 40 steps is twenty more cases per app, and shrinking a violation re-runs Lean once per candidate round ([semantics/difftest/src/lib.rs:512](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/difftest/src/lib.rs:512)). No runtime estimate appears anywhere in §4.

**Resolution:** make the walk stateful — re-observe after each step and draw the next event from the *current* view's controls (the runner already exposes this; it is how `explore` gets its boot list). That one change fixes reachability and makes `--steps` mean something. Publish a measured wall-clock for `contract verify` on Type Tour with walks on, before defaults of 20×40 are set.

### Issue 5 — Stage 1 builds the apparatus before measuring the demand the gist says decides everything, and D5 cannot measure it

The gist's order is explicit: "The first step measures demand: the properties authors would state are written for apps chosen at random, before any proof is tried. That count decides whether the rest is worth doing" ([target/review-1148/gist.md:230](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/target/review-1148/gist.md:230)). The RFC inverts this: stage 1 builds grammar, a compiler entry, a walk, shrinking, reports, and suggestions (D1–D5, §4's five work items), and *then* claims "measuring demand … is stage 1's D5 run over the apps with test files" ([llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:398](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:398)).

That measurement is unsound twice over. First, the population: only 10 app directories carry a `*.test.contract` today, and they are self-selected (the apps whose authors already write tests). Second, the instrument: D5's suggestions are summaries of configurations the search happened to reach ("`screen` is one of `lock`, `home`, …" from 212 commits, [llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:245](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:245)). An author keeping a suggestion that restates the trace proves only that the trace is unsurprising; it cannot reveal whether an author would have *stated* a property that catches a mistake, which is the gist's actual question ("whether D7's counterexamples would have caught their mistakes"). A suggestion engine also cannot propose the relational properties that dominate the real proofs — `increment_adds_one` and `reset_spec` are `after`-properties ([semantics/README.md:349](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/README.md:349)), deferred to D7 — so the demand signal is collected only for the weakest property form.

There is also a rules problem the RFC waves at. It asserts Charlie's 2026-10-27 instruction "is the approval `rules/RULES.md` 'Agents' asks for a design document and the apparatus it adds" ([llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:420](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:420)). The rule requires "explicit human approval saying so" for an agent PR that "adds a check, script, registry, config file, or design doc" ([rules/RULES.md:63](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/rules/RULES.md:63)); an instruction to write an RFC and implement its first stage plausibly *is* that approval for stage 1, but stages 2–4 are specified in D6–D12 detail with, by the RFC's own admission, no implementer ([llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:7](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:7)) — and "a spec needs an implementer and a date, or it isn't written" ([rules/RULES.md:57](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/rules/RULES.md:57)). D6–D12 as written are exactly the speculative-spec corpus [rules/DEFERRED.md:1666](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/rules/DEFERRED.md:1666) forbids. They should be cut to a paragraph of direction, or each given an owner and date.

**Resolution:** reorder. First slice = the gist's measurement, done by hand plus D5's *suggestion printer alone* run over existing `verify` traces (no new syntax — print candidate `always` blocks as text), over apps chosen at random, with keep/discard and would-it-have-caught-a-diary-mistake recorded. That result, not this RFC's say-so, decides whether D1–D3 are built.

## 2. Is stage 1 the right first slice?

No. Stage 1 (D1–D5) is the *second* slice. It spends the grammar, compiler, and harness budget before the one measurement that the source document says gates the rest, and two of its five parts (D2's mechanism, D3's `never refuses`) cannot work as specified (Issues 1–2), so the spend is partly on machinery that must be redesigned.

The slice that buys the most verification per hour:

1. **Demand measurement first** (days, no syntax): hand-write `always` properties for a random sample of apps, run D5-style suggestion printing over existing traces, record keep rates and diary-mistake catches. This is the gist's first step and the RFC's own §5 criterion — just actually first.
2. **Runner-only property checking on authored tests + explore** (the cheap half of D3/D4): a property evaluator invoked post-commit on the runner, checked at every step of scripts that already exist, with the report naming unexercised actions. No Lean embedding change, no walk, no shrinking. This delivers "a property as cheap to write as an `expect`, checked at every point of every script" for the scripts authors already wrote, and it works when Lean is absent — which today is a hard exit, not a fallback ([contract/cli/src/verify.rs:79](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/contract/cli/src/verify.rs:79); [semantics/difftest/src/verify.rs:49](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/difftest/src/verify.rs:49)).
3. **Then** the differential half (property evaluated on the semantics per Issue 1's resolution), the stateful walk (Issue 4), and `never refuses` on top of per-commit action attribution (Issue 2).

That ordering front-loads the two claims authors will actually feel — properties stated beside tests, violations named with a step and a line — and defers every mechanism this review flags as broken until its prerequisite instrumentation exists.

## 3. The open questions

**Q1. Reading a derive in a property — refuse if the derive reads a resource?**
Yes, refuse, and transitively. The checker should refuse an `always` whose body reads, through any chain of derives or `fn`s, a resource, a mutation's status, or the clock. A derive reading a resource settles from the oracle's answer ([semantics/Contract/Runtime.lean:284](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/Contract/Runtime.lean:284): `o.ask`), and a derive reading `performanceNow()` reads `env.now` ([semantics/Contract/Eval.lean:164](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/Contract/Eval.lean:164)), which changes on an `advance` that commits nothing — the exact failure D1's clock ban exists to prevent ([llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:108](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/llp/1151-properties-beside-the-app.rfc.md:108)), except D1 only bans the clock "directly or through a `fn`", not through a derive. Admitting resource-reading derives makes search verdicts verdicts about the seeded oracle's invented answers. *Recommendation: refuse transitive resource/mutation-status/clock reads in stage 1; revisit only with D11's world. Confidence: high.*

**Q2. Should the walk draw typed values from the app's test blocks?**
Yes — it is the only source of values known to reach anything. An input's kind gives a shape, not a term the data has; the test blocks are where authors already wrote the terms that matter, and reusing them costs a scan of steps the verify run already parsed. But typed values are the lesser half of the reachability problem: without re-observation (Issue 4) the walk cannot even *find* the inputs on later screens. *Recommendation: draw from test blocks first, fall back to kind-shaped values, and only once the walk re-observes per step. Confidence: high that test-block values help; high that they are insufficient alone.*

**Q3. Should the runner's outcome line name the action a task ran, so the search covers the clock?**
Yes, and sooner than the question implies: per Issue 2, the outcome must name the action (or `none`/due-work) for *every* commit, not only clock ones, or `never refuses` is unsound even for taps. This is a small, well-scoped observation change — the runner knows the action at `runAction`; the semantics' `dispatch`/`advance` split ([semantics/Contract/Invariant.lean:638](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/Contract/Invariant.lean:638)) knows it too — and it should land as a prerequisite, in stage 1, before `never refuses` checking. *Recommendation: add `action <name>` (or `action -`) per commit to both observations now. Confidence: high.*

**Q4. Where suggestions stop — what is missed, what would no one keep?**
What is missed matters more than what is over-printed. D5's catalogue (≤6 distinct values, whole numbers, one bound per side, never-`none`) cannot express the properties the existing proofs show authors need: relations between states (`turns` changes only under `rotate`, [semantics/README.md:353](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/README.md:353)) and postconditions (`increment_adds_one`, [semantics/README.md:349](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-verify/semantics/README.md:349)) — the latter is D7's form, so stage 1's suggestion engine structurally cannot surface the most valuable class. It will also print trace tautologies at scale: every enum-like state in every app yields a "one of N values" block, and "always at least its smallest observed value" is a property of the sample, not the app — authors will learn to ignore the whole list, taking the good suggestions with it. *Recommendation: keep the four forms, but (a) suppress any suggestion that the app's checker already guarantees (a bool is always a bool-style noise), (b) add one relational form — "state X changed only in commits of actions {…}" — which is observable from traces and matches a real proof shape, and (c) measure the keep rate per form in the demand study and cut forms nobody keeps. Confidence: medium (the keep-rate data does not exist yet; that is the point of measuring first).*

## 4. Verdict

**NOT READY** — Issues 1 and 2 are mechanisms that cannot work as described and must be redesigned (property evaluation channel; per-commit action attribution) before stage 1 is landed, and Issue 5's reordering should put the demand measurement ahead of the apparatus.
