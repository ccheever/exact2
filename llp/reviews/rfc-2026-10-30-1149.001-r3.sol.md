1. **A1’s in-flight count does not establish nonblocking drawable acquisition.**

   RFC §3/M3, lines 203–208, promises that counting in-flight drawables makes `nextDrawable` “never a wait.” The proposed count limits application demand; it does not reserve an available drawable or bound allocation, surface-transition, or Core Animation delays.

   The existing implementation deliberately distinguishes these mechanisms: `Ring::acquire` waits for command completion, then `nextDrawable` acquires the drawable. Neither completion nor the count is a nonblocking acquisition operation. Windows’s rule uses an actual latency waitable and reserves the successful acquisition; G6c is therefore not evidence for the proposed Metal guarantee. (`host/ink/src/device/metal/present.rs:281–295,392–408`; `device/apple.rs:49–55`; RESULTS G6c, lines 2630–2639.)

   Specify an enforceable mechanism—for example, acquisition on the worker followed by a drawable handoff that main can check without waiting. Preserve pending damage, ordered uploads and retirement when presentation is skipped, and the live-resize transaction sequence. Those requirements remain relevant under D11. (`present.rs:247–259,309–321`; parent D7, line 476; D11, lines 553–560.)

2. **A1 improves capture semantics substantially, but still needs a complete snapshot and frame-matching rule.**

   The mid-animation answer is now useful: capture the last encoded sample without advancing or settling time. The two comparisons are also the right ones: offscreen rendering against tiny-skia, and the drawable against the offscreen rendering of that same sample. This answers substantive round-2 findings. (RFC §3/M3, lines 212–224.)

   However, “batches and instance buffer with its resources pinned” still needs to cover the **complete frame**, including clear/load state, dimensions, and ordered passes. `flush()` consumes a separate `Frame`; destination sampling can flush part of the root before later batches are encoded. Retaining only the final flush cannot reconstruct the earlier root contents. Either retain the complete render/copy/effect sequence and its dependencies, or explicitly retain/read the compose result for those frames. (`host/linux/src/ink/effects.rs:5–21,43–50`; `ink.rs:642–684`.)

   The asynchronous parity comparison also needs a frame identity and ownership rule. Its snapshot must survive until that frame’s comparison completes, even if another frame has been encoded meanwhile. “Until … the next frame is encoded” is insufficient for that case. The worker can suppress older queued presentations, and encoding and presentation are separate events. (`host/ink/src/present.rs:59–89`; RFC lines 214–224.)

   Finally, the proposed GPU copy requires a test-build change to the layer’s `framebufferOnly(true)` configuration, plus BGRA/RGBA normalization. Name that configuration and keep its measurement overhead out of ordinary performance samples. (`device/apple.rs:50–52`.)

3. **A2’s whole-pixel translation does not yet prove the promised fresh-walk equality.**

   The diagnosis is sound: Ink rejects fractional device-pixel displacement, and that test precedes the painter’s other validity checks. The kernel has per-node flags and commit/layout receipts; the painter derives row `stale` and `suspect` state. Placement-only changes and image-slot replacement already have separate handling. R3 now describes this correctly. (`kernel/src/id.rs:55–82`, `txn.rs:53–87`, `layout.rs:52–65`; `paint/rows.rs:175–230,433–466,491–549`; `ink/rows.rs:287–303`.)

   The proposed remedy still has two problems:

   - **Recording origin matters.** Glyph phases are chosen before capture translates instances into row space, and `row_local()` is false. Rounding the absolute replay origin is not equivalent to rounding displacement from the original recording. A row first recorded at a fractional origin retains that origin’s raster phase. Define the coordinate rule explicitly. (`ink/text.rs:27–49`; `ink.rs:118–125,726–728`; `ink/rows.rs:312–321`.)
   - **Vertical truncation is not invariant under integer translation across zero.** For example, a recorded baseline at `0.25` truncates to `0`; translating its bitmap by `−1` gives `−1`, whereas a fresh baseline at `−0.75` truncates to `0`. Thus even an integral displacement does not universally establish the equality asserted at RFC lines 230–235. (`ink/text.rs:28–29`.)

   Choose a phase-correct recording/replay rule, with fallback where required, or declare the changed moving-text policy under D3/D9 and qualify it. Cover fractional recording origins, negative coordinates, noninteger scales, clips and image arrival.

   Also specify how the exact settled frame is requested. Currently a clean display tick requests no draw. A row’s final moving frame can remain snapped unless pending snapped rows create one final redraw themselves. (`host/apple-painted/src/window.rs:335–347`; RFC lines 232–235.)

   M7’s immutable-range lifetime and fallback for every non-scissor outer clip **do** answer round 2. A2 should stop calling snapping itself a D4 contingency: D4 proposes per-draw placement, not this new quantization policy. (RFC lines 238–244,315–318; parent D4, lines 318–337.)
