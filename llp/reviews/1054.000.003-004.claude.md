## LLP 1054.000.003 — Dates and relative time in the roster

### Assessment
Careful design with exact Intl reference outputs, but it admits four entries and eleven styles for one out-of-repo consumer, with no in-repo time-varying date label. It also skips the simplest D8 fix: time-invariant labels shipped by the source plus a Contract `fn` that selects between them compiles today with no format change. And its recommended "what an app writes" pattern shows blank labels for the first 30 s.

### Strengths
- **Intl outputs are exact.** Every quoted output was verified against Node 26.9.0 / ICU 78.3: all `dateStyle`s, `month-day`, `weekday`, `timeStyle` short/medium, the `" at "` / `", "` joiners, and all three `RelativeTimeFormat` styles. U+0020 before `PM` confirmed.
- **`numeric: "always"` is the right call.** Checked: `"auto"` gives `yesterday`, `last mo.`, `last yr.`, `last wk.`.
- **The argument against a `list<Label>` source is correct and stronger than stated.** Besides "no indexing", a child component cannot own a per-row resource: probe `/tmp/rv/rowres.contract` gives `type-child-resource`, "a resource, mutation, or task lives in the root".
- **Nothing crosses the seam and no host changes.** Web `glue.js:1362`, Apple `Session.swift:787-790`, Linux `host/linux/src/app.rs:288-296` already deliver the fact. The runner is shared, so the bytes are identical on every host.
- **Offset versus zone is honest.** Relative time is correctly offset-free.

### Concerns
1. **MATERIAL — The consumer bar is not met in-repo.**
   - No in-repo app reads `exactTime`: grep of `apps/` and `contract/corpus` is empty.
   - Typetour's `"12m ago"` is mock lock-screen text (`apps/typetour/app.contract:72-75`).
   - RealWorld's date is time-invariant and formatted once in TS (`apps/realworld/app.ts:22-26`).
   - Messages' labels are fixture strings (`apps/messages/app.ts:10-15`).
   - Caltrain already has its formatter.
   - So D8 exists only in Bluesky, which is not admitted (LLP 1054.000 "Consumer bar").
2. **MATERIAL — The simpler alternative is unexamined, and "Why not a Contract `fn`" is partly wrong.**
   - `"Sep 12"`, `"Saturday"`, `"1:02 PM"` and `"9/12/26"` are time-invariant given the timestamp and offset, so the source can make them once. Only the *choice* between them is time-varying, and that needs only `floor` arithmetic. Calendar days are `floor((n+off*60000)/864e5) - floor((t+off*60000)/864e5)`.
   - Bluesky's `Post` already carries `time` and `date` strings (`shapes.contract` `Post`).
   - Probe `/tmp/rv/ago.contract` reproduces Bluesky's exact `ago` in a `fn`: bare `5m`, `now` under 5 s, then the source's date label. Cost is +360 plan bytes per call site, with no roster change.
   - `fn`s compose (`fn.contract:7` `quad(twice(twice(n)))`), and Hinnant's civil-from-days is closed-form. Probe `/tmp/rv/probe.contract` compiles a full `"Sep 26, 2026"` formatter in `fn`s at about 1.9 KB per site; built, not run.
   - So "can't" should read "costs plan bytes per site". Note also that a `fn` charges only the app that uses it, while roster bodies are linked into every app because `stdlib::call` is one `match` (`runner/src/stdlib.rs:14-96`). That cuts against LLP 1047.
3. **MATERIAL — Styles without fixtures ("by fixture, never by speculation", `format.json:823`).**
   - There is no fixture for `formatDate "full"`, `formatTime "medium"`, or `formatRelativeTime "short"`/`"long"`. Typetour's `"Sunday, September 14"` has no year, so it is not `full`. D1's "Each has one fixture" is false.
   - The one real fixture is not served. Bluesky prints `Sep 3` this year and `Sep 3, 2024` otherwise (`data/src/fmt.rs:139-145`), and the proposed entries give no way to tell "same year". Bluesky's bare `5m` is refused (open question 2).
4. **MATERIAL — The "What an app writes" pattern shows `""` for up to 30 s after launch.**
   - `every` first fires at `now + interval` (`runner/src/runner.rs:739`; LLP 1006:233). `minute` starts at 0, and D3 rule 1 maps `nowMs ≤ 0` to `""`.
   - It also lags `exactTime`'s late arrival on the web: the glue sets it after the first batch, `glue.js:1362`.
   - Use a derive, as Bluesky does (`app.contract:42`). A derive's dependents re-run only when its value changes (`deps.rs:283-289`, `compare::same`).
   - The citation `deps.rs:315-317` is the clock bit, not slot changes (`deps.rs:276-279`).
5. **MINOR — Future timestamps.** AT Protocol `createdAt` is client-set, so clock skew produces `"in 3s"`. Bluesky's `ago` maps negative ages to `now` (`fmt.rs:117-120`). Document the clamp `formatRelativeTime(min(t, n), n, …)`.
6. **MINOR — Seconds with a 30 s tick.** `"5s ago"` freezes for up to 30 s. Document guarding by the tick's granularity.
7. **MINOR — DST.**
   - The example is backwards: a winter timestamp under a summer (DST) offset shows one hour *later*, so 23:30 becomes 00:30 the next day, not the previous day.
   - The offset is also fixed at launch on all three hosts; none watches zone changes (`Session.swift:787-790`, `glue.js:1362`, `app.rs:288-296`). Declare it.
8. **MINOR — The admitted range forces Intl quirks.** With ±8.64e15 admitted, Intl prints year-of-era with no era for years ≤ 0 (checked: `-62198755200000` gives `"Jan 1, 2"`, ISO year −1; `8.64e15` gives `"Sep 13, 275760"`). Engines have historically differed before 1582 (Julian vs proleptic Gregorian). Narrow to years 1–9999, or 1970 onward.
9. **MINOR — Overstated precision.**
   - "Every output is Intl's" holds only for rendering a (value, unit) pair. The buckets, 30.4375, 365.2425 and `"now"` (a `numeric: "auto"` case) are exact2's.
   - The month formula is ambiguous: fractional versus floored days differ (60.9 days gives 2 fractional, 1 floored).
   - The oracle is pinned Node/CLDR data, not "the browser".
10. **MINOR — `calendarDays` on bad input is undefined.** It returns a number, so D5's `""` doesn't apply. Before the date arrives, the D4 `when` fn prints weekday names (`calendarDays(t, 0, off)` is hugely negative, so `< 7`).
11. **MINOR — D6 blocks threading a style through `fn` parameters or props.** Tested with the existing precedent: `fn go2(name) = path(name, "1")` is refused even when called with a literal. The closer precedents are `routes::location` / `path()` (`contract/types/src/routes.rs:123-163`), not `first` at `lib.rs:710`, which is a return-type rule. State the limitation.
12. **MINOR — The take.**
    - `formatClockTime` isn't byte-identical: `NaN` gives `"12:00 AM"` via `as i64` (`stdlib.rs:115`), where D5 says `""`.
    - `contract/cli/tests/it/diagnostics.rs:842-843` also names it.
    - Removing a subsumed entry is a rename, not a doing-list trade. None is needed anyway: the real gate is the fixture rule.
13. **MINOR — "Ten resources" is overstated, and one claim is wrong.**
    - `unread` and `hasNew` use the minute as a *poll key* (`data/src/reads.rs:262-289`, `651-663`) and keep it after migration.
    - "written without `core::fmt`, as `stdlib.rs:108-109`" is wrong: `stdlib.rs:54, 66, 125` still use `format!`.

### Suggestions
- For Bluesky, try the `fn` route first: `createdAt: number` plus static labels, with a view `fn` selecting between them. That fixes D8 with no format change.
- If Charlie still wants roster entries, trim to fixtures: `formatRelativeTime "narrow"`, `formatDate "medium" | "month-day" | "weekday"`, `formatTime "short"`. Drop `calendarDays`, which is a one-line `fn`.
- Narrow the epoch range, define `calendarDays` on bad input, fix the app pattern (use a derive), and document the `min` clamp and the tick guard.
- Report the hello-app wasm delta (LLP 1047 D9).
- Bundle with any other format change into one `FORMAT_DIGEST` bump, one update cohort (`bake/src/compat.rs:300`).

### Answers to the open questions
1. **Consumer.** No: the in-repo uses are cosmetic or already served. Wait for Bluesky's admission, and even then try the `fn` route first.
2. **`narrow-bare`.** No as a roster style: it isn't Intl and has one fixture. That the only consumer needs it is evidence for the `fn` route.
3. **Seconds threshold.** No threshold in the entry. Document `n - t < tick ? "now" : …` and `min(t, n)`.

### Verdict
**NOT READY.** It becomes READY WITH CHANGES if Bluesky is admitted, the scope is cut to fixtures, and the `fn` alternative is weighed in the text.

## LLP 1054.000.004 — Narrower refreshes and bounded feeds

### Assessment
D1 keeps the seam clean, and its in-flight rule is sound. But the assertion it rests on ("adds or removes no rows anywhere") is violated by the consumers the RFC names, and a violation fails as silent staleness. Its cost premise cites pre-fix transfer numbers. It comes before its own measurement and skips the static narrowing that existing syntax already gives. D2/C5 is reasonable but serves only Bluesky.

### Strengths
- **Nothing crosses the seam.** Correct: touches live in the plan, the VM output and `PendingReq` only.
- **Rule 2 is right.** "In flight → asked anyway", evaluated at reply time against current values, closes the staleness hole that 1054.000.000 D3's kept request would open. A pre-write GET is always forced, never kept.
- **Checkpoint is covered.** Send-evaluated keys ride on `PendingReq`, which the checkpoint holds (`commit.rs:16-45`).
- **The walk is sound.** Records have one type row per shape name (`contract/lower/src/lib.rs:625-639`), so the type-indexed walk is sound and reachability pruning is cheap.
- **Refusing the normalized store is correct.** The D3 trigger is honest.

### Concerns
1. **BLOCKER — The assertion is violated by the RFC's own consumers, and the failure is silent staleness.** Tests line 300 says Bluesky's like, repost and delete sends narrow. They shouldn't:
   - A like adds a row to the viewer's Likes tab (`FilterTab "likes"`, `screens.contract:333`; `authorFeeds` with the `likes` filter, `data/src/reads.rs:323`) and to `people` in liked-by (`getLikes`, `reads.rs:694`). Neither list contains the post beforehand.
   - A repost adds to reposted-by and to the viewer's author feed.
   - A delete changes `Profile.posts`, a count; a `Profile` holds no `Post`.
   - The walk sees only existing occurrences, never membership or aggregates. Authors must predict every such effect for every declared resource at its current arguments, and the RFC's own example gets it wrong.
2. **MATERIAL — The cost premise is stale.**
   - F5's 3.75 ms (1,000 rows, "capture replacer as shipped") and 108 ms (25k) predate `f4e7a671` (2026-09-21), which builds paths only for captured strings (`js/src/prelude.js:222-252`). They also predate `51fb550e` (2026-09-18), direct shape-directed decode (`js/src/lib.rs:587-603`).
   - Current figures are the "path only" and "direct decode" rows: 1.40 ms + 0.72 ms per 1,000 synthetic rows, Mac Hermes CLI.
   - The "observation for the executor's owners" is already done.
   - D1 closes no open finding: D2 was closed by 1054.000.000.
3. **MATERIAL — Unmeasured, and ordered before its own trigger.**
   - After .003 (or the `fn` route) and bounded answers, the residual saving is mounted, non-empty resources that don't hold the record. Resources whose route isn't showing already answer at once (RealWorld's `article`/`comments` return immediately on an empty slug, `apps/realworld/app.ts:107, 114`).
   - RealWorld's real saving is one comments GET per favorite on the article page, so the four-way "demonstration" is thin.
   - Gate D1 on D3's iPhone measurement.
4. **MATERIAL — The simpler alternative wasn't considered: split `mutation change` by kind of write, not by resource.**
   - Today it serves like, follow, seen, markRead, repost and delete (`app.contract:212-393`).
   - Like would be `refreshes homeFeeds, authorFeeds, threads, notes, results`: `Profile`, `People`, `Convos` and `Chats` can't hold a `Post`. `seen` and `markRead` would be two resources each.
   - That is static narrowing with existing syntax. LLP 1035.005 §1 step 3 ("remove repetition with the constructs that exist") precedes step 4 (new syntax), which the RFC invokes.
5. **MATERIAL — `lint-touching-unreachable` is new apparatus and misfires.** The compiler has no warning channel (no `lint-` id or warn path in `contract/*/src`). On the RFC's own like send it would fire four times (`profiles`, `people`, `convos`, `chats`), each the *intended* narrowing.
6. **MINOR — Unmentioned paths that must carry touches.**
   - The "one more round" re-enqueue (`commit.rs:715-722`) and `release_refused`.
   - Key comparison should be the VM's `==` (-0 equals 0; NaN never matches; cf. `instance.rs:1209-1219`).
   - The journal is read by `logs`, not `state` (`runner/src/agent.rs:31, 49`).
7. **MINOR — "The Bluesky data layer is moving to TypeScript" has no evidence in the port.** It's a Rust crate, and `DIARY.md:46` records choosing Rust. Cite the direction if it's Charlie's.
8. **MINOR — D2 glosses the feed design.** `homeFeeds` is one resource holding every pinned feed (`app.contract:76`), while 1027.004's cursor window is per list. Either a window move re-asks K×F rows, or the resource splits per feed. The source must also keep all loaded pages to resolve cursors (1027.004 D2).
9. **MINOR — C5's "second consumer" isn't one.** Messages' transcript (`QUEUE.md:122`) is one list and needs no bound arguments. Bluesky is C5's only consumer.
10. **MINOR — Plan format.** The `Send` operand change touches every plan's bytecode, so bundle it with .003 into one digest bump. 1054.000.000, which this amends, is marked "Not reviewed" (`1054.000.000:172-173`).

### Suggestions
- Withdraw D1 for now.
- Split mutations by kind of write in Bluesky and RealWorld.
- Land C5 as its own small LLP.
- Run D3's measurement after the date fix and bounded feeds.
- If D1 returns:
  - Restate the assertion per resource: "no declared resource, at its current arguments, gains or loses a row or changes a field outside a matching record".
  - Prune unreachable resources statically, with no diagnostic.
  - Argue from saved network refetches, the real cost for network-backed sources, not from JSON crossing.
- Review 1054.000.000 first.

### Answers to the open questions
1. **Consumer bar.** RealWorld isn't enough (about one GET per favorite or comment-delete). D1 waits for Bluesky's admission *and* a measured over-budget like after mutations are split by kind. With nothing named to come off, the rules say no.
2. **C5.** Yes, in its narrow form, on its own merits: it fixes D4 (list identity, and progress via `more(f.id, f.cursor)`) and relaxes an arity rule rather than adding syntax (`contract/analyze/src/lib.rs:438-440`). Arguments are evaluated at dispatch in the list node's frames, like `press`. It is gated on Bluesky, its only consumer.
3. **Warn or refuse.** Neither. Unreachable resources are exactly what narrowing should skip. Prune them statically and add no diagnostic channel.

### Verdict
**NOT READY** (D1). D2's C5 recommendation is sound but belongs in its own LLP.
