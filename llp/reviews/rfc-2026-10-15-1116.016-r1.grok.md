## 1. Verdict

**RETHINK.** The end state is right: a Contract expression should mean what that TypeScript expression means, and where it cannot, the diagnostic should name the TypeScript that does. This stage does not implement that rule. It renames `min`/`max` to `Math.min`/`Math.max` even though those functions disagree on `NaN` (`semantics/Contract/Number.lean` `fmin`/`fmax`, `host/web-js/roster.js` lines 6–7), it adds `find` before the `??` its own evidence uses, and it spends a new callback opcode on `toSorted` and `padStart` for demand that shows up in one-shot contracts and in `app.ts`, which is already TypeScript.

## 2. The most important issues

**1. `Math.min` / `Math.max` would compile with another meaning (T4).** Contract's `min`/`max` are Rust `fminnm`/`fmaxnm`: a `NaN` operand yields the other argument (`Number.lean` 158–171; the web roster reimplements that so it will not call `Math.min`). JavaScript's `Math.min(NaN, 1)` is `NaN`, and `Math.min(1, 2, NaN)` is `NaN`. Signed zero already matches (`Math.min(0, -0)` is `-0`, `Math.max(0, -0)` is `+0`), and `floor`/`ceil`/`round` really are JavaScript's (`runner/src/stdlib.rs` `js_round`; `round(-1.5)` is `-1`). Nesting the existing `min` preserves the `NaN` bug and makes it worse with three or more arguments. Same-plan tests would pass and still be wrong, which is the failure LLP 1116.015 §4 T2 already named. Keep the names `min` and `max` until a separate change makes them return `NaN` when any argument is `NaN`, with an `arith` difftest and a check that no app depends on `NaN` being swallowed. That change is a behavior change, not sugar.

**2. The admission evidence is the wrong corpus, and the `app.ts` numbers do not reproduce.** D1 says an operation joins when the census or real builds reach for it. §1 then admits `Math.*` and `padStart` on the data modules, and `toSorted` on the post-D2 guess screens. `app.ts` is already TypeScript: `padStart`, `Math.floor`, and `.sort` already run there. Across `~/bakeoff/guesses/priors1` and `priors2` (112 contracts, scratch-app excluded) the contract side matches the RFC: `.find` 16, `.sort` 2, and `reduce`, `every`, `some`, `toUpperCase`, `Math.max` once each; `null` in 29 of 80 `priors1` screens; `??` or `?.` in 10; `match` on post15 is 22 GPT and 24 Muse. The `app.ts` table does not. Recount, screens / occurrences: `padStart` 22 / 43 against the RFC's 36, `Math.floor` 23 / 35 against 20, `find` 13 / 28 against 19, `sort` 17 / 20 against 11, `localeCompare` 10 / 13 (13 matches occurrences only). By occurrences the leading calls are `trim` 80, `map` 56, `padStart` 43, `filter` 40. Of the 43 `padStart`s, 27 pad a month, day, or year (`formatDate(..., "iso")` already does that) and about a dozen pad a stopwatch or a cent (`formatDecimal` covers cents; `formatTime` is `h:mm AM`, not `mm:ss`). The 469-build refusal list in §1 (`length` 24, `empty` 9, `sum` 2, `flatMap` 2, no `sort`, no `padStart`, no `Math`) is the roster signal. The guess harness has no data module, so it pulls data-module work into the contract.

**3. `find` does not clear the wall that justifies it, and the rewrites are value-honest only.** All 16 unguided `.find`s bind the result as a record (`derive current = members.find(...)`, `members.find(...) ?? members[0]`, `Note | null`). None write `match`. `find` returns `option<T>` via `filter(...).at(0)`; the next error is "this is an option" or "there is no `??`". `some` and `every` return `bool` and do match TypeScript on values, including `every` of an empty list. They are still a full pass: `filter` copies every match, so `xs.find(x => true)` duplicates the list and can hit `IterationLimit` or `ValueTooLarge` where a short-circuit `find` would not. The recipe in `docs/recipes/functions.md` already spells `some` as `xs.map(p).includes(true)`, which copies bools rather than records. Same-plan tests prove the rewrite equals that expansion. They do not prove it equals TypeScript. Difftest cannot see a bad rewrite (LLP 1116.015 §R). Ship `some` and `every` if you want them. Hold `find` for stage 2.

**4. `toSorted` is the expensive row, and the pass condition overstates what it fixes.** Of the 8 post15 `.sort` screens, seven are `(a, b) => a.field - b.field` and one is `.sort()` with no comparator (`grocery-list-2`, string categories). The no-comparator form is the one the RFC correctly refuses: `[10, 9, 1].toSorted()` is `[1, 10, 9]`. Those screens write `.sort`, not `.toSorted`, so a D4 refusal is still a wall on the one-shot corpus. `trivia-quiz-1` also calls `random()` inside the comparator chain. A callback sort is a new opcode beside `Map` and `Filter`. `LowerCorrect.lean` `each_sem` and `Big.lean` `MapR`/`FilterR` are linear passes with a fixed schedule. Sort's comparison count depends on the values, an inconsistent comparator is implementation-defined in ECMA-262, and `SortCompare` reads `NaN` as `+0`, which makes `(a, b) => a - b` inconsistent as soon as any element is `NaN`. A stable sort is uniquely determined only for a consistent comparator. The web runtime must not call `Array.prototype.toSorted` if the runner and Lean pin an algorithm, and it must meter steps the way `host/web-js/budget.js` meters `concat` and `includes` (`step`, bound 65536). `toFixed`'s landing (`d67ba9c20`) is the wrong cost analogue. This is a third host implementation of a callback loop. The real-build census did not ask for it.

**5. D4's suggested repairs can themselves mean something else, and several real next guesses are missing.** `a.localeCompare(b)` led by `a < b ? -1 : a > b ? 1 : 0` compiles and is code-unit order (LLP 1088 D1). That matches TypeScript's `<` and does not match `localeCompare`. The `app.ts` uses are mostly `name` and `date`. ISO dates happen to agree. Names do not (`"Z" < "a"`, accents). LLP 1116.014's "lead with the edit" here leads with an edit that builds a different order. `xs.push(x)` collides with `push(router, location)` (`format.json` `push`). `nav.push("/x")` already falls through `idioms.rs` into the list-`push` refusal. A more confident "write `concat`" message makes the router case worse. `xs[i]` is worth a better diagnostic than "expected end of line, found `[`" (post15 trivia: `game[index].answer`, `questions[questionIndex]`; priors book-club: `members[0].id`). The result is an option, and `at` counts negatives from the end, which is why LLP 1116.015 T4 refused to accept `xs[i]` as `at`. The message has to say both, or the repair is another T4 miss. `questions[i].field` still does not compile until stage 2. Also missing from D4, and already in these screens: `===`, `Date.now` (24 `app.ts` screens, and `now()` is already refused), no-comparator `.sort()`, and `s[0]`.

**6. The token ceiling is real and it argues for a smaller stage, not this one.** Expressions are 0.20 of GPT's 2.06 MB-steps, about 3% (LLP 1116.015 §3). D1 and D2 of that RFC measured level (`ab3`: tokens 1.03, grade 7.3 vs 7.4) and created the `.sort` valley (1 screen to 7). start-here's Expressions bullet is 368 bytes of a 10,658-byte file. The card's function block is 2,212 bytes of 12,176, and most of it is routes, formatting, and the platform, which D5 rightly keeps. A cut near a kilobyte, re-sent every step, sits under the noise LLP 1116.013 could resolve. D5 is the only byte lever in this RFC. It is not the program's lever. Tooling is 0.60 MB-steps and the data protocol is 0.49.

## 3. Per decision

**D1. Change.** Keep the rule as the standard for new roster rows, with the bounds written down. It covers pure total operations on strings, numbers, and lists, where ECMA-262 specifies one value for the values Contract can hold. It does not cover coercion, truthiness, mutation, `this`, exceptions, `Date`, or `Math.random`. Where the specification is implementation-defined, Contract pins one behavior and says so. Where the representation cannot hold the JavaScript value, the deviation is declared the way `toFixed` of a non-finite number already prints `""` (LLP 1102 §3.2) and the way `slice` applies `toWellFormed`. Callbacks take `(element, index)` only, as `map` does today (LLP 1017.003 D1). "Commonly" means Contract sources or real builds, not `app.ts` and not one model generation. LLP 1116.015 §R dropped `Math.*` because unguided contracts wrote it zero times. That reading still holds.

**D2. Change.** Keep `some` and `every` as rewrites, aimed at the recipe's `map` plus `includes`, with tests for empty, all-true, all-false, and a non-bool body whose diagnostic says `.some` or `.every`. State that they are full passes under `MAX_LIST_STEPS`. Hold `find` for stage 2. Keep `floor`, `ceil`, and `round` as the spellings. Refuse `Math.floor(n)` with the edit `floor(n)` until you deliberately rename them. Do not rename `min`/`max`. Drop `first` to `xs.at(0)`: guided post15 writes `first` in 7 screens because the guide teaches it, and unguided contracts write `.find`, not `.at`. `at(0)` is the same function for a literal `0` (`format.json` `first` and `at`), and deleting the readable word is a T6 cost with no census behind it. `performance.now()` is a trivial alias of `performanceNow` (LLP 1109) that no contract screen writes. It is not worth a pseudo-object.

**D3. Drop `toSorted` from this stage. Drop `padStart` / `padEnd` until a Contract screen needs a zero-padded counter.** If `toSorted` comes back, pin a stable algorithm in the runner, Lean, and `budget.js`. Do not call the host's sort. Charge each comparison to `MAX_LIST_STEPS` and trap before a partial list escapes. Specify `SortCompare`, including `NaN` as `+0`. Require the comparator. Treat an inconsistent comparator as specified-by-algorithm, not as "what TypeScript means." For `padStart`, follow `StringPad` including `ToLength` (`NaN` and negatives become 0, fractions truncate toward zero). Return `s` before the bound when the target is shorter or the fill is empty (`"abc".padStart(Infinity, "")` is `"abc"`). Lengths are UTF-16, as `.length` is. A fill that would split a surrogate cannot be stored. `"a".padStart(2, "😀")` is `"\ud83da"` in JavaScript. Declare the same `toWellFormed` deviation as LLP 1088 D2. The bound is `MAX_LIST_STEPS` code units, applied only after those early returns.

**D4. Keep, with a sharper set.** Lead `.sort` with `.toSorted` only once `toSorted` exists. Until then, keep today's "sort in the data module." For `localeCompare`, lead with the fact that Contract has no locale order, and offer `<` as code-unit order, not as a substitute. For `.push`, if the receiver is a `Router`, lead with `push(nav, location)`. For `xs[i]`, lead with `xs.at(i)`, say the result is an option, and say a negative index counts from the end. Add `===` to `==`, and `Date.now()` to the existing `performanceNow()` / `exactTime` refusal. Leave `reduce` and `flatMap` on today's data-module message. The §5 bar belongs in the LLP, not in the diagnostic.

**D5. Keep, small.** Teach the differences: no truthiness, options, no mutation, subtract with spaces, and Exact's own functions. Do not delete `first`, `floor`, or `min` from the card until those renames are real. Count the bytes. Skip the A/B. A kilobyte will not clear LLP 1116.013's noise.

**§6. Change the claim, then write it as its own RFC. Do not block this stage on it, and do not start it only because this stage landed.** "No loss (T1)" is false for nested options. `option<option<T>>` is distinct today (`some(none)` versus `none`). `T | null` collapses them, and `contract/cli/src/typescript.rs` already emits `format!("{} | null", ...)`, so the data boundary already flattens. Stage 2 has to choose flatten or keep a nested option that `| null` cannot spell. `??` must lower to `match`, so the right side does not run when the left is present. That matters for traps, not for purity. Narrowing on `x != null` has to die at an assignment. `if (x)` must stay a type error. The unguided census is real (`null` in 29 of 80, `??` or `?.` in 10) and the guided one is the opposite (post15: `null` in 0, `match` in 46). LLP 1116.015's lesson is that models write what they are shown. The `find` and `questions[i].field` use sites are the actual reason to write the RFC.

## 4. Answers to §9

1. **Yes, with the bounds in D1 above.** "TypeScript's meaning or a refusal" holds for pure total operations where the specification names one value Contract can represent. It breaks on `min`/`max` and `NaN`, lone surrogates, non-finite `toFixed`, implementation-defined sort, coercion, and name collisions (`push`, `replace`). The guide should teach only the differences. It should keep teaching Exact's own library.

2. **No.** Accept a cut-down stage: the D1 bounds, `some` and `every`, and the D4 messages that do not recommend a different meaning. Judge it by the guess corpus's walls and by whether refused TypeScript rises, per model. Do not judge it by total tokens. Do not require the 8 `sort` screens to go green until `toSorted` exists and the guide shows it. One of them is `.sort()` with no comparator and stays a refusal either way.

3. **Write stage 2 when you want the boundary and the surface to agree, and when `find` and `at` are worth their result type.** Not automatically after this stage. The census it was missing is in §1 and it checks out. It shows a spelling guided builders already abandon, not a token win.

## 5. What to measure or verify before deciding

- Re-count the §1 `app.ts` table from `priors1` and `priors2` and publish screens and occurrences. Drop `padStart` unless the remainder, after `formatDate` and `formatDecimal`, still shows up in Contract.
- Confirm the 469-build refusal census still has no `sort`, `find`, `padStart`, or `Math.*`, and say how a refusal was counted (once per build, first error only or all errors).
- Before any `Math.min` spelling, run the `arith` difftest with `NaN` paired against `1`, `-0`, and `Infinity`. Decide the behavior change on its own.
- For `some` and `every`, golden tests against JavaScript for `[]`, `[1]`, all-false, and a predicate that uses the index. A same-plan test beside each.
- If `toSorted` returns, one corpus program with a tie (stability), a `NaN` comparator result, `-0`, a surrogate pair against code-point order, and an inconsistent comparator pinned to the chosen algorithm. Run it on the runner, Lean, and the web runtime. Confirm the web path traps at comparison 65537.
- On the guess corpus after the doc cut, per model: walls by diagnostic id, not only the first error, and a count of `.sort`, `.some`, `.find`, `??`, and `xs[i]`. The pass condition is "no refused form rises," not "the sort screens compile."
- Byte diff of start-here plus the card. No graded A/B for this stage.
