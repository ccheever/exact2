Sequence 018 first, shrink both, and kill 017's D3 middle category.**

## LLP 1116.017 — RETHINK

**Core rule.** As titled, the rule proves too much: the compiler can also name `AccentColor` for `-exact-tint`, which the RFC rightly keeps refused. The real rule is T1–T5, and several of its own rows fail them. The honest split is three-way: (a) canonical form was wrong (the web's word should be the taught word), (b) meaningless layout, (c) platform-shape discipline — which is not friction, it is Exact's differentiator against Expo. D3's rows dominate real builds (button-style 40 sites, grouped-list 9, of 182) precisely because native shapes are where Exact refuses to paint. Meanwhile D1/D2's forms are nearly absent from the 104 real builds; they live in the cold guess corpus that 1116.014's reviewers warned over-counts. So D1/D2 buy first-contact impression, not measured tokens/time/quality for guided builders — worth something, but say so.

**§9 answers.**

1. **Line: further in. Confidence: high.** Accept `h1`–`h6` (the compiler already names the total rewrite at [tags.rs](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/contract/lower/src/tags.rs:973), and LLP 1115 D3 already treats `h1`–`h6` as headings) and the `fn` next-line body (pure layout). Reject `margin-inline`: Contract deliberately refuses `margin-inline-start` with "write `margin-left` in a left-to-right flow" ([compose.rs](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/contract/cli/tests/it/compose.rs:279)); accepting only the one-value shorthand is an incoherent half of logical properties, and the refusing edit is mechanical. Reject component-without-`()`: the parentheses are the reader's only local marker that a use is not an element; that is convenience-syntax creep, not meaninglessness. `image src` (3 screens, and `image` is Exact's tag, not `img`) — fix the misleading message instead.
2. **D3: stay an error; the category dissolves. Confidence: high.** Author > platform governs *values a platform can present*; it is not a licence to paint a styled button inside a grouped cell — that is a tell in tap target, highlight, swipe and VoiceOver traits, and the "author's word" here is an agent's guess, not a human's deliberate choice. Warnings are worst-of-both: agents treat them as errors (the RFC's own §3: warnings acted on in 19 and 15 of 104 builds) and warning text is re-sent on *every* later build, a permanent token tax exceeding the one repair it saved; humans ignore them and ship tells. Split instead: a footer `if`/`else` of single texts and a footer under `if` have one meaning UIKit *can* draw (one string) — accept silently, no warning. Symbol `width` → point size is not faithful (box width ≠ glyph scale) — keep the error, name the point-size edit.
3. **Several roots / view-line properties: yes, a trap. Confidence: high.** The root's identity is load-bearing (root must be an element, LLP 1010; scroller, header projection, safe area hang off it). An implicit block chooses a third layout nobody wrote: the RFC's own motivating example, heading and list side by side, would *stack* — compiles, wrong screen, invisible to corpus scoring. HTML's `body` is written and styleable; this block is neither. View-line properties compound it with an unnameable node and a special-case "inherited only" list to learn. Both are cured cheaper by D4 plus a keeping message ("wrap in `column`/`row`, each node unchanged").
4. **Worth it only sequenced and shrunk. Confidence: medium-high.** Do D4 (start-here at `HEAD` literally invites both errors: "the accent, on the root", "a header holds a heading") and 018's messages for these ids first — days, fully reversible — then `h1` + `fn`-newline on "write the web" consistency grounds. Accepted spellings are near-permanent under "Delete; don't deprecate" and, since [fmt.rs](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/contract/syntax/src/fmt.rs:1) is source-preserving and outside the dev loop, nothing canonicalises them back; corpus apps will teach both spellings forever. That asymmetry (reversible messages vs permanent surface) should order the work.

**Missing framing.** For every form where the compiler names a total rewrite, emit it as a *machine-applicable* fix (`contract fix`, as `fmt --uses`/`fix_uses` already precedents in [contract/cli/src/lib.rs](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/contract/cli/src/lib.rs:397)). One builder step, feature kept by construction, source stays one-spelling, no warning tax, no new language surface. Acceptance then remains only where the written form should be canonical (`h1`). Also uncounted: refusals are the curriculum for agents — removing an error removes the one teaching moment that demonstrably gets read.

## LLP 1116.018 — READY WITH CHANGES

**Core rule.** Right as a standard for messages; wrong as a universal tested promise. The deepest rows (`failed()`, `timer`) were language gaps, not message failures — `failed()` was admitted the same day. Make that the headline: a loss row is first a capability-gap candidate, a message last; otherwise cleverer text entrenches missing forms.

**§9 answers.**

1. **Too strong as stated. Confidence: high.** Many of the ~330 diagnostic ids (I counted ~345) refuse forms with no defined behaviour to keep (unknown names, type errors); T1's "or say where it goes" makes the standard unfalsifiable exactly where it is hard, and data-module cases that "supply" the moved behaviour assume their conclusion. A few `Runner::boot` states chosen by the message's author are a self-oracle, not equivalence. Restrict D2 to: every edit *compiles* (extend 1116.016 D5's test to all refusals — cheap, exact) plus behavioural cases only for corpus-shown losers, frozen-fixture style (1116.014 D2). Everywhere else the enforceable standard is honesty: name a keeping edit, or state plainly what is lost or moved. The full version is apparatus of exactly the kind `rules/RULES.md` taxes, against a 65-second gate.
2. **ARIA: accept, declared per host. Confidence: medium.** Subset-under-one-name, declared in LLP 1001, is already the system's pattern (LLP 1081 D3), and refusal produced the worst accessibility outcome — deletion on the web too. Conditions: verify no native counterpart first (iOS announcements may make `aria-atomic` mappable in the host rather than a permanent no-op), declare it in the host table and `contract vocab`, and no per-build warning. "True on every host" is a purity the rest of the system already does not keep; but because accessibility settings outrank the author (LLP 1115), an *undeclared* no-op would be unacceptable.
3. **Keep rate: a census, not a gate. Confidence: high.** At n≈32–45 per arm, 1116.016 §7's Muse swing (19/32 → 15/33) is roughly one standard error; detecting a 25%→15% partial-rate improvement needs ~250 repairs per arm, an order beyond the corpus. The judge is one uncalibrated model judging its own family, comparing contract texts — it cannot see tells, performance, or whether the *first* draft even implemented the feature. Keep it as triage feeding D3 rows (with humans reading a sample of "whole" verdicts too, not only partials); drop it as a standing per-RFC stop rule.
4. **Missing loss kinds. Confidence: high.** (a) Behaviour kept, nativeness lost (custom face replaces native button) — judge-blind. (b) The first form was *wrong*: keeping its behaviour canonises a guess (`parseFloat("12abc")` → 12 may be an accident, and `parseNumber` refusing it arguably a fix, not a loss). (c) Ex-ante loss: features never attempted because the guide teaches a smaller idiom — invisible to any repair corpus. (d) Harness artefact counted as loss: the guess prompt splits view and data module between authors, so "the order fell between the two" (1116.016, As built) is partly the harness, not the compiler; built apps sorted fine in `app.ts`.

## Measurement (§7 in both) — cannot currently separate success from noise

Three structural problems. **Tautology:** 017's "target ids fall to near zero" is true by construction for accepted forms and says nothing about wrong-meaning compiles; add a wrong-screen oracle — grade the *rendered* accepted screen against the feature (the side-by-side roots that stack), which is Grok's 1116.014 point neither plan answers. **Composition bias:** once D1/D2 accept the easy refusals, the surviving repair pool is harder, so the whole-repair share can fall while total loss falls; the denominator must be all screens that met a refusal under the baseline compiler. **Attribution and power:** both §7s share one baseline and both claim the repair improvement, so landing together attributes nothing — run the frozen guesses factorially (old compiler / new messages / new acceptances), analyse paired discordant screens, and pre-commit the detectable effect at the available n. For real builds, error-sites-per-build (already 1.75, task-mix noisy) is the wrong yardstick; use 1116.014 D3's steps-after-error and lookup-after-error. Finally, measure the new costs nobody counts: warning bytes re-sent per subsequent step, and accepted-spelling drift greppable in the next round's sources.

## Verdicts and top three

- **1116.017: RETHINK** — a shippable residue (`h1`–`h6`, `fn` body layout, footer-as-one-text, D4) needs no new rule; D3 and implicit roots should not land.
- **1116.018: READY WITH CHANGES** — land it first, with D2 cut to edit-compiles-everywhere plus behavioural cases for proven losers, and D4 demoted to a census.

1. **Sequence messages before acceptance, and measure between them.** Messages are reversible in a day; spellings are forever. Most of 017's corpus case may evaporate under 018 — its own Q4 — and only the residue earns language surface.
2. **Add machine-applied fixes as the third option between accept and refuse.** It captures nearly all of D1/D2's value (feature kept, one step) while preserving one spelling, no tells, and no warning tax.
3. **Abolish the warn-and-draw middle.** One-meaning and faithfully drawable: accept silently. A platform-shape conflict: error with the native edit first. No-tells is the product's reason to exist; D3 spends it to save agents a step they will spend anyway on the warning.
