# LLP 1125 r1, blind review: Grok 4.7, xhigh

> **Review provenance.** Family: xAI Grok 4.7 (xhigh) · Provider and runtime: `grok -p … --reasoning-effort xhigh --sandbox read-only --no-subagents --no-plan` · Date: 2026-10-16 · Redacted: no · Method: blind (shown no other review), direction and fundamentals only, nits excluded; given LLP 1125 r1 at `404863b35` in a read-only detached worktree, with the euxy sources under `~/projects/euxy-src` and `~/projects/euxy-ports` readable. The text below is the reviewer's, unedited; the author's dispositions are in the RFC's §R. The CLI's opening narration lines, before the verdict, are omitted.

**RIGHT DIRECTION WITH CHANGES.** The transport, MIDI, the wakelock, and file share belong in the vocabulary. A pattern value, a host clock, and a runner record is the same idea as LLP 1096's voice table, and a metronome, a drum machine, and an interval timer can use it as written. An LLP 1124 extension arrives through the native slot on the main thread (1124 D5) and refuses value streams (1124 §5), so it cannot share the mixer's clock or deliver every note. QR is a pure codec and can still be a linked built-in. The six pieces should be admitted separately. The 2026-10-15 euxy sentence does not admit them.

1. **BLOCKING — D8, receiving.** One FIFO that drops the 257th message breaks the invariant this section states. A fader or MPE sweep is hundreds of CC or pitch-bend messages a second, each proposed as its own commit, and the dropped message can be a note-off. A lighting desk and a recorded take, both named in §2, hit this. Notes, Start, Continue, and Stop must survive a burst. CC, pitch bend, and aftertouch coalesce to the latest value per key between commits. Clock and Active Sensing stay out of the dispatch. `sendMIDI`, `exactMIDI()`, and clock in/out on the sequence are the right surface. One dispatch per musical message is viable. One dispatch per controller sample is not.

2. **MATERIAL — D5, the web clock and the 100 ms window.** The edit horizon and the scheduling lookahead are different jobs. The draft uses one number for both. Audio can be retracted: the mixer applies `End` (`sound_render.c`), and LLP 1096 D7 calls `AudioBufferSourceNode.stop(when)`. A timestamped MIDI send cannot be pulled back. 100 ms is a sound rule for which steps an edit may change, and a poor cap on how far ahead sound may be queued. At 150 BPM a sixteenth is 100 ms, so the window is one step, and at higher tempos it is more than a step. On the web the cap fails. Chromium clamps timers in a hidden document to about one second, and after several minutes to about one a minute, dedicated workers included, with an exemption while the tab is audible. A worker `setInterval` that posts to the page stays inside that clamp, and `start()` still runs on the main thread. A stall longer than the window, which D5 already names, drops steps. Schedule sound seconds ahead and cancel back to the horizon on an edit. Keep MIDI inside the horizon. The stage-2 device bar stays a Mac and an iPhone with the main thread blocked. The web meets that bar after this change, or after an AudioWorklet for a foreground stall. Web MIDI remains main-thread only.

3. **MATERIAL — D5, the Apple ring.** The mixer ring is single-producer, and that producer is the main thread (`SoundArm.swift:218`, `sound_render.h`). `exact_sound_push` publishes one tail. A scheduler thread pushing while `playSound` still pushes from the main thread races, and the stage-2 bar (onsets during an 80 ms main-thread block) depends on that push. The scheduler thread should be the only producer. Main-thread plays and stops hop to it on a second queue. The render callback can stay. "The runner sleeps" overstates the locked-screen metronome. With `audio_session: "playback"` and `audio` in `UIBackgroundModes`, iOS keeps the process scheduled while the output runs, and `Session.swift` still arms `scheduleClock` from `timerDueMs`. The host thread earns its place during a long commit, and when an ambient app is actually suspended.

4. **MATERIAL — D3 and D4, what the record is a function of.** Inside one uninterrupted internal-clock run, hits as a pure function of the pattern and the anchor are right, and one `advance(60_000)` can match sixty short ones. Two cases break a global reading of that sentence. On resume from suspension the draft both skips missed steps (LLP 1073 D2) and expands every beat the clock crossed. A suspension re-anchors at the resume commit and expands nothing in the gap. Otherwise `state.sounds` lists hits the speaker never played. Clock-in is a second mode: position is the clock-message log, and tempo is the arm's 48-tick window. The driver's preferred tempo is a substitute for that measurement. The seek rule covers the internal clock. The record retains the pattern segments (time, tracks, tempo). A hash of the tracks cannot be expanded.

5. **MATERIAL — D2, the schedule.** The gate is the right grammar: arm at the commit that makes it true, restart on `key=`, stop when it goes false (LLP 1092 D8). The arguments are a different kind of thing. `after` and `every` capture a literal and fire an action. `sequence` fires nothing, and its arguments have to be re-read after every commit that stands, compared by value, so an equal pattern leaves the horizon alone and a refused commit changes nothing that sounds. A `sequence` declaration with that same gate is the cleaner surface. Tracks also loop on the host. A one-shot chart or a fixed run of intervals, both in §2, repeats at the loop point until a later commit drops the gate, and that commit is the one that arrives late when the main thread is busy. Play-once belongs on the host clock.

6. **MATERIAL — D12, the web picture.** Apple rendering the element's layer, and Android drawing the view, are the host's own drawing. An SVG `foreignObject` of the subtree is a second render. The web host's look comes from its stylesheet (`host/web-js/build.mjs`), and a cloned subtree does not carry that sheet. Canvas, video, and filters stay behind. Leaving out cross-origin images is the right call. Ship element pictures on Apple and Android. On the web, defer capture, or specify a computed-style inline pass and the cases that stay blank. `files=` for `app:/` paths is LLP 1069.003 D7. An `"#id"` entry beside those paths is a fair addition, and a picture the app cannot read back keeps LLP 1112 parked. Desktop Chrome shares files on Windows, macOS, and ChromeOS. Firefox desktop does not. `canShareFiles` is still the right fact.

7. **MATERIAL — D9, when sounds decode.** LLP 1096 reads every declared sound when the output opens. D7's late start is the behavior of a decode that has not finished: the hit starts late, or is dropped if its end has passed. On-demand as the new default does that to existing sound apps on the first note. Declared sounds stay eager. A sound opts into lazy, and `loadSounds` is how a kit pays for an instant first hit. Tape-style `playbackRate` (duration moves with rate) and equal-power `pan` are the right sampler additions.

8. **MINOR — D11, QR and the JS target.** One encoder held bit-for-bit to `qrcode` 1.5.4 byte mode is the right fix for the Core Image mismatch the ports already shipped. The ~20 KB figure is the JS runtime (`README.md`), and `sound-glue.js` is already an extra module loaded with the plan. The JS target is plain JS. The Rust encoder in the runner covers the wasm host and the native hosts. The JS target gets a port held to the same corpus. LLP 1027.000 D1 keeps a pure helper in the app's data code. The reason to centralize is the cross-host disagreement, which that LLP does not discuss. Square modules only is right.

**§9, where I disagree**

1. The 2026-10-15 line in `rules/DEFERRED.md` was already applied, and the recorded expansion is chrome: grabber, text prompt, header face, swipe paint. Admit each stage on its own waiver or take. The second consumers in §2 are the right basis. Linux MIDI as a stated non-goal for stage 3 is right.
2. Use the declaration with the gate grammar. A `task` schedule is acceptable only with the re-read rule in finding 5 written as normative.
3. One horizon in the record, on every host, is right. Hosts that can retract audio may queue sound past that horizon. See finding 2.
6. Eager stays the default. Lazy is opt-in.
7. A corpus-checked JS port for the JS target. The Rust encoder stays in the runner.

I agree with 4 (a key change restarts; Continue is a new arm with `from` at the old beat), 5 (flat fields, as `playSounds` already takes a list of a shape), 8, and 9.

**Staging.** Small pieces, then transport and sampler, then MIDI, is the right order, and each stage can delete the Swift it replaces. Stage 2's web scheduler waits on finding 2. Stage 1's web picture waits on finding 6. Clock-in stays in stage 3, specified as the second record. The rest of §8's "still out" list stays out.
