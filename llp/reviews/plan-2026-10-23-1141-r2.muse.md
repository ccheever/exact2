Verdict: READY WITH CHANGES — the port loop is the right discovery instrument, but its runtime/faithfulness bars and one-run authoring evidence cannot yet support the pass/close decisions §4.7 wants from them.

### Strengths

- **Ground truth a spec cannot give (§1, Summary).** A shipped original fixes screens, numbers, and behaviour; the table in §1 correctly limits ports vs. the authoring bench (1087), app farm/bake-off (1116), and brand probe (1129). Euxy validates this: LLP 1125 Summary/§1 shows whole subsystems (transport, MIDI, sampler — 1,607 lines of Swift by a later port) and scale costs (401 layers, 256 ms → 601 ms) that 19 bake-off rounds (cf. LLP 1116 §0; 1116.021 §0) missed.
- **Replay vs. fresh port (§4.6)** is the strongest methodological idea: replay holds the builder fixed to isolate runtime/defaults (1116.019's gate); a fresh port isolates authoring friction. Cheap replays after runtime batches, fresh ports only after extracted tasks pass (§4.8), is the right cadence.
- **Pre-commitment (§4.0–4.1, §4.7).** Slice and stopping rules declared before port 1; brief written from the original only, by a non-builder of the Exact arm, frozen, arms mutually blind (§4.2). Classification of authored design / platform-owned / original defects, given to the grader, directly operationalises LLP 1115's "would someone who knows the platform notice?" oracle.
- **Fix discipline (§4.4–4.5).** Every loss gets an owner; Exact-owned items must reproduce in exact2 or stay recorded as unreproduced. Class fixes must name an existing second consumer and be probed there before a new app starts — the correct antidote to app-shaped hacks, modelled on LLP 1125's "drum machine, metronome, rhythm game."
- **Measurement hygiene (§5).** Quiet machine (bones), interleaved launches, load recorded and runs discarded above core count, probe overhead measured per OS, re-benching of frozen originals/comparators when machine/OS/fixtures change, simulator rankings checked on device before device claims.

### Concerns

**BLOCKING — §2/§4.7: "passed" rests on one fresh port.** Runtime medians over 20 launches (§5) can support a runtime pass, but faithfulness rests on one grader's closeness rating exceeding that grader's regrade spread (§4.3), and §4.7 lets a single fresh port + replay + second grader close an app. Grader spread is measured on 10–20% regrades; that estimates grader noise, not build-to-build variance of the same brief — and the bake-off record shows the latter is large (LLP 1116.021 As-built: Muse Exact tokens 3.82M → 4.95M → 3.24M across rounds 19–21 on near-identical briefs). *Resolve:* require two independent fresh ports at the passing commit (or one fresh port plus a pre-registered grader calibration set with known-difference pairs), and report the pass as an interval, not a point.

**BLOCKING — §4.2: model is confounded with app.** Each app pins one model and rotates families only across apps. Within an app this is right (changes are Exact's, per 1116 §R), but across the portfolio "row covered" then means "covered for one model family." If Exact's docs are phrased for one family's habits, the matrix (§6) will read as mechanism coverage while hiding a builder-population failure — the goal is "best way for *a coding agent*," not for the pinned one. *Resolve:* in the pilot, cross at least one app with a second family on the same frozen brief before scaling; thereafter treat model family as a matrix axis alongside platform and original stack (§6 already crosses those).

**MAJOR — §4.3: grader blindness is asserted, not tested.** Builds strip dev affordances (`EXACT_DEV_MENU=0`), order is randomised, and the grader records its guess after scoring — good. But an LLM grader can identify Exact from accessibility trees, bundle layout, or characteristic defaults, and the plan never sets a threshold on guess accuracy or what happens when it exceeds chance. LLP 1116 §0's grader (Opus 5.5 grading Opus-built arms) shows family overlap is routine here. *Resolve:* pre-register a blindness check — guess rate vs. 50% with a confidence interval per grader; if recognition is above chance, report scores split by recognised/unrecognised pairs and treat "at least as close as native by more than noise" (§2) as unproven for recognised pairs.

**MAJOR — §2: two bars are structurally unfair to small apps.** Footprint/peak ≤ 1.2× the reference and size ≤ bare template + reference's own size both charge Exact's fixed runtime against apps whose native reference has almost none — on euxy's numbers (RESULTS.md: 4.0 MB native .app vs. 20 MB Exact, 75 MB Expo) a small native original will fail on fixed cost regardless of per-node quality. §10.1's 1.4× close-limit will become the de facto bar. *Resolve:* report fixed overhead (bare template vs. bare native template) separately from marginal per-screen cost, and set the bar on the marginal part, keeping the absolute footprint as a named-limit item.

**MAJOR — §2: the timing pass rule can be unsatisfiable.** "Medians clear it and p10–p90 bands do not overlap; if bands overlap, take more runs" — with simulator jitter, non-overlap of 80% bands is a far stronger condition than a 1.1× median ratio, and taking more runs narrows bands only until irreducible OS variance dominates. *Resolve:* replace band-overlap with a pre-registered paired/interleaved difference interval on the median ratio (the interleaving in §5 already supports pairing by launch order).

**MAJOR — §4.0: the single Maestro flow can mask the thing it measures.** An arm "may need a small adapter," and a step Maestro finds in the native port but not the Exact port is "diagnosed before it is counted as an accessibility difference." Both are judgement calls by the team being measured. *Resolve:* log every adapter and every diagnosed-not-counted step in RESULTS.md; count diagnosed steps as a reported secondary metric so the exemption cannot silently grow.

**MAJOR — §4.5: the second-consumer rule can strand real gaps.** LLP 1125 itself was justified by *hypothetical* consumers ("a drum machine, a metronome…") that did not yet exist as ports. Requiring an existing consumer in `apps/`, a bake-off spec, or this corpus will push genuinely general capabilities (the next MIDI) into per-port native modules, recreating the 1,607-line problem inside the "Native escape" metric (§2), which is explicitly "a question, not a defect." *Resolve:* allow a named, plausible-but-unbuilt consumer class as justification, flagged as such, with the probe deferred to the next app that touches the mechanism row.

**MINOR — §2: missing goals.** Energy has no bar (honestly deferred, §5), but accessibility is judged only through grader screens at largest Dynamic Type — no VoiceOver journey completion rate; and there is no correctness-under-adversity bar (offline, relaunch mid-edit) beyond "numbers the original defines." §4.9's single follow-up task is a good maintainability probe but n=1 per app. *Resolve:* add VoiceOver completion of the journey as a pass/fail row; keep the rest as recorded-no-bar.

**MINOR — §9: human cost is unbudgeted.** Charlie's time — accepting named limits (§4.7), classification disputes (§4.0), per-showing publication decisions (§8) — is the scarcest input and appears nowhere in the 5–9 hour estimate. The pilot's cost accounting (§6) should separate it, or the cadence (two lanes) will stall on rulings, not builds.

### Section 10 rulings

1. **Bars stand, memory 1.2×, close ≤1.4×.** Agree — 70. Keeping a miss visible beats relaxing to pass euxy. *Would change my answer:* pilot data showing the 1.2× miss is dominated by fixed template overhead on small apps (see above) — then re-base the bar rather than raise it.
2. **Bench into `scripts/ports/` after two apps without app-specific edits; scheduled non-blocking replays.** Agree — 85. *Would change:* if the second app needs harness edits, wait for three; premature in-repo freezing ossifies the probe.
3. **Native arm only on port 1, the passing port, and authoring-claim pairs.** Agree — 75. The runtime reference is the native original anyway (§2); the frozen native build serves replays. *Would change:* evidence that native-port quality drifts with model updates faster than OS majors — then rebuild per fresh port.
4. **No Expo/React arm in the pilot.** Agree, narrowly — 60. The bake-off already pairs Expo/React on small specs, and the pilot's Expo original gives runtime evidence. But the headline goal is "cheaper than Expo," and after the pilot this leaves authoring-vs-Expo resting entirely on small-spec evidence. *Would change:* if the bake-off stops running Expo, this ruling's own revisit trigger fires — hold the plan to it.
5. **Maestro, contingent on finishing a flow on euxy's Exact and UIKit builds, else per-arm drivers.** Agree — 80. The contingency test is the right gate. *Would change:* per-arm fallbacks (§4.0 concern) destroy the one-flow fairness property; if the fallback triggers, treat journey-step failures as incomparable across arms, not merely adapted.
6. **Pilot: React Conf (Expo), Hackers (native), Pocket Casts slice (deep), NetNewsWire held back.** I do not judge the picks, per instructions. The composition satisfies §6 (one native, one Expo, one deep, one unseen transfer check) — agree — 75. *Would change:* if the held-back app is touched by any tuning, classification, or docs work during the pilot, the transfer check is void; the plan should state that quarantine explicitly.

### Missing

- **A power statement per claim type.** §4.8 honestly labels 3–5 pairs "a pilot," but no section says what effect size any instrument can detect; §4.7's "inconclusive" outcome needs a pre-registered run budget per app, not just a four-fresh-port cap.
- **Grader calibration anchors.** A small set of pairs with known differences (e.g., the original against itself with one deliberate tell) graded every round would convert "measured noise" from regrade spread into an actual sensitivity floor.
- **Portfolio split vs. the bake-off.** §1 argues complementarity but §9 budgets only ports. State the intended share of lane-time; otherwise ports — deeper, more interesting — will crowd out the only instrument that supports authoring-cost claims (1116's paired intervals).
- **Quarantine and drift rules for the transfer app and for originals' dependencies** (a "builds today" app, §3.5, may stop building mid-program; §5 covers re-benching but not re-pinning).
