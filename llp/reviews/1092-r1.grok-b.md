1. **MATERIAL** — D2, D3, D8, D4. A refused `next` or `then` either retries forever or stalls the queue.
   **Problem.** D2 puts the queue inside the checkpoint, and `conclude`'s error path restores that checkpoint wholesale. D3 says a refused `next` drops that send and arms the following one, and that a mutation becomes free when its `then` ends whether the `then` stood or was refused. D8 then puts that arming inside `conclude` and says nothing arms in a refused commit. Those three rules cannot be implemented together. `conclude` also does not know which kind of commit just finished, and `then_due` is cleared before the `then` body runs, so "a `then` is armed" is already false during the `then`.
   **Evidence.** `Checkpoint` is `runner/src/runner/commit.rs:18–30`; `checkpoint()` is `:35`; the error restore is `:65–82`. `run_action` concludes, then calls `arm_then` only when the commit stood (`:383–386`, `:395–400`). `advance_within` sets `then_due[m] = INFINITY` before `run_action` of the `then` (`:271–275`). `commit_again` (`runner/src/runner/settlement.rs:63–79`) concludes and does not arm anything. `release_failed` always commits again (`admission.rs:82–107`); `release_refused` returns `Ok(None)` while any other ordered refusal remains (`:67–68`), so no commit runs. `enqueue` still forgets the in-flight ticket for that target (`commit.rs:750–754`).
   **Fix.** Pop a refused head before `checkpoint()`, or drop it after the restore, so the drop is not rolled back. After every conclude, and on `release_refused`'s early return, scan for a queue mutation with no in-flight ticket, not inside its own `then` or `next`, and with waiters, and arm `next_due`. Pass the commit kind into that step. `then_due` cannot be the signal.
