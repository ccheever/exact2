**Q14 — Recommendation:** Adopt a bounded version of **(b)**. Driver compilation remains compilation. Permit it only through explicit, measured platform exceptions. Require Metal’s observed 127 ms archive-miss work to leave the boot path before approving the Apple carrier for shipping. Allow pipeline creation that demonstrably loads native binaries; do not impose (c)’s universal ban on pipeline objects.

**Confidence: 94/100.**

**Reasoning:**

- The existing ruling expressly regulates GPU shader compilation, separately from the module-graph check. Its exception names Linux/vello and accepts a particular first-launch cost; it does not authorize every renderer. `boot.mjs` inspects web scripts and imports, so passing it cannot establish anything about native driver compilation. [DEFERRED §Runtime](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/rules/DEFERRED.md:626), [boot.mjs](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/scripts/boot.mjs:142).
- This is substantively compilation. Apple distinguishes Metal IR libraries from GPU-specific binaries and explicitly says IR requires runtime compilation. Microsoft describes its D3D11 shader-creation callback as converting shader code into a hardware-specific format. Calling those operations “pipeline creation” does not remove their compiler work. [Apple: Metal binary archives](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/metal-binary-archives), [Microsoft: CreatePixelShader driver callback](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/drivers/ddi/d3d10umddi/nc-d3d10umddi-pfnd3d11_1ddi_createpixelshader).
- Metal offers a concrete way to distinguish loading from compiling: packaged binary archives plus `MTLPipelineOption.failOnBinaryArchiveMiss`. An archive miss can select the CPU painter while compilation proceeds after a complete, interactive first screen. Merely supplying an archive is insufficient: Metal otherwise falls back to compilation. This follows the existing after-first-pixel precedent without requiring every successful archive lookup to use the CPU painter. [Apple: archive construction and distribution](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/creating-binary-archives-from-device-built-pipeline-state-objects), [Apple: failOnBinaryArchiveMiss](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/mtlpipelineoption/failonbinaryarchivemiss), [LLP 1009 D4–D5](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/llp/1009-gpu-canvas.rfc.md:93).
- I would consider a narrow exception for DX11’s small shader-object cost without demanding its complete elimination. That exception must specify measured hardware, cache conditions, complete startup cost and an owner. Its 2.7 ms phase alone does not qualify the 406–411 ms first screen. Vulkan and GLES receive no prospective exception from Apple or Windows evidence. Budgets are explicitly trades; the trade must name the cost rather than redefine it away. [RULES, Budgets and Time budgets](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/rules/RULES.md:9), [1149 §4 C6](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/llp/1149-the-renderer.rfc.md:772).

One qualification is essential: **127 ms is an empty-application-cache measurement, not an established first-ever-machine measurement.** The underlying report says the OS cache was not cleared. It reports complete Metal construction at **162.048 ms empty / 31.699 ms retained**, versus wgpu’s **15.334 / 15.564 ms**. The exception must use those definitions, not promise “127 ms once, 0.5 ms thereafter.” Source: `host/ink/MEASUREMENTS.md` at `e83558a4d6`, “C5 → Boot phases.”

**What would change my answer:** Verified archive coverage across supported GPU/OS combinations, with misses prevented from compiling before first presentation, would remove the need for Metal’s compilation exception. Conversely, evidence that a complete CPU first screen and subsequent handoff cost more than a tightly bounded driver compile would justify a measured exception. Either requires cache-invalidated and retained-cache end-to-end measurements, including the handoff’s responsiveness.

**Q21 — Recommendation:** Keep the **20% and 10% improvement margins as prototype investment gates**, but repair their regression and startup requirements. Ink has earned further work, not an unconditional C-over-B pass. Keep direct Metal with a dated boot obligation.

**Confidence: 91/100.**

**Reasoning:**

- The margins are reasonable engineering choices: a larger gain justifies replacing the rendering core; a smaller incremental gain can justify a thin device layer. They are not sufficient alone. D15 leaves workload aggregation and several statistics unspecified, and permits a direct layer to win a small startup phase while losing total startup. A boot-based win should mean **at least 10% better complete startup**, beyond noise; phases explain that result. [1149 D15](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/llp/1149-the-renderer.rfc.md:595).
- **Use medians for typical performance, with a separate peak/tail guard—not median-only acceptance or an automatic worst-of-five veto.** Compare like statistics: median run CPU, frame-time percentiles, and distributions of each run’s peak memory. A five-run maximum is sensitive to sampling and cannot establish a stable tail percentile. Nevertheless, repeatable candidate-attributable peak regressions above 10% remain failures until repaired or explicitly traded. Localization to readback explains responsibility; it does not exempt the allocation from the measured path.
- On the evidence supplied, C-over-B clears the improvement margin but not the complete gate. Actual presented late frames are unmeasured, and C’s memory concern remains open. The primary report explicitly says both things. C5 cannot repair that verdict by substituting D for C: it did not rerun B. Source: `host/ink/MEASUREMENTS.md@e83558a4d6`, “First D15 verdict,” “C5 → D15 verdict,” and “Where the RSS spikes occur.”
- **Direct Metal was retained consistently with D15’s literal wording.** Its clause requires a startup **or** frame-time win and does not inherit C-over-B’s “no metric more than 10% worse” restriction. Shelf and Excord meet its frame-time alternative with separated run ranges. CPU and memory strengthen the investment case. The defect is the incomplete gate, not a violation of a boot veto that D15 never stated. This remains a workload-specific result, not a demonstrated fling improvement everywhere. [1149 D15](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/llp/1149-the-renderer.rfc.md:610), [§4 C5](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/llp/1149-the-renderer.rfc.md:749).

**Boot should have its own acceptance requirement:** complete, interactive first-screen p50 **≤100 ms**, evaluated separately for invalidated/empty caches and retained caches, plus **no more than 10% regression against the existing platform painter**. Where the baseline already exceeds 100 ms, further regression needs an explicit trade rather than silently inheriting that debt. Report tails and investigate reproducible worsening. Measure the actual critical path; neither summing independent phase medians nor giving each phase 100 ms establishes compliance. [RULES, Time budgets](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/rules/RULES.md:31), [1149 §4 metric definitions](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/llp/1149-the-renderer.rfc.md:652).

My proposed obligation is: **Astra closes or explicitly renegotiates Metal boot by 2026-11-04, and before enabling the S1 carrier, whichever comes first.** Closure means the archive/fallback behavior above and measured complete startup against the guard. Without closure or a dated, explicit trade, withdraw the direct layer rather than carry an indefinite “S1 first item.” That is conditional retention, not unconditional shipping approval.

**What would change my answer:** A matched B/C rerun after readback reuse, with peak-memory regressions resolved and actual presentation/late-frame measurements, would establish the full ink gate. If direct Metal’s advantage disappears against an equally optimized wgpu control on the real carrier, delete it. If its complete startup meets the independent guard, retain it without the temporary obligation.

**Errors in §4’s reading of its numbers**

4. **C5 assigns library/archive work to the wrong timer.** The 29.6 ms device timer ends before `setup::pipelines`; library loading and archive population are inside the pipeline timer. Serialization follows that timer and remains inside construction. Moving library/archive work cannot be assumed to remove the measured device-creation regression. [§4 C4](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/llp/1149-the-renderer.rfc.md:716).
