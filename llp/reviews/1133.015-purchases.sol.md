1. **MATERIAL — Entitlement and unfinished delivery need separate states.** D3 (lines 244–250) unions `currentEntitlements` with `Transaction.unfinished`; D5 (311–317) retains unfinished transactions until acknowledgement. Yet Weatherlight grants Plus whenever that combined list contains a `purchased` subscription (1066–1069).

   Suppose a subscription remains unacknowledged because delivery failed, then expires while the app is closed. At relaunch, `currentEntitlements` excludes it, but `unfinished` still supplies it as `purchased`. The prescribed gate grants expired access, potentially indefinitely while acknowledgement keeps failing. D7’s expiry re-ask cannot fix this: it reconstructs the same union. Apple defines [unfinished transactions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/transaction/unfinished?changes=_1) by whether `finish()` was called, independently of entitlement; its [sample explicitly checks expiration before granting access](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/getting-started-with-in-app-purchases-using-storekit-views?changes=_9).

   **Decision needed:** expose current entitlement independently of delivery work, through separate sources or explicit membership fields, and update the consumer and substitute accordingly. Filtering expired transactions out of the combined list alone would violate the promised retained delivery queue.

The earlier finding is **RESOLVED**. D3 separates current entitlement from unfinished delivery; D4 excludes the delivery queue from kept answers; Weatherlight gates on `listPurchases` and delivers from `unfinishedPurchases`. Drive 9 covers the expired, unfinished subscription. The disposition matches r2.

Two material concerns remain:

1. **IN-DELTA — MATERIAL: Play renewals cannot follow the promised delivery path.**

   D3 (263–270) defines Android’s queue as purchases with `isAcknowledged() == false`. But D5 (338–344) promises that renewals enter this queue, table 485 repeats that promise, and the substitute’s `renew` operation (1114) creates a new transaction. Play renewals retain the subscription purchase token and require no new acknowledgement. They therefore do not enter the specified queue. [Google integration guidance](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate), [subscription lifecycle](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/lifecycle/subscriptions).

   **Failing scenario:** a subscription includes a monthly allowance. An app following D5’s token-idempotent delivery loop credits each Apple renewal, but receives no Android renewal to process. A substitute that invents fresh Android renewal transactions can conceal this failure.

   **Decision needed:** limit the queue’s shared guarantee to purchases requiring app completion, explicitly require the server/RTDN path for Play renewal-specific delivery, and make the substitute preserve Play’s acknowledged token. D8 already describes server notifications correctly, but does not reconcile D5’s unconditional one-path promise.

2. **PRE-EXISTING — MATERIAL: Apple review requests cannot provide the promised sheet serialization.**

   D12 (730–735) and D15 (813–818) put `requestReview` in the decision queue and promise that store sheets never overlap other decision sheets. Apple’s selected [`requestReview(in:)`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/appstore/requestreview%28in%3A%29-1q8qs/) returns synchronously, supplies no presentation or dismissal callback, and may display nothing.

   **Failing scenario:** foreground app code queues a review request followed by a purchase or authentication request. Advancing the queue when `requestReview` returns allows the next request while the review prompt may still be appearing or displayed. Holding the queue until dismissal has no documented completion signal; a suppressed prompt could block subsequent decisions indefinitely.

   **Decision needed:** narrow Apple review serialization to admitting its request after earlier observable sheets finish, leaving subsequent presentation to StoreKit, or identify a supported public mechanism that makes the stronger guarantee feasible. This requires changing the guarantee, rather than filling in an implementation detail.

VERDICT: NOT READY

### Dispositions (r3)

Classification (round 2 on): finding 1 new-on-new-content (r2's queue text); finding 2 pre-existing, missed in round 1; no bar-inflation.

1. MATERIAL, IN-DELTA, Play renewals cannot follow the delivery path: FOLDED at D5 ("A renewal differs by store"), D6's while-closed table and §9's `renew` event. The queue's shared guarantee is narrowed to what both stores keep: every transaction that needs the app's completion is in it until completed. On Apple each renewal is one; on Android a renewal keeps the token and the acknowledgement and enters nothing, so per-period delivery on Play is the server's, from Real-time developer notifications, as the recipe says. The Android substitute renews in place, keeping the token acknowledged and adding nothing to the queue, so it no longer conceals the difference.
2. MATERIAL, PRE-EXISTING, Apple's review request cannot be serialized: FOLDED at D12 and D15. The guarantee is narrowed to what each platform reports: the request waits for every earlier sheet in the queue; on Android the queue holds until `launchReviewFlow`'s `Task` completes; on Apple it advances when `requestReview(in:)` returns, and the RFC no longer promises that StoreKit's prompt never meets a later sheet.
