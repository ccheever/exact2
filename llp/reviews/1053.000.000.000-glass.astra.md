I reviewed [the plan at `691b4b5c6`](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/llp/1053.000.000.000-glass-auto-spacing-and-morph.rfc.md:1), accepted §2’s measurements as the record, and changed no files.

For §5:

1. **Keep fixed timing initially**, but specify an exact curve and an authored way to disable the morph. “350 ms, dampingRatio 1, sampled as a cubic” does not uniquely define its samples. Reduced motion should remain the producer’s decision, as [LLP 1002:361](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/llp/1002-motion-v1.rfc.md:361) requires.

3. **Using the CSS name now is reasonable**, provided the row retains CSS’s meaning and the automatic Apple glass effect is explicitly documented as an extension. Do not inherit unspecified decisions from Draft 1013: it proposes an **action-selected** boundary, whereas this plan automatically reacts to commits. Update that relationship substantively, beyond adding a Related link. See [LLP 1013:72](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/llp/1013-view-transitions.rfc.md:72).

The findings follow.

1. **blocker — The current “glass body” is not separable from its content.**

   D3 promises to move bodies while titles, symbols, and children keep their authored behavior. But `glassBody` is the actual material view or native control ([GlassGroup.swift:258](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/GlassGroup.swift:258)). Material children live inside that view’s content container on both platforms ([NodeViewIOS.swift:910](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/IOS/NodeViewIOS.swift:910), [NodeViewMac.swift:554](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/Mac/NodeViewMac.swift:554)). A native button’s body is the whole `UIButton`, whose configuration contains its title and image ([NativeButtonsIOS.swift:47](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/IOS/NativeButtonsIOS.swift:47), [NativeButtonsIOS.swift:131](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/IOS/NativeButtonsIOS.swift:131)).

   Moving or retaining those objects also moves or retains content. Native control frames are additionally rewritten by `ControlHost.sync()` using alignment rectangles, so they are not freely owned morph geometry ([ControlsIOS.swift:137](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/IOS/ControlsIOS.swift:137), [ControlsMac.swift:94](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/Mac/ControlsMac.swift:94)).

   **Change:** Specify and demonstrate a paint-only representation, its effective frame/radius, and which subsystem owns its geometry. Preserve content, focus, accessibility, and hit targets independently. Until that exists, explicitly exclude native buttons and other inseparable bodies from morph eligibility.

2. **blocker — Pairing at batch end cannot recover departures or provide the proposed lifetime.**

   The glass registry holds weak references to **nodes**, not independently owned bodies ([GlassGroup.swift:22](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/GlassGroup.swift:22)). Immediate destruction forgets/removes the node before reconciliation ([PresenterIOS.swift:748](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/IOS/PresenterIOS.swift:748)). An exit does the opposite: its subtree remains mounted, so a mounted-membership comparison does not itself identify its departure. `endExit` then forgets every member and removes the subtree on the exit’s deadline ([PresenceIOS.swift:17](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/IOS/PresenceIOS.swift:17), [PresenceMac.swift:35](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/Mac/PresenceMac.swift:35)).

   Pooling also discards the material and group ([NodePoolIOS.swift:441](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/IOS/NodePoolIOS.swift:441)). These paths conflict with “hold the leaving body if its node goes first.”

   **Change:** Add a before-mutation capture phase and explicit departure classification, including named descendants of an exiting ancestor. Define bounded ownership of retained presentation objects independently of node lifetime. Specify cleanup for short/long/no exit animation, group removal, material replacement, pooling, reset, and destination destruction. Define rapid A→B→C replacement: interrupt from current presentation and retire the superseded pair.

3. **blocker — One presentation batch is not one commit.**

   D3 promises pairing within one commit; §4 implements pairing at batch end. Apple’s Rust host folds multiple `Timed` receipts into one batch, and explicitly notes that those receipts share the final kernel tree ([host.rs:1179](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/src/host.rs:1179), [host.rs:1215](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/src/host.rs:1215)). The motion engine separately consumes each receipt at its `at_ms` ([host.rs:1235](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/src/host.rs:1235)); Swift’s operation vocabulary carries no equivalent commit boundary ([Batch.swift:52](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/Batch.swift:52)).

   A single clock jump could therefore pair a removal from one timer with an insertion from a later timer, although stepping through those timers would produce no pair.

   **Change:** Preserve receipt identity and start time for morph candidates before flattening loses them. Define any intentional coalescing explicitly. Add equivalence tests comparing one large seek with stepped seeks, including removal and insertion in different commits.

4. **blocker — `svg.seek(clock:)` does not supply frame scheduling or settlement for a Swift morph.**

   `SvgHost.seek` responds to changes in the optional agent clock; it returns immediately when that value is unchanged ([SvgScene.swift:609](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/SvgScene.swift:609)). During ordinary operation `session.clock` is nil; `session.now()` supplies elapsed time ([Session.swift:386](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/Session.swift:386), [Session.swift:549](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/Session.swift:549)).

   Frame demand is overwritten from `batch.motion`, and display ticks depend on that demand ([Session.swift:897](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/Session.swift:897), [Session.swift:1336](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/Session.swift:1336)). `clock settle` reads the Rust engine’s deadline, which knows nothing about a Swift-only morph ([host.rs:820](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/src/host.rs:820)).

   **Change:** Specify active-frame demand, spatial-motion classification, sampling, and settlement integration. Prefer existing engine ownership; if Swift owns the curve, include its deadline in the agent’s fixed point and its activity in `Frames`. Test a morph with no other running animation or timer, frozen repeated reads, `clock settle`, and return to idle.

5. **blocker — Isolation must win during the morph, not just at admission.**

   The existing pass reads opacity, masks, and clipping along the node’s current ancestry ([GlassGroup.swift:121](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/GlassGroup.swift:121)). Opacity animations under groups are deliberately sampled so those values remain observable ([svg_lower.rs:429](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/src/svg_lower.rs:429)).

   A pair can start unisolated, then encounter an authored fade, clipping change, or layout-transition mask. A detached leaving body also loses the ancestry that supplied those protections. D3’s “same container for the whole morph” cannot override D4’s CSS guarantees.

   **Change:** Define ongoing eligibility and cancellation/handoff when either path becomes isolated. State how retained paint preserves the source’s clipping, opacity, and hidden state. Add a mid-morph ancestor fade and mask test, including an exit ending before the morph.

6. **should-fix — Percentage-gap support is absent from the representation.**

   Both gap rows are `f32`, not dimensions ([schema.json:1926](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/kernel/tables/schema.json:1926)). Generated conversion calls `value.f32`, whose ordinary path accepts numbers, and layout constructs fixed lengths from those values ([build.rs:1277](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/kernel/build.rs:1277), [style.rs:484](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/kernel/src/style.rs:484), [style.rs:1319](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/kernel/src/style.rs:1319)).

   `Prepared.swift:58` merely lists gap among inert layout rows; it does not establish percentage resolution.

   **Change:** Cut the percentage sentence from D1. Define this increment against the existing numeric gap rows, retaining the spacing clamp and zero default. Include reverse flex directions, gap clearing, and simultaneous direction/gap changes in tests. General percentage-gap support would require its own complete kernel/layout/web change.

7. **should-fix — `"auto"` requires coordinated typing, lowering, and web serialization.**

   `glassGroup` remains a float prop. Ordinary prop lowering compiles the expression before checking its value ([lower/lib.rs:1263](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/contract/lower/src/lib.rs:1263)); the mixed number/string exception belongs to **style** attributes ([types/checks.rs:1167](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/contract/types/src/checks.rs:1167)). The runner accepts only a number for a float prop ([bridge.rs:48](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/runner/src/bridge.rs:48)).

   Also, negative bound values are currently clamped, not refused ([GlassGroup.swift:93](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/GlassGroup.swift:93)). The existing web attribute is `glassGroup`, and dynamic JS prop bindings write their evaluated value directly ([element.rs:728](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/web/src/element.rs:728), [emit.rs:1087](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/web-js/src/emit.rs:1087)).

   **Change:** Specify recursive `"auto"`→sentinel lowering for supported expression forms, type-pass handling, and validation of numeric branches. Explicitly reserve `-1` on the wire and define bound-negative behavior. Choose one DOM attribute spelling and translate the sentinel consistently in static and dynamic emission on both web targets. Test toggling `"auto"`↔number.

8. **should-fix — The new style row has no chosen end-to-end representation, and native buttons would reject it.**

   “Add one style row” leaves its codec, parser, default, clearing behavior, and dynamic-name transport undecided. The generator has explicit codecs ([build.rs:584](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/kernel/build.rs:584)). Borrowing 1013’s `u16` proposal requires actual name mapping: generic numeric CSS emission would otherwise produce a length such as `7px` ([css.rs:506](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/web/src/css.rs:506)). The JS target also needs an explicit supported binding path ([web-js/style.rs:585](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/web-js/src/style.rs:585)).

   Independently, native-button style rows are allowlisted; a newly added row is refused unless that list changes ([controls.rs:567](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/contract/lower/src/controls.rs:567)).

   **Change:** Choose the representation now—prefer a validated CSS-name value using the existing CSS-value codec pattern—and enumerate its parser, wire, Apple serialization, and both web-emission changes. Define `none` as nonparticipation, noninheritance, clearing, and computed-name behavior. Update the native-button whitelist if those buttons remain supported.

9. **should-fix — Compile-time co-mountability is the wrong duplicate-name contract.**

   D2 rejects elements that *can be mounted* simultaneously. D3 explicitly admits an already-mounted element becoming visible, so this rule obstructs hidden alternatives. It also supplies no runtime policy for computed names, repeated component instances, or classes.

   CSS checks participating rendered elements during capture; a duplicate skips the transition rather than making the document invalid. See the [capture algorithm](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-view-transitions-1/#capture-new-state). The repository requires CSS semantics where applicable ([RULES.md:51](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/rules/RULES.md:51)); the existing glass scope is actual mounted ancestry ([GlassGroup.swift:145](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/GlassGroup.swift:145)).

   **Change:** Cut the general compile-time co-mountability analysis. Define before/after participation sets and deterministic runtime handling of ambiguous candidates. Match this extension by actual group identity plus name, explicitly distinguishing it from future document-wide view-transition validation. Define hiding as well as unhiding, ancestor visibility, material changes, and reparenting.

10. **should-fix — The name is not inert on the web without `startViewTransition()`.**

    D2’s statement is too broad. A non-`none` `view-transition-name` forms a stacking context and backdrop root even outside a running transition. That is particularly relevant to glass descendants using backdrop filters. See [CSS rendering consolidation](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-view-transitions-1/#view-transition-name-rendering-consolidation).

    This affects the plan’s stated immediate web fallback, which already paints a blur approximation ([LLP 1001:827](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/llp/1001-kernel-v1.spec.md:827)). [CLAUDE.md:9](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/CLAUDE.md:9) requires applicable CSS behavior and explicit deviations.

    **Change:** Say “starts no transition,” document the property’s ordinary rendering effects, and test named glass with overlapping content and nested backdrop filters on both web targets. Record any native difference in LLP 1001.

11. **should-fix — Timing and reduced-motion behavior are underspecified.**

    Damping ratio alone does not define the curve, and a cubic approximation needs coefficients or a reproducible fitting rule. The repository’s spring specifies stiffness, damping, mass, velocity, and a rest criterion ([spring.rs:16](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/motion/src/spring.rs:16), [spring.rs:51](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/motion/src/spring.rs:51)). §2 does not establish the claimed 350 ms UIKit default.

    The plan also ignores `transition`, while existing reduced-motion policy works through authored declarations ([DEFERRED.md:404](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/rules/DEFERRED.md:404)).

    **Change:** Specify an exact 350 ms easing, including endpoint behavior, and label it Exact’s chosen default. Document conditional `view-transition-name="none"` as the initial opt-out, with examples using `prefersReducedMotion`. Specify whether clearing the name during an active morph cancels it, and test that behavior.

12. **should-fix — Verification stops short of the promised behavior.**

    D5’s structural samples do not establish that content stayed put, controls remained usable, settlement completed, or frame scheduling worked. Its abbreviated screenshot instruction also omits the parent plan’s warning that default macOS capture does not draw materials ([glass-groups RFC:93](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/llp/1053.000.000-glass-groups.rfc.md:93)).

    The binding working instructions require running affected apps, and explain why Cargo builds do not verify Apple application behavior ([CLAUDE.md:42](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-wt-buttons2/CLAUDE.md:42)).

    **Change:** Add the regression cases above to existing suites; film macOS through window capture; drive the real consumer on Apple and the default JS web build; verify wasm web emission too. Cover both supported Apple generations, earlier-design fallback, focus/hits/accessibility, and idle frame cost. Run the existing five checks and actual Swift builds.

I would cut percentage-gap support, general compile-time co-mountability analysis, and solo platform-timed materialization. I would also cut native-button morphing from the first implementation unless the body/content separation is demonstrated. Keep general view-transition activation and timing authoring deferred.
