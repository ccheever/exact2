**2.3 A1's contract has three gaps against the Metal path (L164–181, 249–252).**
- *The bound.* Today the wait is the worker's: `Ring::acquire` blocks on the present command from three frames ago (`present.rs` 393–405), then `nextDrawable` (281–296) returns in 0.2 ms at p99 (S1.4 1392). Moved to the main thread, the only bound the layer gives is its own: `allowsNextDrawableTimeout` is true (`apple.rs` 55) and `nextDrawable` blocks up to one second before returning nil. GPUI and egui inherit the same one second; "what GPUI and egui do" is a thread precedent, not a bound. A one-second main-thread stall inside a fling is 120 late periods. Say the bound is the carrier's: skip the frame when the oldest in-flight present has not completed (`completed()` is already non-blocking, `metal.rs` 225–254, 667–675), the analogue of `prepare_present`'s one-period wait on Windows (`composition.rs` 438–451, 269).
- *When the choice is made.* "The choice is per frame at encode" (L171) does not fit the walk: `begin()` allocates the compose target and sets it as the draw target before any instance exists (`ink.rs` 352–383); `destination()` flushes mid-walk and copies the partial frame for a blended instance (`effects.rs` 43–51, `clips.rs` 5–22). A frame that starts into the drawable cannot copy from it (`framebufferOnly`, `apple.rs` 52). The amendment must say how a frame knows before its first flush (the painter pre-scans the visible tree for backdrop filters and blend modes, which the kernel knows; or rendering is deferred to the walk's end; or `framebufferOnly` is false and the first `destination()` copies out of the drawable into a pool target). Each has a cost; name the one chosen.
- *Formats.* `render` refuses any target that is not single-level RGBA (`metal.rs` 491–495); the layer is BGRA (`apple.rs` 50) and only the present pipeline is built for BGRA (`present.rs` 198). Rendering the scene into the drawable needs a BGRA set of scene pipeline states, which is a second archive entry per pipeline and touches 0b's boot phase. Sol raised this in r1 (point 1); r2 does not mention it.

The rest of A1 holds: encode on the host thread and submit/present on the worker keeps D11 (`metal.rs` 189–210 already sends the commit to the worker); a live screenshot of "the last encoded frame" is well defined if the re-render happens on the request's turn before the next `begin()`, since `begin()` retires pool targets and ages atlases (`layer.rs` 28–44) — add that clause. The window-capture gate needs its API and its permission named (screen capture is a per-terminal TCC grant on this Mac and the async lane runs the suites), and the band must absorb the display's colour management.

**2.4 A2's glyph snapping (L182–188, 243–245, 253–255) does not match the code's placement and conflicts with M7.** A fresh walk places each glyph at a quarter-pixel horizontal phase and a truncated whole-pixel y (`ink/text.rs` 28–29, `swash.rs` 246–258). Rows are already recorded in row space (`record_transform`, `ink/rows.rs` 115–120) and replayed by a per-instance translate (312–321); `reusable` refuses a fractional delta because the recorded phases would be wrong at the new position (287–293). Snapping "hinted glyphs at their replayed position" means per-glyph work in `replay`, which M7's persistent range with a per-draw constant cannot do unless the vertex shader rounds glyph positions; and `trunc(y₀) + dy` differs from the fresh walk's `trunc(y₀ + dy)` by up to a whole pixel, not half, while the row's boxes move by `dy` unsnapped, so glyphs shift against their backgrounds. The pixel-exact test `fractional_row_scroll_matches_a_fresh_walk_with_clips_and_hinted_text` (`kept_rows_tests.rs` 312–340) fails by design under this rule. The rule that is one line, M7-compatible and keeps every recorded relationship is: round the per-draw offset to whole device pixels, so the whole row sits at most half a pixel from its layout position while it moves.
