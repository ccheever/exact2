## Review (verbatim)

# Independent review: LLP 1050 (research) and LLP 1050.000 (RFC), revision at `65d2a585`, uncommitted

## 1. Overall assessment

The direction is right, but the RFC is not ready for the owner to rule on as written.

It answers two of Charlie's three questions well:
- **Q1, can the list choose?** Yes. The research's matrix is sound, and its key findings hold up: F1 (who scrolls decides), F2 (a hole is always available) and F3 (app-initiated moves can always wait).
- **Q3, one abstraction or several libraries?** One engine is the right answer.

Its answer to **Q2 (is this the best thing to want?)** is half right. The ranking in §3 correctly puts the policy last. But it leaves out the option WinUI actually ships, phased rows: show a cheap but real part of the row first, not a hole and not a hitch.

The blocking problems are internal contradictions:
1. **The motivating case isn't fixed by `auto`.** The 256-column table is not established as a fling. During a drag or wheel, `auto` waits for visible rows, which is the behaviour that produced 3–5 fps.
2. **The 100 ms synchronous "ceiling" contradicts the bar it cites.** LLP 1044.001 says "no event-handling span >4 ms" and "pending feedback within one refresh".
3. **The ceiling can't be enforced** with a count-based, clock-free runner limit.
4. **Stage 2's "Done when" can't be met** under that ceiling.

Most code citations are accurate. A few are imprecise or characterised wrongly (listed below).

## 2. Strengths

- **Research §1.** The trade-off is stated once, cleanly: block, defer, budget, or slow the scroll. It separates the adjacent levers (cheaper rows, earlier rows) from the choice itself. That framing is what makes the rest analysable.
- **Research F1/F3, §3.** "Who scrolls decides", plus "app-initiated moves can always wait", is the correct organising insight. It explains why the web can't block a user scroll but can block a jump.
- **Research §5 Confidence.** Inferences are labelled as inferences, and it says no host was run. That is honest and useful.
- **RFC §2 rule.** "Rows where the scroll comes to rest are never skipped" is a real improvement on a per-list toggle. Applying it per scenario is the key idea, and it matches how reading works.
- **RFC §3.** The levers are ranked by what they remove, and the policy comes last. The strongest part of the answer to Q2.
- **RFC §4, third bullet.** The drift between the two engines is real and verified. Only the legacy path has a budget (`window.rs:647–704`, `lists.rs:88–131`), a coverage rescue (`ScrollPumpIOS.swift:214–244`, `PresenterMac.swift:797–830`) and velocity (`Session.swift:426`). `CollectionFeedback` has none of these (`collection/api.rs:17–39`). This is the strongest argument for one engine.
- **RFC §5.** No budget knob in Contract. The budget comes from the refresh interval (`ScrollPumpIOS.swift:28`). Correct.
- **RFC §6.** The runner stays clock-free and the host converts time to a row count (`FillCost`, `ScrollPumpIOS.swift:35–49`). This keeps determinism and matches the existing legacy pattern.

### 3.2 HIGH: the 100 ms synchronous ceiling contradicts the bar the RFC claims to meet

RFC §2.2 takes "1044.001's cold-jump p95" and turns it into "a synchronous fill may take at most 100 ms". But LLP 1044.001 §6.2 sets two different bars:
- Ordinary cold jump: "Correct target viewport p95 ≤100 ms; **no event-handling span >4 ms**".
- Overload: "**Pending feedback within one refresh**".
- Warm scroll: "no stall >2 refresh intervals".

Slice C (§7.3) goes further and says "remove the unlimited visible-row fallback". The 100 ms figure is an end-to-end latency budget. It is not permission to block the main thread for 100 ms. RFC §2.1's third bullet ("It answers 1044.001's bar") is therefore false for every "wait*" cell.

**What would resolve it:** either argue explicitly that 1050.000 amends 1044.001's span bar, or change "wait" to mean "wait up to N refresh intervals" (for example 2, matching "no stall >2 refresh intervals"), with pending shown after that.

### 3.3 HIGH: `auto` does not fix the case that motivated it, and Stage 2's Done-when can't be met

RFC §2.1 says "The 256-column table at 3–5 fps is a fling." The source (1044.000 §11, lines 474–479) says only that it "scrolls at 3–5 fps because … the scroll callback builds visible rows before the frame". The 1044.000 probe drives phased trackpad input with momentum, unphased wheel input, and scroller jumps (§3.1). Nothing says the 3–5 fps was momentum.

Under `auto`, "Drag or wheel" means "wait* for the visible rows", which is exactly the current behaviour. Only `smooth` changes the drag case.

Stage 2 then requires the table to "fly over pending and **land complete**". At 3–5 fps each new row costs very roughly 70–300 ms (inference from fps and rows per frame). A landing viewport holds tens of rows. "Complete at arrival" would take seconds, far past the 100 ms ceiling, so pending shows. Even Stage 3's landing-first prefetch can't close it: a 1–2 s fling at a ~4 ms slice per frame buys about 0.25–0.5 s of fill.

**What would resolve it:**
- State which input produced 3–5 fps.
- Admit that `auto` fixes the momentum phase only.
- Name the lever that actually fixes this table: horizontal (column) windowing or cheaper cells. This is really a 2-D virtualization problem.
- Rewrite the Done-when as something reachable, for example "pending, not frozen, during motion; correct content ≤500 ms p95 after rest", which is 1044.001's overload bar.

### 3.4 HIGH: the ceiling can't be enforced with count limits and a clock-free runner

The runner never reads a clock (§6). The host passes a row count computed from an average per-row cost (`FillCost.rows(in:)`, `ScrollPumpIOS.swift:44–48`). But:
- A single row is atomic. One 2,000-cell row can exceed 100 ms by itself. The summary's claim that "`complete` therefore never freezes an app for seconds on a 2,000-cell row" is false for exactly that row.
- Landing rows are rows nobody has costed yet.
- The legacy loop already lets the first report exceed the budget ("An indivisible expensive row must still make progress", `PresenterMac.swift:807–809`).

**What would resolve it:** pick a mechanism and say what it costs. Options:
- One row per report, with the host checking the clock between reports (costs FFI/batch overhead per row).
- A deterministic cost budget in kernel nodes created, not rows (deterministic and closer to time).
- Accept that the ceiling is soft at row granularity and say so.

### 3.5 MEDIUM: causes are incomplete, and some can't be classified reliably on every host

Missing causes:
- Keyboard paging (PageDown, Space, Home/End). On the web these are compositor-animated and can't wait.
- Focus-driven scroll-into-view (Tab).
- VoiceOver/TalkBack scrolling (`accessibilityScroll`).
- iOS status-bar scroll-to-top. It is animated, the target is known, and it passes over rows.
- `scrollTo({behavior:'smooth'})` and animated app scrolls.
- Selection autoscroll and drag-and-drop autoscroll.
- Viewport resize, rotation, and keyboard-inset changes. These are not "data change".
- Rubber-band and overscroll.

Classification by host:
- **macOS.** Phase and momentum phase are visible on the hand-scrolled route (`NodeViewMac.swift:180`). On the `.appKit`/responsive route, the main thread mostly sees `boundsDidChange`, not per-event phases (my knowledge, not verified). Knob drags can already be classified: `KnobDrag` uses `willStartLiveScroll` plus `leftMouseDown` (`CollectionMac.swift:34–38`). The RFC should cite it.
- **iOS.** Drag vs fling is available via `isTracking`/`isDecelerating`. Dragging the interactive scroll indicator is, as far as I know, not publicly distinguishable (knowledge, unverified).
- **Web.** Desktop trackpad drag vs momentum isn't exposed. A wheel event has no phase in the standard. A free-spinning mouse wheel looks like a "wheel" but behaves like a fling. Thumb drag isn't distinguishable. RFC §2.3 hedges this for the web and then Stage 3 relies on it.

**What would resolve it:** make "coming to rest" a function of velocity plus host-reported phase, not of the event source. For example: visible rows are owed when speed is below a readable threshold, or when rest is predicted within k frames. That also fixes the slow scrub. As written, "Scrollbar thumb drag: budget, then pending; wait at release" leaves pending rows under a user who is scrubbing slowly and reading. Add the missing causes to §2, or map them explicitly onto existing ones.

A related point: the runner already knows app-initiated causes, because it emitted the `scrollTop` op or the data change. Have the runner infer those. The host then supplies only gesture phase, velocity and landing. That is fewer fields and less trust.

### 3.6 MEDIUM: the RFC amends LLP 1010 without saying so

LLP 1010 §6.2 (lines 463–488) and the `list_viewport_within` doc comment (`lists.rs:91`: "rows the reader can see are never rationed") make visible-row completeness an invariant on native. `auto`'s fling pass-over and `smooth` both break it. The RFC lists §6.2 under Related, but §2.3 only calls the *web* a deviation. It should say explicitly that it amends LLP 1010 §6.2 on native.

### 3.7 MEDIUM: web is the standard, but the default diverges from the web in the opposite direction

CLAUDE.md makes the web "the parity oracle for every other surface". Under `auto`, a native drag waits while a web drag shows holes, and the RFC declares the *web* as the deviation. The argument that drag means reading is good. But the owner should rule explicitly on whether the native default follows the oracle (never wait on user scroll) or the reading rule. This is not in §8.

### 3.8 MEDIUM: internal contradiction on stale content

Summary point 4 says "Old rows never stand in for new ones". The §2 table's data-change row says "keep the accepted result until the new one is ready (1044.000 §5.3)", which is showing old content. LLP 1044.001 §5.3 forbids some of that ("do not … stretch old pixels to a new width"). The RFC needs to say which is meant.

### 3.9 MEDIUM: fill order is missing a category

§6 orders fill as:
1. rows required now
2. landing viewport
3. rows ahead in the direction of travel
4. rows behind

Under a `auto` fling pass-over, or `smooth`, visible rows are not "required". So currently visible, optional rows fall into none of the four categories. Should the frame's budget go to the visible pass-over rows or to the landing? Say which.

### 3.10 MEDIUM: first mount and the cold-start budget

First mount means "wait*", up to 100 ms. RULES.md sets cold start to interactive first frame at 100 ms p50. One synchronous first-mount fill could use the whole budget. LLP 1010 §6.5's 16-row, 32-point bootstrap bound is the existing answer. The RFC should defer to it, or bound first mount by the boot budget.

### 3.11 MEDIUM: a per-fill ceiling is not a rate

A list getting streamed insertions ("data change in view: wait") can take a 100 ms wait on every update, which is 10 fps with no single violation. The ceiling needs a rate as well, for example waited milliseconds per second.

### 3.12 MEDIUM: accessibility

Pending intervals are `aria-hidden`. VoiceOver's sequential navigation would skip the rows inside them, and under `smooth` those rows can exist at rest. Assistive-technology-driven moves should be a cause that always waits. Alternatively, `smooth` could degrade to `complete` while an assistive technology is active.

### 3.13 MEDIUM: the `pending` block's cost and mechanics are underspecified

- **Structure.** "Rasterized once per distinct size and repeated" needs a new render-to-image path on every host. The web has no portable way to repeat a laid-out subtree as an image (`element()` exists only in Firefox, knowledge). Realistically the web clones one element per visible pending row, which contradicts "never one per row".
- **Distinct sizes.** Rows that were measured and then retired have distinct known heights, so "per distinct size" can mean many rasters.
- **Compiler rule.** LLP 1010 §6.5 requires exactly one direct keyed `each`. A sibling `pending` block changes that rule.
- **Existing precedent.** The repo already has an authored-placeholder concept: resource `else` placeholders (`contract/lower/src/lib.rs:451–464`, LLP 1048.003 D6) and LLP 1041 §8.14's content-region placeholder. The RFC should align with that vocabulary (for example `else`, or state why `pending` differs).
- **Suggestion.** Cap bands at "one per visible pending row", and move `pending` to a later, separately ruled stage.

### 3.14 MEDIUM: Stage 5 (view reuse) is overstated

"Reused view carries no stale state" (§3.3) covers the runner's state only. Host-side state would carry over:
- layers and text rasters
- nested `ChainingScrollView` offsets
- `KnobDrag` associated objects
- first responder
- video playback
- accessibility focus

Kernel view IDs are "never reused during a runner lifetime" (`collection/api.rs:20`), so a pool means rebinding IDs in `presenter.views`. That is an architectural change, not a small tweak.

The payoff is also bounded. 1044.000's figures (QUEUE.md:136–137) are about 0.5 ms in runner and kernel versus 0.35 ms in the presenter per created view, and a native-view pool saves only the presenter share, roughly 40%. The Done-when should name that ceiling.

### 3.15 MEDIUM: Stage 0 adds apparatus that RULES.md reserves for human approval

Stage 0 adds:
- agent `holes`/`waits`/pending state
- a "two-number scroll bench" on three hosts
- a ported Expo app
- a bare-expo comparison build

Stage 4 needs agent tests that can inject fling causes and landings, which no LLP 1012 operation provides.

RULES.md (Agents) says an agent PR that adds "a check, script, registry, config file, or design doc" needs explicit human approval. LLP 1044.001 §8 went through the same process and asked approval item by item. Slice F adds that "no new permanent benchmark harness is implied". Stage 1's Done-when depends on the 1044.000 probe, which is "kept out of the repository" (1044.000 §3.1).

§8 asks only about the Expo build (Q6). Each addition should be listed as an approval request, with what it replaces.

### 3.16 MEDIUM: Stage 1's Done-when is weaker than Slice F's

Slice F's gate (1044.001 §7.6) requires selection, full-document copy, focus pins, nested scrolling and anchor replacement to match before the legacy path is deleted. It also requires the paired matrix. Stage 1 checks only hitch numbers. Carry Slice F's correctness gates over. Stage 1 is also Slice F's work with a different Done-when, so say which document governs.

### 3.17 LOW: the Implementer is unassigned, and the RFC fills the working set

RULES.md: "A spec needs an implementer and a date, or it isn't written." Strictly this is an RFC, but it has staged Done-whens.

Adding it to `llp/current/` makes the working set exactly 15, the budget: 14 tracked plus this one. An unassigned Draft takes the last slot. Consider keeping it out of `current/` until it is assigned.

### 3.18 LOW: other issues

- **Naming.** `smooth` collides with CSS `scroll-behavior: smooth` (animated scroll), which is confusing under the web-is-the-standard rule. camelCase `fillPolicy` does match existing exact-specific props (`scrollFollowEnd`, `retainFocus`, `contract/lower/src/tags.rs:303,342`).
- **Cause count.** The "six causes" of §6 versus seven rows in §2 needs a sentence: fling pass-over and landing are one cause plus `landing`.
- **Research vs RFC on the web.** Research §3 says web landing-first is "no". RFC §2.3 and Stage 3 use web velocity projection. Pick one.
- **Landing drift.** The predicted landing drifts as estimated heights are replaced during the fling. That happens under `complete`, and under budgeted overscan when rows above the landing get measured. Name it.
- **Research §2.5.** It attributes InteractionTracker to `ScrollView`, while `ListView` uses `ScrollViewer`/DirectManipulation. Both are off-thread, so the conclusion stands.

### 3.19 Host matrix: platform facts

**Verified on the web during this review:**
- WinUI `ShowsScrollingPlaceholders` defaults to `true` (Microsoft Learn page).
- Expo PR 49975 was merged 2026-09-23, and its test plan says "Can see some blanks on ultra fast scrolls". I did not verify the pool arithmetic, the overscan default, `Color.clear`, or the revision-clears-heights claim.

**My own knowledge, not verified, and I agree with it:**
- Chrome made document-level touch listeners passive by default in 56, and wheel listeners in 73.
- UIKit scrolling is committed from the main thread, and `targetContentOffset` can be both read and changed.
- RecyclerView's `GapWorker` uses `FOREVER_NS` for immediate prefetch.
- WPF `IsDeferredScrollingEnabled` applies to thumb drags only.
- Android `OverScroller.getFinalY` gives the fling's end.
- The RenderThread does not scroll.
- AppKit has no public fling target for wheel momentum.

**Not verified, and specific enough to be risky:**
- The 40 ms `BUDGET_MANAGER_DEFAULT_LIMIT`.
- Compose pausable composition "enabled in 1.10 alpha, disabled in 1.10.6".
- GTK's 200 widgets and 2 extra items.
- Safari's macOS timeout behaviour.

The Windows row "Block: thumb only" for the WinUI scroller is also uncertain to me for mouse-wheel input.

**My own knowledge, uncertain:** under responsive scrolling, the main thread may not see per-event phases (see 3.5).

### 3.20 The opposite of §4, steelmanned

The best case for separate engines is not "switch libraries mid-fling", which is a strawman. It is separate engines per *content shape*:
- a text document reader: selection, copy, giant paragraphs
- a fixed-size grid: no measurement, O(1) index, heavy images
- an inverted chat: end anchoring, streaming inserts
- a 2-D table: column windowing

Each would get a fixed policy and much less conditional logic. This also caps the test matrix: 4 hosts × 3 policies × roughly 7 causes, before layouts.

**My judgment: one engine still wins.** The shared invariants (keys, the height index, anchors, pins, `aria-setsize`/`posinset`, settle) are where the bugs are. `NOT-DOING.md`'s "no virtualList v2" and 1044.001's "one way to ration a viewport" already rule that way. Platform precedent agrees: UICollectionViewLayout, LayoutManager and Flutter slivers are all one engine with pluggable layout.

But §4's last bullet ("layout, not fill") understates something. Layout changes fill cost a great deal: fixed grids make `complete` cheap, and 2-D tables need horizontal windowing. The RFC should say that the engine's layout extension point is where the 256-column table gets fixed.

## 4. Suggestions (concrete edits)

1. **§2.2.** Replace "synchronous fill ≤100 ms" with "wait ≤2 refresh intervals (1044.001's 'no stall >2 refresh intervals'); pending shown by the next refresh". Add a rate cap on waited milliseconds per second. Either cite 1044.001 §6.2 correctly, or state that this RFC amends it.
2. **§2.1 and Stage 2.** Correct the 256-column claim. Say that `auto` changes only the momentum phase of that case. Set the Done-when to 1044.001's overload bar (pending within one refresh; correct viewport ≤500 ms p95 after rest). Add column windowing or cheaper cells as the actual fix, perhaps as a Stage 5 sibling.
3. **§2 table.** Make "rest" velocity-based, with host phase as input. Add rows for keyboard paging, focus scroll-into-view, assistive-technology scroll, status-bar scroll-to-top, animated app scrolls, autoscroll, resize/keyboard inset, and overscroll. Say which hosts can classify each, and cite `KnobDrag` (`CollectionMac.swift:11–64`) as the existing macOS thumb classifier.
4. **§6.**
   - Define `limit` once: `None` = unlimited; `Some(0)` = zero beyond required.
   - Note the current Apple/web encoding split (`exports.rs:302–305` vs `abi.rs:407`) and fix it in Stage 1.
   - Let the runner infer app causes itself.
   - Add "visible, not required" to the fill order.
   - Specify how the ceiling is enforced at row granularity.
   - Restate retirement to include "or once nothing is left to create".
5. **Summary point 4 vs the data-change row.** Pick one and reconcile with 1044.001 §5.3.
6. **Amend statements.** Name LLP 1010 §6.2 ("rows it shows are never rationed") as amended on native.
7. **§5 `pending`.**
   - Move it to its own later stage, gated on need.
   - Consider the existing `else` placeholder vocabulary.
   - Replace "never one per row" with "at most one per visible pending row".
   - State the compiler change to 1010 §6.5's single-`each` rule.
8. **§3 / Q2.** Add phased or partial rows (WinUI `x:Phase`: a cheap true subset of the row first) as a fifth outcome between hole and hitch. Also consider keyed snapshots of unchanged retired rows (key + revision + width) as cheap fill on reversal: not stale, so they don't violate "old rows never stand in".
9. **Stage 1.** Import Slice F's correctness gates.
10. **Stage 5.** Name the host-state reset list, the ID-rebinding cost, and the roughly 40% ceiling.
11. **Stage 0.** Turn each instrument into an explicit approval request in §8, per RULES.md (Agents) and following 1044.001 §8. Don't depend on the out-of-repo `scrollbench` without saying so.
12. **Measurement.** Record the 11–36 ms jump measurement somewhere citable, or remove it.
13. **Research fixes.**
    - §4: iOS and macOS collections share the 2-report turn budget.
    - The window sizes are equal.
    - Fix the `Collection.swift:215` citation to `NodeViewMac.swift:710–713` / `:1110`.
    - Reconcile the web "landing-first: no" with RFC §2.3.
14. **Naming.** Rename `smooth` (for example `"fast"` or `"prefer-frames"`) to avoid colliding with `scroll-behavior`.

## 5. Open questions the documents should put to the owner but don't

1. **Web is the oracle.** Should the native default for user drags follow the web (never wait) or the reading rule (wait)? (See 3.7.)
2. **1044.001's bars.** Does this RFC amend 1044.001's "no event-handling span >4 ms" and "pending within one refresh", or must "wait" fit inside them?
3. **LLP 1010 §6.2.** Is it acceptable to amend "rows it shows are never rationed" on native?
4. **Phased rows.** Is a cheap true partial row (phased rows) wanted, as a middle option, in addition to hole and hitch?
5. **The `pending` block.** Should it see the item, WinUI phase-0 style (more useful, costs per-row construction), or stay data-free?
6. **Assistive technology.** Should pending ever be visible to an AT user? Should `smooth` degrade to `complete` when AT is active?
7. **Instrumentation.** Approval for each Stage 0 instrument (agent fields, bench, Expo port), and what each replaces under RULES.md.
8. **Owner and date.** Who implements, and by when? Should an unassigned Draft occupy the 15th `llp/current/` slot?
9. **The 256-column table.** Is it scheduled as 2-D windowing, which is what actually fixes it, separately from the fill policy?

## 6. Recommended next step

**Revise and stay Draft.** The research record needs only small citation fixes. The RFC's core idea (a per-scenario rest rule with overrides, one engine) is worth ruling on. But in its current form Charlie would be ruling on:
- a ceiling that contradicts the LLP it cites (3.2)
- a mechanism that can't enforce that ceiling (3.4)
- a motivating example that `auto` doesn't fix (3.3)
- a Stage 2 bar that can't be met (3.3)

Fix 3.2 through 3.6, move `pending` and view reuse into separately gated stages, and turn Stage 0 into explicit approval requests. Then it is ready for the owner to rule.
