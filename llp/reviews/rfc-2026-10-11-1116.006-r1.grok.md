## 2. The larger lever

Counted tokens are mostly cached context, re-sent every step. Fewer commands move the total. More than smaller files do.

On Muse's server apps, the multiplier is repair turns after the app works. Chat-rooms was driving on a simulator eight minutes in, then spent about thirty minutes on the bar, the contrast false positive, `back`, and the database reset (128 commands against 64). Polls' own diary is about five minutes of database resets, five on the bar, and eight on curl-versus-poll timing. Round 8's 5× on polls is also the SwiftUI arm collapsing from 7.3M and 54 minutes in round 7 to 2.1M and 8 minutes. Household-list, also Muse and also a server, beat Expo (7.2M against 10.1M). Server apps are not inherently 2–5×. Round 9 should read command counts for D1, D3, D4, and D5, not D7's file sizes.

The same shape is the React 1.4×. Fresh tokens are already at or under React where the app is small: tip-splitter 53k against 62k, focus-timer 55k against 49k, flashcards 76k against 72k. Totals are 1.5–1.9× because of extra commands (24 against 16, 20 against 15, 32 against 22). Docs read rose from 729 KB to 866 KB, and that required start-here stays in every later turn. That is the cross-stack token lever this round does not take.

Deferring a new web chrome system is right. Water-tracker read `content-design`, used `RingProgress` and `Badge`, and still lost one point on a plain history list. LLP 1115 leaves grouping and layout to the author. Round 8 already measured "do they reach for the parts?" They reach for the parts a recipe names, and they skip a pattern that does not match the spec. Round 9 will not answer a new question. Accept that one-point loss, or put a bar-and-badge history row in the recipe they actually open. Do not open a visual redesign.
