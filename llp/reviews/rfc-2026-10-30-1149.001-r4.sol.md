1. **A2 still does not establish the fresh-walk equality it promises.**

   Snapping the scroller rather than individual rows fixes the seam problem, and requesting an exact final redraw fixes the settling omission. However, the coordinate rule still needs correction.

   `group_begin` receives the presenter’s scroll coordinates, which the painter adds to its offset in **points**. Row origins are also points. The RFC instead says the backend already receives device pixels and adds the device-pixel remainder directly to the origin. At 2×, an adjustment of `r` device pixels requires `r / 2` points. State the conversion explicitly. *(RFC §3/M6 L251–267; `host/linux/src/paint/rows.rs:295–297`; `paint/backend.rs:282–301`; `paint.rs:1261–1268`.)*

   More significantly, glyph phases are selected **before** the instance transform: `physical_glyph_runs` applies the device scale, then Ink chooses the horizontal phase and truncates y, then applies `ts` to the resulting bitmap instance. The row path permits axis-aligned scaling—it rejects skew, but not `ts.sx != 1` or `ts.sy != 1`. An integer translation after such a scale need not be an integer translation in the coordinates where rasterization was chosen. Consequently, “whole device-pixel shift” does not prove the claimed fresh-walk equality for every currently eligible row. Merely changing capture/replay origins also does not move the rasterization coordinates. *(RFC L254–258; `host/linux/src/ink/text.rs:13–29,41–51`; `text.rs:1106–1115`; `paint/rows.rs:535–546,596–604`.)*

   Finally, excluding sign crossings from the assertion acknowledges the remaining mismatch without defining acceptable behavior. A baseline at `0.25` truncates to `0`; translating its bitmap by −1 gives −1, while a fresh baseline at `−0.75` truncates to `0`. This affects visible ink at the viewport edge. Existing whole-pixel reuse having the same defect does not justify extending it to newly reusable fractional-scroll frames. *(RFC L265–267; `ink/text.rs:28–29`.)*

   **Smallest correction:** define the snap in the coordinates used before glyph rasterization, including units; restrict the fast path to transforms for which the equivalence holds; retain fresh recording elsewhere, including sign crossings. Keep the common scroller snap and explicit final redraw.

2. **A1’s parked drawable fixes steady-state acquisition, but its lifecycle is incomplete.**

   Worker acquisition followed by a nonblocking handoff is a sound answer to round 3’s objection. It preserves the intended host-thread encoding and worker submission division. The remaining problem is the proposed “after each present” protocol:

   - Before the first present, nothing has acquired the first parked drawable. As written, `begin()` skips indefinitely.
   - A resize or scale change can leave an already acquired drawable with the previous dimensions. A handoff needs a surface generation and size check.
   - An unsuccessful acquisition needs a new acquisition request independently of another successful present.

   These are particularly relevant because current resize changes the layer dimensions, while acquisition currently happens afterward for that frame. Parking reverses that relationship. *(RFC §3/M3 L220–226; §7/A1 L346–352; `host/ink/src/device/apple.rs:72–78`; `device/metal/present.rs:274–295`.)*

   Also specify progress when acquisition blocks. The existing worker serializes uploads, commits and presentation; synchronous resize and resource waits can wait for that queue. Unconditional prefetch must not strand those operations behind acquisition for an unavailable surface. *( `host/ink/src/present.rs:33–44,94–101`; `device/metal/present.rs:92–109,247–250`; `device/metal.rs:634–651`.)*

   **Smallest correction:** add one bootstrap/reacquisition protocol covering attachment, surface generations, failed acquisition, retained damage and retry wakes, with independent progress for resource work and captures.
