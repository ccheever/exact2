1. **A1’s main-thread acquisition needs an actual wait budget and mechanism.**

   Naming the acquisition thread answers r1’s architectural question. “A bounded wait” does not yet make the choice implementable. The existing Metal path first calls `Ring::acquire`, which can wait for GPU completion, then calls `nextDrawable`; the layer merely enables drawable timeout. Moving that sequence onto main does not establish a display-period bound. Windows’s explicit latency-handle wait is a different mechanism. *(RFC §3/M3, L166–181; `host/ink/src/device/metal/present.rs:281–295,392–408`; `device/apple.rs:49–55`; RESULTS G6c, L2630–2639.)*

   Specify the maximum main-thread stall and how it is enforced. Otherwise keep acquisition on the worker and choose an encoding handoff. Preserve pending damage after refusal, ordered uploads and retirement for skipped presentations, occlusion behavior, and the existing live-resize transaction sequence. The amendment appropriately changes D7/D11, but precedent from comparison arms cannot substitute for these contracts. *(Parent D7, L476; D11, L555–560; Metal `present.rs:247–259,309–321`.)*

2. **A1 defines the screenshot’s time, but not a sufficient retained snapshot.**

   Keeping compose targets for destination sampling and adding a window-capture comparison are substantive fixes. The intended comparisons should remain explicit: **offscreen replay against tiny-skia**, plus **the actual presented path against replay of that same sampled frame**.

   “The last encoded frame’s batches and instance buffer” omits dependencies needed to reproduce it: clear state, dimensions, texture/content revisions, atlas entries, and intermediate layer or effect outputs. Encoding consumes separate frames at each flush; masks and effects render into pooled targets. Those resources can become reusable after GPU completion, before a later screenshot requests them. Retaining buffer bytes alone is insufficient. *(RFC §3/M3, L175–179; §7/A1, L249–252; `host/linux/src/ink/effects.rs:5–40,43–50`; `host/ink/src/device/metal.rs:523–525,618–625`; `host/ink/src/layer.rs:58–78`.)*

   Require an immutable snapshot, including its resource dependencies, until capture finishes or that snapshot is replaced. A mid-animation capture must replay its sampled state without advancing or settling time.

   Also remove or enforce “up to one ahead of the glass.” The current handoff queues work and suppresses older pending presentations; it does not establish that bound. S1.5 records GPU-completion-to-display delays of **15.8–55.1 ms**. Comparing an asynchronous window capture with whichever frame was most recently encoded can compare different animation positions. Specify frame identification and synchronization for the parity capture. *(RFC L177–179; `host/ink/src/present.rs:59–89`; RESULTS S1.5, L1526–1541.)*

3. **A2’s fractional-reuse diagnosis is sound; its glyph-snapping solution is incomplete.**

   The kernel has **per-node flags and commit/layout receipts**, not an exported per-retained-row change bit. The painter derives `stale` and `suspect` row state from those receipts. It already handles placement-only changes and image-slot replacement. r2 now uses that machinery correctly; §R’s “change bit exists” should describe the painter’s derived state precisely. *(RFC §2, L92–97; §R, L273; `kernel/src/id.rs:55–82`, `txn.rs:50–87`, `layout.rs:50–65`; `host/linux/src/paint/rows.rs:175–230,433–466,491–578`.)*

   Ink’s whole-device-pixel reuse restriction is real. However, current glyph recording chooses a **quarter-pixel horizontal raster phase** and truncates vertical position before storing the atlas UV and bitmap rectangle. Translating that bitmap and snapping its rectangle cannot generally reproduce rasterization at a different phase. Row-local storage alone does not recover the original unsnapped glyph position. *(RFC §3/M6, L182–188; `host/linux/src/ink/rows.rs:287–303`; `ink/text.rs:27–60`; `host/ink/src/glyph/swash.rs:95–103,131–137,245–265`.)*

   Choose phase-correct replay, or explicitly propose a text-rendering change under D3/D9 and qualify it. The “half a pixel, only while moving” claim also needs an answer for a row that stops at a fractional offset. Preserve fresh-walk parity across fractional positions, negative coordinates, scale changes, coverage clips and image arrival; the existing fractional-scroll test is directly relevant. The UIKit assertion does not establish equivalent behavior here. *(RFC Q5, L243–245; parent D3.3, L308–314; `presenter/kept_rows_tests.rs:312–340`.)*

   M7’s rectangular-scissor restriction is a useful improvement. Add the minimal lifetime rule: persistent ranges remain immutable while referenced by submitted frames or capture snapshots; replacement and reclamation respect those references. State the fallback for **all** unsupported outer clips, including coverage clips. This remaining r1 finding was only partly answered. *(RFC L189–192,253–255; parent D4, L318–337.)*

4. **A4 still gives two conflicting service rules.**

   r2 correctly preserves headless `clock`/`settle` and permits progress at rest. But “at most once per display period” conflicts with servicing every due timer, reply or image report “on that wake.” A timer and a reply arriving after the period’s first pass require additional service turns or deferred delivery. *(RFC §3/M1, L134–142; §7/A4, L257–260.)*

   The smallest correction is: **coalesce redundant polling; service actual pending work and deadlines independently, preserving ordering**. If timer delivery is intentionally deferred during presentation, specify that policy and consume/rearm the deadline so it cannot cause a past-deadline wake loop. Today’s carrier explicitly services expired timers without requiring a frame. Headless seeks must continue firing timers at their own instants and draining settlement rounds. *(Apple-painted `window.rs:552–589,636–646,742–757`; `host/linux/src/agent.rs:994–999,1091–1141,1223–1269`.)*

5. **Stage 0 is the right response to unpublished attribution, but its measurement definitions need tightening.**

   The §2 phases sum to **43.1 ms/s**, consistent in magnitude with G4’s **43.83 ms/s** main-thread CPU. S1.4’s inclusive totals also make a several-ms/s walk and approximately 3-ms/s scene encode plausible. That is a consistency check, not verification of the unavailable control attribution.

   Two issues remain:

   - The trace markers measure **elapsed `CACurrentMediaTime`**, not thread CPU. Publishing an exclusive-span script does not turn elapsed time into CPU attribution. Label both quantities and validate savings with CPU measurements. *(RFC L65–83,110–118; `device/apple.rs:215–227`; RESULTS S1.4, L1381–1402.)*
   - Make count denominators explicit. **358 walks × 0.16 ms over ten seconds = 5.7 ms/s**. Conversely, “3,362 layouts a second at a microsecond each” implies approximately **3.4 ms/s**, not the listed 0.4. *(RFC L73–76.)*

   Stage 0 also needs counters from the **live carrier being measured**. Windows’s existing agent `perf` drives the headless presenter, not its desktop window. Renderer statistics are published at encode/finish, rather than presentation. Use cumulative carrier counters and distinguish paint attempts, submitted frames and displayed frames; include pool allocation, completion-blocked reuse and retired bytes. Otherwise “per presented frame” can conceal precisely the scheduling and retention problems under investigation. *(RFC L113–120; RESULTS G6b, L2468–2480; `ink/stats.rs:29–51`; `ink.rs:683–690`.)*

6. **The pool lead is worth checking, but shortening retention does not yet follow from it.**

   The pool reuses a matching target **as soon as its last GPU use completes**. Its 120-frame rule controls eviction of unused targets; it is not a mandatory wait before reuse. Shorter retention cannot free resources still needed by outstanding GPU work. *(RFC §2, L87–91; M4a, L145–148; `host/ink/src/layer.rs:28–34,58–78`.)*

   Moreover, S1.4 reports **60.656 MiB of all tracked textures** in the normal run, alongside the unexplained graphics charge. A further 335 MiB of viewport targets needs reconciliation with that evidence. G6b’s 1,201 targets occurred during thousands of redundant paints; G6c already removed that churn. It does not establish an excessive current retention policy. *(RESULTS S1.4, L1444–1468; G6b, L2476–2516; G6c, L2688–2713.)*

   Keep the hypothesis, but condition M4a on evidence distinguishing idle retention from incomplete GPU work or driver allocations. Withdraw “CPU unchanged”: aggressive trimming can add allocations and driver work.
