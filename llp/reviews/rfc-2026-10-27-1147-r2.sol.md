D1’s syntax holds up. D3 resolves r1’s cap issues, and D4’s live sampling and completion-based phase rule match the code; the existing JS live-clock tests pass 6/6. Removing cost prediction resolves r1’s `update` admission-API mismatch. Five fundamental issues remain.

1. **A refusal can strand the remaining uncapped timers permanently.**

   [D6](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/llp/1147-timers-that-may-wait.rfc.md:194) gives `release` the behavior of `advance_timed`, which [stops on a timer’s refusal](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/runner/src/runner/advance.rs:613). [D7](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/llp/1147-timers-that-may-wait.rfc.md:211) supplies no wake for an uncapped held timer.

   Suppose two `after` timers await the same scroll’s end. The first action gets a recoverable `TaskKey` refusal. Its firing is consumed, release stops, and the second remains held. The scroll has already ended; no further end event or cap wakes it. Nothing specifies a continuation after the partial release.

   **Instead:** Make release eligibility persist while the condition remains satisfied. Specify how remaining firings resume after a refusal or fire limit, while preserving consumption of the refused firing and rollback of its action.

2. **D5 cannot assign its promised times to timers already held when the agent takes over.**

   [D5](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/llp/1147-timers-that-may-wait.rfc.md:159) makes `scrollend` run at its deadline. But the agent can take over a running session, [freezing at the current clock floor](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/Agent.swift:771). Switching the runner to a seek clock [does not normalize timer state](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/runner/src/runner/advance.rs:77).

   A timer due at 100 ms remains held through a scroll. An input commits at 400 ms; the agent takes over at 500 ms. D5 now requires the held action to run at 100 ms, observing state committed at 400 ms. Clamping it to 500 ms avoids the reversal but contradicts the table.

   **Instead:** Define clock takeover explicitly: release previously held work at the takeover floor, once, preserve repeating timers’ phase, and cancel outstanding platform release callbacks. Reserve D5’s calculated times for firings that become due under the seek clock.

3. **D7’s 100 ms fallback cannot provide D2’s scroll exclusion.**

   [D7](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/llp/1147-timers-that-may-wait.rfc.md:204) substitutes silence between `scroll` events for a scroll’s end on older Safari. Those are different conditions: [CSSOM requires the gesture to have completed](https://drafts.csswg.org/cssom-view/#scrolling), and Exact’s UIKit implementation explicitly [keeps waiting while tracking or dragging](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/IOS/ScrollEndIOS.swift:26).

   Drag a list, pause with the finger down for 150 ms, then continue. The fallback releases an uncapped timer after 100 ms inside the same unfinished gesture. A resumed movement can then encounter the timer’s synchronous batch.

   **Instead:** Retain the quiet timeout only for unbracketed moves. Track gesture and animation lifetimes independently of offset events, including cancellation and scroller destruction; explicitly qualify any host that cannot establish the strict condition.

4. **The new idle predicate is incompatible with releasing every held idle timer together.**

   [D2](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/llp/1147-timers-that-may-wait.rfc.md:107) requires no other timer due and no pending frame or batch. [D6](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/llp/1147-timers-that-may-wait.rfc.md:194) leaves that predicate to the host but releases a whole kind. Apple [combines the returned receipts into one batch](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/host/apple/src/host.rs:1020), whose receipts [share the final kernel tree](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/host/apple/src/host.rs:1303).

   Literally, two due idle timers prevent each other from qualifying until their caps. If held idle timers are implicitly excluded, both can release—but the first might enable a gated frame task, while the second still executes before the host can apply the first batch and recheck eligibility.

   **Instead:** Define which held timers the “other timer” test excludes. Give idle a single-firing release boundary, apply that firing, and recheck before admitting another.

5. **R2 still incorrectly dismisses the idle budget raised by r1.**

   [D2](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/llp/1147-timers-that-may-wait.rfc.md:114) says overrunning an idle period delays no awaited frame. Its predicate checks timers already due, not work becoming due during the batch. The [idle-callback specification](https://w3c.github.io/requestidlecallback/#idle-periods) explicitly bounds idle periods by upcoming tasks and frames and requires accounting for subsequent layout work.

   At 100 ms, all four conditions hold. A precise UI timer is due at 101 ms, so the offered idle period is only 1 ms. An admitted 4 ms Contract batch delays that timer until 104 ms and can miss its frame, despite fitting comfortably within a nominal frame.

   **Instead:** Require an admission policy that respects the available idle time and upcoming precise deadlines, including application cost. Defer `idle` until that policy exists; `scrollend` addresses the stated consumers without it.
