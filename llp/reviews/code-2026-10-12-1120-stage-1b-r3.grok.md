DO NOT LAND

1. **BLOCKING** — A parked upload that is not the lane head is invisible to admission, so a later request can take the bytes it is waiting on and the lane stalls. `Book::parked` counts only `jobs.front()` (`host/apple/src/executor_async.rs:108-116`), and `admit` treats that as already taken (`host/apple/src/executor_core.rs:483-486`). A park is reinserted in ticket order (`executor_async.rs:887-901`), and the independent lane passes over a head that fits but is at its origin cap (`executor_async.rs:403-414`). That head stays queued in front of the upload. `select` then stops at the upload because a head short of bytes is not passed over (`executor_async.rs:403-406`), so everything behind it waits. After the origin-limited head finishes, the upload is the head and still does not fit, because the later job's buffers are in `bytes` and that job cannot run. Example: six in-flight requests to one origin hold about 37 MiB, a small seventh stays queued at the cap, a 61 MiB upload parks behind it, and a 1 MiB POST is admitted. The upload needs about 63 MiB and never gets it. Question 8 said this outstanding charge counts for the whole lane, not only when the upload is the head. Sum `limit - charge + size` for every sized job in that lane's queue.

2. **MATERIAL** — A hop already chosen still starts after its run has settled, and then takes a new in-flight place. The "settled, drop it" check runs only in `select` (`executor_async.rs:367-388`). The starter then invokes those closures with no second look, and bails only if the machine has retired (`executor_async.rs:347-352`). The upload closure always lends and calls `fetch` (`executor_async.rs:935-955`). If the finisher settles in between, `physical` returns the reservation (`executor_async.rs:1236-1238`) and `Tracked::start`'s missing-reservation arm counts the origin and the in-flight slot again and starts the exchange (`executor_async.rs:1302-1310`). `FetchTask::hop` avoids this only once `done` or the abort signal is set, and the timer sets those after it has posted (`executor_async.rs:968-979`), so the gap is real. At invocation, start nothing unless that run is still unclaimed. Do not take places for a settled run.

3. **MATERIAL** — A reader that unwinds never posts `Ended`, so the run's `live` count stays above zero and the charge never reaches its physical release. The job posts `Ended` only on the normal path (`executor_async.rs:624-646`). A panic in `read_at_most` drops the reader permit in `Reader`'s destructor and skips the post. An ordered upload that already settled then keeps its bytes, and one that has not settled keeps the blocking slot. The handoff path already uses `catch_unwind` and posts `Ended` after the call (`executor_async.rs:507-513`). Post `Ended` from a drop guard at the start of the reader job.

4. **MINOR** — The question 8 tests never park behind a queued head. `a_parked_upload_starts_when_its_bytes_drain_and_is_not_crowded_out` parks in front of a running request (`executor_async_tests.rs:376-412`). `an_upload_that_cannot_fit_beside_the_requests_behind_it_is_refused` refuses at sizing, before a park (`executor_async_tests.rs:433-466`). Neither admits a request after a park that is not `jobs.front()`. There is also no test that a hop already sitting in `starts` is abandoned when its run is claimed before `start` runs.

5. **NIT** — Settled hop closures are dropped while the state lock is held: `select` drops one at `executor_async.rs:373-374`, and `physical` drops the rest at `executor_async.rs:1240`. A queued upload closure owns the file bytes. That free runs under the lock. Drop them after releasing it.

Round 2's fixes hold: the claim is taken under the lock when the outcome is produced, a forget cancels only a safe read, a reader is registered only for an open run, upload bytes follow the buffer once lent, the exact read buffer is capped at the charged size plus one detection byte, pass-over restarts at the head, a park keeps admission order, the adapter holds the body until `end` and stops between chunks when aborted, and a partial base set calls `stop` on the caller's lock (`executor_core.rs:355-365`). With the switch unset, `parked` is zero and the worker path is the old one. Linux calls `Core::start` and render calls `start_on`. Only Apple's `start_switched` reads `EXACT_HTTP_ASYNC`. `executor_async.rs` is 1479 lines and `executor_core.rs` is 1500, so both sit on the cap.
