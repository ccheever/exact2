## 3. Directional risks

- **D3's darker fill** surprises the author and fails the hand-built screen. It is the default most likely to create a new tell (buttons darker than Settings and Clock).
- **Broad focus retention** surprises the author and, if it lands in the kernel rather than `PointerIOS`, breaks the web's blur-on-click. The Mac does not get UIKit's rule copied over.
- **The grouped wrap** is safe at default sizes (web 16, Mac 13, an 8rem field still sits beside a short label). It is a shared lowering, so say so. A long label on a narrow web page will wrap. That is acceptable.
- **The segmented-height fix** shares a path with the tab bar (`style.rs` around 1446). The exception is the segmented control only.
- **A warning that matches a minus symbol or the word "Remove"** will fire on a stepper. Match a destructive button or a Delete label, or skip the warning until `contract vocab swipe` is a snippet that compiles. `list-rows.md` does not compile as written; pointing builders at it spends the tokens D4 is trying not to spend.
- **D7's one-liners** are the next uncanny-valley guess: haptics on every tap, `autocapitalize="words"` on an email, `aria-hidden` on a symbol that was the only name. The line has to name the case (a selection, a completion, a person's name, a decorative symbol).
- **Cheaper most of the value:** D1, the agent rendering at the device text size during the build, white-on-accent, and swipe plus a collapsing title in the two patterns. That is three lanes. The late `verify` pass, the fill recolour, the focus change, and the D7 lines are the rest of the cost and the new risk.
- **The larger lever** is the chrome the patterns and `exact:content` demonstrate. Graders are docking custom headers, custom search, answer tiles, and round buttons. Demote those parts from start-here's required list until each one survives T1 beside a named Apple screen. Another swipe helper or another one-liner adds a name and a guess.
