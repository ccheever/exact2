### 4. The Android edit path cannot keep a gain map, and its crop runs after the scale

D15 (lines 686–704) makes "keep HDR" the unsaid default of `transformImage`, and the ceiling (line 755) assigns that to tier 1. The same paragraph uses `ImageDecoder.setTargetSize` and `setCrop`. The method the RFC quotes crops "a subset of the (possibly) scaled image", so scale happens before crop. **Resolve:** give Android the same shape as Apple: one oriented decode, then rotate, flip, crop, and resize in D14's order, then attach a gain map transformed by that same matrix when the API level has one. **1. The Android codec's order is not D14's, and the below-34 decode is not upright.** `llp/1133.019-photos-image.rfc.md` line 832, against D14 at lines 764–771.

D14's order is upright, then rotate, then flip, then crop in that frame, then resize. The new Android sentence puts turn, flip, and scale in one `Matrix` on the whole bitmap, and crops with a second `Bitmap.createBitmap` of a subset. `createBitmap(source, x, y, w, h, matrix, filter)` takes its rectangle in the source frame, so splitting the crop out is the right fix for a free-angle crop. Scale is still in the first matrix, so the resize happens before the crop, and the crop rectangle is in pre-resize pixels of the turned frame. A crop plus `resizeWidth` then either misses the scaled bitmap or returns the crop's size rather than the resized crop.
