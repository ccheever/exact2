The design has unresolved correctness and privacy defects. References below are to `llp/1133.033-foreground-service-voip.rfc.md` unless another file is named.

1. **MATERIAL — D8, lines 469–472; §9.2, line 980: the proposed native mute does not silence consumers.**

   The plan relies on `setTrackEnabled(false)`. Both native implementations currently change the `enabled` flag without gating delivered audio: `packages/media-capture/apple/MediaCapture.swift:93–99` and `packages/media-capture/android/MediaCapture.kt:97–103`. Consumers receive the shared capture, and the recorders write its samples without checking that flag (`packages/recording/apple/Recording.swift:234–254`; Android `Recording.kt:247–260`). Harmony can therefore show “muted” while recording speech. Disabled audio tracks must deliver silence to consumers under [Media Capture’s semantics](https://www.w3.org/TR/mediacapture-streams/#dom-mediastreamtrack-enabled).

   **Decision needed:** make per-track disabling affect audio delivery while preserving other tracks sharing the capture. Include a native drive that checks recorded content during mute, rather than only the reported fields.

2. **MATERIAL — D16, lines 775–779: tools and wakes may turn the microphone back on.**

   The origin table permits `setCallMuted` regardless of its Boolean argument. But D8 explicitly makes `setCallMuted(false)` re-enable the call’s capture. This contradicts inherited recording D9 (`1133.009`, lines 749–757), which refuses `setTrackEnabled(enabled=true)` to tools and wakes even with permission already granted. The existing declaration expresses that conditional restriction in `packages/media-capture/exact.json:174–179`.

   **Decision needed:** distinguish muting from unmuting in admission. Apply capture-start authority to unmuting, and to unholding wherever it resumes capture; retain tool access to privacy-reducing operations.

3. **MATERIAL — D9, lines 498–522: Android needs a different transaction protocol.**

   Two decisions incorrectly apply a common callback model. App-initiated Core-Telecom operations complete through `CallControlResult`; they do not generally return through the remote-action callbacks that D9 proposes matching to outstanding sends. Separately, returning from a remote callback immediately after queuing an event acknowledges success before the app performs the action. An absent or delayed handler could leave media running after Telecom accepted a hold or disconnect. Google requires the requested action to execute within five seconds. See the [Core-Telecom guide](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/telecom/voip-app/telecom#remote-surface-support) and [AndroidX’s separate request and callback paths](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/androidx/androidx/androidx-main/core/core-telecom/src/main/java/androidx/core/telecom/internal/CallSession.kt).

   **Decision needed:** complete app sends from operation results. For external requests, await bounded execution or enforce the transition synchronously in the host before acknowledging it.

4. **MATERIAL — D2, lines 211–214; D9, lines 492–496; D16, line 773: the hidden incoming-call path is circular.**

   `reportIncomingCall` promises to proceed when a hidden app learns of a call through its connection. Yet D9 places `addCall` inside the `phoneCall` service, while D2 refuses the first service claim unless the reader is resumed or supplies a permitting platform event. Before ringing, there is no notification interaction. An ordinary socket callback or the fixture’s delayed timer does not itself supply Android’s background-start exemption. [Android’s restrictions](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/restrictions-bg-start) distinguish those exemptions from ordinary background execution.

   **Decision needed:** define how an incoming call obtains Telecom support and service authorization, in what order, including the older `ConnectionService` path. The locked-screen **Ring me** drive currently depends on an undefined transition.

5. **MATERIAL — D10, lines 579–582; §4, line 736: a video call does not authorize arbitrary background camera capture.**

   CallKit audio activation provides no blanket camera exemption. Apple’s supported video-call multitasking path requires the appropriate PiP/multitasking configuration and still interrupts capture when PiP is stashed. See [background camera restrictions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturesession/interruptionreason/videodevicenotavailableinbackground) and [video-call PiP](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avkit/adopting-picture-in-picture-for-video-calls).

   Android additionally requires the `camera` foreground-service type and its permission; D11 and D16 derive only the call and microphone types. Its [camera service requirements](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types#camera) also retain while-in-use start restrictions. Both built camera implementations enforce foreground lifetimes.

   **Decision needed:** retain foreground-only camera capture, or explicitly design the supported camera lifecycle and platform prerequisites. A video-call Boolean is insufficient.

6. **MATERIAL — D9, lines 509–517; D10, lines 571–576; D21, lines 906–913: lock-screen answering and audio readiness have incompatible ordering.**

   D9 promises that `callanswer` may open the microphone, but withholds CallKit fulfillment until that handler’s commit. D10 admits hidden capture only after `audio` becomes true. Apple’s sequence is answer fulfillment, system activation, then `didActivate`; consequently that initial hidden handler cannot yet pass admission. [Apple explains this sequence](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2016/230/?time=2007).

   The substitute sets `audio` true with the answer, making the same handler succeed under test.

   **Decision needed:** separate answer acceptance from audio readiness. Start media after activation, as Harmony’s outgoing flow already does, and reproduce that ordering in the substitute.

7. **MATERIAL — D8, lines 466–473: Android cannot report the effect of its own mute operation.**

   `setCallMuted(true)` disables local capture, but `Call.muted` reads only Telecom’s read-only `isMuted` flow. If that flow remains false, a successful local mute still reports false. A button toggling from `Call.muted` repeatedly requests mute and cannot represent or undo its own state. Google distinguishes system mute from per-call outgoing-audio silence in its [audio-state and call-silence documentation](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/telecom/voip-app/telecom).

   **Decision needed:** define effective mute from local and system state, or expose them separately with clear semantics. Assess the local-silence extension against the pinned library before choosing its mapping.

8. **MATERIAL — D11, lines 617–623; D13, line 681: Android’s full-screen incoming UI has no owner.**

   Self-managed Telecom ordinarily expects the calling app to provide its call UI. `CallStyle` supplies a notification; a full-screen intent launches an Activity supplied by the app or host. It does not summon a general system call screen. D13 instead assigns the screen to Telecom and places the app’s custom incoming view after answering. See [Android’s incoming-call flow](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/telecom/dialer-app#answer-incoming-calls) and [full-screen notification behavior](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/time-sensitive).

   **Decision needed:** choose a host-owned ringing Activity or an authored pre-answer surface and define its lifecycle, or narrow the claim to the system-rendered notification. This determines ownership and feasibility, not merely appearance.

9. **MINOR — D2, lines 178–186; D11, lines 590–595: the service declaration contradicts its promotion mask.**

   D2 declares `.PhoneCall` with its individual `phoneCall` type; D11 promotes that service as `phoneCall|microphone`. Android throws `IllegalArgumentException` when promotion includes a type absent from that service’s manifest. Declaring the corresponding permissions alone does not fix it. [Android’s launch requirements](https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/launch#promote-a-service-to-the-foreground) state this explicitly.

   **Decision needed:** explicitly give the call carrier the permitted combined mask, or coordinate separate services, and reconcile that choice with “one service per type.”

10. **MINOR — §9.2, lines 982–984; §12, lines 1103–1105: the web consumer does not establish the promised PiP control surface.**

    The described consumer records microphone audio and plays an `<audio>` resource. It supplies neither a PiP surface nor an entry operation, yet promises ending the call through PiP controls. Installing Media Session handlers does not itself create that surface; Google’s [conferencing example](https://web.dev/articles/media-session#video_conferencing_actions) assumes a call already placed into PiP.

    **Decision needed:** provide a concrete supported PiP consumer or narrow the claimed browser-control validation.

Define persistent hold/mute gates as part of that ownership: opening a replacement track or changing its authored `enabled` value must not accidentally defeat the call’s state. 4. **IN-DELTA · MATERIAL — D11, lines 686–696 conflicts with D12, lines 795–805: the designed push path starts a microphone service without current microphone authority.**

Android explicitly warns that a stored `PERMISSION_GRANTED` result does not establish current background authority. The resulting promotion can throw `SecurityException` before the incoming notification is posted. Two material concerns remain.

**1. IN-DELTA · MATERIAL — D9, lines 590–607; D17, lines 987–991: global gates cannot safely isolate held and active calls.**

The new restriction allows held call A alongside active call B. A owns tracks created while A was connected, but A’s hold gate applies to every app capture track, including newly opened tracks.

Consider A owning track TA, then B being answered and opening TB:

- If A’s hold gate remains app-wide, TB is silent and B cannot use the microphone.
- If gates follow only active B, TA becomes audible again. A’s still-running consumers can receive microphone audio while A remains held.

The owning-call field is expressly used to stop the correct tracks at end; it does not reconcile these conflicting gate scopes. This defeats lines 604–608’s guarantee that a held call remains silent regardless of its handler. It is a public lifecycle decision, not a mapping an implementer can choose without changing the promised behavior.

**Decision needed:** preserve each held call’s gates on its owned tracks while defining how app-wide gates affect independent tracks, or restrict the module to one **live** call. Validate the chosen rule by holding A, answering B, and confirming A remains silent while B receives sound.

**2. IN-DELTA · MATERIAL — D9, line 589; D10, lines 618–620, 647–648 and 663–666: `didDeactivate` does not end a call’s logical audio claim.**

The revision ends the claim at `didDeactivate` and restores the app’s audio configuration. But a VoIP call held for a cellular call remains live while CallKit deactivates its audio session. That callback sequence is documented in [Twilio’s CallKit interruption guidance](https://github.com/twilio/voice-quickstart-objc#callkit). Apple also explicitly distinguishes process-level audio activation from individual call lifetimes: switching between an app’s calls need not change the audio session. [Apple DTS](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/800247).

Under the proposed rule, holding a call releases its configuration claim. Unhold has no corresponding claim reacquisition: D10 creates claims at start or answer. If the intended interpretation is instead “release an already ended call at the next deactivation,” ending an already deactivated held call has no guaranteed subsequent callback to complete cleanup.

**Decision needed:** separate logical call claims from provider audio activation. Retain the claim while its call survives a hold; after the last call ends, restore app configuration once outstanding CallKit audio ownership is relinquished, including when the session is already inactive.
