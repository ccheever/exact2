## 1. The decisions

**D1 — RIGHT.** These are measured losses, two of them regressions of LLP 1116.000. Trivia's results open under the bar on both runs (6 vs 8). Grocery is cheaper and faster than SwiftUI and loses because swipe-to-delete does nothing (platform 6 vs 9). Event RSVP's date and time inputs have no border. Stopwatch at AX5 shows only ":48." Keep `-exact-fit="shrink"` opt-in on the readout. The fallback that only caps the font size would hide Dynamic Type. Fit-to-width does not fix the bare lap list; that is content, and it belongs outside D1.

**D2 — RIGHT WITH CHANGE.** Set `accent-color` once. The mechanism exists, and the core's "leave colours unsaid" tells builders not to use it. Add `RingProgress`: focus-timer and water-tracker lose a ring to a bar. Stop there. Drop the typeface instruction, and keep a page tint web-only and optional. Flashcards and habit-tracker already shipped eyebrows, icon tiles, summary cards, and a blue accent, and graders still called them generic. The React apps that beat them are a different object: a warm page, a wordmark, a hero illustration, and state drawn as a second color (habit's done and not-done dots are the same blue). A custom font on iOS fights LLP 1115, where unsaid type is the platform's and Dynamic Type is the point, and it spends the steps the token gap is made of. Another paragraph has the failure mode this RFC already names for sheets: a sentence did not change what Muse built.

**D3 — RIGHT WITH CHANGE.** Polls is still one long screen (platform 6 vs 8) after the core grew a sheet sentence. A tested `list-detail` and `rest` pattern is the right answer to that. It is not, by itself, the small-web token lever. Tip-splitter, focus-timer, habit-tracker, and recipe-finder are one-screen apps. Their first command reads `start-here`, `content-design`, and `fetch-loading-error`, not `navigation-stack`. Each pattern has to replace the recipe it stands in for, or it adds a third file to a context that is already 41k against React's 25k. `one-screen` and `dashboard` need the desktop composition water-tracker still missed: it painted a phone layout across 1280px after the adaptive recipe shipped.

**D4 — RIGHT WITH CHANGE.** Early bytes ride every later step, and round 2's arithmetic still holds. The budget is wrong. Cutting the core from 14.2KB to 10KB is about 1k tokens, roughly 25k across a 25-step web run. The tip-splitter gap is 0.58M. About 16k tokens per step times the shared steps is ~256k. About nine extra steps at 41k is ~369k. So the step half is larger, and the context half is the whole first command: core plus `AGENTS.md` (3.7KB, auto-loaded) plus the two or three recipes. Measure that sum. Keep a one-line pointer for every pitfall that leaves the core. Round 1's token bill was grepping long guides, and moving the test grammar out without a pointer buys steps back. Batching `vocab` is the one item aimed at the larger half.

## 2. What is missing

D2 is a piece of the React quality gap, not the answer to it. Accent plus three more `exact:content` parts will not outdraw a designed page, and round 2's library was already used in four of nine builds without moving the React losses. The zero-token version of brand is the pattern: `dashboard` and `one-screen` already contain the accent, a filled summary, and a ring, so the builder does not read `content-design` (5KB) and does not spend a design pass.

D3 and D4 are real levers and not the biggest ones for small web apps. The biggest is fewer steps. Patterns and batched `vocab` are the right tools only when the pattern is the app those steps were spent rediscovering.

The SwiftUI token gap is a different population. Round 2's Muse iOS builds ran long because context was 15–25k higher by step 20 and the runs were longer (chat 101 steps against 65). Round 3 still has chat-rooms at 6.9M against 4.7M and polls at 7.4M against 4.2M. A 4KB core cut does not touch a 70-step simulator loop. Say that is out of scope, or round 4 will "fix the floor" and miss the stack where all-three is still zero.

## 3. Risks

- A taught typeface, or a page tint that reaches iOS, spends the native-and-dark-mode wins (mood-journal, notes, book-club) on a look SwiftUI and Expo often buy by dropping dark mode.
- D2's paragraph and new components land in the same early read D4 is trying to shrink. Brand-by-documentation raises both tokens and step count.
- Patterns that stay long, or that builders open and then re-read `navigation-stack`, copy the old skeleton failure: the file rides every later step. A pattern that teaches a stale save or a one-screen app teaches it to every build.
- D1.4's size-cap fallback would make the stopwatch fail the access it was meant to pass.

## 4. Verdict

**READY WITH CHANGES.**

1. D2 keeps accent and `RingProgress`. No typeface step. Page tint stays web-only. State color lives in the component, not a new essay.
2. Patterns replace the recipe they cover, and the one-screen and dashboard patterns include a width-aware web composition.
3. D4's budget is the first command's total bytes, with pointers left behind for anything moved out.
4. The Muse iOS step loop is named as the remaining SwiftUI token lever, and is not claimed as solved here.
