1. **MATERIAL — A polled `audioLevel` has no rule for a seek, a coalesced gap, or one request per target.** D1 (lines 212–221) says `levels` exists so two coalesced answers lose no bar, and that the implementation "answers the latest" after computing levels as audio arrives. D10 item 7 (lines 629–631) makes the runner re-ask every `interval` of the session clock. §10 (lines 700–704) defines one sample, at step ⌊t/50⌋, and says energy "integrates it." §11 (lines 760–761) reads `mediaRecorderState.duration` "at the polled rate," and the declaration (lines 595–599) gives that source no `"poll"`. On a seek clock a repeating timer fires every beat (`runner/src/runner/advance.rs` lines 509–528, `coalesce = live && !once`). A jump stops after each commit that sends, because the runner keeps one request per target (`host/web-js/agent.js` lines 490–493, `host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/Agent.swift` lines 941–944), up to 4096 stops and then one advance. Under the agent no audio arrives between asks, so "the latest" is one sample. Drive 1 (lines 773–777) asks for the waveform's bars after `clock +3000`. The recorder does that today by synthesizing every step from elapsed time in one draw (`apps/recorder/modules/web/index.js` lines 43–49). **Decision:** a polled answer's `levels`, `totalAudioEnergy`, and `totalSamplesDuration` cover every interval of session time since the last delivered answer, including a jump, synthesized from §10's formula under the agent. The runner keeps at most one ask of that source in flight. `duration` joins that poll, or Contract derives elapsed time from `now()` minus the pause intervals.

2. **MATERIAL — Capture ownership is two designs, and §12 points the track table at the wrong object.** D6 (lines 362–366) keeps one capture per input device for the process, with a subscription per session, which drive 7 (lines 790–792) requires. D6 (lines 393–394) makes the engine at the session's first capture and stops it when that session's last track ends. D3 (lines 303–308) says consumers, including the camera, never open the input, and the fan-out is "the module's" `AVAudioEngine`. D5 (lines 341–347) gives recording the only tap on the input node's bus 0. Apple documents one tap on any bus (`installTap(onBus:bufferSize:format:block:)` discussion: "You can install only one tap on any bus"). D7 (line 415) lets the first disagreeing claim hold, and changes the category when a capture starts and when the last ends (line 431), with no handoff when the winning claim ends and another remains. §12 (lines 819–820) amends LLP 1133.003 D2 so "the track table holds audio tracks." That D2 (lines 181–184) ties a track to the data resource that created it and stops it when the resource is forgotten. 1133.003 §13 (lines 666–669) leaves a camera's `getUserMedia` tracks out of that table. LLP 1133.008 is cited throughout and is not in the tree. A camera's video-with-sound path is one `AVCaptureSession`. Freezing "consumers never open the input" and "recording owns bus 0" before that RFC agrees locks the camera to this engine's fan-out. **Decision:** `@exact/media-capture` owns one process-wide capture per device, the single input tap, the fan-out, and the session claim. The capture lives until the process's last subscriber ends. Recording subscribes and encodes. The hatch is a mixer on that subscription and does not install on bus 0. When the winning claim ends, the owner recomputes mode and options from the claims still held. The capture track table is a new host table. It is not 1133.003 D2, and a later `AVCaptureSession` either consumes this fan-out or this capture yields the device, which the camera RFC agrees before this one freezes it.

LLP 1096 D8 (lines 600–605) records that nothing observes that notification, because the observer the engine's queue waits on deadlocks when the main thread tears the engine down. Apple's discussion of the notification says the engine stops and uninitializes itself and then posts, and that the handler runs on an internal queue and must not deallocate the engine there. A capture start changes the category (D7 line 431), which is the route change drive 8's `playSound` during a take has to survive (lines 798–799). ## BEYOND

- **Polled live sources on the seek clock.** Recording is the first `"poll"`. Motion and the other sensors will meet the same seek, the same one-request-per-target rule, and the same question of what one answer contains after a jump. The rule in finding 1 belongs on the seam, in the form geolocation D5 already uses for readers: one acquisition, many readers, and a defined answer across a gap.

- **A host-owned object table shared by packages.** Tracks and audio-session claims are a new kind of seam object. 1133.003 already has a track-id namespace for `video srcObject=` with a resource lifetime. Notifications D7 is the precedent for one process-wide object the host keeps and packages reach through the context. Camera, speech, and screen-capture will look up this table. One table, with one lifetime, or `srcObject` will not find a camera track and a recording track will die when its creating resource is forgotten.

- **The audio-session owner is process-wide.** Camera and speech will claim it beside recording, the sound arm, and the video players. The claim math (who wins, what happens when the winner ends, the background predicate, the route-change restart) is host architecture. Findings 3 and 4 are the recording-shaped cases of that owner.

- **Caller origin and app foreground are different facts.** `"origin": "foreground"` mixes LLP 1133.002 D12's "who called" with "is the app in the foreground." Camera, speech, and screen-capture need the same split as finding 14. The seam should grow a foreground fact, and leave `person` meaning the person.

- **An implementation-as-substitute does not drive a target that has no implementation, and a bound implementation does not see `prefer`.** Clipboard already declared the flag (1133.006 D8 item 4). Recording is the first module whose acceptance needs a `prefer` overlay and a synthetic file on a host with no native code. Adopting the flag as "the bound implementation runs" gives up 1133.001's "drive every target without native code" for Linux and Windows until those implementations exist.

- **`grant-missing` is per interface, satisfied by any one of its lines** (`contract/cli/src/grants.rs` lines 235–257). That matches geolocation's two accuracy lines. It breaks as soon as one interface declares two capabilities. The camera will meet it on `@exact/media-capture` the day that package lists both `device.camera` and `device.microphone`. The per-name rule in finding 7 is the general fix.
