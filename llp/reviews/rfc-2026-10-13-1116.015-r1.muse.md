# Review of LLP 1116.015 — What Exact asks a builder to learn

## 1. Verdict: RETHINK

The direction — delete knowledge that buys no guarantee — is right, and D2/D3 are well-judged. But the RFC as a package should not be accepted: its 17% ceiling (§3) is an upper bound built on the same accounting that LLP 1116.013's as-built just falsified, its largest decision (D6) is not sugar and breaks the transaction guarantee it claims to preserve (T1), and D7 trades a proven data-loss guard for silent corruption risk against the 100 ms cold-start rule. The cheap, reversible wins should be split out and measured one at a time before any 449-file rewrite is authorised.

## 2. The most important issues, ranked

**1. The bound does not support a language-wide rewrite.** §3 attributes 0.88 MB-steps (GPT) / 1.83 (Muse) to the RFC's three topics and converts at 0.24M tokens per MB-step from [LLP 1116.013](</Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/llp/1116.013-what-the-resent-context-asks.rfc.md:34>) (R² 0.60 for GPT — steps alone explain most of the variance). But [topics.py](</Users/ccheever/bakeoff/topics.py:5>) on r13+r14 gives a different split (card 0.42, start-here 0.41, data 0.33, tests/verify 0.46 MB-steps/build for GPT) than §3's re-allocation of the 22.5 KB first read “by its sections' bytes” — that re-allocation is unauditable. Decisively, LLP 1116.013 stage 1 already ran this experiment: shrinking the first read from 22 KB to ~7 KB moved knowledge into lookups (`contract vocab` 2.1 → 4.6/build, 0.65 MB-steps re-sent) and B÷A total tokens were **1.03 (0.96–1.11)** — not adopted. The RFC assumes “the knowledge has to go, not move” (§0), but D1–D6 still leave Exact-specific knowledge to teach (required `key`, `dialog open=` semantics, `persist` caps, grants, the lifted-`await` rules). At its own ceiling it closes Muse's 8% gap and half of GPT's 33% (§3); against round 15's steadier medians (1.67× React, 1.26× SwiftUI) it closes less. **Change:** require a per-decision A/B on a branch *before* the rewrite (see §5), and drop the claim that S1 (D1–D4 bundled) can be judged as one stage — §7's “one mechanical commit per decision” (T6) contradicts S1's single rewrite.

**2. D6 is semantic, not sugar, and violates T1.** An action today is a transaction: reads see the pre-state, a refused action changes nothing (see [Runtime.lean's](</Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/semantics/Contract/Runtime.lean:696>) `splitSends` / queue machinery and [Axiomatic.lean](</Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/semantics/README.md:18>)). Splitting an action at `await` into a mutation plus a lifted `then` (LLP 1089 D9) means post-`await` reads see a *later* commit, interleaved sends and `refreshes` reorder, and a second `await` nests lifts whose capture sets depend on queue state (`queueBound` 64, stalled queues). The RFC concedes this — “state reads see the state when the reply lands, as JavaScript's do” (D6) — which is exactly the transaction law T1 says stays. It also inverts the checker’s resource model: `state items = await loadItems()` makes a `state` slot's initializer perform I/O, breaking boot settlement and the seeded-oracle difftest. **Change:** split D6: keep the `app.ts` surface (plain exports, generated `Sources`/`answer`, `import`) which is genuinely presentational, and drop or separately RFC the `await`-in-action and `state = await` spellings until their interaction with `queue`, `refreshes` and two `await`s is specified in `Runtime.lean` terms.

**3. The census measures priors without teaching, then prices them as if teaching were free to remove.** The 1.1 KB guide in [priors-guide.md](</Users/ccheever/bakeoff/priors-guide.md:1>) deliberately withholds `when`/`each`/free functions; with today's start-here as guide ([main1](</Users/ccheever/bakeoff/guesses/main1/summary.json>)), the same models write `when` 36/31, `each` 30/28, `if`/`for` **0/0** and compile 36/80 clean — the guide works. So the census counts “what a model guesses cold”, not “what it costs to teach”. Worse, [priors1](</Users/ccheever/bakeoff/guesses/priors1/summary.json>) compiled **0/80 clean**: every guess failed on something, so the RFC has never measured its own §7 metric (walls hit per feature). The regex census ([census.py:10](</Users/ccheever/bakeoff/census.py:10>)) also inflates D3: “HTML headings” matches `div|span|img|a`, and D1's headline “40/0” conflates `.length` (23+17) with method calls (14+17). **Change:** publish wall counts (errors by id, first-error and all-errors) per construct on priors1/priors2/main1 before using any row to justify deleting a spelling.

**4. Overfitting to 24 specs and two builder models.** All 80 priors1 features derive from the 24 bake-off ideas ([ideas-v2.json](</Users/ccheever/bakeoff/ideas-v2.json>), 6 features each, non-independent), written by GPT-6.1 Sol (high) and Muse Spark 1.3 (xhigh) only. Several “near-universal” rows are one model's dialect: HTML elements 40/40 GPT vs 4/40 Muse; `return` 22 vs 8; `try/catch` 24 vs 5; `localStorage` 0 vs 22; `[T]`/`-> T` Muse-only. T3's own exception (keep both spellings when the census splits, as for `and`/`&&` 35 vs 26) would, applied consistently, block D3 and parts of D1. As models are retrained on more TypeScript/React, today's priors drift; the RFC has no re-census plan. **Change:** add a third model family and a held-out app set *before* deciding (the RFC proposes held-out only as a judging guard), and treat split rows as “keep both or keep Exact's” rather than “adopt the majority”.

**5. The uncanny valley is priced in walls the RFC does not count.** Adopting `null`, `?.`/`??`, `xs[i]`, `T[]`, method calls and `await` teaches the builder that Contract *is* TypeScript — the census already queues the next walls: `null` on non-options (29/80), `try/catch` beyond `await` (29), effects/`onMount` (16), spread (8), object literals (6), truthiness (6). Each newly-adopted spelling raises confidence in the next refused one, and the refusal now arrives later (after a plausible-looking program). D1's narrowing (`if x != null` rewrites to a `match`) is the sharpest case: it requires flow-sensitive typing in the Rust checker *and* its mirror [Types.lean](</Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/semantics/Contract/Types.lean>) (478 lines) plus the soundness proof — it is not “more than spelling” (§D1), it is a type-system change whose `ValTy`/`conforms` interaction is unexamined. **Change:** keep `none`/`match` (or adopt `null` as a pure synonym *without* narrowing, `?.`, `??`), and require the §7 census to report walls *created* on held-out features, not only walls removed on the 24.

**6. D7 risks user data and the cold-start budget for a word.** Today [persist.rs](</Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/contract/types/src/persist.rs:41>) refuses lists of records precisely because they are app data, capped at 16 KB in a runner-owned store ([store.rs:273](</Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/runner/src/store.rs:273>) `exact.state.*`, the platform preference store). D7 raises this to 1 MB read-and-decode *before the first frame* against [RULES](</Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/rules/RULES.md:42>)'s 100 ms p50 cold start, writes the whole value after every changing commit, and decodes field-by-field with empty-value defaults (`""`, 0, `false`). That turns a shape change that today drops loudly (and `logs` names) into silent zeroing of a renamed or retyped field across an app update — for a journal, worse than loss. The cited 294 ms for 25,000 rows (LLP 1027.003) argues against whole-value writes, not for them. **Change:** drop D7 as written; if kept records are wanted, keep them behind the existing source/SQLite path, or cap `persist` records at the current 16 KB with an explicit version field and a refusal (not a default) on type change.

**7. D6's storage and D5's presentation quietly move security and platform boundaries.** `localStorage` in `app.ts` “needing no grant” bypasses the grant check every other store access passes ([grants.rs](</Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/runner/src/grants.rs:1>), `storage.fs` under `fs.read`/`fs.write` in [start-here.md:125](</Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/docs/start-here.md:125>)); on native hosts it also has no defined backing (web quota vs file vs IndexedDB fallback in D7). `dialog open=…` is a two-way binding — Cancel/swipe-down must *write* the bool — which is a new runtime effect, not a lowering to today's modal route/`showModal` ([menus.rs](</Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/contract/lower/src/menus.rs:58>)); its behaviour under `verify`'s relaunch and the test clock is unspecified. D5's `screen note` block similarly hides `navigationKey`/absolute-inset machinery whose failure modes (split view, deep link) the RFC lists for the alternative but not for its own form. **Change:** keep `grants` for any `app.ts` storage spelling, and specify `open=`'s write-back, dismissal and test semantics against LLP 1115 D5/D6 before adopting it.

**8. Migration and consistency costs are understated.** The 449 files / 33,493 lines figure checks out (`git ls-files "*.contract"`), but the rewrite also touches ~500 KB of docs (`contract-for-agents.md` 184 KB, `reference.md` 97 KB alone), every `contract vocab` answer, the corpus/rejects fixtures, and out-of-repo apps (Bluesky, Interview) that consume exact2 by path — with “Delete; don't deprecate” (T3) giving them no window. Internally the language keeps the old words anyway: a task's `when` gate stays (D2), `every`/`after` stay under `timer` (D4), `match` stays for options — so authors must learn both vocabularies while the docs claim one was deleted. For human readers, `when`/`each` currently signal “view” at a glance; `if`/`for` erase that. **Change:** cost the branch-conflict and out-of-repo breakage explicitly, and keep `when` in views if `when` remains in tasks.

## 4. Answers to the RFC's §9 questions

1. **Is the bound right, and worth the rewrite?** No on both as stated. The 17% is a ceiling from an R² 0.60 fit with an unauditable topic split, and the only controlled test of shrinking taught bytes (1116.013 stage 1) returned 1.03×. A rewrite of every source and guide is worth it only after a single-decision branch shows total tokens falling beyond noise (§7's own rule).
2. **Does TypeScript-looking invite more refused TypeScript?** Yes — the census is the evidence: every adopted JS spelling co-occurs with `null`-on-non-options, general `try/catch`, effects, spread and object literals (29/29/16/8/6 of 80). The RFC's T4 test (walls per feature go down) is the right gate, but it has never been run; run it on held-out features before adopting the spellings that raise confidence.
3. **Is `null` with narrowing worth the checker change?** No. Spelling-only `null` for `none` is cheap and harmless; narrowing is a flow-typing change across the Rust checker, [Types.lean](</Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/semantics/Contract/Types.lean>) and its soundness proof, for a construct (`?.`/`??`, 10/80) models use least. Keep `none` and `match`.
4. **Should a pushed screen follow the census (option state, no URL)?** No — the RFC is right to refuse it. It forfeits Back gesture, deep link and split view that [LLP 1115 D5](</Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/llp/foundation/1115-write-the-web-ship-the-platform.principle.md:125>) guarantees. Keep routes; reduce their boilerplate (`screen` block) instead.
5. **Is `await` over a lifted continuation sound, and is `state = await` the right resource spelling?** Not as specified. With `queue` mutations, `refreshes` and two `await`s, lifting changes read-your-writes and ordering (Issue 2); no `Runtime.lean`-level argument is given. `state = await` is the wrong spelling — it hides pending/failed states that `resource` makes explicit and testable. Keep `resource`/`mutation`; generate their declarations from the `import` if boilerplate is the cost.
6. **Is field-by-field decoding enough, is 1 MB before first frame in budget, is D7 needed if D6 lands?** No; unproven; and no. Field-defaulting silently zeroes renamed/retyped fields; 1 MB decode on the slowest fleet iPhone is unmeasured against the 100 ms budget; and the RFC's own staging says D6 may moot it. Measure decode/write cost and require an explicit version/migration story first.
7. **What does the RFC not see?** Three larger levers it defers: tooling/tests are the largest remaining topic (0.60 MB-steps GPT, §8) — bigger than screens+sheets it does include; fresh tokens are already at parity ([LLP 1116.012](</Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/llp/1116.012-what-round-14-asks.rfc.md:39>)), so steps, not bytes, are the multiplier — a decision that cuts *steps* (one-command verify/drive, fewer repair loops) may beat any spelling change; and model priors are a moving target that fine-tuning/retrieval on the card could shift without changing the language humans must read.

## 5. Anything the RFC should measure before deciding

- **Walls, not constructs:** run §7's census metric (all-errors and first-error by id, per feature) on priors1, priors2 and main1 as they stand, per decision row — the data exists under `~/bakeoff/guesses/` and has never been reported this way. No decision should cite a construct count without its wall delta.
- **One decision, one branch, graded A/B:** D2 alone (cheapest, strongest census row) on 8+ specs, both builders, reporting steps by class as well as tokens — round noise is ±15–20% ([r14 findings](</Users/ccheever/bakeoff/rounds/r14/findings.md>)), so 8 specs paired twice is the minimum to see a 17%-of-gap effect; S1's bundle cannot attribute anything.
- **A third builder and a held-out census:** repeat priors with a model family outside GPT/Muse and with features from apps outside the 24, before treating any row as stable.
- **Roster cost per addition:** for each proposed roster entry, the runner + Lean + JS-target + difftest-generator diff size and any new opcode (comparator-bearing `toSorted`/`reduce`/`find` are not `stdlib`-only).
- **D7 budgets on hardware:** cold-start read+decode and per-commit write time for 16 KB / 256 KB / 1 MB record lists on the slowest fleet iPhone, plus behaviour on rename/retype/field-drop across an update.
- **Human cost:** a timed read/edit task for Charlie/James on `when`/`each` vs `if`/`for` sources, since T6 asserts the census spellings “are theirs too” without evidence.
