VERDICT: NOT READY

1. **MATERIAL — The generic event rule can upgrade a tool’s authority.** [1133.004:243](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:243) gives every event a person’s input origin, while implementations freely call `emit` at [1133.004:260](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:260). A tool-caused callback or future Bluetooth event must not become person-origin work. This conflicts with causation and least privilege in [1133.002:520](llp/1133.002-geolocation.rfc.md:520). Define trusted event provenance, preserve tool/wake origins, and assign person origin specifically to genuine notification activation.

2. **MATERIAL — The substitute requires an undeclared third seam.** Moving listings and badges into `exact.json` is asserted at [1133.004:419](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:419), but geolocation’s declaration cannot express keyed-list insertion/replacement/removal, badge state, clickable references or reference retirement. Its current substitute also rejects non-status operations while denied and treats `when-prompt` as source-only: [modules.rs:317](runner/src/runner/modules.rs:317). Notifications need operation-specific permission rules, including status-returning permission requests and badges that may work without notification authorization. Specify these generic declarations and prove them through an external replacement fixture, as [1133.001:351](llp/1133.001-modules-anyone-can-write.rfc.md:351) requires.

3. **MATERIAL — Delegate forwarding does not define who controls completion.** [1133.004:193](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:193) promises forwarding after host handling, but [Notify.swift:128](host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/Notify.swift:128) already completes callbacks and chooses foreground presentation. Forwarding afterward cannot honor a hatch delegate’s presentation choice; forwarding the original completion risks completing twice. Define sole completion ownership, presentation arbitration, per-session forwarding and `silent` precedence. Apple explicitly makes the completion result control presentation. [Apple documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/usernotifications/unusernotificationcenterdelegate/usernotificationcenter(_:willpresent:withcompletionhandler:)).

4. **MATERIAL — The web support claim exceeds the proposed implementation.** The ceiling promises cold and warm clicks at [1133.004:305](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:305), and stage 1 demands launching clicks on each target at [1133.004:475](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:475). Yet [1133.004:427](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:427) retains page-owned `new Notification`. Its handler cannot receive clicks after the page closes; Chrome Android rejects that constructor, already acknowledged in [notify-glue.js:17](host/web/notify-glue.js:17). Declare desktop/page-lifetime limits and visible absence elsewhere, or design the persistent service-worker route. The earlier RFC explicitly records this limitation at [1114.001:273](llp/1114.001-background-refresh-and-notification-taps.rfc.md:273). [Notification lifecycle](https://notifications.spec.whatwg.org/#lifetime).

5. **MATERIAL — The ceiling inventory is incomplete.** The web has no hatch at [1133.004:200](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:200), yet neither the ceiling nor exclusions names text direction/language, original-event timestamp, notification badge images, or dismissal/close events. These are ordinary Notifications API uses. Android channel importance is required by [1133.000:157](llp/1133.000-the-roster.rfc.md:157), but channel declarations are merely referenced at [1133.004:177](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:177), without being defined. Assign each a tier or explicit exclusion with a reason. Also reconcile custom sounds being tier 3 at [1133.004:314](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:314) with `sound` being module-owned at [1133.004:194](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:194). [Web options and events](https://notifications.spec.whatwg.org/#api).

6. **MATERIAL — `badge` confuses API support with visible presentation.** [1133.004:352](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:352) defines it as “where a badge will show,” then uses method existence. The RFC itself acknowledges successful calls without visible badges at [1133.004:427](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:427). Define this as support or best-effort capability, not visibility. Clarify [1133.004:332](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:332) too: notification permission is not universally required for badging. Chromium permits badges without it; installation and OS preferences separately affect presentation. [Chrome documentation](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/capabilities/web-apis/badging-api), [Badging specification](https://w3c.github.io/badging/#displaying-a-badge).

7. **MATERIAL — Stage 3 scheduling is not sufficiently designed for approval.** Android’s receiver-based exact alarms at [1133.004:430](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:430) require separate exact-alarm authorization, while [1133.004:327](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:327) derives only `POST_NOTIFICATIONS`. Denial, recovery and fallback are unspecified. Windows’ next row names immediate posting and delivered-notification removal, without scheduling or scheduled cancellation. Specify those semantics, or explicitly reserve them for a stage-3 amendment instead of approving that stage wholesale in Q6. [Android restrictions](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/changes/schedule-exact-alarms), [Windows scheduling](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/develop/notifications/app-notifications/app-notifications-scheduled).

8. **MATERIAL — The promised data-code use encounters an existing linking gap.** [1133.004:547](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:547) puts imports in nested consumer files. Native implementation discovery scans only literal mentions in root `app.contract` and `app.ts`: [typescript.mjs:968](js/bake/src/typescript.mjs:968). Thus those imports alone do not link the Apple implementation. Complete dependency-graph discovery and test nested/data-only imports. An explicit root import can unblock this consumer, but would leave the general D6 promise incomplete.

9. **MINOR — D9 has coverage, but not every item is fully answered.** In addition to the substantive tier and substitute gaps above, D9.7 lacks an explicit synchronous-call policy. D9.10 is deferred to a future measurement at [1133.004:569](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:569). D9.11 names T3 on macOS/web, while iOS gets the fixture at [1133.004:475](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:475). State the sync policy, mark cost evidence as pending acceptance, and identify the iOS acceptance app explicitly.

10. **MINOR — Several API claims need correction.** At [1133.004:131](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:131), `[EnforceRange]` truncates fractions before checking range; `2.9` becomes `2`. At [1133.004:179](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:179), Windows has separate `incomingCall` and `urgent` values, not `urgentCall`. Q4’s “as the web does” at [1133.004:523](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:523) incorrectly associates permission rejection with `NotAllowedError`; the RFC correctly says `TypeError` earlier. Match the APIs or declare deviations. [Web IDL](https://webidl.spec.whatwg.org/#abstract-opdef-converttoint), [Windows schema](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/uwp/schemas/tiles/toastschema/element-toast), [showNotification](https://notifications.spec.whatwg.org/#dom-serviceworkerregistration-shownotification).

11. **MINOR — The probe supports narrower empirical claims.** [.probe-notifications.mjs:14](.probe-notifications.mjs:14) checks whether `onchange` exists; it neither changes permission nor observes delivery. Lines 10–13 check badge exposure and Promise settlement, without observing an OS badge. Narrow [1133.004:361](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:361) to interface exposure and provide separate evidence for visible-presentation claims. The silent-property readback claim accurately describes the probe, but does not establish audible behavior.

12. **MINOR — The consumer migration needs concrete corrections.** The guard at [1133.004:549](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:549) must await `notificationStatus()`, whose generated binding returns a Promise at [typescript.mjs:1042](js/bake/src/typescript.mjs:1042). Tags also differ from destinations in [shell-notify.ts:156](/Users/daehyeonmun/orca/workspaces/exact2/t3-code-main/examples/t3-code/src/shell/shell-notify.ts:156) and [pages-pr-guide.ts:160](/Users/daehyeonmun/orca/workspaces/exact2/t3-code-main/examples/t3-code/src/pull-requests/pages-pr-guide.ts:160); specify self-contained tag encoding and test cold thread and guide clicks. Finally, [1133.004:556](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:556) deletes the owner, routing and state needed by the retained sound methods. Extract a sound owner and retain its route, or migrate sound in the same consumer PR.

13. **MINOR — The ruling questions need a more complete and neutral presentation.** Q1 at [1133.004:507](llp/1133.004-notifications.rfc.md:507) treats a second permission mechanism as inevitable for the vocabulary alternative; that is not established, since admission could be shared. Q3 must include event provenance and the public substitute/broker decisions above. Q6 should distinguish approved stage-1 scope from later amendments. Q7 should explicitly request Charlie’s waiver when offering no take, as [RULES.md:46](rules/RULES.md:46) requires.

The central direction is sound. Answerless sends fit the command use case; keeping Apple’s launch-installed delegate in the host respects module loading after first pixel; bounded event holding with explicit overflow is implementable; and reusing `tap` adds no agent operation. Silent posts, routerless clicks, data-code calls and badge operations can meet T3’s actual needs. This was a read-only review; browser and OS presentation checks were not rerun.
