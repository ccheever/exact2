Three fundamental concerns, most serious first. Both of the first two have one root: the host keys behavior to "the agent's socket is open" where this RFC's premise is "the socket is open *and* the timing is the platform's".

### 1. The macOS report measures a scroller a person never uses

**Issue.** `fling.mjs macos` holds the segment and reads counts over the agent's socket, so the app runs in agent mode. In agent mode every `scroll` node's wheel is applied by hand, at once, and clamped: `static var appliesAtOnce = ExactEnv.agentMode` (`host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/Mac/ChainingScrollView.swift:100-108, 167`), and `agentMode` is just `EXACT_AGENT == "1"` (`ExactKit/Session.swift:36`), not the timing. LLP 1142 §12 states the consequence: "AppKit's momentum and rubber band never run". The RFC's own list of what janked on the Mac is the rubber band fighting AppKit's elastic animation (`068772f00a`) and hover hit-testing on rows a fling carried under a still pointer (`4d404187a7`), which needs momentum frames to exist. A person's trackpad fling on the member list or DM list in this report has neither; the transcript's collection may differ, but the two plain `scroll` lists are exactly the ones D5 names.

**Why fundamental.** r2 added the macOS report to answer "the jank was found on macOS". As designed it is green on the two Mac causes by construction, and an agent reading it would conclude the Mac is fine.

**Instead.** Key `appliesAtOnce` on `agentFreezes` (`Session.swift:47`: agent mode *and* not platform timing), not on `agentMode`. The reasons the comment gives for applying at once (the drive reads an offset back in the same turn; AppKit's scrolling does not follow the agent's clock) are reasons about the agent's clock, and under `--timing platform` neither holds. 1142 D3's `fling`/`hold` gesture already flips it off for a gesture's span, so the precedent exists. Then `fling.mjs macos` measures AppKit's scrolling, which is what a person has.

### 2. D1's `--to` ops cannot be the driver's, and the RFC does not say what they are instead

**Issue.** D1 step 1 runs `tap`/`type` over the agent's socket and "never sends `clock`". But every input through the driver ends in `landed`, which sends `{op: 'clock', land: true}` unconditionally (`scripts/agent.mjs:1248-1252`), and under platform timing the first `clock` op of any form takes the clock at the wall and stops the sampler (`ExactKit/Agent.swift:749-754`); `activity()` never reopens while `session.clock != nil` (`ExactKit/FrameSampler.swift:124`), and no form gives the clock back. So the script must drive the raw socket with no landing, and then has no signal that a tap's `then`s have applied and the transcript is up, other than polling `tree`. §1 names the symptom ("every input takes the clock back"); D1 asserts the workaround without the mechanism.

**Why fundamental.** The design works around a host behavior in a script, and the workaround removes the one primitive the driver has for "the input's effects are in". Every other platform-timing drive keeps the bug.

**Instead.** Fix it where it is: under `EXACT_AGENT_TIMING=platform`, `clock land` lands the `then`s (`landThen`, tick at the wall's now) without taking the clock, or a `clock wall` form (a form of `clock`, within DEFERRED's ten) returns it and lets the sampler resume. Then `--to` is ordinary driver ops, `perf frames` stops answering `virtual` in every platform-timing drive, and `fling.mjs` is only the flings and the report.

### 3. Open question 1 is the decision D5 depends on; the RFC should carry a recommendation, and D3 as built still blames the author for it

**Issue.** The first thing D5 flings is Excord's transcript, which has `scroll=scrolled` (`apps/excord/app.contract:1290-1292`). On iOS every scroll event with that handler runs `sendScrollEvent` before the frame paints and main waits on the runner (`ExactKit/IOS/NodeViewIOS.swift:790-800`). Whatever D1 reports, `runtime` will sit at the top of each late frame's phases, and "what they find is fixed in Excord or in the host" cannot start on the largest item until Charlie answers. Meanwhile the D3 line as built says "a runner turn and a pass over what reads it, every event of a fling; write only when a value crosses a threshold" (`scripts/agent-inspect.mjs:582-584`); §R says the authoring warning was dropped, but the moral survived, and it is misattributed: the runner turn and main's wait are per *dispatch* on the synchronous path, whether or not the handler writes.

**Why fundamental.** LLP 1115: a hand-built UIKit app does not run app code on the paint path of every scroll frame, and DEFERRED already routes scroll-linked presentation to engine timelines with "no app code per frame". Leaving this as a bare question while the report charges the author keeps the wrong layer responsible.

**Instead.** Recommend in the RFC (after-frame, coalesced delivery as the default; synchronous delivery opt-in if any handler truly needs pre-paint geometry), ask for the decision before D5, and make the D3 line a count with no advice: "N dispatched, N committed, each a runner turn main waited on".
