## 1. Fundamental issues

**1. The search D3 describes reaches almost nothing today, and the RFC's numbers hide it.** The RFC says `verify` checks "every property at every commit of every authored test" and that "sixteen apps have a test file". Ten do (`find apps -name '*.test.contract'`: agents, caltrain, design-tool, excord, harmony, messages, metronome, reflow, shelf, variable-type). Those files hold 228 `test` blocks, and the differential run can deliver 17 of them: `script::test_case` refuses a whole block at its first step it cannot deliver (`semantics/difftest/src/script.rs:153-160` for any `clock` that is not `+ms`, `:190-195` for everything else) and `verify` prints `SKIP` and moves on (`semantics/difftest/src/verify.rs:80`). The steps that sink them are `clock data` (900 occurrences across the ten apps) and `clock settle` (497), then `request` (391). Excord's 88 blocks and design-tool's 95: zero deliverable. What is left for a property to be checked on is the explore script (every `testId` visible at boot, tapped twice, then two clock advances: `semantics/difftest/src/lib.rs:455-491`) and the walk, which D3 draws from "the controls the explore observation showed" (a fixed list) with typed values "of the input's kind", so it cannot get past a search box, a form, or a screen a tap on a later screen opens. A property that passes that search was checked on the boot screen and a few taps; D4's "unexercised" line is the only thing that stops the green from lying. Resolution: (a) deliver `clock data` and `clock settle` in the semantics' world, where every answer lands in the asking commit (`semantics/Contract/Runtime.lean:820`), as a no-op or `clock +0` on both sides; that alone makes 40 of the 228 blocks deliverable (the ones whose only undeliverable steps are those two), at a few lines in `test_case` and `Event::lean`; (b) make the walk adaptive: choose each event from the `view` lines of the current observation (`observe::run` prints them after every step, `semantics/difftest/src/observe.rs:173-181`; a stepwise driver beside `run` is small) and draw typed values from the `type` steps of the app's own test files and the string literals of its source, which answers open question 2; (c) count the skipped blocks in the summary line, with the step that skipped each, and correct §1's and D3's counts.

**2. "Poisoned ones included" cannot be checked by D2's mechanism.** D1 says an `always` holds in every reached configuration "poisoned ones included, read after the commit's settlement", and D2 says "then nothing else is new" because the observation already prints every derive. The observation prints nothing for a poisoned configuration on either side: the runner prints `outcome poisoned` and breaks (`semantics/difftest/src/observe.rs:241-247`, `:255-257`), and `Contract.Observe` returns the head lines alone when the outcome or the configuration is poisoned (`semantics/Contract/Observe.lean:84-92`, `:165`). The derive is settled before the render that poisons (settlement precedes rendering, `semantics/Contract/Runtime.lean:253`, `:483`), so the value exists; it is never observed. Resolution: either print the settled state for a poisoned step on both sides (a format change that re-runs every cached corpus case) or, cheaper and honest, state in D1 that stage 1 checks unpoisoned configurations and D6 proves the poisoned ones, as `Reachable.slotIn` already does (`semantics/Apps/Proofs/UpdateLab.lean:147-149`).

**3. `never refuses` as specified misattributes, and its attribution mechanism is the wrong tool.** D3 counts "an `outcome refused`" at a step "whose dispatch reached a handler naming the action" as a violation. A step's outcome is "the event's when it failed, else the due work's" (`semantics/difftest/src/observe.rs:286-291`, `semantics/Contract/Observe.lean:122-127`): a tap on `reset` whose own commit succeeded is `outcome refused` when a due timer's action or a queued send refused first, and the curried arguments can refuse before the action runs (the gist's D3 says so; the RFC drops it). Both would be reported as `REFUSES "reset"`. And `observe::site` resolves a `testId` to a plan node by booting and replaying the prefix (`semantics/difftest/src/observe.rs:266-284`); it yields a node for `SourceMap::node`, not an action, so attributing 800 walk steps means 800 replays plus a new node-to-handler lookup. Resolution: have both observations name what a step ran, as a `#` line the comparison skips (`semantics/difftest/src/lib.rs:243-249`) or a compared line: the action the handler dispatched and whether that action's own commit refused, and the actions the due work ran. Lean's `dispatch` and `advance` know both. Attribution becomes a lookup, the clock's actions come for free (open question 3 closes), and shrinking a `never refuses` violation has a predicate to shrink against; `lib::shrink` today shrinks only on `Verdict::Diverge` (`semantics/difftest/src/lib.rs:512-533`; there is no `script::shrink`, and `difftest random` shrinks random programs, not walks over an app, `semantics/difftest/src/gen/mod.rs:1-21`), and a false property is an `Agree` on both sides.

**4. The walk runs on Lean by default, and the cost is not stated; the runner should search and Lean should confirm.** D3 runs 20 walks of 40 events per app through `check`, which emits every case to Lean (`semantics/difftest/src/lib.rs:130-218`), with the cache keyed by the embedding and the events (`semantics/README.md:183-188`), so any edit to the app or a property reruns all 800 steps on the Lean interpreter. The README's numbers are "a few seconds" warm for a small app and one to three minutes cold for the corpus; the RFC gives none for 800 steps over a 1,499-line app, and `verify` is the command the author runs after every edit. The runner delivers the same 800 steps in milliseconds, and D4 already admits that the runner alone checks a property. Resolution: walk on the runner, check properties there, shrink on the runner, and send Lean the shrunk violation and a bounded sample of walks (say two) for the model comparison; print the time per side. This is the cheaper way to the same claim (a violation found is still confirmed by both evaluators), and it makes the no-Lean fallback the same code path rather than a second one.

**5. The syntax lands before the demand it is meant to answer, against the gist's order and the deferred list's take.** `rules/DEFERRED.md:1646-1650` records the take for the node map: "no new Contract syntax until formatting and navigation have landed (1035.005 §1's order), and `contract` blocks as executable assertions (LLP 1006 §8) stay off the doing-list"; `fmt` and `symbols` have landed (`contract/cli/src/main.rs:77-79`), so what remains is 1035.005 §1.4, "new syntax only for friction that remains, demonstrated in source" (`llp/1035.005-contract-authoring-ergonomics.rfc.md:47`), and the gist's own first step, which the RFC cites: measure whether authors state properties at all before any syntax. The RFC's demonstration is the length of four Lean proofs, not friction in Contract source, and its measurement (§5) is "stage 1's D5 run", so it measures after it has built the grammar, the lexer keywords, the compiler entry and the lints. The RFC's "Decisions needed" treats Charlie's one-line instruction as the approval, but `rules/DEFERRED.md:1694-1699` wants a take or a waiver recorded beside the entry, in his words; the RFC proposes no edit to that file. Resolution: either land D3 to D5 first with no syntax (the walk, the refusal findings, the suggestions, all of which are observation statistics and need no grammar), measure what the suggestions find on the ten apps, and land D1/D2 with that number; or keep the order and record the waiver beside the entry in the stage-1 PR.

Lesser corrections, not fundamental: "836 theorems" is 837 in `semantics/Contract/` (857 with `Apps/`); `UpdateLab.lean` proves `counter_always` in lines 79-149, about 70, not 60; the "same entry" of plan item 2 does not exist yet, since `lean_path` re-reads the file from disk (`contract/cli/src/lean.rs:52-56`) and `prepare` embeds from the path, not the case's source (`semantics/difftest/src/lib.rs:98-105`), so the hidden derive must be added to the parsed `File` before `check_all` runs in both paths, not "to the expanded root", or `expand_typed(checked.file)` (`contract/cli/src/lean.rs:129`) loses it; D11's "from the stored cases the corpus has" is not something `verify` runs today (`stored_variants` is corpus-only, `semantics/difftest/src/lib.rs:413`, never called from `verify.rs`).

## 2. Is stage 1 the right first slice?

Not as cut. The mechanism (a property as a hidden derive, evaluated by both evaluators and compared by the harness that exists) is the right one and is the cheapest honest way to check a property on the runner and the model at once; D4's report is right and should not be reduced; D5 is nearly free. What is wrong is where the hours go. Stage 1 spends most of them on the grammar, the compiler entry, the lints and the reports, and leaves the search as it is, which issue 1 shows is a boot screen, a few taps and a blind walk; a property language over that search produces green runs that say little. The slice that buys the most verification per hour is the search itself: deliver `clock data` and `clock settle` (40 of 228 blocks become differential tests of the app, property or not, and every one of them is also a differential check of the runner against the model that nobody runs today), make the walk adaptive and runner-first with values mined from the tests, and print what was reached and what refused, attributed as issue 3 says. That work has no new syntax, no deferred-list question, and every hour of it is verification the ten apps get at once. Then D1/D2 with `always` only; `never refuses` after the observation names the action; D5 with the first two, since it is what measures demand. Proofs (D6) stay deferred; the RFC is right that nothing should be sent to Lean to prove before a search has tried to falsify it.

## 3. Open questions

1. *A property over a derive that reads a resource.* Refuse it in stage 1, with the line. Its truth is the oracle's, the suggestions never produce one (they are over states), and admitting it means the first thing an author writes over `items` is a property about seeded data. Admit it when D11 says what a resource's value means. Confidence: high.
2. *The walk's typed values.* Yes, from the app's test blocks and the string literals in its source, plus the kind's generic value; a value a test typed is the one the author knows reaches a screen. Confidence: high.
3. *`never refuses` and the clock.* Yes, and before stage 1 ships `never refuses` at all: the outcome line should name every action a step ran and which commit refused (issue 3). Without it the stage-1 form misattributes; with it the clock is covered at no extra cost. Confidence: high.
4. *Where suggestions stop.* The four forms are the right floor and will be kept more often than they are discarded; what is missing is the one agents' apps most need, a relation between two states ("`editing` implies `selected != none`", "`turns` is 0 after `reset`"), which is D7's territory and should stay out until D7. Print a suggestion only when the search exercised at least two distinct values or two distinct actions that wrote the slot, so that a slot the walk never touched does not yield "`x` is one of 1 value". Confidence: medium.

## 4. Verdict

NOT READY: issues 1 and 3 need the search and the attribution redesigned before the property blocks mean what the RFC says they mean; issue 2 and 5 are a sentence and a recorded waiver each.
