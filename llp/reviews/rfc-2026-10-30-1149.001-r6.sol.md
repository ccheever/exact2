1. **A1 now answers the acquisition objection substantively.**

   A dedicated acquirer prevents a blocked `nextDrawable` from holding up jobs arriving later on the serial render worker. The ordinary path checks the park before walking, retains damage on refusal, and uses the same parked drawable for direct and compose frames. Live resize explicitly acquires before host-thread encoding and permits a synchronous acquisition wait. That preserves D11’s host-thread walk/encode and render-thread commit/present division. These are actual answers to round 5, not acknowledgments. ([RFC §3/M3, L237–259](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/llp/1149.001-the-renderers-cost-program.rfc.md:237); parent D11, L553–560; current worker `host/ink/src/present.rs:33–44,94–98`; Metal present path, L247–318.)

   Two implementation precisions deserve attention:

   - §7/A1 still says acquisition happens on “the worker … last in its turn,” retaining r5’s wording. Make it agree with the dedicated acquirer in §3. ([L399–403](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/llp/1149.001-the-renderers-cost-program.rfc.md:399).)
   - Coalesce acquisition requests and discard results that return after resize, shutdown, or the transition to rest. Apply the live-resize timeout to the complete request, including any older acquisition already running. A separate thread establishes render-worker progress; its mailbox still needs disciplined lifetime handling.

   Neither requires another architectural decision.

2. **A1’s capture and parity contracts are coherent.**

   A compose frame reads its existing result; a direct frame replays the last encoded frame’s sampled state and retained resources. A mid-animation capture therefore neither advances time nor settles motion. Holding the referenced targets and atlas contents until replacement addresses requests arriving between frames. The important implementation meaning of “pinned” is preservation of contents as well as object lifetime. ([RFC L263–274](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/llp/1149.001-the-renderers-cost-program.rfc.md:263).)

   D9 now has two comparisons: offscreen rendering against tiny-skia, and a frame-tagged GPU copy of the actual drawable against that frame’s readback, with channel normalization. The text correctly distinguishes presentation submission from proof of scanout and keeps shipping-configuration window captures separate. S1.5’s **15 never-shown frames out of 348** makes that distinction necessary. ([RFC L275–286](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/llp/1149.001-the-renderers-cost-program.rfc.md:275); RESULTS S1.5, L1519–1535.)

   One preflight detail should be explicit during implementation: an unresolved or invalidated SVG scene must conservatively require composition until its sampling requirement is known. Today SVG resolution occurs inside the paint walk, so a count learned only there cannot justify `begin()` choosing direct rendering for that same frame. Pre-resolving it or treating unknown state as sampling satisfies the RFC’s existing conservative rule. ([RFC L229–236](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2/llp/1149.001-the-renderers-cost-program.rfc.md:229); `host/linux/src/paint/svg.rs:326–334,411–471,540–555`.)
