For **Q1, I favor the alternative for valid inputs — 92% confidence**. Refusing the entire band rejects 20% of fixture inputs, mostly correctly reconstructed. But “slightly off UIKit” understates the tradeoff: my read-only nearest-root experiment recovered all 27 rejected cases, yet sampled trajectory error reached **1.43 times the move**. Other in-band mismatches already reach 1.85 times the move. A real root establishes consistency with the reconstructed equation, not UIKit’s root choice or smooth dependence on velocity.

The precise rule I would write:

- Keep D2’s zero-velocity closed form and twelve-step procedure, accepting results through its existing residual gate.
- On failure, enumerate admissible positive roots of `g(W)=0`, with `0 < W ≤ 10⁴`, by isolating monotone lobes, including tangent roots.
- Choose the root minimizing **absolute distance in W** from the last finite positive Newton iterate, including the initial `W=5`. Break ties toward smaller W.
- Bisect each bracket to adjacent f64 values; choose the endpoint with smaller residual, again breaking ties downward.
- Retain argument and coefficient validation. “Never refuse” applies to band membership and Newton failure within the supported domain.

Use identical pinned arithmetic and the same resolver for literals and dynamic values on every host. Keep literal warnings; remove the physical rewrite that erases duration-form metadata. Implementation risk is moderate: root isolation and numerical boundary fixtures are necessary, but the fallback is bounded. This policy concerns duration springs, whose damping ratio clamps to one; bounce and overdamped evaluation remain separate. Zero-distance normalization belongs to Q7.

For **Q7, I favor a scoped alternative: re-solve scalar duration releases, with explicit fallback cases — 87% confidence**.

First, the premise needs correcting: [D3 already cuts duration springs off at `d`](llp/1099-uikit-springs.rfc.md). Keeping authored stiffness changes the trajectory and terminal snap, **not completion time**. Re-solving improves fidelity to UIKit calls; neither solving nor fallback guarantees natural settling or frame-for-frame agreement.

My rule: for signed displacement `Δ = target − presented` and physical release velocity `V`, resolve using `v = V/Δ`, `u = vd`, and Q1’s resolver. Set `k=(W/d)²`, `c=2ζ′W/d`; start from the presented value with actual derivative `V`, and cut at `d`. Preserve the authored duration and damping ratio through compilation.

For zero distance or unrepresentable normalization/resolved coefficients, retain authored coefficients and inject the finite physical velocity. Preserve absolute scale residuals, including releases toward scale zero. This deliberately differs from [Apple’s documented zero-distance normalization](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uispringtimingparameters/initialvelocity), which sets that axis’s normalized velocity to zero.

For vectors, re-solve once when moving axes share a normalized velocity. Otherwise retain shared authored coefficients pending further measurement: fixture X shows shared stiffness for both `(1,5)` and `(5,1)`. Independent axis solves cannot claim UIKit vector parity.

Do not extend this rule to bounce: its stiffness is velocity-independent, while UIKit’s cutoff changes with velocity. Keep the RFC’s textbook gesture-release branch there; copying negative-bounce behavior would inject the extra `2ζωΔ` derivative and break velocity continuity.

Q7’s implementation risk exceeds Q1’s: preserve authored metadata, handle degenerate releases, and verify shared runtime resolution and web lowering. Scalar UIKit fidelity justifies that work; universal frame-for-frame fidelity remains unsupported.
