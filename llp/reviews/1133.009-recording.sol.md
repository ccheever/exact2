1. **MATERIAL — Physical capture ownership and session ownership conflict.** [D5, line 345, and D6, lines 362–394](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-ccheever-projects-exact2/f4b5c629-4ed6-4746-9045-74f6106a629d/scratchpad/capsule-r1/llp/1133.009-recording.rfc.md:345) promise one capture per device per process, but hand each session an engine/mixer hatch and stop its engine when that session’s last track ends. Sharing the engine requires deciding whose graph, voice processing, format and Android builder settings prevail, and what happens when the session that configured it disappears. D7’s first-claim rule covers only mode and category options. Today these are different ownership models: [Recorder.swift:193](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-ccheever-projects-exact2/f4b5c629-4ed6-4746-9045-74f6106a629d/scratchpad/capsule-r1/apps/recorder/modules/apple/Recorder.swift:193) stops a session-owned engine, while [Extensions.kt:49](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-ccheever-projects-exact2/f4b5c629-4ed6-4746-9045-74f6106a629d/scratchpad/capsule-r1/host/android/canvas/src/main/java/dev/exact/canvas/Extensions.kt:49) holds process-owned implementations.

   **Decision needed:** specify a process-owned producer with leased session subscriptions, configuration arbitration and isolated hatch ownership, or choose independent captures. Prove conflicting configurations and destruction of the first owner with real capture; §11’s agent drive creates no engine.

2. **MATERIAL — The shared interface requires a permission model absent from the proposed seam.** [D3, lines 292–296](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-ccheever-projects-exact2/f4b5c629-4ed6-4746-9045-74f6106a629d/scratchpad/capsule-r1/llp/1133.009-recording.rfc.md:292) requires `getUserMedia` to select microphone permission, camera permission, or both from its arguments. The current declaration carries one interface-level permission ([declaration.rs:192](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-ccheever-projects-exact2/f4b5c629-4ed6-4746-9045-74f6106a629d/scratchpad/capsule-r1/contract/cli/src/declaration.rs:192)); runner permission admission examines one permission without arguments ([modules.rs:303](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-ccheever-projects-exact2/f4b5c629-4ed6-4746-9045-74f6106a629d/scratchpad/capsule-r1/runner/src/runner/modules.rs:303)); grant lists mean alternatives, rather than jointly required grants ([grants.rs:235](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-ccheever-projects-exact2/f4b5c629-4ed6-4746-9045-74f6106a629d/scratchpad/capsule-r1/contract/cli/src/grants.rs:235)). Notifications’ static per-name permission extension does not resolve this.

   **Decision needed:** add public, argument-dependent permission requirements with conjunction, mixed-state handling and failure cleanup. Include this in D10 and prove audio-only capture without a camera grant, plus combined requests with differing permission states. Otherwise implementations need private admission logic.

3. **MATERIAL — A cancelled start can leave an unowned live microphone.** [D6, lines 356–376](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-ccheever-projects-exact2/f4b5c629-4ed6-4746-9045-74f6106a629d/scratchpad/capsule-r1/llp/1133.009-recording.rfc.md:356) deliberately keeps objects beyond answers and activations, but never distinguishes successful handle delivery from an acquisition whose answer was superseded during permission or device opening. The runner drops forgotten replies ([commit.rs:1070](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-ccheever-projects-exact2/f4b5c629-4ed6-4746-9045-74f6106a629d/scratchpad/capsule-r1/runner/src/runner/commit.rs:1070)); the package receives a reply callback with no cancellation or acceptance notification ([ExactExtensions.swift:7–10](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-ccheever-projects-exact2/f4b5c629-4ed6-4746-9045-74f6106a629d/scratchpad/capsule-r1/host/apple/modules/ExactExtensions.swift:7)). Session destruction alone leaves that acquisition alive until the whole session ends, although the app never received its ID.

   **Decision needed:** make pending acquisitions request-owned, reclaim provisional objects on cancellation or rejected delivery, and explicitly transfer successfully delivered handles to session ownership. Test supersession and reload during asynchronous opening.

4. **MATERIAL — Web teardown and crash recovery promise guarantees the selected API cannot provide.** [D6, lines 359–385, and D10, lines 607–621](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-ccheever-projects-exact2/f4b5c629-4ed6-4746-9045-74f6106a629d/scratchpad/capsule-r1/llp/1133.009-recording.rfc.md:359) promise finalization on page reload and at most one second lost through one-second slices. MediaStream Recording defines `timeslice` as a minimum, delivers the final data asynchronously, and guarantees playability only for the complete recording. HTML document destruction removes queued document tasks. A one-second `destroy()` allowance cannot overcome browser teardown. [MediaStream Recording §2.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/mediastream-recording/#dom-mediarecorder-start), [HTML §7.5.10](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/document-lifecycle.html#destroy-a-document).

   **Decision needed:** distinguish orderly shutdown from unload/crash. Either weaken web recovery to the durably received prefix, without guaranteed playability or a one-second loss bound, or choose a recoverable segment/container protocol. Add browser kill/reload tests for whichever guarantee remains.

5. **IMPLEMENTATION-PHASE — The replacement recorder must resolve stream IDs through the public seam.** [D10, line 589](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-ccheever-projects-exact2/f4b5c629-4ed6-4746-9045-74f6106a629d/scratchpad/capsule-r1/llp/1133.009-recording.rfc.md:589) passes a stream ID, while D3 and D10 lines 626–628 expose only track lookup. Add stream membership/native-stream resolution to the shared registry. The replacement fixture in §14 must exercise `startMediaRecorder(stream=…)`, rather than receive a track directly. The existing shared-object decision points toward this fix; the missing lookup alone does not block readiness.

6. **IMPLEMENTATION-PHASE — Apply D9’s capture prohibition to track re-enabling.** [D1, line 143, and D9, lines 548–557](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-ccheever-projects-exact2/f4b5c629-4ed6-4746-9045-74f6106a629d/scratchpad/capsule-r1/llp/1133.009-recording.rfc.md:143) expose `setTrackEnabled`, but name only three restricted operations. Enabling a disabled track can restart room audio in an already-running recorder and can reacquire released hardware. Apply the already-chosen foreground authority rule to enabling, and add a tool-origin drive for this path. [Media Capture §4.3.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/mediacapture-streams/#life-cycle-and-media-flow).

**BEYOND — Native handle creation needs an explicit ownership-transfer protocol.** Finding 3 also affects camera sessions, decoders and future connections. Cancelling a value reply and destroying a successfully returned native object are different operations; the general seam needs to distinguish them.

**BEYOND — The file seam needs sustained streaming behavior.** D10 lines 620–621 selects appended slices, but [storage-fs.js:195–213](/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-ccheever-projects-exact2/f4b5c629-4ed6-4746-9045-74f6106a629d/scratchpad/capsule-r1/host/web/storage-fs.js:195) recopies and rewrites the entire previous file on every append, giving quadratic cumulative work. D6 lines 387–390 also leaves queue bounds and slow-writer behavior unspecified. Choose chunked/streaming storage and bounded audio handoff with an explicit overflow policy. The ten-second take and waveform timing budget do not establish sustained recording feasibility.

Three new material findings:

1. **(IN-DELTA; MATERIAL) A permitted meter read can reopen the microphone.** [D6, lines 441–449](llp/1133.009-recording.rfc.md:441) makes an `audioLevel` reader reacquire an idle capture and calls that a D9 start. But D9, lines 662–669 explicitly permits tools to read, and [D10’s declaration, lines 721–725](llp/1133.009-recording.rfc.md:721) gives `audioLevel` neither origin nor foreground admission. A tool reading an idle track, or a reader appearing in the background, therefore reaches a capture-start transition through an allowed read.

   **Decision:** either keep meter reads passive until an explicit admitted activation, or define state-sensitive admission for subscription acquisition, including synchronous reads and reactivation. Reading an already-running capture and reopening hardware need distinct authority.

2. **(IN-DELTA; MATERIAL) The public seam cannot identify when a particular track loses its last meter reader.** D6’s new idle policy requires per-track consumer retirement, but [D10.6–7, lines 764–772](llp/1133.009-recording.rfc.md:764) adds only track lookup and polling. The existing runner records live use by operation name and sends a release only when **all** readers of that name disappear ([bound.rs, lines 78–114](runner/src/runner/modules/bound.rs:78)); Apple exposes only `release(name)` ([ExactExtensions.swift, line 10](host/apple/modules/ExactExtensions.swift:10)), as does the web ([modules.js, lines 94–106](host/web-js/modules.js:94)).

   If one meter switches from track A to B while another meter remains, the implementation cannot determine that A has lost its final reader. A can retain its microphone lease indefinitely.

   **Decision:** expose subscription identities, arguments and atomic replacement/retirement—or an equivalent active-reader set—through the public seam. Exercise independent readers and a reader switching tracks.

3. **(IN-DELTA; MATERIAL) The prescribed Apple recovery mechanism has no macOS event source.** [D7, lines 525–538](llp/1133.009-recording.rfc.md:525) prohibits engine configuration observers and instead uses the owner’s `AVAudioSession.routeChangeNotification`. But [AudioSession.swift, lines 3–5](host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/AudioSession.swift:3) explicitly excludes macOS. Apple documents that hardware sample-rate or channel-count changes stop and uninitialize `AVAudioEngine`; its warning concerns synchronous teardown inside the notification handler, rather than observation itself. [Apple’s configuration-change documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/nsnotification/name-swift.struct/avaudioengineconfigurationchange).

   Continuous capture has no subsequent `Play` to invoke the sound arm’s existing recovery path ([SoundModule.swift, line 193](host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/SoundModule.swift:193)). A Mac take can therefore remain logically recording after its engine stops.

   **Decision:** specify safe asynchronous configuration recovery on macOS, using engine notifications or CoreAudio listeners, and verify a hardware-format change during a take.

**BEYOND (PRE-EXISTING; MATERIAL):** Finding 2 exposes a general live-source seam limitation. Geolocation’s [D5, lines 233–254](llp/1133.002-geolocation.rfc.md:233) already promises individual reader lifetimes and composed options, while the built transport exposes only name-level retirement. Fix this publicly for all implementations.

1. **(IN-DELTA; MATERIAL) Media-services reset incorrectly restarts recording automatically.** [D7, lines 567–575](llp/1133.009-recording.rfc.md:567) newly says capture reopens after a reset as after a route change, with recorders merely journaling a gap. Apple distinguishes these events: reset requires reinitializing audio objects and restoring session configuration, while playback, recording and processing should restart only upon user action. [Apple’s reset documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/mediaserviceswereresetnotification?changes=_4). The existing [sound reset handler, lines 362–366](host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/SoundModule.swift:362) disposes its engine and waits for a subsequent operation.

   This chooses the wrong recovery transition for live microphone capture; correcting it changes recorder state, session activation and the public owner’s recovery contract.

   **Decision:** distinguish reset from route recovery. Replace invalid objects and restore configuration, leave affected recorders paused, expose the reason, and require an admitted foreground resume before restarting capture. Verify with iOS Settings’ Reset Media Services during a take.

2. **(PRE-EXISTING; IMPLEMENTATION-PHASE) Clarify whether hatch taps hold capture demand.** [D5, line 413](llp/1133.009-recording.rfc.md:413) permits spectrum and speech taps, while [D6, lines 456–471](llp/1133.009-recording.rfc.md:456) counts only recorders and meter readers as consumers.    **Decision:** state whether audio hatch taps are passive observers of an otherwise-running capture or can hold and retire a consumer claim. This needs alignment during implementation, rather than another ownership architecture.
