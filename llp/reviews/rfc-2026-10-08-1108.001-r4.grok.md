# Design review: LLP 1108.001 r4, Grok

Reviewer: Grok 4.7 (xhigh, read-only), 2026-10-08, blind, same tree and brief as Astra's.

NOT READY

The §1.3 gate (2xx and a whole-value `when` match, decided before any body byte; refusals stay readable; the client publishes a unique path from the media queue) does resolve the parent's "R2 needs admission". The r3 folds are in the text. What follows is new.

1. **BLOCKER** — §2.3 and §2.5 deadlock the store. A finalizer, a decoder, or the video loader drops the last `Arc` and takes the one mutex. `Object::setExternalMemoryPressure` (`jsi.h` in the linked Hermes; `hermes.cpp` around the `GCScope` before `canAllocExternalMemory`) can collect on the calling thread, and §2.5's quota path runs a full GC so those finalizers can free buffers. `std::sync::Mutex` is not reentrant, so a charge or a pressure update that still holds the mutex waits on itself. Release the mutex before any JSI call or GC, and have the engine drop sweep its buffers the way §2.4 revokes its URLs. A reload in-process otherwise keeps the dead engine's bodies against the 256 MiB bound.

2. **MAJOR** — §1.4 and §1.6 stall every ticket on `rename`, and they contradict the lost-race rule. The publication check is specified under the executor state mutex (`forget` at `host/apple/src/executor_core.rs:394`, `complete` at `:543`). `rename` is inside that same hold, so a wedged directory blocks let-go and every other completion, which is the stall r3 moved off the worker. Record the decision under the mutex and call `rename` after releasing it. Do not reuse `executor_body.rs`'s `acquire` (`:139` prefers `stop()` once the read returns): §1.4 says the writer works "as `exactBodyFrom`'s read does", but §1.6 says a deadline that loses the race settles with the file.

3. **MAJOR** — §1.4's decoding claim is false on Linux. Ibex's ureq is `default-features = false` with only `rustls` (`vendor/ibex/crates/ibex2/Cargo.toml:31`), so `content-encoding: gzip` is not decoded and the header stays. URLSession delivers decoded bytes and keeps the coding (`darwin_http.mm:171` versus `:404`). The same save therefore stores a JPEG on Apple and a gzip blob on Linux, and the "skip the length compare" test still passes. Enable ureq's gzip feature and then do not compare lengths (ureq 3.4 strips both `Content-Encoding` and `Content-Length`, its `gzip.rs` test `gz_strips_content_encoding_and_content_length`), or declare the split.

4. **MAJOR** — §2.2 contradicts parent D5 and D8. §2.2 says the browser's limits apply and §2.5 does not. Parent D5 still states 256 MiB per app on every executor, and D8 requires "the 256 MiB bound refuses" on Hermes and the JS target. D6's amendment sentence covers the URL form and the lifetime only. Extend that sentence and D8, or count Blob and response bytes on the JS target up to the same refusal.

5. **MINOR** — §1.1 cannot see the combined header it specifies. `Headers::set` replaces (`vendor/ibex/crates/ibex2/src/stdlib/fetch.rs:140-144`); both transports use it (`rustls_http.rs:505`, `darwin.rs:276`), so a repeated `accept-ranges` is last-wins before `SaveTo::admits`. Append, as Fetch joins with `, `. Say the name compare is ASCII-case-insensitive too: §1.1 says that only of the value, and URLSession preserves `Accept-Ranges`. `Response::saved` (`runner/src/request.rs:529-533`) also leaves `:exact-saved` in `headers` for Rust; strip it there as the prelude does.

6. **MINOR** — §1.6 says a header arm with no `size` is `E_ASSET_ONCE_UNSUPPORTED` "in slice 1". Slice 1 never sends that arm. Say it lands with the slice 2 driver and means that until slice 3. Parent D2's table (`llp/1108-media-from-snapback.rfc.md:241`) is still `{status, headers, size}`, and §0 still calls the parent's client sections authoritative.
