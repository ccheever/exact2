## 1. Encoding for the compositor

**Claim (D3, and the D4 rows that depend on it).** The mark and the zoom are CSS animations whose segments are cubic Béziers. The Apple host lowers exactly those, on an SVG element's transform, opacity, dash offset, fill and stroke, to Core Animation (`host/apple/src/svg_lower.rs`). Android's reader plays their transform, opacity and dash offset. A transition or a `linear()` easing is sampled on the main thread. D4 then says keyframed transform, opacity and `stroke-dashoffset` on SVG groups are lowered on both hosts, fill and stroke are lowered on Apple wherever every stop has one alpha, and only the morph's 500 ms and the chart's 400 ms are sampled.

**What is true.**

Transform and opacity on a group are lowered on both hosts. On Apple, a group scene item carries opacity specs only, and translate, rotate and scale only on its transform pair (`host/apple/src/svg.rs:575-595`, `host/apple/src/svg.rs:647-651`). On Android, `LOWERED` includes those properties (`host/linux/src/lower.rs:30-38`) and `playable` allows them on an SVG element (`host/linux/src/lower.rs:534`).

Dash offset, fill and stroke are lowered only on a shape, and a group that animates any of them is sampled in full.

- Apple emits fill, stroke and dash-offset specs from the shape record only (`host/apple/src/svg.rs:834-846`). For any other SVG element, animating `fill`, `stroke`, `color` or `stroke-dashoffset` marks the node sampled (`host/apple/src/svg_lower.rs:375-384`). A sampled node contributes no Core Animation specs at all (`host/apple/src/svg_lower.rs:152-155`), so a transform or opacity animation on that same group is sampled too.
- Android lowers `stroke-dashoffset` only when that node is an SVG shape, is not also translating, scaling or rotating, and has a stroke (`host/linux/src/lower.rs:531-533`). A group fails that test. `playable` then rejects the node (`host/linux/src/lower.rs:122-123`), and a dash-offset bit on a non-shape never opens a reader layer (`host/linux/src/paint/layer.rs:123-125`). Fill and stroke are not in `LOWERED`.

`linear()` is lowered, not sampled. Apple turns a `linear()` or `steps()` interval into linear sub-keyframes inside the Core Animation track (`host/apple/src/svg_lower.rs:35-36`, `host/apple/src/svg_lower.rs:41-43`). Android sends `linear()` as easing code 3 (`host/linux/src/lower.rs:481-487`). The `linear` keyword is already the cubic `(0, 0, 1, 1)` (`host/apple/src/svg_lower.rs:28`).

Transitions are sampled on Apple (the engine samples every running transition, and the Apple host never takes one over). On Android, transitions of opacity, translate, scale and rotate are played by the reader (`host/linux/src/lower.rs:183-196`). A dash-offset transition is not.

The shipping check is a shape-layer `strokeEnd` over 400 ms, from 350 ms to the morph at 900 ms (`.refs/ios/modules/apple/EasNative.swift:1084`, `.refs/ios/modules/apple/EasNative.swift:1430`, `.refs/ios/modules/apple/EasNative.swift:1517-1518`). D3 puts that factor on a group. On a group it is sampled for those 400 ms, inside the success window the native mark was moved off the main thread to survive, and D4's "only the morph and the chart are sampled" is then false. The same is true of colour if it is a group `fill`, `stroke` or `color` rather than a shape's paint.

The zoom's geometric scale is matched at the authored stops only when the segments between them are linear. A non-linear cubic between samples of `s·(f/s)^zoom` leaves the Swift curve. Those linear segments are already lowered.

**What r2 should say.** Put translate, rotate, scale and opacity on groups. Put `stroke-dashoffset`, `fill` and `stroke` on the shape that paints them. Android lowers a shape's dash offset only when that shape does not also turn and its stroke is a solid colour. Apple lowers a shape's fill and stroke when every colour in the track, the underlying colour included, has one alpha, and samples those four properties on a group. Sample the `d` transitions only. Encode the zoom as at most 32 geometric samples with linear segments, or as one `linear()` easing. Both are lowered. Keep the device gate, and do not describe the check or group colour as already off the main thread.

## 2. Scrub precedence

**Claim (D3).** The plot takes `pointerdown`, `pointermove` and `pointerup` with `touch-action="none"`. A drag that starts there scrubs, and it neither scrolls nor opens the drawer. That needs one host rule, on Apple and Android: an ancestor `pan` or scroller does not take a contact that a `touch-action: none` node holds. If a host lacks it, repair it within LLP 1057.001. No gesture arena.

**What is true.**

Rule 2 already makes scroll lose on an axis `touch-action` does not leave it. `none` leaves none. iOS implements that by intersecting `touch-action` from the hit view through the scroll view (`host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/IOS/ScrollViewIOS.swift:52-64`). Android's reader does not. It scrolls any axis a scroller under the finger has, unless exact2's own recognizers claim that axis (`host/android/canvas/src/main/java/dev/exact/canvas/Scrolling.kt:186-203`). Claims for a `pan` are computed from the pan node's own `touch-action`, not the hit node's (`host/linux/src/presenter/scrollers.rs:210-223`). A chart that only has pointer handlers claims nothing, so the reader scrolls it.

Rule 3 gives an ancestor `pan` the contact once it begins, including over a descendant `press` (`llp/1057.001-gesture-precedence-and-pinch.spec.md:37`, `llp/1057.001-gesture-precedence-and-pinch.spec.md:59-63`). Pointer handlers are not a boundary. iOS refuses the layout pan at a text field only, and `shouldBegin` reads the pan view's own `touch-action` (`host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/IOS/GesturePrecedenceIOS.swift:19-28`, `host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/IOS/NodeViewIOS.swift:123-132`, `host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/IOS/NodeViewIOS.swift:176-179`). The Linux and Android presenter walks up to the first `pan` with no `touch-action` test (`host/linux/src/presenter/contact.rs:270-276`) and, past the slop, dispatches that pan (`host/linux/src/presenter/contact.rs:510-528`). macOS does the same walk (`host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/LayoutPan.swift:31-48`). Pointer moves are delivered as well on that presenter (`host/linux/src/presenter/contact.rs:441-448`, `host/linux/src/presenter/pointer.rs:207-225`), so the chart can scrub while the drawer pan runs. A repair that stays inside the current table can make Android honor rule 2. It cannot stop the ancestor pan. Rule 3 says that pan takes the contact.

**What r2 should say.** iOS scroll already yields to `touch-action: none`. Android scroll has to grow the same intersection, which is rule 2. The drawer is a `pan`, and rule 3 keeps it. Either give the plot its own `pan` so the innermost recognizer wins and the ancestor does not begin, and take the bucket from the contact position, or amend LLP 1057.001 so a `touch-action: none` hit is a boundary an ancestor `pan` does not cross. That amendment is a spec change, not a host repair inside the current precedence.

D2's gradient op is not a material miss. The chart and the existing screens use straight linear fades (user-space SVG linears, and CSS `linear-gradient`s of one hue). One recorder op that carries linear endpoints, spread, straight stops and a gradient-to-local matrix can paint those with `LinearGradient`. Radial, conic and SVG focal circles are outside what §1 has to paint.
