The stall is real, and round 12 went the wrong way. Own-arm tokens moved 0.87×, 0.95×, 0.99×, then 1.17×, with commands 1.22×, while doc bytes stayed near 1,040 KB. Cutting bytes is finished. Fewer steps is the lever for the number you publish. A name sheet produces fewer steps only when the prompt stops asking for more lookups. Builders already read `functions.md` and then call vocab. A sheet generated from the schema also cannot carry the host facts they grep Swift for, such as the ± key. Report fresh beside total.

Preference has slid for three rounds (17–5–2, 15–8–1, 14–9–1). D2 and D3 cover the regressions that cost minutes. The graded framework losses (grocery-list's swipe, again; chat-rooms' white band; water-tracker's header buttons) are smaller than the step lever and are outside these decisions.

## 3. Risks

D1 as written adds required reading. LLP 1115 D7 says a short starter is the only required reading. An 8 KB sheet is fresh once and then cached on every later command, while the lookups continue, which can raise the published token count. D2 can make graded quality worse if `check` is taught to ignore a fill a person cannot see. D4 fits LLP 1115: more of the sample is judged against the iPhone reference. The risk is the headline. Replacing the stack you win, and then quoting a pooled ratio, makes the next round look like a regression and hides whether the sheet worked. The round-15 Expo check does that job only if that round is actually run.
