**1. Verdict: RIGHT WITH CHANGES**

**2. Overall assessment**

The RFC’s main direction is sound: investigate the captions already carried by Bluesky’s streams, expose playback observations to agents, and admit `.vtt` picking. Deferring a separate native WebVTT renderer is sensible. One architectural decision remains unresolved: what delivers captions when the browser can play HLS video but cannot process its subtitle renditions?

**3. Concerns**

- **MAJOR — The web path needs an explicit fallback or a narrower delivery promise.** [D1–D2](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-review-1114/llp/1114.005-video-captions.rfc.md:108) treat browser failures as issues to file and estimate the remaining work as approximately 100 lines of observation. However, the Chromium HLS parser available to this review explicitly skips subtitle and closed-caption renditions, with their support left as TODOs. That points to a missing playback capability, beyond whether native controls offer a menu. Reading `textTracks` cannot supply tracks the engine never ingests. This is source evidence, not a playback result on the installed Chrome. [Chromium’s HLS playlist parser](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/media/+/refs/heads/main/formats/hls/multivariant_playlist.cc).

  This matters because a failed Chrome drive could require admitting an HLS adapter, with a materially different dependency and implementation cost. Bluesky’s own web player explicitly loads hls.js and obtains subtitle tracks through it; its working captions therefore do not establish that a plain `<video>` suffices. [Bluesky’s web player](https://github.com/bluesky-social/social-app/blob/main/src/components/Post/Embed/VideoEmbed/VideoEmbedInner/VideoEmbedInnerWeb.tsx).

  **Resolution:** retain the initial drive, but make its outcome a scope decision. If native subtitle playback fails, either admit an adapter loaded on demand after first paint, or explicitly limit the initial caption milestone to supported engines. Keep the “small” estimate attached to investigation and observation until that decision is settled. General sidecar `<track>` support should remain deferred; it does not itself implement HLS subtitle rendition loading.

**4. What it gets right**

- The distinction between caption blobs in the post record and playback through the playlist is supported by Bluesky’s schema and player. It prevents building an unnecessary sidecar system for this consumer. [Video lexicon](https://github.com/bluesky-social/atproto/blob/main/lexicons/app/bsky/embed/video.json).
- D5 addresses a real compiler restriction without making the clone register as a caption-file viewer. [Picker admission code](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-review-1114/contract/cli/src/picker.rs).
- Leaving native playback and selection with AVKit, and extending the existing agent observations, fits [LLP 1042’s ownership model](/Users/ccheever/projects/exact2-review-1114/llp/1042-video.spec.md:25).
- Keeping stage 2 deferred respects the existing consumer requirement and avoids committing to a substantial renderer prematurely.
