- **Scope:** semantics and Web Audio fidelity: the Contract surface, the runner clock, the browser claims.
- **Transcription:** the run's output, unedited (from `~/projects/x2apps/_reviews/llp1096-a.grok.md`).

The Web Audio finding was re-measured for r2 (Playwright 1.63: Chrome 154 by `channel: 'chrome'`, Firefox and WebKit by Playwright's builds): `start(-0.01)` throws `RangeError` in all three, the first `getOutputTimestamp()` is `{0, 0}` in Chrome and WebKit, and offline `start(0.25)` / `stop(0.5)` then `stop(0.3)` sound from frame 12000 to 14399 at 48 kHz in all three. r2 resolves every finding (round 1 of 3).

D6 clamps every `start` and `stop` with `max(0, …)`, starts a due voice with `start(0)` and holds a future one until the timestamp is live, and D3 schedules the downbeat from the press, since the first gated tick is 25 ms late (LLP 1092 D8).
  2. **MATERIAL, `HOST_COMMANDS` moves under LLP 1089: taken.** Confirmed: LLP 1089 D1 moves the list into `contract-syntax` and its expander consults it; on main today it is still `checks.rs:744` (1089 stage 1 has not landed).   3. **MATERIAL, the recommended take is not a take: taken in part.** The orchestrator had accepted r1's Q1 (the take, with a waiver as its fallback) before this review was read. D12's recorded entry names the take as offered and accepted, and says that to the extent it is not doing-list work the admission stands on the orchestrator's waiver under the same delegation, so the exception is a visible act either way.   4. **MINOR, the latency fallback points the wrong way: taken.** D6 takes `contextTime` as `currentTime - outputLatency` where `getOutputTimestamp` is missing, and names Chrome's and WebKit's `baseLatency` rather than adding them. Every engine measured has `getOutputTimestamp`.
  5. **MINOR, named arguments never reach `type-play-sound`: taken.** Confirmed: the generic loop `infer`s every argument (`checks.rs:1096–1098`) and `infer` refuses a `NamedArg` (`types/src/lib.rs:683–688`).   6. **MINOR, one `playSound` per tick cannot cover the window: taken, differently.** A fixed `let` unroll caps hits per window and multiplies by tracks. ## MATERIAL

### D6, D3, D7. A past runner time is not a past `when`, and a zero timestamp schedules the page age.

**Problem.** D6 passes the formula's `when` straight to `start(when)` and `stop(when)`, and D3 says a past time starts immediately, attack included. Web Audio does that only for `0 <= when < currentTime`. A negative `when` throws `RangeError` and the voice is neither started nor stopped. Before the graph has rendered a block, `getOutputTimestamp()` is `{0, 0}`, and the same formula turns runner time into seconds-since-epoch while `currentTime` is still 0. The documented downbeat is in that hole: a gated `every(25)` first fires at now+25 (LLP 1092 D8), so the hit at 0 is already late, and early in the context that lateness is negative.

**Evidence.** Web Audio 1.1 (WD, 22 Sep 2026), `AudioScheduledSourceNode.start` / `stop`: a negative `when` is a `RangeError`; `0` or a `when` below `currentTime` means immediately. `getOutputTimestamp`: both members are 0 until the graph has processed a block; after that `currentTime` is always ahead of `contextTime`. Measured here with Playwright 1.63 against Chrome 154.0.8037.98 (headed and headless), Firefox 155, and WebKit 26.6, first gesture, no wait:

- Chrome headed, pointerdown: `contextTime` 0, `performanceTime` 311.3, `performance.now()` 312.7, `currentTime` 0.0107. Formula for "now" is `when` 0.0014 (plays immediately). Formula for 40 ms late is `when` **-0.0386**. Probe 1 called `start` with that value: `RangeError: The start time provided (-0.0489) is less than the minimum bound (0)`. WebKit's message was `when value should be positive`. Firefox's suspended context produced `whenLate` **-0.04** the same way.
- Chrome and WebKit at construction, timestamp `{0, 0}`: formula `when` was **0.193 s** and **0.064 s** while `currentTime` was 0. That is the page age. On a page that has been open for seconds, the first scheduled hit waits that long.
- Once the timestamp is live the formula is right: Firefox `currentTime - contextTime` equals `outputLatency` (0.01110 s); a hit 100 ms ahead lands 100 ms ahead on that clock. Offline, `start(0.25)` first becomes non-zero at frame 12000 on all three, and `stop(0.50)` then `stop(0.25)` ends at frame 11999. Last-stop-wins is true. The unclamped number is the bug.

**Fix.** Clamp with `max(0, when)` before every `start` and `stop`. If `performanceTime` is 0, do not use the formula: play a due-or-past voice with `start(0)`, and hold a future voice until the timestamp is live. State that the downbeat is scheduled from the play action itself, because the first tick is one interval late and that lateness is what goes negative.

### D2, §4. `playSound` is registered where LLP 1089 will not look.

**Problem.** D2 adds the two names to `HOST_COMMANDS` in `contract/types/src/checks.rs`, and §4's stage 1 (2026-10-06) edits that list. Accepted LLP 1089 moves the list into `contract-syntax` on 2026-10-05, and the expander treats a name that is not on it as an action call. After that lands, `playSound(...)` is `type-unknown-command`, or `syntax-call-cycle` if an action wraps the command.

**Evidence.** LLP 1089 D1 (`llp/1089-action-composition.rfc.md:83-93`): a statement is a host command only when the name is in `HOST_COMMANDS`, the expander consults that list before any action, and the list moves out of `checks.rs:744`. Status and date: lines 4 and 9, stage 1 on 2026-10-05. Today the list is still `contract/types/src/checks.rs:744-778`. This RFC's evidence cites that same range (`1096:103-105`) and §4 sends stage 1 at the old file.

**Fix.** Say the names are added to the list 1089 places in `contract-syntax`, and that the expander sees them, so a call keeps the command name. An action may be bound `playSound` and still cannot be called.

### D12, §9 Q1. The recommended take is not a take.

**Problem.** Q1's recommended trade does not remove anything from the doing-list. Declining the unbuilt R1 glue change, and deleting the Ding recipe and LLP 1042 §8's sound-effect paragraph, withdraws documentation and a bug that was never admitted. `rules/RULES.md` allows a new doing-list item only with a real removal or a waiver. Writing Q1's recommendation into `rules/DEFERRED.md` records a bargain the rule does not recognize.

**Evidence.** `rules/RULES.md:46-48`. LLP 1042 §8 (`llp/1042-video.spec.md:431-436`) already describes the Ding path, a sound that is allowed to finish; drums R1 says that path is a different case from a hit repeated while `paused` stays false, and it is not a `DEFERRED` or `QUEUE` line. The same 2026-10-04 delegation waived LLP 1089, LLP 1092, and LLP 1094 with "Take: none offered" (`rules/DEFERRED.md:426-433`, and the 1092 and 1094 motion entries). Q1's own fallback is that waiver (`1096:642-644`). The command shape is fine: no new tag, same as `haptic`, so the Components count stays put.

**Fix.** Record the waiver, not the recommended take. Keep the "Still out" list.

## MINOR

### D6. The latency fallback points the wrong way.

**Problem.** "currentTime plus outputLatency (or baseLatency)" is not a stand-in for `getOutputTimestamp().contextTime`. On a live context that value is behind `currentTime`, by about the latency. Adding the latency schedules a hit late by about two latencies.

**Evidence.** Same probe, after the graph was running. Firefox: `currentTime - contextTime` = `outputLatency` = 0.01110 s, `baseLatency` 0. Chrome: the gap was 0.0218 s, and `outputLatency` 0.016 + `baseLatency` 0.0053 = 0.0213. WebKit: the gap was `baseLatency` 0.00267 s, while `outputLatency` was 0.00477 s. The spec says the currentTime/contextTime gap is not a latency estimate and that `currentTime` exceeds `contextTime`. All three browsers already have `getOutputTimestamp`.

**Fix.** Prefer the timestamp. Where it is missing, use `currentTime - outputLatency` on Firefox's terms, and name Chrome's extra `baseLatency` and WebKit's `baseLatency`-sized gap instead of adding.

### D2. Named arguments never reach `type-play-sound`.

**Problem.** `at=`, `gain=`, and `group=` die in the generic command check as canvas bindings. `share` and `scrollIntoView` avoid that only by returning before `infer`.

**Evidence.** `check_command` falls through to `infer` on every argument (`contract/types/src/checks.rs:1096-1098`). `infer` of a `NamedArg` is `type-named-argument`, "named arguments belong to a canvas surface binding" (`contract/types/src/lib.rs:683-688`). `share` returns at `checks.rs:1057-1058`. `command_args` (`contract/lower/src/expr.rs:39-41`) never sees a named argument that failed the type pass. `docs/contract-for-agents.md:222` still lists only `share` and `scrollIntoView` as commands with named arguments; D13 does not amend that sentence.

**Fix.** Give `playSound` and `stopSounds` an early checker, as `share` has, and add them to that sentence.

### D3, D13. One `playSound` per tick cannot cover the window the prose describes.

**Problem.** The tick example schedules one hit and then assigns `nextAt`. That keeps the grid only when the step is at least 25 ms and the window never holds two hits of one track. Contract actions have no loop, and a second `playSound(at=nextAt)` in the same action reads the same starting `nextAt`. The adoption text says the tick schedules each hit due before `now() + 100`.

**Evidence.** The example is `1096:30-36`. "No loops" is `docs/contract-for-agents.md:258`. Reads see the starting state (`llp/1089-action-composition.rfc.md:126-129`). LLP 1092 D8 arms `every(25)` at now+25 (`llp/1092-sends-that-queue-and-timers-that-wait.rfc.md:355`), so the open window is not scheduled by the play action.

**Fix.** Show the play action scheduling the open window, and a fixed `let` unroll (`t`, `t+step`, `t+2*step`) from the snapshot, with `nextAt` advanced by that count. State that a step shorter than the tick falls behind forever.

### D8, Q5. `audio_session` does not own the iOS category.

**Problem.** Q5 says `app.json` `audio_session: "playback"` selects `AVAudioSession` `.playback` and settles the ring-switch queue item for an app that sets it. A canvas that wants audio already sets `.ambient` the first time, with its own flag, and will overwrite `.playback` if it runs after the sound arm.

**Evidence.** `host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/CanvasSeams.swift:668-684`: `CanvasAudio.activate` calls `setCategory(.ambient)` when its private `configured` is false. Nothing else in `host/apple` sets the category. Trivia, a stage-2 consumer, has a canvas (confetti). The category is process-wide, so the two setters are one session.

**Fix.** One setter reads `audio_session`. `CanvasAudio` activates the session and stops choosing the category.

### D5, D10. `expect sound` cannot see a choke, and it only sees 64 voices.

**Problem.** `has` matches a cut voice the same as a voice that plays out, and the driver reads `state.sounds`, whose `voices` are the last 64. A 120 BPM kick over a minute is about 120 voices, so the early grid drops out of the assertion. `by` for a `stopSounds` of a voice that has not started is also unnamed: D4 calls it cancelled, and D5's `cut` is only the group case where end equals start.

**Evidence.** `1096:401-421` and `:256-257`. The sample test (`1096:423-430`) only sees two kicks, so it passes either way.

**Fix.** Let `expect sound` take `by` and `ends`, and say it reads the runner table, not the 64-line snapshot. Name `by` for a stop at or before the start.

### D5, D7. The JS call site still refuses the command.

**Problem.** `rt.js` is told to gain only a call after `command()`. `command()` logs `refused:` for a name that is not in `Hosts`. The wasm runner journals `command playSound(...)` and does not refuse. §5 compares those journals.

**Evidence.** `host/web-js/rt.js:222-226` refuses an unknown name after `say(\`command ${name}\`)`. The specified call is the line after `rt.js:182`. `1096:271-272` and `:291-293`.

**Fix.** `command()` must recognize the two names and not refuse them. The following call applies `take_sounds()`.

## NIT

### D5. Drop lines for a non-finite `at` or `gain` are unnamed.

**Problem.** Undeclared `src` and the 33rd voice have journal text. A non-finite `at` is "journaled" with no line. A non-finite `gain` is not mentioned; `min(1, max(0, NaN))` is `NaN`, and a `NaN` gain poisons the mix.

**Evidence.** `1096:273-279` lists two drop lines and then says a non-finite `at` is dropped. No third string.

**Fix.** Add the line, and drop a non-finite `gain` the same way as a non-finite `at`.

Verdict: READY WITH CHANGES
