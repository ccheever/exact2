1. **MATERIAL — D6 resumes acquisition under the wrong origin.** [Lines 418–424](llp/1133.013-motion.rfc.md:418) retain the original `startSensorInput` send’s origin when the canvas reappears. An app can enable tilt, hide the canvas until acquisition stops, and then have a tool-origin commit reveal it. Sampling restarts under the original app origin, defeating D11’s refusal of tool-origin acquisition starts. This also contradicts the adopted [camera lease rule](llp/1133.008-camera.rfc.md:388) and D12’s displayed reports carrying the displaying commit’s origin. **Decision needed:** admit a display-triggered lease using the displaying commit’s origin; distinguish that from foreground recovery of an existing lease.

2. **MATERIAL — D4’s attitude composition cannot preserve relative-orientation semantics.** [Lines 300–307](llp/1133.013-motion.rfc.md:300) derive relative orientation from magnetic attitude by subtracting the initial yaw. That removes a constant heading offset, but subsequent magnetic corrections and interference remain. The [W3C relative-orientation model](https://www.w3.org/TR/orientation-sensor/#model-relative-orientation) uses accelerometer and gyroscope and explicitly distinguishes its freedom from magnetic interference. Adding an absolute-orientation consumer therefore changes an existing relative reader’s behavior. **Decision needed:** provide independent relative fusion or define a conflict policy for incompatible Core Motion frame requests. Static yaw subtraction cannot implement the promised composition.

3. **MATERIAL — D5’s shared ring cannot retain the promised history after thinning.** [Lines 333–345](llp/1133.013-motion.rfc.md:333) size the acquisition ring by one second or the requested history count at the highest rate. Consider a 10 Hz reader with `interval=10000, history=100`: alone, its 100-sample ring retains ten seconds. Beside a 60 Hz canvas, those 100 raw samples retain only 1.67 seconds—about 17 eligible samples. Another consumer causes losses although the reader’s own history bound accommodates the complete batch, contradicting D4’s independence and D5’s guarantee. **Decision needed:** retain bounded thinned histories per consumer, or size raw retention by each reader’s `history / frequency` horizon with an explicit memory bound.

4. **MATERIAL — D2 cannot infer the acceleration convention from the first reading.** [Lines 225–228](llp/1133.013-motion.rfc.md:225) compare `accelerationIncludingGravity.z` with orientation to decide whether to negate readings. Orientation determines gravity’s projection, but this acceleration also contains movement: it can be zero or have the opposite sign during perfectly valid motion. The [W3C motion model](https://www.w3.org/TR/orientation-event/#model-devicemotion) expressly includes this behavior. A mistaken initial classification reverses subsequent readings; a magnitude threshold alone cannot resolve the ambiguity. **Decision needed:** use a known-backend normalization or a validated calibration/unknown policy instead of an unconditional first-reading inference.

5. **MINOR — §9 contradicts D7’s clock behavior.** [D7](llp/1133.013-motion.rfc.md:455) says an advance crossing multiple poll periods asks once, so `clock +1000` can return a 60-sample batch. [§9’s transition table](llp/1133.013-motion.rfc.md:882) instead says each crossed poll answers and commits, which would advance the cursor repeatedly and leave a smaller final batch. **Decision needed:** make §9 follow D7’s already-established single ask after the advance.
