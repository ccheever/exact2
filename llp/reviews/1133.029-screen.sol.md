I found no unresolved **MATERIAL** concern. The architecture and documented platform limits support proceeding.

The vocabulary choice is defensible. D11’s reasons concern arbitration over arbitrary existing elements and controllers owned by the host, which fits LLP 1133 D5’s exception for capabilities needing the runner or kernel (`llp/1133-the-standard-library.rfc.md:262–291`; RFC D11, `llp/1133.029-screen.rfc.md:420–445`). The attribute form explicitly declares its deviation and inherits an established lifetime rule from `runner/src/wakelock.rs:45–58`. `current` qualifies as a shared word.

D3 makes coherent policy choices: descendants override ancestors, unrelated holders intersect, conflicts release to the manifest, and embedded sessions cannot control their embedder’s window (RFC D3, lines 175–243). The narrowing-only bound is conservative and implementable. D10’s origin decision also fits the inherited boundary: orientation changes presentation; it does not start capture or open system UI. Camera’s retained display origin serves device acquisition (`llp/1133.008-camera.rfc.md:732–743`).

The platform cells have documentary support:

- Apple documents the root/full-screen-controller orientation mask, its intersection with app support, and the multitasking exception. Its newer lock preference is conditional and may be lifted, as D5 acknowledges (RFC lines 258–277). [Supported orientations](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiviewcontroller/supportedinterfaceorientations?changes=_7), [orientation-lock preference](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiviewcontroller/prefersinterfaceorientationlocked?changes=_7&language=objc).
- Android’s ignored large-screen values exclude `locked`; D6 therefore has a reasonable documented basis. The repository targets API 36 (`host/android/app/build.gradle.kts:41`). Android 17 removes the opt-out when targeting API 37. [Android 16](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-16), [Android 17](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/17/changes/ff-restrictions-ignored).
- The web’s fullscreen restriction and unlock on exit match the specification. Windows exposes a process preference, and D8 explicitly adapts that through the focused window (RFC lines 318–357). [Screen Orientation §9](https://www.w3.org/TR/screen-orientation/#interaction-with-fullscreen-api), [Windows API](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/winuser/nf-winuser-setdisplayautorotationpreferences).

The remaining concerns belong to implementation:

1. **IMPLEMENTATION-PHASE — Preserve `current` across interruptions.** D3 captures it once per continuous hold (lines 223–228). D7’s re-asks must reuse that captured type. Conflicts and platform lifting also must preserve enough information to distinguish an interrupted hold from a new one. Where the platform cannot restore the captured orientation, readback must show nonapplication. This needs lifecycle coverage, not a different architecture.

2. **IMPLEMENTATION-PHASE — Resolve device-dependent constraints at the host.** `natural` also depends on the device, so D3’s intersection cannot always finish in the runner (lines 230–237). D6/D8 already identify its host mappings. Carrying that constraint until host resolution follows the existing design. [Specification §2.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/screen-orientation/#screen-orientation-types).

3. **IMPLEMENTATION-PHASE — Prove effects separately from requests.** Android’s requested value and Windows’ preference do not prove physical rotation was prevented. The Android drive needs the observed-orientation comparison inherited from drive 2; its large-screen leg needs an applicable OS version. Web readback must clear after fullscreen exit (RFC §9.1, lines 539–566; D7, lines 337–338). Actual device behavior cannot be verified from this snapshot.

Beacons supplies a concrete consumer: its gravity readings and canvas input use the screen frame (`game/games/beacons/app.contract:23,38`). Deferring brightness, capture controls, and Contract orientation readings is justified; motion already receives the angle through the package context (`llp/1133.013-motion.rfc.md:860–864`). The admission waiver remains explicitly requested in §13, lines 694–696.

I found no unresolved MATERIAL concern. R2’s main decisions are coherent; the remaining issues are minor inconsistencies and implementation coverage.

For the three round 1 findings:

1. **RESOLVED — Preserve `current` across interruptions.** [D3, lines 234–242](llp/1133.029-screen.rfc.md#L234) now explicitly preserves the capture while any counted holder says `current`, including fullscreen re-entry and system lifting. Implementation must preserve that membership through a runner-detected conflict and compare the retained orientation with actual geometry after restoration. These remain implementation-phase obligations. Apple’s documentation establishes conditional locking, but does not establish which orientation automatic restoration will choose. [Apple lock preference](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiviewcontroller/prefersinterfaceorientationlocked?changes=_7&language=objc).

2. **RESOLVED — Resolve device-dependent constraints at the host.** [D3, lines 221–231](llp/1133.029-screen.rfc.md#L221) explicitly assigns `current`, `natural`, device support, manifest intersection and resulting conflicts to the host. The runner retains tree arbitration and device-free intersection. This addresses the architectural boundary identified previously.

3. **RESOLVED — Prove effects separately from requests.** [§8, lines 530–539](llp/1133.029-screen.rfc.md#L530) expressly distinguishes requests from effects. [§9.1, lines 586–630](llp/1133.029-screen.rfc.md#L586) adds observed Android rotation, an applicable large-screen OS, web holding-lock coverage and the older iOS mask path. These are adequate verification commitments; actual device behavior remains unverified from this snapshot.

The delta’s cover → descendant → intersection ordering fixes the ordinary sheet case. The recipe constructs [sibling routes](docs/recipes/navigation-stack.md#L55), while [`inactive` and `hidden_route`](runner/src/head.rs#L86) deliberately distinguish an inactive covered route from a hidden one. [`under_a_sheet`](runner/src/head.rs#L264) also identifies the covered split list. Thus cover precedence belongs before ancestry arbitration. The native-control exception matches [`wakelock::displayed`](runner/src/wakelock.rs#L45), and the web’s corresponding visibility rule is in [`wakelock-glue.js`](host/web/wakelock-glue.js#L49).

The host-before-platform manifest check is also sound. It must operate on semantic orientation sets before reusing the manifest helper: [`phoneOrientations`](host/apple/devices.mjs#L766) deliberately maps manifest `portrait-secondary` to upright. D5’s expanded controller requirement accommodates both [`ModalController`](host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/IOS/ModalIOS.swift#L27) and the separate [`DialogSheetController`](host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/IOS/DialogSheetIOS.swift#L42), plus UIKit containers. Apple documents the app/device-mask intersection and full-window-controller query. [Supported orientations](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiviewcontroller/supportedinterfaceorientations?changes=la_2_5).

Two delta-related observations remain:

- **IN-DELTA — MINOR: the bake example contradicts its new criterion.** [D3, lines 262–268](llp/1133.029-screen.rfc.md#L262) refuses a literal only when disjoint on *every* device, yet gives `"landscape"` with a portrait manifest as its example. A universal iPhone/iPad build passes that criterion because [D3, lines 244–249](llp/1133.029-screen.rfc.md#L244) and [`orientationKeys`](host/apple/devices.mjs#L775) give iPad all four orientations. Clarify the example or intended bake bound. Runtime safety remains covered by the host check.

- **PRE-EXISTING — IMPLEMENTATION-PHASE: stacked-sheet display parity.** [`under_a_sheet`](runner/src/head.rs#L307) retains only the immediate preceding route, whereas [web navigation](host/web/navigation.js#L436) retains the page and intervening sheets. A locked page beneath two unlocked sheets can therefore lose its native holder while retaining its web holder, contrary to [D3, lines 193–194](llp/1133.029-screen.rfc.md#L193). This is an inherited helper mismatch, not a defective arbitration decision; include it in the shared table and reconcile the helper during implementation.

The fresh whole-document pass found no additional deep architectural problem. One **PRE-EXISTING — MINOR** inconsistency remains: [D4, line 293](llp/1133.029-screen.rfc.md#L293) overpromises respect for the person’s rotation lock. [D6, line 333](llp/1133.029-screen.rfc.md#L333) reuses `sensorPortrait`/`sensorLandscape`, matching [`build.gradle.kts`](host/android/app/build.gradle.kts#L20); Android explicitly says those modes use the sensor despite the user’s rotation lock. R3 leaves no unresolved MATERIAL concern. All six findings from this family’s round 2 review are resolved at the RFC level.

1. **RESOLVED — Preserve `current` across interruptions.** D3, lines 254–262, retains the capture while any counted holder says `current`, including re-asks and system lifting. Hosts must reconcile that capture with actual geometry after restoration; this remains the implementation obligation already identified in round 2.

2. **RESOLVED — Resolve device-dependent constraints at the host.** D3, lines 240–252, preserves the boundary: the runner arbitrates the tree; the host resolves `current`, `natural`, device support and manifest intersection. Sending holder identities also supports useful host-detected conflict reports.

3. **RESOLVED — Prove effects separately from requests.** §8, lines 552–561, still expressly distinguishes them. §9.1, lines 608–640, retains observed rotation checks for the older iOS mask path, Android large screens and a web browser that actually holds locks.

4. **RESOLVED — Bake criterion contradicted its example.** D3, lines 264–290, now bounds locks by the manifest’s own semantic value on every device, including iPad. This coherently distinguishes that bound from [`phoneOrientations` and `orientationKeys`](host/apple/devices.mjs#L758), which map secondary portrait to upright on iPhone and give iPad all four orientations. Semantic intersection must precede that mapping.

5. **RESOLVED — Stacked-sheet display parity.** D3, lines 197–214, §10, lines 668–677, and the fixture, lines 654–662, explicitly require the web’s covered set and topmost holding sheet precedence. This addresses the discrepancy between [`hidden_route`](runner/src/head.rs#L93), [`under_a_sheet`](runner/src/head.rs#L299) and [`navigation.js`](host/web/navigation.js#L436).

6. **RESOLVED — Android rotation-lock promise.** D4, line 315, now matches [`build.gradle.kts`](host/android/app/build.gradle.kts#L18): `sensorPortrait` and `sensorLandscape` follow the sensor despite the user’s rotation lock. [Android documents that behavior explicitly](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/manifest/activity-element#screen).

The new top-layer exclusion is coherent with the named code. [`wakelock::displayed`](runner/src/wakelock.rs#L45) reads display, whole host covers and hidden routes; it does not know whether a dialog is open. [`geometry.rs`](runner/src/geometry.rs#L202) receives presenter-reported top-layer frames and explicitly identifies the [host as the source of truth](runner/src/geometry.rs#L1178). [`closed.rs`](host/apple/src/closed.rs#L30) limits closed-box covering to iOS/tvOS, while [`bars.rs`](host/linux/src/bars.rs#L116) covers popovers for native top-layer presentation. Excluding dialog/popover holders on every host, with bake refusal and runtime journaling, removes the dependency rather than introducing another arbitration authority. Leaving the underlying screen’s lock intact is consistent with that decision.

One **PRE-EXISTING — IMPLEMENTATION-PHASE** coverage point remains: the covered-set correction should reach the native presenter too. [`host/linux/src/navigation.rs`](host/linux/src/navigation.rs#L53) currently preserves only the immediate modal predecessor, and [Android shares this host](host/android/README.md#L5). Widening that presenter path alongside `under_a_sheet` implements r3’s existing decision; it requires no architectural change.

The fresh whole-document pass found no additional deep defect. Actual rotation and restoration effects cannot be established from this read-only review. In particular, [Apple documents conditional orientation locking](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiviewcontroller/prefersinterfaceorientationlocked?changes=_7&language=objc), but does not establish which orientation automatic restoration selects. The planned device drives remain necessary; this does not reopen the resolved capture decision.
