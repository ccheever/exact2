1. **The watchdog can be throttled along with the rendering it watches.**

   [D7’s web row](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/llp/1147-timers-that-may-wait.rfc.md:302) recognizes stopped rendering only if its 100 ms timeout arrives within one frame period of its scheduled time. A later timeout is interpreted as a busy main thread.

   That inference is false. Chromium documents both stopped animation callbacks **and timer throttling** in offscreen cross-origin frames. HTML also permits padding timeouts independently of main-thread work. ([Chromium throttling](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/HEAD/third_party/blink/renderer/core/frame#throttling), [HTML timeout algorithm](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/timers-and-user-prompts.html#run-steps-after-a-timeout))

   Consider an otherwise quiet, offscreen iframe containing `after(100, save, wait=scrollend)`. Its deadline callback requests an animation callback and the watchdog. Rendering remains stopped, and throttling delivers the watchdog hundreds of milliseconds late. D7 therefore never recognizes the stop. There is no scroll completion, visibility transition or cap; the [wake rule](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/llp/1147-timers-that-may-wait.rfc.md:338) supplies no further wake. The save can remain unfired indefinitely. Even adding retries would not fix a predicate that rejects every throttled timeout.

   **Instead:** Define progress without requiring punctual timeouts. Treat a late watchdog as unknown rendering availability and explicitly choose a fallback with a qualified scroll guarantee, or refuse uncapped `scrollend` where that availability cannot be established.
