1. **D4 contradicts the runner’s existing live-clock semantics.** [D4](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/llp/1147-timers-that-may-wait.rfc.md:115) says precise callbacks observe their deadlines and released callbacks all run at *t*. The runner instead samples the live clock for each commit and reschedules repeats after completion ([advance.rs:102](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/runner/src/runner/advance.rs:102), [advance.rs:593](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/runner/src/runner/advance.rs:593)); the existing JS live-clock tests passed, 6/6.

   Suppose `release(..., 100)` first runs a precise callback that finishes at 120 ms. The held callback cannot then observe 100 ms without reversing the clock. Similarly, choosing an `every`’s successor after its start can leave it overdue before its callback finishes.

   **Instead:** Separate release eligibility from observed commit time. Preserve live sampling and completion-based rescheduling; reserve fixed logical times for seeks.

2. **The proposed API cannot perform `update`’s per-timer admission check.** [D2](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/llp/1147-timers-that-may-wait.rfc.md:83) decides separately whether each timer fits, but [D6](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/llp/1147-timers-that-may-wait.rfc.md:169) releases every held timer of a kind in one call. Apple combines all returned receipts into one batch, sharing the final kernel tree ([host.rs:1020](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/host/apple/src/host.rs:1020), [host.rs:1303](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/host/apple/src/host.rs:1303)).

   With 8 ms remaining and two timers historically costing 5 ms each, the host must admit the first and defer the second. `release(update, t)` gives it no intervening decision point or separate apply measurement. Prerequisite precise work can also consume the budget after admission. D7’s web fallback, “otherwise at once,” directly contradicts D2’s deferral rule.

   **Instead:** Defer adaptive `update` from this RFC. Retaining it requires per-timer release and measurement boundaries, with budget checks after prerequisite work.

3. **`frame` and passive `update` have no established liveness bound.** [D3](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/llp/1147-timers-that-may-wait.rfc.md:109) removes their caps because a frame supposedly arrives within one period. HTML explicitly permits throttled rendering opportunities; Chrome stops animation callbacks in background tabs. ([HTML rendering model](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/webappapis.html#event-loop-processing-model), [Chrome background behavior](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/background_tabs/))

   A visible page’s deadline timeout requests an animation callback; the page then becomes hidden before that callback. D7 supplies no wake for this held timer. D2’s deadline fallback does not specify what happens when presentation stops *after* the deadline. Passive `UIUpdateLink` likewise supplies no notification that there will be no next update. ([Apple’s passive-observer behavior](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiupdatelink/requirescontinuousupdates))

   **Instead:** Define explicit handling for held timers when presentation stops, plus a timeout fallback. Describe frame callbacks as scheduling opportunities and remove the universal one-period bound.

4. **D7’s idle callbacks do not establish D2’s quiescence.** [D2](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/llp/1147-timers-that-may-wait.rfc.md:100) excludes moving scrolls and pending work. However, `requestIdleCallback` deliberately runs between frames during active animations, and Android’s `IdleHandler` may run with future messages pending. ([Idle-callback specification](https://w3c.github.io/requestidlecallback/), [Android reference](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/os/MessageQueue.IdleHandler))

   During a compositor fling, Exact may commit nothing while the platform continues scrolling. Safari’s proposed “first task after” such a frame therefore qualifies mid-fling. Chrome may offer a 1 ms idle period and receive a synchronous 4 ms Contract batch, delaying the next frame even though that batch fits within a whole frame.

   **Instead:** Make these callbacks wakeup hints. Specify a shared, rechecked eligibility predicate and what happens when the available idle budget cannot accommodate an indivisible batch.

5. **The default cap defeats scroll exclusion for the proposed consumers.** [D3](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/llp/1147-timers-that-may-wait.rfc.md:106) forces execution after one interval, while [D7](/Users/admin/Developer/exact2-timer-wait-points/llp/1147-timers-that-may-wait.rfc.md:189) claims a held timer never fires inside a scroll turn.

   Change Messages’ candidate timer to `every(250, network, wait=scrollend)`. During a one-second fling, its 250 ms deadline reaches its default cap at 500 ms and runs synchronously mid-scroll. Ordinary glides exceed this window; the advertised protection disappears precisely where this consumer needs it.

   **Instead:** Make `scrollend` uncapped by default. Require explicit `max=` to authorize execution during scrolling, and state that exception in the guarantee.
