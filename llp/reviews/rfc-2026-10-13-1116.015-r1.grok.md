## 1. Verdict

**RETHINK.** The diagnosis is right and the migration is not. Rounds 13–15 show the token gap is docs re-sent on later steps, and LLP 1116.013 already showed that moving those bytes into lookups does not shrink a build. This RFC answers that by deleting Contract's spellings wherever two models' unguided sketches differ from Exact. That census does not support those deletions, the §3 ceiling of about 17% counts whole topics the edits do not remove, and stage S1 (the rewrite of every Contract source) sits inside the noise of the A/B that is supposed to judge it. The robust, cheap part is small: `if` and `for` in a view, a type on `state`, and method calls on the roster that already exists. The part large enough to matter, the data protocol, is mislabeled as sugar and is the decision that can break transactions, grants, and replay.

## 2. The most important issues

**1. The bound is a ceiling on the wrong bundle, and the judging rule cannot see the stage that rewrites the repo.**

The arithmetic in §3 is internally consistent. At 0.24M tokens per MB-step (LLP 1116.013), the first three rows are 0.88 × 0.24 = 0.21M for GPT and 1.83 × 0.24 = 0.44M for Muse. Two extra steps and half of LLP 1116.014's repair cost (70k GPT, 121k Muse) land near 0.27M of GPT's 1.64M and 0.64M of Muse's 3.67M, which is the stated 17%.

That 17% is not what S1 buys. Of GPT's 0.88, the expressions row is 0.20 (about 48k tokens, 3% of a build) and screens are 0.19. Data is 0.49 of the 0.88, and 1.21 of Muse's 1.83. Data is D6, which the RFC calls the least certain decision and schedules as S3. S1 is D1–D4, priced as "spelling," and it also deletes `task` and adds roster functions and `Date.now()`. A 3–5% token move will not pass "fall beyond the noise." LLP 1116.013's paired A/B was 1.03 with a ±1 SE band of 0.96–1.11. LLP 1116.012 cut a fifth of the commands and the token ratio stayed inside one standard error.

The gap the 17% is compared with is also two different numbers. Rounds 13–14, same specs: GPT 1.64M against 1.23M (33%), Muse 3.67M against 3.41M (8%). Round 15's 1.67× React median is a high outlier. `rounds/r15/findings.md` says React's builds were unusually small (0.69M) and the same-round ratio was 1.75×. LLP 1116.012's series of Exact2 against the React arm's median sits between 1.39× and 1.59× from round 8 through round 14. Half of the 33% paired gap is a real goal. Half of 67% is a different goal, and this RFC cannot reach either unless D6 actually deletes the data topic. It does not. `pending`, `failure`, grants, SQLite, and the clock stay.

Landing D1–D4 as one rewrite of 446 `.contract` files (33,337 lines) against a few hundred branches, then judging the bundle, repeats the mistake LLP 1116.013's review corrected: a regression, or a null result, has no owner. Muse is already within 8%. A GPT-shaped language change can raise Muse's tokens by more than it cuts GPT's, and a bundled stage hides that.

**2. The census does not say "take the guess," and one of its strongest rows is prompted by the guide.**

I re-ran `~/bakeoff/census.py` on `guesses/priors1` (40 features × GPT-6.1 Sol and Muse Spark 1.3). The table in §2 matches. The decisions do not follow from it.

`guesses/main1` is the other half of the experiment, and it is decisive. With today's start-here, the same 40 features produce 0 `if` in a view, 0 `for`, free-function calls in 27 and 33 features, `resource` in 34 and 31, `mutation`/`send` in 22 and 22, and HTML tags in 2 and 1. The current guide works. Its cost is the bytes, which is the right problem. It also means these models do not have a fixed idea of Contract. They write what the page in front of them shows. A language designed to today's unguided sketches will be wrong for the next guide and the next model, and `main1` shows the correction is one read away.

Where the two models agree, the signal is real: `if` in a view is 35/40 and 36/40, `for` is 28 and 31, typed `state` is 21 and 24, `app.ts` exports plain functions in 38 and 37, and neither writes `when`, `each`, `list<T>`, or a `Sources` map (all 0). Where they do not agree, the RFC still deletes the old spelling under T3:

| Proposed change | GPT | Muse | What the sketches actually contain |
|---|---|---|---|
| HTML tags (D3) | 40 | 4 | The guide says "Tags are HTML's names" (`priors-guide.md`). `h1 "…"` appears in 0 features. `text` appears in 40 and 40. `p` is 17 and 0. `ul`/`li` is 12 and 1. |
| `T[]` (D1) | 20 | 12 | Muse also writes `[T]` (6) and `->` (11). `: String` / `: Number` is 2 and 19. `T \| null` is 1 and 1. |
| `return` (D2) | 22 | 8 | A bare `return` with no value. |
| `try` (D6) | 24 | 5 | Left refused except around `await`. |
| `localStorage` (D6) | 0 | 22 | GPT invents `import { storage } from "exact2"` and `storage.getItem` (7 features), not Web Storage. |
| A timer declaration (D4) | 6 tag / 5 `every` | 9 / 9 | `Date.now` in the contract is 0 and 1. In `app.ts` it is 16 and 16. |
| `state x = await load()` (D6) | 0 | 0 | `state x = someCall()` is 8 and 11. An assignment of a call inside an action is 16 and 15. |
| `Math.*` in the contract (D1's roster) | 0 | 0 | `min`, `max`, `round`, `floor`, `ceil` are already roster functions (`docs/contract-grammar.md`). |

`and`/`or`/`not` against `&&`/`||`/`!` is a real split, and keeping both is already the language. It is not evidence for a second spelling of anything else. Counts are "at least once per feature," features are slices of the same 24 apps, and `priors2` is another cut of those apps, so there is no held-out set yet. The effective sample is two model families on two dozen specs.

**3. The uncanny valley is already visible in the sketches, and the pass rule rewards compiling the wrong program.**

T4 is the right worry and the proposed test does not catch it. `~/bakeoff/guesses/priors1/gpt/grocery-list-1/app.contract` is the shape GPT writes once tags are "HTML's names": `h1` wrapping a `text` child, `label` wrapping an `input`, `ul`/`li`, `select`/`option`, `disabled=!valid`, `items = addItem(...)`. Muse's matching file writes `text`, `[Item]`, `String`, `draftName.trim()`, `items.isEmpty()`, `items + [Item(...)]`, and `appear=init`. Accepting the overlapping surface (`if`, methods, `null`, `h1`, `await`) makes the rest of those guesses more confident. The rest is already common: `try` 29/80, camelCase style 34/80 (`fontSize` 13 and 15), effects 16/80, spread 8, object literals 6, truthiness 6 on a regex that only recognizes some names.

LLP 1081 exists because a name that looks like the web is read as the web. `contract/lower/src/tags.rs` `html_tag` already applies that lesson: `h1` is refused with the edit `text role="heading" aria-level=…`, `ul` with "`list` (or a `column` of rows)", `form` with the field's `submit`. That is LLP 1116.014's strategy, and it is the right one for a guess that does not mean the same thing. `rules/DEFERRED.md` still refuses a JavaScript UI tier. A Contract that looks like TypeScript and then refuses nullability on non-options, truthiness, `try`, mutation, closures that assign, and effects is that tier's cost without its semantics.

The §7 rule, "walls go down, and no wall the census did not show goes up," can pass while this gets worse. Known walls may rise as long as the total falls, and a wall that disappears because a state-and-`if` screen now compiles is counted as a win. `guesses/priors2/gpt/notes-nav/app.contract` and the Muse twin are a selected note, `if selected != null`, and a Back button the model drew. Exact refuses that because LLP 1115 D5's system Back, the link, and the split view are the quality win against React. A metric that only counts compile walls will prefer the language that lets that sketch through.

**4. Two of the "unchanged" decisions change the semantics that the proofs are about.**

Sugar, as T2 defines it, is real for a view `if` that is today's `when`, a `for` that is today's `each`, and `e.f(args)` that is today's `f(e, args)`. The grammar already has expression arrows (`x => x * 10` on `map` and `filter`), `let`, `if`/`else` in actions, and a `refresh` statement. `contract/types/src/component.rs` already fills an action parameter's type from its event handlers. D2's last bullet is the current checker.

It is not sugar past that.

An action is one transaction. `semantics/Contract/Axiomatic.lean` states the laws: reads see the pre-state, a refused action writes nothing, the body runs to the sends and the writes, and those commit together. `semantics/Contract/Runtime.lean` then arms a `then` only for a mutation that was answered, and runs it on a later clock advance. `await` in the middle of an action is two transactions. That can be an expander lowering to `send` plus a `then` action, with locals passed as captured parameters the way LLP 1089 lifts a call. The proofs stay valid only if the lowered program is that program. The RFC's own reading of it is not. "After the `await`, state reads see the state when the reply lands" is the later transaction. "Without `try`, the failure is the action's, as today" does not match today: the prefix has already committed before the reply exists, and a failed reply does not roll it back. There is no author-written failure continuation to lower `catch` into. `failure(name)` observes a resource (`docs/contract-grammar.md`). `try` in 29 features is a new control feature.

`state items = await loadItems()` was written in 0 of 80 features, and it makes a resource look like a slot. The grocery sketches then assign `items = addItem(...)`. That assignment is the guess D6 does not accept, so the wall moves rather than disappears.

D7 reverses a decision that was deliberate. LLP 1116 D5 persists settings only, refuses records and lists of records, caps a value at 16 KB, and stores it where no grant can name it (`exact.state.<name>`). Fourteen of 24 apps keeping records on the device is a product fact. It is not a reason to decode a journal field by field into `""`, `0`, `false`, `[]`, or `null`. A missing `id` then becomes `""` and the `key` collapses. An extra field dropped on a forward write is the user's data deleted by an update or a rollback. The 294 ms figure is not this store. LLP 1027.003's table is a whole-record comparison of a Messages prototype at 25,000 rows on an M5. It says nothing about a 1 MB JSON decode on the oldest iPhone before the first frame. RULES budgets that frame at 100 ms p50, and today's read is synchronous, on the boot path. IndexedDB cannot serve "read before the first frame." A 1 MB cap usually fits in `localStorage` anyway, so the second web engine is a case the cap forbids. A whole-file write on every commit, unspecified as to atomic replace and coalescing, is how a crash corrupts the only copy of a journal.

**5. The larger lever is the one the RFC defers, and the cheap experiment is the one it skips.**

For a GPT build the doc topics that remain after this RFC, on its own table, are tooling and tests at 0.60 MB-steps and the look at 0.30. Expressions are 0.20. LLP 1116.014 already concluded that most of what builders fetch is Exact's protocol (tests, kept values, data, composition, timers, signatures), and that synonyms are a few percent. This RFC promotes the synonyms to a language migration and leaves the largest topic to a sequel.

The experiment that matches the thesis is smaller than any of D1–D7. Rewrite only the guide section a decision would delete, rerun the census and a doc-byte accounting on the existing round logs, and see whether the walls move. LLP 1116.013's stage 1 was that experiment for packaging, and it failed closed. The same instrument can price `if`/`for` in a day, without a fleet-wide conflict.

## 3. Per decision

**D1. Change.** Method calls on the existing roster (`trim`, `length`, `filter`, `map`, `isEmpty`, `toLowerCase`, and the rest of the grammar's table) are a parser rewrite to `f(e, args)`, and `.length` as a checker rewrite is justified by the record-field ambiguity the RFC names. A type ascription on `state` is `Expr.typed` and matches 21 and 24 features. Keep inference, so the ascription stays optional. That does reverse LLP 1116.014's "drop the type" diagnostic, which is a few days old, so the fixture and the message have to move together.

Drop the new roster from this decision. `find` / `reduce` / `toSorted` are real Lean and difftest work, `reduce`'s accumulator is where inference grows a new wall, and `Math.*` appears in 0 contract sketches. `min`, `max`, `round`, `floor`, and `ceil` already exist, and JavaScript's variadic `Math.min()` with no arguments returns `Infinity`, which this language does not need. Drop `null`, `??`, `?.`, and narrowing. The value `null` is common (16 and 13). The operators that would replace `match` are not (6 and 4), `main1` writes `match` once taught (23 and 20), and narrowing is the piece that `semantics/Contract/Types.lean` and `TypeCheck.lean` would have to re-prove. A non-option remaining non-null is a standing wall that gets more confident if `null` becomes the spelling. `T[]` as sugar for `list<T>` is fine. It does not cover Muse's `[T]`, `String`, or `->`, which are the larger type wall.

**D2. Keep, narrowed.** A view `if` is the action `if` the grammar already has, and a view `for` is `each`. Both models write them, and neither writes `when` or `each` unless taught. Keep `key` required. Muse omits it in 27 of 40, and an `id` field is not an identity (LLP 1116.014). The existing "name the `id` field" diagnostic is the right companion.

Leave `return`, `const`, and block-bodied `fn`s out of the deletion wave. `return` is 22 against 8 and lowers cleanly only while the body is an `if` whose suffix becomes `else`. A `fn` body lowers to `letE` and `ternary` only while it is pure. Muse's block `fn`s and `fn loadItems() -> [Item]` signatures are a different construct. `const` as a second spelling of `let` breaks T3 for a census that did not ask for it. Untyped action parameters already infer from handlers.

**D3. Drop.** The 40/4 split is what the one-kilobyte guide produces by saying tags are HTML's names, while also teaching `text "…"`. Both models write `text` in every feature. The proposed form `h1 "Groceries"` occurs in none. GPT nests `text` under `h1` and nests the `input` under `label`. Lowering that `label` to `aria-labelledby`, or that `ul` to the platform `list`, compiles a different UI than the sketch. start-here is explicit that a `list`'s rows draw no separator and no press. `img`/`image` and `a`/`link` are the only renames with a single lowering, and they are not separately supported by the census. `form` staying refused is correct. CSS kebab-case over `fontSize` is correct under LLP 1115, and it is the wall D3 makes more expensive: HTML tags plus TypeScript expressions plus React Native's style names is the valley T5 warns about.

**D4. Drop.** Fifteen features sketch some timer wording. None write `task`, and almost none write `Date.now()` in the contract. The clock guess is in `app.ts` (16 and 16), and T1 forbids the data module from seeing the clock. `task … mount` is the mount and timer primitive the corpus and the benches use (`contract/corpus/motion.contract`, the list benches). Replacing it with `timer every= when= tick=` adds a declaration models still have to be shown, and it leaves the 16 `effect` / `on mount` sketches refused with no remaining mount spelling. `now()` is already refused with both repairs (`type-now-renamed`).

**D5. Change.** Keep routes. A second screen in the targeted sketches is a selected-value `if` and an authored Back button (`priors2`, `notes-nav`, both models). priors1 has 0 `navigate` / `push` / `goto` calls. `screen note` is a new construct to learn, in the same class as the route boilerplate it replaces, and it is not the guess. Accepting the guess drops LLP 1115's Back, link, and split view, which is the fidelity this series wins on.

A `dialog open=<bool>` that lowers onto the existing modal route is the one piece worth a separate trial, because a sheet bound to a bool is the sketch and HTML's `open`. It needs an explicit rule for who writes the bool when the host swipes the sheet down, so the bool and the route cannot diverge. `command="show-modal"` can stay. That is two spellings for a reason T3 already allows: one of them is HTML's own and has no state.

**D6. Change.** Generating the `Sources` map and `answer` from plain exported functions is the decision that matches the census (38 and 37, and 0 `Sources` maps) and the topic that dominates §3. Keep grants as the boundary. Keep the contract's calls implicit. Requiring `import` adds a wall Muse hits in 16 of 40 features, and `main1` writes 0 imports once the current rule is taught.

Drop `await` inside an action, `try`/`catch`, and `state x = await …` until a difftest corpus shows the lowering. The corpus has to pin one reply, a failed reply whose prefix stays committed, two sequential replies with the first value flowing into the second, a queued send from another action between them, and a `refresh` of something the continuation reads. Drop ungranted `localStorage`. It matches Muse (22) and misses GPT's invented `exact2` storage module (0 `localStorage`). persist's grant exemption is a key grants cannot name, not a general string store in the data module. A second persistence API beside `persist` and `storage.fs` is another spelling of the same job.

**D7. Drop from this RFC.** It is not a guess (`persist` appears once in priors1). It reopens LLP 1116 D5, which sent collections to a source on review. If D6's generated functions measure well, the RFC already suspects D7 is unnecessary. If on-device records are the product goal, the one-line `persist` guide is the thing to census first, and the storage design (atomic write, no silent defaults for a missing `id`, a measured cold-start curve, the same key isolation grants have today) comes before any decoder.

## 4. Answers to §9

**1. The bound, and whether half of GPT's gap is worth the rewrite.** The 17% figure is a ceiling on three doc topics, and it is not recoverable by these edits. S1 is the expressions row, about 0.05M tokens on a 1.64M GPT build, below the A/B's noise. The data row is most of the ceiling and survives as grants, loading, failure, and time. Half of GPT's paired 33% gap would be worth a lot. This program does not buy that half. A rewrite of about 450 sources and 670 KB of docs is the wrong price for the part the census actually supports. `if` and `for`, plus method sugar, can land as their own measured commits because they lower to the AST `contract lean` already emits.

**2. Does a TypeScript-looking language invite more refused TypeScript than it saves?** Yes, on the evidence already in the census. The unguided sketches contain `try`, camelCase style, effects, `String`/`[T]`/`->`, spread, object literals, label-wrapped inputs, and assignment of data calls, at rates comparable to the constructs D1–D3 would legalize. `main1` shows the current, less familiar spellings drive those rates to about zero when the guide shows them. Familiar syntax removes the need to show `when` and `each`. It increases the need to show every JavaScript feature Exact will still refuse. T4's "total walls fell" test will not see that trade. Per-model counts of the refused forms have to be the gate, and they have to be measured before any old spelling is deleted.

**3. `null` and narrowing, or `none` and `match`?** Keep `none` and `match`. Spelling the value `null` without TypeScript's null behavior is the valley, `??` and `?.` are 10 features out of 80, and narrowing is a change to the checker that `Types.lean` mirrors and `Soundness.lean` proves. A purely local rewrite of `x == null` to `match` is the most that stays sugar, and it is not worth a second spelling under T3.

**4. Should a pushed screen be an option state?** No. The sketches write that because a bool and an `if` are what the one-kilobyte guide makes available. The platform answer is a route with system Back (LLP 1115 D5). `screen for=selected` gives the guess a URL-less name and drops the deep link and the split view. Teach a shorter route, if a shorter route can be shown to cost fewer doc bytes. Do not teach a second navigation model.

**5. Is `await` sound, and is `state = await` the right resource?** A single `await` whose continuation is a `then` action can be sound, because that is the runtime's existing split, including queued sends and a declared `refreshes`. It is sound as two transactions, with the prefix committed before the reply. It is not "the action fails" when the reply fails, and `try`/`catch` has no existing failure continuation to lower to. Two `await`s are a nested `then` that difftest has to pin before the surface exists. `state items = await loadItems()` is the wrong spelling: nobody wrote it, and the sketches assign to that state afterwards. A resource should keep a name that is not a slot.

**6. Field-by-field decoding, 1 MB before the first frame, and whether D6 makes D7 unnecessary.** Field-by-field defaults are not enough to keep a user's records. They turn a missing field into a value that looks real, and a type change still drops the whole value. A 1 MB synchronous decode is an unmeasured claim against the 100 ms cold start. The cap has to come from that measurement. The 294 ms number is a different system. D7 is unnecessary in this RFC either way: if generated `app.ts` functions work, they are the path the sketches already invent; if they do not, record persistence is its own design, with atomic writes and an explicit migration, judged on data loss.

**7. What the RFC does not see.** The guide contaminates D3. The intersection of the two models is much smaller than the union T3 would delete. `main1` is the control and shows the current language is learnable in one read. Several proposals are already true or already diagnosed (`html_tag`, untyped action parameters, `refresh`, `min`/`max`/`round`). Tooling at 0.60 MB-steps is a larger GPT topic than expressions, screens, and the look's smaller neighbors. The quality risk is asymmetric: Exact's grades come from platform behavior the census treats as a wall. Model drift cuts against a TypeScript-shaped subset, because the next models will know more JavaScript, not less, and `rules/DEFERRED.md` still forbids making that knowledge true. The durable alternative is the small sugar set, plain exported functions with the current runtime protocol generated underneath, and diagnostics that name the edit for everything T1 and LLP 1115 require to stay different.

## 5. What to measure before deciding

Measure these on the existing logs and the census harness, before a compiler change and before any spelling is deleted.

- **Bytes a decision actually deletes.** For each candidate, cut only that topic out of start-here and the card, and recompute MB-steps on the round 13, 14, 15, and A-arm logs. Report the residual of the data row after grants, SQLite, failure, and the clock are left in. Expect the `if`/`for`/method cut to land near the expressions row's 0.20 and 0.31, and treat anything short of the A/B's noise band as a language improvement judged on repair steps and grader scores.
- **A census of the intersection only.** Guide of one kilobyte, then the rewritten guide, same 40 features plus a held-out set from apps outside the 24. Accept `if`, `for` with `key` still required, state ascriptions, and method calls on the current roster. Count walls by diagnostic id, per model. The gate is that `try`, camelCase style, truthiness, effects, `: String`, object literals, and assignment to a resource do not rise.
- **Power of the A/B.** Eight specs did not resolve a 3% token change in LLP 1116.013. Pre-register the smallest effect that would count as "beyond the noise," per builder. If the predicted effect is smaller, do not use total tokens as the keep rule for that stage. Use repair steps, fresh tokens, and feature scores, and run one decision per comparison so a regression has a cause.
- **The data ceremony, separately.** How many of the 0.49 and 1.21 MB-steps are the `Sources` / `answer` shape, against grants and SQL. That number is the only token case for touching `app.ts`.
- **One line of `persist` in the guide,** on the keep-on-device features only, before any record decoder. The question is whether models stop inventing `localStorage` and `storage.getItem` when `state entries = [] persist` is visible. That is the D7 census the RFC notes was never run.
- **Cold start, if record persistence is still wanted after that.** Bytes of JSON against time to first frame on the slowest iPhone in the fleet, read on the boot path, including decode. Choose the cap from the 100 ms budget. Alongside it, a crash case for a whole-file write and a shape-change case where a missing `id` must not become `""`.
