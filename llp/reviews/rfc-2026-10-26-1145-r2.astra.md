1. **D2’s download-versus-decode diagnosis omits delays central to scroll jank.** An image can wait for admission or memory, or finish decoding promptly and then wait for the main thread. The existing loader explicitly [queues delivery onto the main thread](/Users/admin/.config/tuft/sessions/1792950840.054969/exact2/host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/RasterLoader.swift:294). Neither download time nor decode duration identifies that last delay. On the web, [Resource Timing](https://w3c.github.io/resource-timing/#sec-performanceresourcetiming) measures fetching; it does not establish when the image decoded or painted.

   This matters because D2 is the evidence used to choose scheduling changes versus earlier prefetch. A report that attributes admission delay to downloading, or delivery delay to decoding, can recommend work that leaves the visible problem untouched.

   **Instead:** distinguish waiting for admission, fetching, waiting for decode/budget, decoding, and waiting for delivery. Record the relevant boundaries and retain these categories in the fling report. Treat layer assignment as a delivery timestamp, with presentation latency explicitly unmeasured; report unavailable web timings as unknown. This needs a small timing record, not a trace or devtools UI.
