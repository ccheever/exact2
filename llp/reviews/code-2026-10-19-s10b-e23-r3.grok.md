## The five checks

**(1) Fallback.** A candidate is planned as literals, then sent only to `dials` (`tamber/src/export.rs:1366-1370`). Disagreement, a `set-dial` refused alone, and dials refused together all `fall` to those literals (`export_dials.rs:186-248`). An adopted dial drops its literals. `kept` counts a dial already at that value and drops them too. `mapped` is `kept` plus each adopted dial's `from` plus one per accepted entry, including a fallback (`export.rs:1381-1402`). A token is not counted twice, and a literal is not proposed beside its dial. If the literals themselves fail, the token is reported, with both reasons. Two consumers and no third: the second pin is reported `tamber-dial-inert`. That path is untested and behaves as it should.

**(2) Baseline.** A forged body that passes the digest and the full context and axes check cannot hide a revert or apply an operation absent from the review. Token values in that body can add operations; those operations are in the held proposal. That is the bound in the ruling. An unknown digest and a structure mismatch are both 409 `stale: true`. A baseline that passes can still be expensive to diff, in the same way `source` already can; that walk predates these three commits and does not choose a write the review omits.

**(3) `import-plan {marks}`.** A client cannot call it. `import-plan` is not in `METHOD_SCOPE` (`scripts/tamber-pairing.mjs:30-59`), so the wire answers 400 "has no scope" (`tamber-server.mjs:652`). `importHeld` builds its own request and does not forward `marks` (`1105`). The only caller is `remark` during accept, and it sends the held operations (`994`). The handler reads the current design text plus those operations (`tamber_import.rs:163-171`): no baseline and no path. Cost is one check plus one apply-and-check per `set-dial`, bounded by the proposal already held. The colours it returns are the same ones `export` already shows a reader.

The proposal is removed before the recompute (`968`) and put back on every failure of that path: `remark` returning an error, `hold` returning an error (`replaced`, `980-983`), and a throw (`1001`). `marked` rebuilds `items` from the kept operations after the fresh marks (`1455-1459`); each dial keeps `from` by name. A revert has `review.revert` and no `dials` or `pins`, so it is planned again from the commit. A shadcn or Tailwind review omits those lists, so it does not remark. A client can attach its own lists on `propose` and a later partial accept will remark; the operations remarked are the held ones.

**(4) Pins on a new `when`.** A non-empty `when` that matches no line takes `branch` per tuple and per scheme side (`export_dials.rs:286-292`, `export.rs:1425-1448`). The light side skips axes under `scheme=dark`. The test writes `day` on black and `night` on dim, and the light side stays `day`. Names compare in any ASCII case. The empty-`when` over-report and the bare role alias are the two gaps.

**(5) Tests.** They prove the third-consumer fallback, a swapped context map refused before planning, the under-dark new `when`, hex case agreement, an explicit baseline and foreign axes, and on the wire the 409, a partial accept that keeps `from` and refreshes impact tokens, and a review whose `items` are not indices. They do not prove: a default-line pin beside another `when`; `#F00` against `red`, or hex against `color(srgb 1 0 0)`; `Canvas` against `canvas`, or a bare role; a `set-dial` the checker refuses; a recognised digest whose only forge is token values; restoring the proposal when `remark` itself fails; exact impact CSS; or `surface.raised` staying out of the operations.
