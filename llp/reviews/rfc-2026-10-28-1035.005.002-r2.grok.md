## Q2

**Keep `and`, `or`, and `not`. Delete `&&`, `||`, and `!` in the same commit.** Confidence: 77.

Both spellings are one precedence and one `BinOp` (`contract/syntax/src/parser/expr.rs:103–106`, `:146`). The repo writes the words. LLP 1116.015 measured a respelling as level, and LLP 1116.016 §7 measured that telling builders the expressions are TypeScript's raised other refusals. `and` is not a TypeScript expression, so it stays a deviation row under 1116.016 D1, the way `==` on lists already is. LLP 1017 §8.1 ruled CSS attribute names. The operator words are Sol's arm in §9 and the corpus, not a separate ruling.

The strongest case for the symbols is that D1's direction is TypeScript's spelling, Astra's r1 review held the duplicate spelling as a real removed exception, and an unguided model emits `&&`. Refusing it adds a first-screen wall the census cannot see while both spellings work.

This flips if a fresh one-shot corpus, shown a guide that teaches neither spelling, writes the symbols and does not repair them in one edit once the diagnostic names `and`.

Under "Delete; don't deprecate" (`rules/RULES.md:48`) the symbol arm is a flag day with no shim and no leftover alias. The parser refuses `&&`, `||`, and `!` with the edit. Meaning does not change, and the plan, both executors, and Lean do not change. The landing rewrites the few real expression lines, the grammar, and any out-of-repo app that used the symbols, in that commit. A syntax-aware rewrite has to leave `not` alone in test expectations (`contract/syntax/src/parser/steps.rs` treats it as an expect word) and leave the symbols inside strings, including the Excord spoiler strings. The word arm would be the same flag day across the ~670 operator lines, the docs, and every external app, which is the cost that makes the symbol choice fail the rule.
