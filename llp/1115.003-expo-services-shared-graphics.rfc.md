## 1. Outcome

The Expo Services app draws its chart, its numeric transitions, its login mark and success zoom, and its custom icons from one authored implementation in Contract and SVG, on iOS, Android and macOS. The iOS app keeps its appearance, motion and frame pacing; each Swift drawing module is deleted when its replacement passes §5. Android's Canvas host paints every gradient those components and the existing screens use.

That is the scope. A shippable Android app is a separate decision, and §7 lists what it needs. The components remain app code: Exact gains only host paint and, if D4's gate requires it, lowering.

## 2. Evidence and references

The app at [`729cd7a`](https://github.com/expo/expo-services-app-ios/tree/729cd7af4c2e07546136242f9ca78d6516da0bca) boots on the Android Canvas host with its Contract and TypeScript unchanged, and reports its native modules missing. Its drawing modules are in [`EasNative.swift`](https://github.com/expo/expo-services-app-ios/blob/729cd7af4c2e07546136242f9ca78d6516da0bca/modules/apple/EasNative.swift): `trend-chart` (Core Animation layers, a `CADisplayLink` morph over a 1,024-sample grid, a pan recognizer the drawer and scroll view wait on), `numeric-text` (SwiftUI `.contentTransition(.numericText)`), `login-mark` and `login-zoom` (each phase sampled at 120 Hz into Core Animation keyframes when it starts), and `sf-symbol`. On macOS these are empty stubs. The app's `GAPS.md` records why the mark left a TypeScript canvas: at 30 to 60 Hz on a busy main thread it repeated frames.

The Expo Android app at [`6214132`](https://github.com/expo/expo-services-app-android/tree/6214132e9a08b877fa1b471b440599fbe70277bf) is the behavioural reference for the same artwork: `login-mark.tsx`, `success-morph.ts`, `success-zoom.ts` and the [login decision](https://github.com/expo/expo-services-app-android/blob/6214132e9a08b877fa1b471b440599fbe70277bf/llp/0001-login-flow-animation.decision.md); `chart.tsx`, `chart-curve.ts`, `use-chart-morph.ts`, `nearest-bucket.ts`; `animated-value.android.tsx`; `icon.tsx`. Its geometry matches the iOS modules' where both exist. Its React Native dependencies are not proposed for Exact. Gyroscope tilt is in neither the iOS app nor this scope.

## 3. Design

### D1. The components are app-local Contract and SVG

`TrendChart`, `NumericValue`, `LoginMark` and `LoginZoom` are Contract components with pure TypeScript geometry beside them (fitting, curve sampling, nearest bucket, the 240-point outlines). They use the session clock, CSS transitions and animations, and pointer events; no second animation graph, no per-frame TypeScript.

Icons: every SF Symbol name the app can reach, including those built in TypeScript for menus, is inventoried. A name with a `symbol:` role becomes the role. A brand or status glyph with no role becomes a shared SVG asset, with its licence checked. `sf-symbol` and `symbol:sf/` go when the inventory is empty. A role draws its web outline on Android until LLP 1115.001 D14 adds Material glyphs; that is D14's work, not this RFC's.

### D2. Android paints every gradient

The Canvas recorder gains one gradient op, used by a box's `background-image` layers and by an SVG fill or stroke ink: geometry (linear, radial, conic as `paint/gradient.rs` resolves them), spread, stops in straight ARGB, and gradient space to local space. The Kotlin reader builds Android's `LinearGradient`, `RadialGradient` or `SweepGradient` shader with that matrix and draws the shape it precedes. Clipping and transforms are already in the resolved scene. A box gradient keeps its first-stop stand-in nowhere.

An SVG `clipPath` on this host is completed if the authored artwork uses one. Masks, filters, patterns and blends (the island module) stay unpainted on Android; the components do not use them, and the recorder says once per kind that it skipped one rather than drawing nothing silently.

No keyframed `d`, no native Canvas 2D executor (LLP 1056 stands), no runtime shaders.

### D3. Each animation's encoding

**Login mark.** Phases (intro, code, success) are state the app sets from its own events; each phase's animations are keyed by that phase, so each starts at its own origin (LLP 1055.002 D3–D4). The mark is nested SVG groups, one eased factor per group, so the Swift's products and sums of curves compose exactly: the lift, settle and resting scales; the two bands' rotations (a fixed 60 × 90 ellipse, entrance sweep plus code sweep plus success sweep); opacity; colour and the check's `stroke-dashoffset` on the shapes that paint them, never on a group. The check-to-logo morph is a `d` transition between the two 240-point outlines, 500 ms after a 900 ms delay on `cubic-bezier(0.77, 0, 0.175, 1)`; at its end the exact logo path replaces the samples. Reduced motion shows each phase's end state.

**Success zoom.** `scale` and `translate` follow the geometric curve as keyframes sampled from it with linear segments (at most 32 stops, denser toward the end, where it grows) or as one `linear()` easing; both lower, and a cubic between samples would leave the curve.

**Encoding for the compositor.** The mark's phases and the zoom are CSS `animation`s. The Apple host lowers them to Core Animation keyframes (`host/apple/src/svg_lower.rs`), which is what the Swift modules do by hand: `translate`, `rotate`, `scale` and `opacity` on a group (`svg.rs`), and `fill`, `stroke` and `stroke-dashoffset` only on a shape, a colour track only when its colours share one alpha. A group that animates a colour or a dash offset is sampled whole, its transform with it. Android's reader plays transform and opacity on a group, and a shape's dash offset when that shape neither turns nor has a gradient stroke. Cubic, `linear` and `linear()` segments all lower. Transitions are sampled on Apple; on Android the reader plays a transition of opacity or transform.

**Handoff.** The iOS rule stands: the root's tick advances `success → handoff → done` at fixed times on the session clock, and session activation is an app event, never a motion callback. The Android decision's layout-and-completion gate is a recorded difference; adopting it would first need an end-of-motion event, which Contract lacks.

**Chart.** Fitting and curve math are shared TypeScript. The data module draws every curve as its 1,025 samples on a fixed grid (`M` and 1,024 `L`), so any two curves are paths of the same commands, and the path's `d` transitions between them over 400 ms on the `gentle` curve; a retarget mid-morph starts from the presented path (CSS Transitions §3, `observe_path`). The chart stays on its samples at rest: at about 0.4 pt a segment they are within antialiasing of the cubic, and swapping back to the cubic would need a second commit timed to the morph's end, which Contract cannot time. On first show the curve rises from the plot's bottom, as the Swift does. The area's fill and the two tails are SVG `linearGradient`s in user space; the line's `stroke` transitions between green and grey with the shape, and the two tinted areas and tails cross-fade on the same timing. The chart stays mounted while a timeframe loads, and an answer for another project is never shown as current.

**Scrub.** As in both references, a drag that starts on the chart scrubs; it neither scrolls nor opens the drawer. Under LLP 1057.001 an ancestor `pan` takes a contact past the slop whatever the hit node's `touch-action` (rule 3), so pointer handlers alone would lose to the drawer. The plot therefore has its own `pan` with `touch-action="none"`: the innermost recognizer wins, so the drawer's never begins, and a scroller yields every axis (rule 2; iOS intersects `touch-action` from the hit view, Android's reader honours the pan's claim). The position is `pointerdown`'s `offsetX` plus the pan's deltas; it picks the original bucket (later bucket on a tie) from a per-sample lookup the data module computes, the handle sits on the curve, a change is reported once per bucket, and release or cancel clears it. Selection is off while loading. No gesture arena and no change to LLP 1057.001.

**Numbers.** One shared transition: each glyph is keyed by its place from the right, a changed glyph enters from below and leaves upward (reversed when counting down) with opacity, on iOS's spring (0.4 s, no bounce), in tabular digits, as one accessible value, scaled to fit at large text. SwiftUI also blurs the moving glyphs; the shared roll blurs them if the host's `filter` animates within budget, and otherwise that blur is the stated iOS difference.

### D4. Playback under load is measured before the logo switches

| Component | Animated | Apple | Android |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mark, zoom | keyframed `transform`, `opacity` on SVG groups | lowered (Core Animation) | lowered (`lower.rs`, `LOWERED`) |
| Mark | keyframed `stroke-dashoffset` on the check's shape | lowered | lowered (the check does not turn) |
| Mark | keyframed `fill`, `stroke` on shapes | lowered where the track's colours share one alpha | sampled |
| Mark, chart | `d` (a transition) | sampled, scene rebuilt | sampled, path re-recorded |
| Chart | a gradient-filled shape | sampled | sampled (a paint-server shape is never lowered) |
| Numbers | glyph `translate`, `opacity` on boxes | as the host plays box motion | as the host plays box motion |

Only the morph's 500 ms and the chart's 400 ms are sampled on Apple, with the mark's colours on Android. The gate: the shared mark and zoom, played on a 120 Hz iPhone through the real post-login load, against the native modules' frame intervals on the same device; and the chart morph during a timeframe fetch. If sampled playback meets the native intervals within the baseline's spread, it ships. If not, lowering the failing properties (Core Animation keyframes on Apple, reader tracks on Android) is the next host stage of this RFC, and the Swift stays until it lands.

## 4. Protecting iOS

The baseline is the app commit, the exact2 commit and `patches/exact2-ios-host.patch` applied to it, recorded with the device and OS. D2's host work keeps the patch applying; upstreaming the patch is separate work. No credentials, sessions or customer data enter fixtures, commits or captures.

Each component is built first as a preview screen fed by fixtures, on iOS, Android and macOS, and is the final implementation from the start. The app then switches one component per change, in this order: chart, numbers, logo and zoom (after D4's gate), icons. Each switch carries its comparison and deletes the Swift it supersedes. A component that fails §5 keeps its Swift, and the gap returns here.

## 6. Decisions, as recorded

1. **Shared artwork, platform presentation** as the end state, no Kotlin copy of the chart or the mark. Yes.
2. **The host work** is D2's gradient op (and SVG clip if used), plus D4's lowering only if the gate fails. Keyframed `d` and a native Canvas 2D executor are out.
3. **References:** the shipping iOS app for iOS behaviour; the Expo Android app for the artwork's geometry and for Android behaviour where iOS is silent. Where they differ (the handoff gate, the numeric effect) r2 keeps iOS and records the difference.
4. **The Android port** beyond these components is §7, a separate decision.
