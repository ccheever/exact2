## 1. The precedence table

A static rule, derived from the tree and its rows. Each host implements it
with its own mechanism. It is not a runtime structure and authors cannot
change it.

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1 | The system's own gestures keep their edges (back swipe, home indicator). An authored `swiperight` yields the first 20 pt from the leading edge (on Android, the system's gesture insets on both sides), and on iOS a `swipe` to the right does not begin within the left 20 pt, where §7's back gestures begin (§8.4). |
| 2 | Scroll wins on every axis `touch-action` leaves to it. `touch-action` intersects from the hit node up through the scroll container, as CSS does. |
| 3 | The innermost node with a matching recognizer wins; its ancestors do not also fire (DOM bubbling, as `press` does). A text input or control between the hit node and an ancestor's recognizer keeps the contact for itself. A `press` handler between keeps it from an ancestor's `swiperight`, `swipe` or drag binding, and from an ancestor's `pan` only within the slop: the pan that begins takes the contact and the press, as a draggable element's pointer listeners hear a drag that starts on a button inside it (kanban F6, 2026-10-04). Two exceptions are `swipe`'s (§8.4): on iOS a finger's `swipe` passes a text input, and a `swipe` on a Mac's trackpad passes a text input, a control and a `press` handler, since it lands on none of them. A `swipe` never holds up a continuous gesture, inner or outer. |
| 4 | On one node, one contact: drag binding (reorder > transform > height) > `pan` > `swiperight` > `swipe` (§8.4) > long press / `contextmenu` > `press`. |
| 5 | Double click follows the web: `press` fires on each click, `dblclick` after the second, with no delay. |
| 6 | Exactly one built-in simultaneity: pinch with pan on a transform binding. It is host-owned; authors cannot request simultaneity. |

**Candidate order.** Rules 3 and 4 together order a contact's candidates:
innermost first, then by rule 4 on one node. When the contact crosses its slop,
the first candidate whose axis rule accepts the movement begins; candidates
before it that refused fall away, and nothing after it runs. A drag that begins
cancels the armed `press`.

| host | mechanism |
|---|---|
| web | independent listeners on each element; bubbling puts the innermost first. A `swiperight`, which does not capture at down, marks its pointer `pending` in `exact.contacts` until it claims or refuses; an ancestor's pan, transform or height drag that would capture waits for that verdict. A recognizer that begins stops propagation and suppresses the click, the pan's included |
| macOS | `NodeView.mouseDown` arms every recognizer (`MouseChainMac.swift`), which orders the armed candidates by depth, then rule 4; `mouseDragged` and `mouseUp` offer the event in that order and the first taker keeps it |
| Linux | `pointer_down` collects the candidates and orders them the same way (`presenter/contact.rs`); at the slop the first that accepts begins |
| iOS | UIKit's recognizers and their delegate predicates (`NodeViewIOS.swift`, `GesturePrecedenceIOS.swift`): `ScrollView.gestureRecognizerShouldBegin` for rule 2; an ancestor's drag `shouldRequireFailureOf` a descendant's (rule 3; never of a pinch, which stays possible with one finger); `shouldReceive` refuses a touch under a nested press or editor to the drags; the pinch pair's `shouldRecognizeSimultaneouslyWith` for rule 6 |

**Rule 3's boundary** holds on every host: a `press` handler, a control or a
text input between the contact and an ancestor's drag keeps the contact from
it, so a drag that starts on a button in a swipe row does not swipe
(web: `pressable` in `motion-glue.js` and the pan's check in `input-glue.js`;
iOS: `pressBoundary`). A `pan` passes a `press` (not a control or text input):
the web's pan neither captures nor prevents the pointer until it begins, AppKit
and Linux arm it under the press, and UIKit's pan receives the touch and
cancels the press when it recognizes. **Rule 4's press** arms on a `pan` node on AppKit too
and fires when the pan never leaves the slop; a pan that begins cancels it.

**Declared.** On iOS the order of recognizers on *one* view that cross their
thresholds in the same touch event is UIKit's and undocumented, so rule 4's
tie order on a single node is intended, not enforced; across nodes rule 3 is a
failure requirement. iOS arbitration is checked by unit tests that set
recognizer phases and by delegate predicates. Those tests do not establish
real-touch scroll or long-press arbitration.

**UIKit press forwarding, issue [408](https://github.com/ccheever/exact2/issues/408),
2026-10-10.** A plain text hit beneath an Exact-inserted horizontal
`ScrollView` must reach its authored ancestor's press. On down, a non-canvas
view captures each responder hop across that wrapper; moves, end and cancel
follow the captured contact. Weak responder identity, incarnation and the
downstream contact record prevent delivery to a replacement view or contact.
Retirement, reparenting, disable and inertness cancel armed feedback. A canvas
receives every touch directly in all four phases, without the single-press
contact gate, so its input can track multiple fingers. A child hit inside a
surface control follows its captured responder hop for move, up and cancel,
even though the control's canvas path keeps no press contact. A touch on an overflow child of a surface control is forwarded as one contact; a second finger on that child is not tracked. The original hit,
recognizers, scroll policy and geometry stay UIKit's.

The touched node makes the single `focusForPress` decision: a plain button
takes no touch focus, a descendant that asked for it keeps it, and
`retainFocus` preserves editing. Press acceptance resolves before focus
callbacks can move the owner; a callback that retires the hit cancels the
armed ancestor before activation. Unhandled ground reaches the session's
blur path.

**Acceptance.** `PressFocusIOSTests` covers the scrolled text hit, forwarding
across two wrappers, cancellation and late callbacks, replacement contacts,
retirement and reparenting, nearer and inline handlers, focus and blank ground.
`NodePoolIOSTests` covers a retired forwarding row. `SurfaceControlIOSTests`
covers two canvas fingers through separate downs, moves and ups without cancel,
and a surface control's child hit through move, up and cancel.
The normal iPad fixture accepted gutter and text taps, horizontal and vertical
scroll without a press, a text tap after scrolling, long-press preview and
Detail, and a popover with its focused editor. The unchanged web fixture
accepted gutter and text clicks, a drag without activation, context preview
and Detail. Physical hardware, other iOS versions and normal UI nested-handler
counters remain unaccepted.

**Evidence.** `bun scripts/smoke.mjs <host>` step 11b drives
`contract/corpus/precedence.contract` (a swipe row with a button inside a
panning surface) on raw input: horizontal drag, vertical drag, a drag from the
button, a tap on it. Charlie approved this case as apparatus (2026-09-27).

## 2. `touch-action`: CSS's whole vocabulary

`TouchAction` in `kernel/tables/schema.json` is CSS's grammar, `auto | none |
[ [ pan-x | pan-left | pan-right ] || [ pan-y | pan-up | pan-down ] ||
pinch-zoom ] | manipulation`, in CSS's canonical serialization order: 34
values. The first 13 keep their wire bytes; `pinch-zoom` and the multi-value
forms follow. An author writes the canonical order (`pan-y pinch-zoom`, not
`pinch-zoom pan-y`); the compiler's diagnostic lists every value.

`pinch-zoom` is the browser's zoom, not the app's pinch (Astra's review): a
value that allows it leaves a two-finger pinch to the platform, and an app
pinch (§4) needs the handle's effective value to exclude it. `TouchAction::
pinch_zoom()` answers that (`auto`, `manipulation`, or a value naming
`pinch-zoom`); `TouchAction::pans()` drops `pinch-zoom` for the hosts whose pan
rules match whole values (Linux's `swiperight` admission).

| host | what it does with the row |
|---|---|
| web | emits it as CSS; the browser decides |
| iOS | `NodeView.allowsTouchPan` splits the value into its pan tokens, so the scroll view's intersection (rule 2) ignores `pinch-zoom`; the transform pinch recognizer begins only where a node from the hit view up excludes `pinch-zoom` (§4) |
| macOS | `swiperight` admission reads the pan tokens; trackpad magnify is not a touch and is not governed by the row (as the browser's `ctrlKey` wheel is not) |
| Linux | `swiperight` admission and the retained region's `swiperight` policy match `pans()` |

## 3. One velocity estimator, one threshold table

**Velocity.** `VelocityTracker` (recency-weighted least squares over 100 ms,
`motion/src/velocity.rs`) is the estimator wherever the platform measures
none. The engine keeps one per live hold, fed by every value the hold is given
(`begin_*`, `update_hold`, `update_transform_hold`), and answers
`Engine::hold_velocity(token, now)`; an ended or replaced hold has none.

| path | velocity |
|---|---|
| transform pair, web and Apple | the engine's: op 13's three velocity slots must be zero, the host fills `TransformRelease`'s `vx, vy, vscale` from `hold_velocity`, and the reply carries them as `velocity` for the two hold ends. Swift's `TransformDragVelocity` and the JS endpoint estimator are gone. |
| `swiperight`, web | motion op 10 (`release-measured`) releases at the engine's estimate; the JS estimator is gone |
| `swiperight`, macOS | `exact_hold_end(…, cancel: 2, …)` releases at the engine's estimate; `MouseSwipe`'s last-sample difference is gone |
| `swiperight`, iOS | UIKit's pan velocity, times the resistance past the knee (the platform measures it) |
| Linux | `VelocityTracker` on the contact's hold, as before |

**Constrained displays.** The height binding's display is CSS-clamped
(`min-height`/`max-height`), so the held value can outrun what is shown. The
host records the shown height into the same hold with `Engine::track_hold`
(web: motion op 12 after each move, at the move's instant; Apple: after each
update's layout, from `height_catch`), and `VelocityTracker::push` lets a
sample at the same instant replace the held one. The release then uses the
engine's estimate: web op 9's velocity slot must be zero, and
`exact_height_drag_release` lost its velocity argument. Swift's
`HeightDragVelocity` and the JS height estimator are gone. Web Arrange's
source row releases at the engine's estimate over its hold (the packet's
velocity slots must be zero); Apple Arrange keeps UIKit's or its own and
Linux its contact tracker (`QUEUE.md`).

**Thresholds.** `exact_motion::gesture` holds the values exact2 defines
itself: `SWIPE_KNEE` 64, `SWIPE_RESISTANCE` 0.2, `SWIPE_EDGE` 20, `SLOP` 4,
and `swipe_displacement`. Linux calls them; Apple reads `exact_gesture_constant(i)`
(`Gesture.knee` etc.); the web asks motion op 11 (`gesture`) once. One slop,
4 px, on AppKit, Linux and the web's drags (swipe, height, transform; they
were 8); the web's `pan` keeps the literal because a pan-only plan links no
motion export to ask (LLP 1047). UIKit's pans keep their own hysteresis.

## 4. Pinch on the photo binding

No new Contract surface: `transformDragFor` admits a two-finger pinch where
the host has one, and `transformrelease(x, y, scale, vx, vy, vscale)` already
carries it. It lives inside the existing paired hold (`begin_transform_hold`
and the 120-byte v2 wire): the pair's move carries a scale, and nothing else
changed in the engine or the runner.

**Focal point.** Every sample is the pair anchored at the contact's focal
point (the pinch centroid, the pointer for a trackpad): with the anchor's
value `(x₀, y₀, s₀)`, focal point `F₀` and the current `F`, both relative to
the clip's centre, and the pinch factor `k` since the anchor,

    x = Fx − k·(F₀x − x₀),  y = Fy − k·(F₀y − y₀),  s = k·s₀

so the content under the fingers stays under them and translate follows the
centroid (`k = 1` is a pan). The anchor moves whenever the contact changes:
a recognizer starts or stops, a finger lands or lifts. That absorbs UIKit's
centroid jump and lets a lifted pinch pan on. `TransformDragPosition.focused`
(Swift) and `position` in `motion-glue.js` are the one formula.

**One release.** Whatever mix of pan and pinch the contact was, it ends once:
the terminal sample and time are preflighted whole, the action runs while both
tokens are live, then each surviving original token ends (LLP 1002 D4). The
terminal payload stays finite (the host refuses before the action otherwise);
`vscale` is the engine's measured scale velocity (§3), no longer zero.

**Bounds.** The binding has none of its own; the consumer's release action
clamps. interaction-gallery's `releasePhoto` adopts the released scale held
between Fit (1) and 4× and clamps pan against that zoom; its Fit, 2× and
Center buttons stay the accessible alternative that needs no gesture.

| host | recognition |
|---|---|
| web | `motion-glue.js`'s transform path: up to two pointers (Pointer Events; the handle's `touch-action` must exclude `pinch-zoom` or the browser takes the pinch), a trackpad's `ctrl`+`wheel` (Chromium, Firefox; the listener is not passive, so the page does not zoom; the pinch ends 150 ms after the last wheel) and Safari's `gesturestart`/`gesturechange`/`gestureend`; while the photo is zoomed (`scale > 1`) a plain wheel over the handle pans it, as on macOS, ending 150 ms after the last wheel; unzoomed it stays the page's scroll |
| iOS | a `UIPinchGestureRecognizer` beside the binding's pan, which now takes two fingers; `shouldRecognizeSimultaneouslyWith` admits exactly that pair (rule 6); the pinch begins only where a node from the handle up excludes `pinch-zoom` (§2) |
| macOS | `NodeView.magnify(with:)` pinches about the pointer (no `NSMagnificationGestureRecognizer`); while the photo is zoomed (`scale > 1`) a phased two-finger scroll over the handle pans it, as Preview does, and its momentum is swallowed (no decay driver); unzoomed, the scroll stays the scroll view's |
| Linux | none: evdev tracks no multitouch slots and no touchpad gestures (`host/linux/src/input.rs`). Authored zoom controls are the path; the agent's `pinch` answers `unsupported` |

Catching a pair reads the layer's model transform unless Core Animation is
running a curve on it: the render tree's copy can lag the last presented
frame, and a pinch caught right after another read the scale before it.

## 5. The agent: `tap <target> pinch <scale> [at <x> <y>]`

A form of `tap` (DEFERRED §Agent API), not a ninth operation. The fingers
spread from `d` to `d·scale` about the target's middle, or about `at`
(offset from its top-left), over eight steps.

| carrier | delivery |
|---|---|
| web | `platform`: two CDP touch points, injected without touch emulation so the page's `(pointer)` and `(hover)` stay the device's (Charlie, 2026-10-07) |
| macOS | `platform`: magnify events (began, changed ×8, ended) built from a gesture `CGEvent`'s zoom fields (110, 113, 132; AppKit has no public constructor) and sent to the view under the point, as the wheel form is |
| iOS | `recognized`: UIKit synthesizes no touch, so the recognized scale goes through the same paired hold (`TransformDragHold.recognizedPinch`) |
| Linux | `unsupported` |

LLP 1035.003 D1's one contact per session stands; a pinch is one `tap`, not
two contacts. The macOS carrier also carries `tap … dblclick` now, as two real
clicks, the second with `clickCount` 2.

## 6. `panrelease`: a pan's release velocity

Phase 2's item, built 2026-09-28 for its consumer, `apps/spark` (a card
dragged by `pan` and flung by its throw; Charlie approved it,
`rules/DEFERRED.md` §Motion). `panrelease=action` appends `(vx, vy)`, the
release velocity in viewport CSS pixels per second, pan's units over time.
It completes `pan`; it is not a phase, and the action commits state as a
`pan` does (LLP 1043.000 D8). No hold, no spring, no engine value.

**When.** Once per contact, and only for a pan that *began* (crossed its
recognition: the 4 px slop, or UIKit's own), after that contact's last `pan`
delta. A contact that never began releases nothing: it is a tap, and rule
4's `press` on the same node fires instead. A node without a `panrelease`
handler is sent nothing; `panrelease` without `pan` never fires.

**Cancel.** A contact cancelled after it began releases at rest, `(0, 0)`:
the app always hears the end of what it started, and a card springs back.
Cancelled means `pointercancel` or a lost capture on the web, UIKit's
`.cancelled`/`.failed`, Escape or the window resigning key on AppKit, and
Linux's `pointer_cancel` (Escape, wheel takeover, disconnection). A node
that is gone or no longer live is sent nothing.

**Velocity** is §3's rule: the platform's where it measures one, the one
estimator elsewhere.

| host | velocity |
|---|---|
| web | the pan's pointer samples, each at its event's own `timeStamp` (so a driven drag under the agent's clock still has a real velocity), go to motion ops 13 (`pan-sample`) and 14 (`pan-velocity`), a `VelocityTracker` in the wasm (`host/web/src/pan_velocity.rs`); ABI kind 28 delivers it. A plan with `panrelease` uses motion (`runner/src/uses.rs`), so the export is linked (LLP 1047 stage 3) |
| iOS | UIKit's `velocity(in:)` at `.ended` |
| macOS | the mouse chain's samples at their `NSEvent` timestamps into a `VelocityTracker` behind the Apple ABI; no Swift estimator |
| Linux | the contact's `VelocityTracker`, fed every sample since down |

**Agent.** No new operation: the contact phases end a pan, `tap <t> down`,
`tap move by <dx> <dy> over <ms>`, `tap up` (or `tap cancel`). Web and
macOS deliver them as platform input, Linux through its presenter; on iOS,
where UIKit synthesizes no touch, a contact on a `pan` node is delivered
`recognized` through the same pan path, its release velocity the tracker's
over the synthesized samples.

**A pause before lifting releases at rest.** The tracker weighs only the
last 100 ms (§3), so a finger that stops and then lifts has no throw left;
UIKit's `velocity(in:)` at `.ended` says the same. That is kept on purpose.
What a driver must not do is invent the pause: the web carrier stamps each
touch event with the contact's own timeline (CDP `timestamp`), so a `tap
up` lands one frame after the last move however long the driver took
between operations, and `tap hold <ms>` is how a pause is said
(`host/web/tests/pan-release-engine.test.mjs`, the real wasm, nothing mocked).


## 7. A swipe beside the back gesture on iOS (2026-10-04, Signal Clone)

A tester found the clone "hard to swipe back sometimes". With HID touches on
the iOS 27 simulator, a back swipe from the edge of a chat became a reply,
and others did nothing. Three fixes:
- **A `swiperight` row judges the edge where its finger landed.** It records
  its first touch in `gestureRecognizer(_:shouldReceive:)`, as the pop
  recognizers already did (LLP 1080.000 §11). The pan's location less its
  translation at `shouldBegin` leaves out the travel before recognition: a
  swipe from x = 2 read as starting at about 30, past the 20-point edge.
- **A `swiperight` row waits for the screen-edge pop to fail**
  (`interactivePopGestureRecognizer`), as UIKit's swipe actions and Signal's
  swipe to reply do: from the edge, back wins. Only that recognizer. iOS
  26's content pop is the same class and already waits on the swipe; waiting
  on it in turn left neither able to begin.
- **The pop's direction rule takes no motion as no evidence against.** UIKit's
  pop recognizers asked `shouldBegin` with zero velocity and zero
  translation, and `velocity.x > |velocity.y|` refused every such back swipe.
  Zero velocity now falls back to the travel so far, and a zero vector is
  allowed (still refused past the edge over a `swiperight` node, and over a
  canvas). UIKit's pop recognizers judge the direction themselves: vertical
  drags from x = 10 and x = 150 on a pushed screen without `swiperight`
  rows scrolled and did not pop (simulator, HID).

Proofs: `NavigationRulesTests.testAPanYieldsToASwipeRightNodePastTheEdge`
(the zero vector, and `NavigationRules.popDirection`'s fallback to travel),
`GesturePrecedenceIOSTests.testASwipeWaitsForTheEdgePopOnly`, and HID drives
on the clone (iOS 27 only; iOS 18's screen-edge path is not yet driven): edge swipes from x = 4, 12
and 30 over a transcript go back, a swipe from x = 60 on a bubble replies,
vertical drags scroll.


## 8. `swipe`: a discrete swipe in the directions a node names

Its consumer is the composer bar of the chat template (`chat/` in
github.com/expo/exact-templates): a quick flick down on the bar, the field in
it included, ends editing, and the keyboard goes down at once on its own
animation. `swiperight` is not that: it is a drag that follows the finger past
a knee and releases, and only to the right. `swipe` is the platform's discrete
recognizer: nothing follows the finger; the action runs once, when the
platform says a swipe happened.

```
column id="composer-bar" swipe=hideKeys swipe-direction="down"
  …
  row id="composer-pill"
    textarea id="composer" field-sizing="content" max-height=…
    …
action hideKeys
  blur("composer")
```

The bar is the last child of the conversation's column, under the transcript
and inside no scroller; its field grows with its text to a maximum and then
scrolls. A flick down that starts on the bar, or on the field while its text
fits, hides the keyboard. One that starts on the field while its text
overflows scrolls the field, as any scroller wins (§8.4).

### 8.1 Surface

- `swipe=action` runs once per contact, when the platform recognizes a swipe
  in one of the node's directions. The action may take a `SwipeEvent {
  direction: string }`: `"left"`, `"right"`, `"up"` or `"down"`, the one
  recognized, as a `contextmenu` action takes a `PointerEvent`.
- `swipe-direction` names the directions, space-separated, one or more of
  `left`, `right`, `up` and `down`, in any case; lowering lowercases them and
  keeps each once, and the runner does the same to a computed value. They are
  physical, as UIKit's are: a right-to-left layout does not mirror them, and
  on the Mac they are the fingers' way whatever the person's scrolling
  direction (§8.2). Required with `swipe`.
- `touch-count`, a whole number of at least 1, is how many fingers the swipe
  takes; a swipe for one count hears no other. Omitted, the swipe is the
  platform's own: UIKit's one-finger swipe, Android's fling, and on the Mac
  AppKit's swipe event (§8.2).
- `swipe-direction` and `touch-count` are kernel props, lowered as
  `intersect-margin` is (`contract/lower/src/tags.rs`). A literal is checked
  when the plan is built: `lower-swipe-direction` for no direction or a word
  other than those four, `lower-swipe-count` for a count that is not a whole
  number of at least 1, `lower-swipe-unused` for either without `swipe`. A
  computed value that would fail these checks is invalid, and an invalid
  computed value is unset, as CSS has it (CSS Custom Properties §3.1); the
  runner journals a line each time a binding computes a new invalid value. An
  unset `swipe-direction` leaves the swipe off; an unset `touch-count` leaves
  it the platform's own.
- A node may have `swiperight` beside `swipe` only when the swipe cannot be a
  one-finger swipe to the right, which on a touch screen is the same contact
  (`lower-swipe-twice`). Lowering proves it from a literal: a
  `swipe-direction` that does not name `right`, or a `touch-count` other than
  1. When neither literal proves it, a computed value included, `swiperight`
  beside the swipe is refused.
- A node that is disabled, inert or hidden, or inside a disabled or hidden
  node, takes no swipe (on Apple, `SwipeInput.allows`, which a pan, the drags
  and the pinch ask too). The contact passes over it to an ancestor whose
  swipe takes it.

### 8.2 Recognition

The platform recognizes; the engine follows (LLP 1002 D4). Nothing is held,
sprung or sampled per frame, so `swipe` alone links no motion module
(`runner/src/uses.rs`).

| host | recognizer | counts |
|---|---|---|
| iOS | one `UISwipeGestureRecognizer` per named direction on the node's view (one recognizer given several directions does not say which it saw), `numberOfTouchesRequired` the count | any |
| macOS | AppKit's swipe event, `swipe(with:)` (below) | omitted only: a swipe event carries no finger count (the person's settings decide how many fingers make one), so on the Mac a node that names a count never fires |
| Android | `GestureDetector`'s `onFling` (below) | omitted: the detector's own flings, a hand-off between fingers included; a count: a fling counts when the most pointers its touch had down at once is that count. The detector reports no count; the carrier reads it from the touch's `MotionEvent`s |
| web | none: the web has no swipe of its own, and Exact makes none; the JS target accepts the handler and nothing fires it (§8.3) | none |
| Linux, Windows | none: the hosts read one pointer (a mouse, and on Linux a tablet's pen or a touchscreen's first touch), and Exact makes no swipe from it | none |
| tvOS | none: no app needs a swipe on the remote's touch surface yet, and no test can make one (`XCUIRemote` only presses the remote's buttons) | none |

**macOS.** AppKit offers a view a discrete swipe as its swipe event
(`swipe(with:)`). A trackpad sends it for a three-finger swipe where "Swipe
between pages" uses three fingers, and some mouse drivers send it for their
side buttons. The event names the way the fingers went: `deltaX` is 1 for a
swipe left and −1 for one right, and `deltaY` 1 up and −1 down (`NSEvent.h`),
whatever the person's scrolling direction: natural scrolling inverts only
scroll events (`NSEvent.h`), and three fingers moving right on a trackpad gave
−1 with it on (macOS 27). The event goes up the responder chain from the view
under the pointer, so a node takes it in a direction it names and passes the
others up, to the root view, where history steps Back for `deltaX` 1 and
Forward for −1, as WebKit's web view does (`ExactView.swipe(with:)`,
`ExactViewMac.swift`; `WebViewImpl::swipeWithEvent`). For a point on a row
that presses, AppKit's hit test gives the vertical list's table, which hands
the event down to what is under the pointer, as it does a scroll
(`RunnerTableView`). Over a node whose swipe names `left` or `right`, a mouse
driver's side button is that node's swipe, as elsewhere it is history's step.
`navigationHistory` withholds only history's steps: a node's swipe is still
heard. The person's trackpad settings decide whether a swipe up or down
reaches an app at all: Mission Control and App Exposé can take three fingers
moving vertically.

The two-finger "Swipe between pages" and a Magic Mouse's one-finger swipe are
AppKit's page navigation, which history takes (LLP 1115.000 D5.5): a view that
returns true from `wantsScrollEventsForSwipeTracking(on:)` gets the sideways
scrolls `NSScrollView` has no room for, and tracks them with
`trackSwipeEvent`. With Apple's default settings, which give swipes between
pages two fingers, a node's sideways swipe comes from a mouse driver's side
buttons alone, since a trackpad sends the event only where swipes between
pages use three fingers; the control §8.6 asks the author for is the way to
its effect.

A mouse's wheel and its drags make no swipe. This section covers the Mac
host's pointers, a trackpad and a mouse; a Mac's touch screen (macOS 27) is
not one of them.

**Android.** `GestureDetector`'s `onFling`, the platform's swipe, is fed the
carrier's own touches whole (`ExactView.kt`, on the UI thread, every pointer
of each `MotionEvent`), with `ViewConfiguration`'s touch slop and fling
velocities, which the carrier's scrolling uses too (`Scrolling.kt`). It fires
at the lift, as Android's fling does: a fling whose touch the carrier's
scrolling took is none; one it did not take goes to the presenter, its
velocity's larger axis and that axis's sign the direction. The presenter
dispatches it to the innermost node on the hit path saved at the touch's first
down whose swipe takes that direction at that count (rule 3), unless one of
its own gestures took the contact (rule 4) or a `press` node, control or text
input lies between. The presenter's own contact is one pointer and a second
ends it (`ExactView.kt` sends a pointer's coming or going as a cancel); the
saved path and the touch's fling outlive that end.

### 8.3 The web

The web has no swipe of its own, and Exact makes none (§8.2). An app that
needs the effect there gives it a control, as §8.6 asks anyway.

### 8.4 Precedence

A swipe is discrete: pending from the first touch until it recognizes or
fails. Rules 1–4 apply (on the Mac only rule 3: the swipe event arrives
recognized, from a gesture no scroll or drag shares), with this order of
`swipe` against the rest:

- **Continuous gestures do not wait for it.** Scroll, a `pan`, a drag binding
  and `swiperight`, on the hit path, begin at their own thresholds; one that
  begins takes the contact, and the pending swipe fails. That holds for a
  continuous gesture outside the swiping node as for one inside it: rule 3
  orders continuous recognizers among themselves and swipes among themselves,
  and never makes a continuous gesture wait for a `swipe`. On iOS the swipe
  recognizer, through its delegate's
  `gestureRecognizer(_:shouldRequireFailureOf:)`, requires the failure of the
  pan of each scroll view on the touch's hit path (from the view it landed on
  up to the window, an editor under the node included) that admits travel in
  the swipe's direction from the touch's start. That is the pan's own
  admission, both halves of it: UIKit's, that the view scrolls on that axis
  (its content overflows there, or it bounces there: `alwaysBounceVertical`,
  which Exact sets on some scrollers whose content fits, `NodeViewIOS.swift`);
  and Exact's, the same predicate its `gestureRecognizerShouldBegin` asks. For
  a scroller that is `ScrollView.wouldPan` (`ScrollViewIOS.swift`: rule 2's
  `touch-action` from the hit through the scroller, then scroll chaining, as
  `admitsPan` asks them), asked with the swipe's direction; for a textarea,
  `TextArea.pans` (`TextAreaIOS.swift`): its text overflows and its
  `overflow-y` is not `hidden` or `clip`, which is all a textarea's pan
  asks: it reads no `touch-action`, so an overflowing field with
  `touch-action: none` still scrolls. The swipe waits for a required pan to
  fail, so a pan that begins takes the contact and the swipe fails, whatever
  distance UIKit asks of a swipe at that speed:
  scroll wins by construction. The requirement only orders recognizers that
  would both succeed. A pan the swipe does not wait for can still begin, for
  example when the finger first moves another way; it then takes the contact.
  A pan the swipe waits for but that refuses to begin fails a few points in
  (UIKit fails a recognizer whose `gestureRecognizerShouldBegin` returns
  false), and the swipe may then recognize. §1's candidate order at the slop
  does not apply to `swipe`: it is never a candidate that begins at the slop.
- **It cancels what has not happened yet.** A swipe that recognizes cancels
  the contact's armed `press` and a long press that has not begun, as a drag
  that begins does (rule 4). A long press that began (UIKit's runs its action
  at `.began`, `ContextGesturesIOS.swift`) has already won: the finger moved
  less than its allowance until then, and a swipe after it fails. A contact
  that never leaves the slop is still a tap.
- **Rule 3, for a text editor, on iOS, for a finger.** A native text editor
  between the hit and the swiped node keeps taps, long presses, selection and
  its own scrolling, but not a finger's swipe that starts on it: the swiped
  node's recognizers receive the editor's direct touches
  (`NodeView.gestureRecognizer(_:shouldReceive:)` admits a touch of type
  `.direct` for swipe recognizers). A pointer's or pencil's contact on an
  editor stays the editor's, since its drag selects text, and starts no swipe
  there. A finger's selection is the editor's whatever its timing: a drag of a
  selection handle, a long press's caret drag, and a double or triple tap's
  selection all change the editor's selection as they act. (A quick flick from
  the caret moves nothing, measured on the iOS 27 simulator, so it is a
  swipe.) So any change of the editor's selection while the contact is down, a
  programmatic one included, fails its pending swipe before it can recognize,
  whatever the selection comes back to: the host records when the editor's
  selection last changed (`textViewDidChangeSelection`, `TextAreaIOS.swift`,
  and `textFieldDidChangeSelection` for a one-line field, which UIKit calls as
  the change is made), and the recognizers' delegate answers false in
  `gestureRecognizerShouldBegin` when that is later than the contact's first
  touch; UIKit asks it before a discrete recognizer recognizes, and the false
  answer fails it. A swipe refused this way never recognizes, so it cancels
  none of the editor's touches (`cancelsTouchesInView`), and a handle keeps
  dragging however far it goes. A touch that comes down on a field moves no
  caret before it lifts, so a flick leaves the selection as it was. An editor
  that scrolls on the swipe's axis takes the contact by the scroll rule above:
  the composer's field, while it overflows. On Android a touch on a native
  field goes to the field alone (`ExactView.kt`), so there a swipe starts only
  outside a field.
- **Rule 3, for a trackpad.** A swipe on a Mac's trackpad presses nothing and
  selects no text, so no `press` node, control or text input between keeps it
  from the node: rule 3's boundary is for a contact that lands on one. A text
  view and a control pass `swipe(with:)` up the responder chain
  (`NSResponder`'s default).
- **The back gestures.** On iOS each swipe recognizer waits for the
  screen-edge pop to fail, as a `swiperight` row does (§7). A swipe to the
  right does not begin within the left 20 points, where §7's back gestures
  begin, as `swiperight` does not (rule 1); other directions may begin there.
  A node with a one-finger swipe to the right is protected from the content
  pop as a `swiperight` node is (`NavigationHost.popMayBegin`,
  `NavigationIOS.swift`), judged at the touch's first location, recorded in
  `shouldReceive` (§7), and only when that swipe could take the touch: the
  node is one §8.1 lets take a swipe and has that recognizer, and no `press`
  node lies between the hit and it. Left and right here are physical, as §7's
  are. On macOS `swipe(with:)` goes up the responder chain from the view under
  the pointer, so a node's swipe has it before history's Back or Forward
  (`ExactView.swipe(with:)`) in the directions it names, and passes the others
  up (§8.2).
- **Nested swipes (rule 3).** The innermost node whose swipe takes the
  contact's direction and number of fingers wins. On iOS an outer node's
  recognizer requires the failure of each recognizer for the same direction
  and number of touches (an omitted count is one) on a node between the hit
  and it (`gestureRecognizer(_:shouldRequireFailureOf:)`); an inner node that
  differs in either does not hold it up. On Android the presenter gives the
  fling to the innermost match on the contact's saved hit path. On macOS the
  responder chain is that order.

### 8.5 Wire and runner

ABI kind 61, `swipe`: UTF-8 `left`, `right`, `up` or `down`
(`host/apple/include/exact.h`, `host/apple/src/abi.rs`; `Event::Swipe` decoded
in `Event::of_host_kind`). The plan format gains `EventKind::Swipe` at its
next free ordinal; the plan's numbers and the ABI's are separate (plan 61 is
`focusin`).

A host dispatches only for the configuration it is running. On UIKit each node
keeps its recognizers with the directions and count they were made for, and on
a change of either (or a computed value going invalid), or when the node's
view is replaced, removes them and makes new ones, which fails a pending
contact's swipe; the new ones take no part in that contact. A recognition from
a recognizer that is no longer the node's current one is dropped by the host
before dispatch; that identity stays inside the host, and the payload stays
the direction. AppKit and Android keep no recognizer per node: each reads the
node's configuration as it dispatches. The runner's dispatch, which knows the
node, checks that its `swipe-direction` names the direction now, and drops a
mismatch with a journal line, not as an error batch. The web hosts fire no
`swipe`: the JS target binds its handler to an event nothing fires.

### 8.6 Accessibility

A swipe adds nothing to the accessibility tree. VoiceOver's own swipes move
its focus; a person makes an app's swipe under it with VoiceOver's standard
gesture (double-tap and hold until three rising tones, then swipe: "Use
VoiceOver gestures on iPhone", iPhone User Guide), and with Voice Control by
its "Swipe" commands. TalkBack's gestures and the Mac's VoiceOver move their
own focus, and the control below is the way to the effect there. A swipe is a
path-based gesture, so its effect must also be reachable with a single pointer
without one, by a tap or a click, unless the path is essential (WCAG 2.2,
2.5.1 Pointer Gestures), as the keyboard recipe's Done button puts the
keyboard away (`docs/recipes/keyboard-and-focus.md`); Exact makes none, and an
author who binds `swipe` owns that obligation.

### 8.7 The agent

`tap <target> swipe <left|right|up|down> [at <x> <y>]` checks, before anything
is delivered, what the host's own delivery needs, and refuses with the reason
when any fails. Everywhere: the node takes a swipe in that direction at its
count and is the innermost node on the hit path that does (a nearer one would
take the contact, §8.4), and the start point (the target's middle, or `at`, a
point in it, so the field and the bar can each be the start) is on screen and
not covered.

- On iOS, with the hit-path eligibility a recognized `pan` uses
  (`AgentPanIOS.swift`), §8.4's rules: the left 20 points for a swipe to the
  right, a `press` node between, and a scroll view or continuous recognizer on
  the hit path that would begin in that direction; and, where the drive moves
  a finger (the touch runner), the path, 120 points in that direction over 100
  ms, stays inside the window. UIKit synthesizes no touch, so the drive is
  delivered `recognized` at the start point, which moves nothing and checks no
  path: it proves the wiring, and real-touch tests prove the arbitration.
  Under `--touch platform` the touch runner swipes for real with one finger
  (`host/apple/touches.swift`), checking the path, and refuses a larger count.
- On macOS the drive is a three-finger `swipe(with:)`, the event that reaches a
  node whatever scrolls around it. AppKit has no public
  constructor for the event; a gesture `CGEvent` of IOHID's swipe type carries
  its direction, as a pinch's carries its zoom (`AgentSwipeMac.swift`), and
  the view under the start point gets it, so it is delivered `platform`, up
  the real responder chain. Its checks: the node is on the path up from the
  view under the start point, by the hit test that sees a table row's own
  views (`TableRowCell.whatIsThere`), since a table hands a swipe event down
  to what is under the pointer (§8.2); no node nearer on that path takes a
  swipe in that direction; and the node names no count. A scroll view on that
  chain does not stand in the way: `swipe(with:)` is a different event from
  scrolling.
- On the Android reader, whose driver speaks to the presenter and injects none
  of the platform's touches, it is delivered `recognized`
  (`Presenter::agent_swipe`, `presenter/fling.rs`): the fling a touch at the
  start point would make, at the node's count, once that touch would reach the
  node, by the presenter's own rule (§8.2). A hosted field's box is the
  field's, and the reader's scrolling takes a touch before the presenter sees
  it; the carrier's tests prove those halves.
- On tvOS, the web, Linux and Windows, which make no swipe, the drive refuses,
  saying so.

### 8.8 Vocabulary

`contract vocab swipe` answers the attribute and keeps the concept page it
already has (`contract/cli/src/vocab/concepts.md`), and the page names the
three kinds apart: `swipe`, a discrete swipe; `swiperight`, a drag that
follows the finger and releases; and a list row's swipe actions
(`swipeContent`, `swipeLeading`, `swipeTrailing`).

### 8.9 Proofs

- The compiler's diagnostics: each refusal, a direction list's case and
  repeats, and `swiperight` beside `swipe` refused unless the literals prove
  the swipe is no one-finger swipe to the right. The runner's decoding; its
  drop of a direction the node no longer names, journalled; and a computed
  value of either prop that is invalid: unset and journalled.
- The web: the JS target builds a plan with `swipe`, whose handler nothing
  fires, and links no motion for it.
- iOS, `DiscreteSwipeIOSTests`: the recognizers' directions and counts; new
  recognizers after a change of direction, of count, and of a count that goes
  unset; their failure requirements on the hit path's scroll views (an
  overflowing editor's and a fitting one's, an overflowing editor's whose
  `overflow-y` is `hidden`, a bouncing scroller's, one whose `touch-action` or
  scroll chaining refuses the direction, and one that fits) and on the
  screen-edge pop alone; the 20-point rule; the content pop left to a node
  with a one-finger swipe to the right, and not past a `press` node, nor to a
  node that is disabled, inert or inside a disabled node, nor to a swipe of
  another count or direction; a node that is disabled, inert or inside a
  disabled node taking no swipe, and an ancestor's swipe beyond it taking
  the contact; the editor's touches
  (a direct touch admitted, a pointer's and a pencil's not); a selection that
  moved during the contact refusing the swipe, on an ancestor of the editor
  and on the editor's own node; nested swipes, with an inner node for the same
  direction and count, one for another direction, one for another count, and
  one whose count is omitted beside one of 1; a recognizer the node no longer
  has, replaced by a new configuration or left on a view the node no longer
  has, sending nothing.
- iOS, real touches on the iPhone simulator, on a fixture
  (`contract/corpus/swipe-touch.contract`, driven by
  `scripts/smoke-touch.mjs`): a flick down on a bar and on its fitting field
  fires the bar's swipe, and on the field once its text overflows fires none;
  on the focused field of three lines, a flick from the caret fires the
  swipe, a flick from a selection handle fires none and keeps the selection,
  and a long press's caret drag moves the caret and fires none; on a node
  with `press` and a context menu, a flick fires the swipe and not the press,
  a tap presses, and a long press opens the menu and, moved on, fires no
  swipe. These show what a person sees; the host's comparison
  (`selectionMovedAt` against the contact's first touch) is the unit tests'.
- Android, the carrier's tests, which run without Android's classes: the
  direction from a fling's velocity; the count, the most pointers the touch
  had down at once, a hand-off between fingers included; a touch the carrier's
  scrolling took, or that a hosted field kept, flinging none. The presenter's
  tests: a fling goes to the innermost match on the touch's saved hit path; a
  `press` node, control or text input between, a gesture of the presenter's
  that took the contact, and a long press that began each keep it; a node that
  is disabled, inert or inside a disabled node passes it over; with no count a
  fling of any number of fingers fires, and with a count only that many. On an
  Android emulator, a real fling (`adb shell input swipe`, one pointer) on a
  swipe node fires its direction. Two-finger and hand-off flings are proven in
  the carrier's bookkeeping above; that `GestureDetector` reports them is the
  platform's.
- macOS: the direction word for each sign of `deltaX` and `deltaY`;
  `swipe(with:)` reaching a node in each direction from inside a scroll view
  that can still scroll, and from a textarea's text view; the innermost node
  that names the direction taking it, and a direction no node names going on
  up; a node that names a count, is disabled or inert, is inside a disabled
  node, or whose view is no longer its node's taking none; a row that presses
  in a vertical list's table taking it; and a node's swipe before history's
  Back and Forward, history stepping each way beside the node, and a node's
  swipe still heard under `navigationHistory="none"`.
- The agent's `tap … swipe` on each host, refusals included: on macOS its
  three-finger drive accepted at a node inside a scroll view, a zoomed
  transform and a `wheel` handler alike, as `swipe(with:)` reaches it there,
  and at a node in a row that presses in a vertical list's table;
  on tvOS, the web, Linux and Windows refused.
