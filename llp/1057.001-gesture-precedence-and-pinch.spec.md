## 1. The precedence table

A static rule, derived from the tree and its rows. Each host implements it
with its own mechanism. It is not a runtime structure and authors cannot
change it.

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1 | The system's own gestures keep their edges (back swipe, home indicator). An authored swipe yields the first 20 pt from the leading edge. |
| 2 | Scroll wins on every axis `touch-action` leaves to it. `touch-action` intersects from the hit node up through the scroll container, as CSS does. |
| 3 | The innermost node with a matching recognizer wins; its ancestors do not also fire (DOM bubbling, as `press` does). A text input or control between the hit node and an ancestor's recognizer keeps the contact for itself; a `press` handler between keeps it from an ancestor's swipe or drag binding, and from an ancestor's `pan` only within the slop: the pan that begins takes the contact and the press, as a draggable element's pointer listeners hear a drag that starts on a button inside it (kanban F6, 2026-10-04). |
| 4 | On one node, one contact: drag binding (reorder > transform > height) > `pan` > `swiperight` > long press / `contextmenu` > `press`. |
| 5 | Double click follows the web: `press` fires on each click, `dblclick` after the second, with no delay. |
| 6 | Exactly one built-in simultaneity: pinch with pan on a transform binding. It is host-owned; authors cannot request simultaneity. |

**Candidate order.** Rules 3 and 4 together order a contact's candidates:
innermost first, then by rule 4 on one node. When the contact crosses its slop,
the first candidate whose axis rule accepts the movement begins; candidates
before it that refused fall away, and nothing after it runs. A drag that begins
cancels the armed `press`.

| host | mechanism |
|---|---|
| web | independent listeners on each element; bubbling puts the innermost first. A swipe, which does not capture at down, marks its pointer `pending` in `exact.contacts` until it claims or refuses; an ancestor's pan, transform or height drag that would capture waits for that verdict. A recognizer that begins stops propagation and suppresses the click, the pan's included |
| macOS | `NodeView.mouseDown` arms every recognizer (`MouseChainMac.swift`), which orders the armed candidates by depth, then rule 4; `mouseDragged` and `mouseUp` offer the event in that order and the first taker keeps it |
| Linux | `pointer_down` collects the candidates and orders them the same way (`presenter/contact.rs`); at the slop the first that accepts begins |
| iOS | UIKit's recognizers and their delegate predicates (`NodeViewIOS.swift`, `GesturePrecedenceIOS.swift`): `ScrollView.gestureRecognizerShouldBegin` for rule 2; an ancestor's drag `shouldRequireFailureOf` a descendant's (rule 3; never of a pinch, which stays possible with one finger); `shouldReceive` refuses a touch under a nested press or editor to the drags; the pinch pair's `shouldRecognizeSimultaneouslyWith` for rule 6 |

**Rule 3's boundary** holds on every host: a `press` handler, a control or a
text input between the contact and an ancestor's drag keeps the contact from
it, so a drag that starts on a button in a swipe row does not swipe
(web: `pressable` in `motion-glue.js` and the pan's check in `input-glue.js`;
iOS: `pressBoundary`). A `pan` passes a `press` (not a control or text input):
the web's pan neither captures nor prevents the pointer until it begins, AppKit
and Linux arm it under the press, and UIKit's pan receives the touch and
cancels the press when it recognizes. **Rule 4's press** arms on a `pan` node on AppKit too
and fires when the pan never leaves the slop; a pan that begins cancels it.

**Declared.** On iOS the order of recognizers on *one* view that cross their
thresholds in the same touch event is UIKit's and undocumented, so rule 4's
tie order on a single node is intended, not enforced; across nodes rule 3 is a
failure requirement. iOS arbitration is checked by unit tests that set
recognizer phases and by delegate predicates. Those tests do not establish
real-touch scroll or long-press arbitration.

**UIKit press forwarding, issue [408](https://github.com/ccheever/exact2/issues/408),
2026-10-10.** A plain text hit beneath an Exact-inserted horizontal
`ScrollView` did not arm or invoke its authored ancestor's press. The raw-touch
regression in `PressFocusIOSTests.testAPlainTextTouchReachesItsAncestorThroughAHorizontalScroller`
failed both assertions on main `e2c6fcc`, after confirming the actual window hit
was the text and its parent was the inserted scroller. The normal iPad fixture
also failed text taps while the gutter and the same authored web row worked.

`PointerIOS.swift` now records each ordinary responder hop on down, crossing
Exact's overflow wrapper to its authored owner. Local canvas, SVG, inline and
ordinary press handling still runs at each node first. Moves, end and cancel
use the captured hop and contact; weak responder identity and incarnation
checks, plus the exact downstream contact record, prevent delivery to a
replacement node or contact. Retirement, reparenting,
disable and inertness cancel armed feedback. The original hit, recognizers,
scroll policy and geometry stay UIKit's. Press acceptance still resolves before
focus callbacks can move the owner. Unhandled ground still reaches the
session-scoped blur path.

The selected ten UIKit classes passed 117 tests with no failures. The added
raw-touch cases cover the failing text hit, explicit cancellation and late
callbacks, movement outside and back, nearer and inline handlers, two overflow
wrappers with offsets, source retirement and reparenting, owner rebind, focus
resize, retainFocus and blank ground. `NodePoolIOSTests` also checks that a
retired forwarding row cancels its ancestor rather than being reused, both
with the touch held and after an autorelease scope proves the weak touch is
nil with no terminal callback. An unfinished contact pins the forwarder
independently of weak touch lifetime. A replacement-contact case uses the
same UITouch with distinct contact records to check stale move, end, cancel
and retirement without depending on allocator address reuse. These are
callback and lifecycle tests. Normal-app device
acceptance for scroll and long-press cancellation remains separate.

## 2. `touch-action`: CSS's whole vocabulary

`TouchAction` in `kernel/tables/schema.json` is CSS's grammar, `auto | none |
[ [ pan-x | pan-left | pan-right ] || [ pan-y | pan-up | pan-down ] ||
pinch-zoom ] | manipulation`, in CSS's canonical serialization order: 34
values. The first 13 keep their wire bytes; `pinch-zoom` and the multi-value
forms follow. An author writes the canonical order (`pan-y pinch-zoom`, not
`pinch-zoom pan-y`); the compiler's diagnostic lists every value.

`pinch-zoom` is the browser's zoom, not the app's pinch (Astra's review): a
value that allows it leaves a two-finger pinch to the platform, and an app
pinch (§4) needs the handle's effective value to exclude it. `TouchAction::
pinch_zoom()` answers that (`auto`, `manipulation`, or a value naming
`pinch-zoom`); `TouchAction::pans()` drops `pinch-zoom` for the hosts whose pan
rules match whole values (Linux's swipe admission).

| host | what it does with the row |
|---|---|
| web | emits it as CSS; the browser decides |
| iOS | `NodeView.allowsTouchPan` splits the value into its pan tokens, so the scroll view's intersection (rule 2) ignores `pinch-zoom`; the transform pinch recognizer begins only where a node from the hit view up excludes `pinch-zoom` (§4) |
| macOS | swipe admission reads the pan tokens; trackpad magnify is not a touch and is not governed by the row (as the browser's `ctrlKey` wheel is not) |
| Linux | swipe admission and the retained region's swipe policy match `pans()` |

## 3. One velocity estimator, one threshold table

**Velocity.** `VelocityTracker` (recency-weighted least squares over 100 ms,
`motion/src/velocity.rs`) is the estimator wherever the platform measures
none. The engine keeps one per live hold, fed by every value the hold is given
(`begin_*`, `update_hold`, `update_transform_hold`), and answers
`Engine::hold_velocity(token, now)`; an ended or replaced hold has none.

| path | velocity |
|---|---|
| transform pair, web and Apple | the engine's: op 13's three velocity slots must be zero, the host fills `TransformRelease`'s `vx, vy, vscale` from `hold_velocity`, and the reply carries them as `velocity` for the two hold ends. Swift's `TransformDragVelocity` and the JS endpoint estimator are gone. |
| `swiperight`, web | motion op 10 (`release-measured`) releases at the engine's estimate; the JS estimator is gone |
| `swiperight`, macOS | `exact_hold_end(…, cancel: 2, …)` releases at the engine's estimate; `MouseSwipe`'s last-sample difference is gone |
| `swiperight`, iOS | UIKit's pan velocity, times the resistance past the knee (the platform measures it) |
| Linux | `VelocityTracker` on the contact's hold, as before |

**Constrained displays.** The height binding's display is CSS-clamped
(`min-height`/`max-height`), so the held value can outrun what is shown. The
host records the shown height into the same hold with `Engine::track_hold`
(web: motion op 12 after each move, at the move's instant; Apple: after each
update's layout, from `height_catch`), and `VelocityTracker::push` lets a
sample at the same instant replace the held one. The release then uses the
engine's estimate: web op 9's velocity slot must be zero, and
`exact_height_drag_release` lost its velocity argument. Swift's
`HeightDragVelocity` and the JS height estimator are gone. Web Arrange's
source row releases at the engine's estimate over its hold (the packet's
velocity slots must be zero); Apple Arrange keeps UIKit's or its own and
Linux its contact tracker (`QUEUE.md`).

**Thresholds.** `exact_motion::gesture` holds the values exact2 defines
itself: `SWIPE_KNEE` 64, `SWIPE_RESISTANCE` 0.2, `SWIPE_EDGE` 20, `SLOP` 4,
and `swipe_displacement`. Linux calls them; Apple reads `exact_gesture_constant(i)`
(`Gesture.knee` etc.); the web asks motion op 11 (`gesture`) once. One slop,
4 px, on AppKit, Linux and the web's drags (swipe, height, transform; they
were 8); the web's `pan` keeps the literal because a pan-only plan links no
motion export to ask (LLP 1047). UIKit's pans keep their own hysteresis.

## 4. Pinch on the photo binding

No new Contract surface: `transformDragFor` admits a two-finger pinch where
the host has one, and `transformrelease(x, y, scale, vx, vy, vscale)` already
carries it. It lives inside the existing paired hold (`begin_transform_hold`
and the 120-byte v2 wire): the pair's move carries a scale, and nothing else
changed in the engine or the runner.

**Focal point.** Every sample is the pair anchored at the contact's focal
point (the pinch centroid, the pointer for a trackpad): with the anchor's
value `(x₀, y₀, s₀)`, focal point `F₀` and the current `F`, both relative to
the clip's centre, and the pinch factor `k` since the anchor,

    x = Fx − k·(F₀x − x₀),  y = Fy − k·(F₀y − y₀),  s = k·s₀

so the content under the fingers stays under them and translate follows the
centroid (`k = 1` is a pan). The anchor moves whenever the contact changes:
a recognizer starts or stops, a finger lands or lifts. That absorbs UIKit's
centroid jump and lets a lifted pinch pan on. `TransformDragPosition.focused`
(Swift) and `position` in `motion-glue.js` are the one formula.

**One release.** Whatever mix of pan and pinch the contact was, it ends once:
the terminal sample and time are preflighted whole, the action runs while both
tokens are live, then each surviving original token ends (LLP 1002 D4). The
terminal payload stays finite (the host refuses before the action otherwise);
`vscale` is the engine's measured scale velocity (§3), no longer zero.

**Bounds.** The binding has none of its own; the consumer's release action
clamps. interaction-gallery's `releasePhoto` adopts the released scale held
between Fit (1) and 4× and clamps pan against that zoom; its Fit, 2× and
Center buttons stay the accessible alternative that needs no gesture.

| host | recognition |
|---|---|
| web | `motion-glue.js`'s transform path: up to two pointers (Pointer Events; the handle's `touch-action` must exclude `pinch-zoom` or the browser takes the pinch), a trackpad's `ctrl`+`wheel` (Chromium, Firefox; the listener is not passive, so the page does not zoom; the pinch ends 150 ms after the last wheel) and Safari's `gesturestart`/`gesturechange`/`gestureend`; while the photo is zoomed (`scale > 1`) a plain wheel over the handle pans it, as on macOS, ending 150 ms after the last wheel; unzoomed it stays the page's scroll |
| iOS | a `UIPinchGestureRecognizer` beside the binding's pan, which now takes two fingers; `shouldRecognizeSimultaneouslyWith` admits exactly that pair (rule 6); the pinch begins only where a node from the handle up excludes `pinch-zoom` (§2) |
| macOS | `NodeView.magnify(with:)` pinches about the pointer (no `NSMagnificationGestureRecognizer`); while the photo is zoomed (`scale > 1`) a phased two-finger scroll over the handle pans it, as Preview does, and its momentum is swallowed (no decay driver); unzoomed, the scroll stays the scroll view's |
| Linux | none: evdev tracks no multitouch slots and no touchpad gestures (`host/linux/src/input.rs`). Authored zoom controls are the path; the agent's `pinch` answers `unsupported` |

Catching a pair reads the layer's model transform unless Core Animation is
running a curve on it: the render tree's copy can lag the last presented
frame, and a pinch caught right after another read the scale before it.

## 5. The agent: `tap <target> pinch <scale> [at <x> <y>]`

A form of `tap` (DEFERRED §Agent API), not a ninth operation. The fingers
spread from `d` to `d·scale` about the target's middle, or about `at`
(offset from its top-left), over eight steps.

| carrier | delivery |
|---|---|
| web | `platform`: two CDP touch points, injected without touch emulation so the page's `(pointer)` and `(hover)` stay the device's (Charlie, 2026-10-07) |
| macOS | `platform`: magnify events (began, changed ×8, ended) built from a gesture `CGEvent`'s zoom fields (110, 113, 132; AppKit has no public constructor) and sent to the view under the point, as the wheel form is |
| iOS | `recognized`: UIKit synthesizes no touch, so the recognized scale goes through the same paired hold (`TransformDragHold.recognizedPinch`) |
| Linux | `unsupported` |

LLP 1035.003 D1's one contact per session stands; a pinch is one `tap`, not
two contacts. The macOS carrier also carries `tap … dblclick` now, as two real
clicks, the second with `clickCount` 2.

## 6. `panrelease`: a pan's release velocity

Phase 2's item, built 2026-09-28 for its consumer, `apps/spark` (a card
dragged by `pan` and flung by its throw; Charlie approved it,
`rules/DEFERRED.md` §Motion). `panrelease=action` appends `(vx, vy)`, the
release velocity in viewport CSS pixels per second, pan's units over time.
It completes `pan`; it is not a phase, and the action commits state as a
`pan` does (LLP 1043.000 D8). No hold, no spring, no engine value.

**When.** Once per contact, and only for a pan that *began* (crossed its
recognition: the 4 px slop, or UIKit's own), after that contact's last `pan`
delta. A contact that never began releases nothing: it is a tap, and rule
4's `press` on the same node fires instead. A node without a `panrelease`
handler is sent nothing; `panrelease` without `pan` never fires.

**Cancel.** A contact cancelled after it began releases at rest, `(0, 0)`:
the app always hears the end of what it started, and a card springs back.
Cancelled means `pointercancel` or a lost capture on the web, UIKit's
`.cancelled`/`.failed`, Escape or the window resigning key on AppKit, and
Linux's `pointer_cancel` (Escape, wheel takeover, disconnection). A node
that is gone or no longer live is sent nothing.

**Velocity** is §3's rule: the platform's where it measures one, the one
estimator elsewhere.

| host | velocity |
|---|---|
| web | the pan's pointer samples, each at its event's own `timeStamp` (so a driven drag under the agent's clock still has a real velocity), go to motion ops 13 (`pan-sample`) and 14 (`pan-velocity`), a `VelocityTracker` in the wasm (`host/web/src/pan_velocity.rs`); ABI kind 28 delivers it. A plan with `panrelease` uses motion (`runner/src/uses.rs`), so the export is linked (LLP 1047 stage 3) |
| iOS | UIKit's `velocity(in:)` at `.ended` |
| macOS | the mouse chain's samples at their `NSEvent` timestamps into a `VelocityTracker` behind the Apple ABI; no Swift estimator |
| Linux | the contact's `VelocityTracker`, fed every sample since down |

**Agent.** No new operation: the contact phases end a pan, `tap <t> down`,
`tap move by <dx> <dy> over <ms>`, `tap up` (or `tap cancel`). Web and
macOS deliver them as platform input, Linux through its presenter; on iOS,
where UIKit synthesizes no touch, a contact on a `pan` node is delivered
`recognized` through the same pan path, its release velocity the tracker's
over the synthesized samples.

**A pause before lifting releases at rest.** The tracker weighs only the
last 100 ms (§3), so a finger that stops and then lifts has no throw left;
UIKit's `velocity(in:)` at `.ended` says the same. That is kept on purpose.
What a driver must not do is invent the pause: the web carrier stamps each
touch event with the contact's own timeline (CDP `timestamp`), so a `tap
up` lands one frame after the last move however long the driver took
between operations, and `tap hold <ms>` is how a pause is said
(`host/web/tests/pan-release-engine.test.mjs`, the real wasm, nothing mocked).


## 7. A swipe beside the back gesture on iOS (2026-10-04, Signal Clone)

A tester found the clone "hard to swipe back sometimes". With HID touches on
the iOS 27 simulator, a back swipe from the edge of a chat became a reply,
and others did nothing. Three fixes:
- **A `swiperight` row judges the edge where its finger landed.** It records
  its first touch in `gestureRecognizer(_:shouldReceive:)`, as the pop
  recognizers already did (LLP 1080.000 §11). The pan's location less its
  translation at `shouldBegin` leaves out the travel before recognition: a
  swipe from x = 2 read as starting at about 30, past the 20-point edge.
- **A `swiperight` row waits for the screen-edge pop to fail**
  (`interactivePopGestureRecognizer`), as UIKit's swipe actions and Signal's
  swipe to reply do: from the edge, back wins. Only that recognizer. iOS
  26's content pop is the same class and already waits on the swipe; waiting
  on it in turn left neither able to begin.
- **The pop's direction rule takes no motion as no evidence against.** UIKit's
  pop recognizers asked `shouldBegin` with zero velocity and zero
  translation, and `velocity.x > |velocity.y|` refused every such back swipe.
  Zero velocity now falls back to the travel so far, and a zero vector is
  allowed (still refused past the edge over a `swiperight` node, and over a
  canvas). UIKit's pop recognizers judge the direction themselves: vertical
  drags from x = 10 and x = 150 on a pushed screen without `swiperight`
  rows scrolled and did not pop (simulator, HID).

Proofs: `NavigationRulesTests.testAPanYieldsToASwipeRightNodePastTheEdge`
(the zero vector, and `NavigationRules.popDirection`'s fallback to travel),
`GesturePrecedenceIOSTests.testASwipeWaitsForTheEdgePopOnly`, and HID drives
on the clone (iOS 27 only; iOS 18's screen-edge path is not yet driven): edge swipes from x = 4, 12
and 30 over a transcript go back, a swipe from x = 60 on a bubble replies,
vertical drags scroll.
