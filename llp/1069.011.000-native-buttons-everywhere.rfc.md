## Summary

LLP 1069.011 refused a native button (`button appearance="auto"`) wherever a host reads a button some other way than as a button — a toolbar, a tab, a menu row or invoker, a swipe action, a keyboard shortcut, a `header` — and kept rows holding any control out of list reuse. Each host projection finds a button by `kind == "button"` and reads its label and glyph from its child views; a native button is kind `control` with no child views (its face is kernel data, LLP 1069.011 D5).

This RFC lifts those refusals with one change underneath: **a button is recognised by `isButton` and read by `pressFace`** — its label, title and symbol, from its child views for a custom button and from the kernel's `buttonFace` for a native one — while each projection keeps its own eligibility rules and its own image path for custom buttons. Native menu invokers get one activation routine across pointer, keys and the agent. Rows holding controls join iOS row reuse with their controls recreated, never carried.

## 1. What exists (at `485020542`)

Unchanged from r1 §1 in substance; the reviews added: menu rows are any direct child with `press`, not `kind == "button"` (`MenusIOS.swift:290`, `MenusMac.swift:76`); a confirmation invoker is its own overlay with `.touchUpInside` and `openConfirmation`, which presses the invoker once (`MenusIOS.swift:89-104`, `:248`); macOS opens a menu only through `Presenter.press → menus.pressed(id)` (`PresenterMac.swift:684-688`) and refuses dialog commands (`MenusMac.swift:51`); Enter/Space on Apple and macOS shortcut discovery require a `press` handler (`NodeViewIOS.swift:289-294`, `NodeViewMac.swift:217`, `ShortcutsMac.swift:53`); segments sync before controls (`PresenterIOS.swift:808-809`), so a native button's control-written title is stale on its first batch; `SegmentFace.image` holds a `NodeView` and `TabBarFace` takes its tint from the image child's `tint-color` (`Accessibility.swift:36`, `SegmentsIOS.swift:48`, `:217`); the swipe table is a plain `UITableView` (`SwipeActionsIOS.swift:179`), and swipe-action discovery keeps an image map to find and name UIKit's rendered buttons (`:337`, `:359`); Linux focus and Enter/Space and the Apple agent's accessible-name enrichment key on role `button`/`link` (`surface_controls.rs:454`, `presenter/typing.rs:205`, `Accessibility.swift:211`); a reused row's view must be indistinguishable from a new element (`rules/DEFERRED.md:332-335`), and the pool already reuses rows around **holes** — heavy leaves destroyed on park and created fresh on take (`NodePoolIOS.swift:203`, `:263`).

## 2. Measured and checked

AppKit 26: `NSToolbarItem.style` (`plain`, `prominent`) and `backgroundTintColor` (prominent only), `macos(26.0)`; UIKit 26 has both on `UIBarButtonItem`. `UIAction.image`, `NSMenuItem.image` and `UIContextualAction.image` take a symbol image; `exact_button_face` already names the symbol as the platform does.

## 3. Design

**D1. `isButton` and `pressFace`.** `NodeView.isButton` is `kind == "button" || isNativeButton`. `NodeView.pressFace -> PressFace { label: String?, title: String?, symbol: String? }`: for a custom button what each projection reads today (its child views' text; `label` its `aria-label`); for a native button `presenter.buttonFace(id)` — the kernel's current face, never the control's last write — `title` its title, `symbol` its platform symbol name, `label` its `aria-label` or its title. A textual platform entry uses `title ?? label`; an accessible name uses `label ?? title`. `PressFace` replaces child-view reads only; each projection keeps its eligibility rules and, for custom buttons, its own image path (toolbar rasters, segment and swipe snapshots); a native symbol is a `systemName`/`systemSymbolName` image at those three call sites. Button identity outside the projections also uses `isButton`, not the role: the Apple agent's accessible-name enrichment (`Accessibility.swift:211`), and on Linux focusability and Enter/Space (`surface_controls.rs:454`, `presenter/typing.rs:205`) recognise a `Control` of type `button` under any role.

**D2. Toolbars.** Contract admits `toolbarPlacement` on a native button and a native button inside a toolbar. iOS projects no toolbar (unchanged: the native button is itself). macOS: a native button with `press` is an item like a custom one, its label, image and title from D1, `toolbarPlacement="navigation"` as today; in the 26 design (`LinkedDesign.liquidGlass`) a prominent style (`filled`, `bordered-prominent`, `prominent-glass`, `prominent-clear-glass`) is `NSToolbarItem.style = .prominent` with `backgroundTintColor` its `accent-color` (absent: the system accent); every other style, and every style in the earlier design, is the plain bordered item the toolbar makes today (a silent stand-in, as LLP 1069.011 D2's are). A custom button stays plain.

**D3. The header.** Contract admits a native button inside `header`; nothing reads `header` on Apple today. LLP 1075.003 now says its header buttons are read through D1 and a prominent style is `UIBarButtonItem.style = .prominent`; not built here.

**D4. Tabs.** Contract admits `role="tab"` on a native button (its fixed role yields) inside a `tablist`. A native tab is a tab on both platforms (`isButton`, role `tab`, `press`). Eligibility is decided per projection, separately from the face: iOS's tab bar needs a symbol and a title on every tab (a custom tab: its two child views, as today; a native tab: `pressFace.symbol` and `.title`); a segment needs a symbol alone or a title alone (a native tab whose `aria-label` differs from its visible title stays authored, as the custom guard does, `Accessibility.swift:47-49`). `SegmentFace` gains `.symbol(name)`, drawn with `systemName`/`systemSymbolName` and carrying the label; a custom image segment keeps its view snapshot. The tab bar's selected tint: a custom tab's image `tint-color`, a native tab's `accent-color`, else the label colour. macOS has no tab bar: a symbol-and-title face stays authored there, as a custom one does. Rendering reads the kernel face each sync (D1), so a changing title and an unavailable symbol (no image, the label kept) are current on the first batch. A native tab has no context menu (`contextmenu` stays refused on native buttons). Mixed custom and native tabs project together when each qualifies.

**D5. Menus.**
- *Rows.* Unchanged eligibility: any direct child with `press` (no `isButton` filter). A row's title is D1's (`title ?? label`); a native row's symbol is its item's image (`UIAction.image`, `NSMenuItem.image`), a custom row stays text-only; `aria-checked`, `disabled`, `destructive` as today. A confirmation's action and cancel are `isButton` rows (`MenusIOS.swift:251-254`); a native action's alert tint is its `accent-color`, a custom one's its `text_color` as today. A native button nested deeper than a popover's direct child (inside a menu row) is refused: it would compile and never be shown.
- *Native invokers — one routine.* A native button with `popovertarget` (or `commandfor` on iOS) has three states, set by `menus.sync` after `controls.sync`: *ordinary* (its primary action runs `activateNative`), *menu invoker*, *confirmation invoker*. `invokeNative()` is the one activation for an invoker, used by its control's action, Enter/Space on its node (even with no `press` of its own), and the agent's `tap`: it presses the applicable target once — its own handler, or the nearest ancestor's, per LLP 1069.011 D4 — then performs the default action once, unless the press removed or retargeted the invoker (re-checked after the batch it applied):
  - iOS menu invoker: the `NativeButtonIOS` carries the deferred menu (`menu`, `showsMenuAsPrimaryAction = true`), attached once per invoker–target pair and kept while that pair is unchanged (not replaced every sync); its primary action is removed meanwhile; the deferred provider is the overlay's (its press, then its rows) — no overlay, no second accessibility element. A keyboard or agent activation calls the same provider's press and then `presentMenu` from the control.
  - iOS confirmation invoker: no menu; the control's primary action is `openConfirmation` (which presses once) instead of `activateNative`.
  - macOS menu invoker: `invokeNative()` presses the target (if any) and opens through `menus.pressed(self.id)` exactly once (when its own handler was the target, `press` has already called it).
  - When the invoker stops being one (`popovertarget` gone, target removed, node destroyed or parked), `menus.sync` detaches the menu or confirmation action and restores *ordinary*.
- *Supported presentation:* iOS: menus (`popovertarget`, `commandfor`) and confirmations; macOS: menus by `popovertarget`, dialog commands refused as today (`MenusMac.swift:51`); the web: the browser's popover and invoker commands; Linux: refused (D9). Custom invokers are unchanged, including macOS's need for a `press` handler.
- `role` `menuitem`, `menuitemcheckbox`, `menuitemradio` are admitted on a native button (its fixed role yields).

**D6. Swipe actions.** Contract admits a native button as a named action and anywhere in a swipe row's body. iOS: the swipe table cancels a touch in a `NativeButtonIOS` (`touchesShouldCancel`, as `ScrollView` does), so a pan that starts on a native button in the body becomes the swipe or the scroll. A native action's label is D1's; its image is its symbol (`UIImage(systemName:)`, no title text), recorded in the action-image map as a snapshot is today, so discovery finds and names it; its colour is its `accent-color`; with none, system blue, and for `destructive` no colour is set (UIKit's red; the custom path's override is not copied); a press is `presenter.press` as today, the action's own control hidden with the node. macOS, the web and Linux render the authored row.

**D7. Keyboard shortcuts.** Contract admits `aria-keyshortcuts` on a native button; `ShortcutsMac` admits `isButton` nodes and titles their item `title ?? label`, its image the native symbol.

**D8. Row reuse: controls are holes.** A row holding controls parks and is reused on iOS; its controls are not. The pool accepts kind `control` in a row's shape (its token the control's `ControlKinds.kind`, with `file` given its own, so a switch row never takes a checkbox row's views) and treats the node's control (and a native glass button's slot) as its own platform subviews. Parking a row removes its controls as a hole's content is removed — `ControlHost` forgets the id and the `UIControl` is removed from its view — and recycles the slot (`syncGlassSlot` after clearing the glass flag); a take creates fresh controls for the new ids in the next `controls.sync`, so no state, tracking or callback crosses rows (`rules/DEFERRED.md:332`). A row whose control is tracking (`isTracking`), or whose control owns an open menu, picker or confirmation, is not retired. Every control kind joins (a fresh control is always correct).

**D9. What stays refused.** On the button: `href`, `action`, `popover` (a native button is not a popover), `swipeContent`/`swipeLeading`/`swipeTrailing`/`swipeIndicator` (the row carries those), any other `role`, a `type` other than `button`, `contextmenu`. As a context: inside a `canvas`, inside a native button, deeper than a popover's direct child. Linux: a native invoker is found where a custom one is — `popover_invoker` stops at a native button (an invoker if it has `popovertarget`, not one otherwise, whatever its ancestors) — and an unsupported `commandfor` is refused at the originating invoker before any ancestor's handler runs (in `navigation.rs`/`surface_controls.rs`; `host.rs` is at its line cap).

**D10. Tests.** Contract: each lifted place compiles; each D9 refusal holds (with the parent's face, type and style rules); roles as literals and choices. XCTests (iOS and macOS): toolbar items native and custom (prominence, tint, image, the earlier design's stand-in, press); native tabs as a tab bar and as segments, mixed with custom, a changing title, an unavailable symbol, authored fallback; native menu rows (title, symbol, symbol-only with a label); a native menu invoker (no overlay, one menu and one press, by action and by Enter/Space without a handler, re-attachment only on a changed pair, restoration when it stops being one); a native confirmation invoker; a native swipe action (label, symbol, colour, destructive red, image map); a shortcut on a native button; a row with a native button and a switch parked and taken (fresh controls, `checked` absent after present, presses to the new ids) and a tracking control blocking retirement. Real touches on the simulator (the agent's held-contact pointer, `tap … down`): a tap, a horizontal swipe and a vertical scroll starting on a native button in a swipe body, with no accidental press. Ordinary-launch (not agent-mode) drives of a native menu invoker. Linux: a native invoker refused as a custom one, a native `commandfor` inside a pressable ancestor refused before the ancestor runs, keyboard activation under role `tab` and `menuitem`. The web (JS target, and the wasm oracle for its emission): a native tab, menu row and invoker. The fixture gains a toolbar, a tablist, a menu and a swipe row of native buttons; Messages' swipe actions become native.
