## Summary

An `image` whose source is a GIF or WebP with more than one frame plays on
iOS and macOS as Chrome plays it: the same frames, the same times, the same
number of plays, nothing done while it is off screen. Under the agent's clock
it shows the frame the clock says on every host, the web included. Linux
still paints the first frame (§4).

## 1. Chrome's rules, and where they come from

Read from Chromium (`cc/trees/image_animation_controller.cc`,
`third_party/blink/renderer/platform/graphics/deferred_image_decoder.cc`,
`…/image-decoders/webp/webp_image_decoder.cc`) and checked against headless
Chrome 2026-09 by sampling real-time screenshots of generated fixtures:

- **Durations.** A frame of 10 ms or less shows for 100 ms, GIF and WebP
  alike (`DeferredImageDecoder::FrameDurationAtIndex`). 20 ms is 20 ms.
- **Plays.** A GIF without a NETSCAPE extension plays once; with loop count
  *n* it plays *n* + 1 times; 0 is forever. A WebP's loop count *n* is *n*
  plays; 0 is forever. It then holds its last frame. ImageIO's
  `LoopCount` already counts plays this way; WebCodecs' `repetitionCount`
  is plays − 1.
- **Start.** Frame 0 begins at the first frame the image is drawn visible.
  Frame *i* shows from the sum of the durations before it.
- **Off screen.** No work. Seen again it catches up to where the time says,
  with two exceptions: more than five minutes behind, it resumes the frame it
  left, from now; and when the end of its first play passed unseen (a frame
  late or more), it starts over from frame 0, from now.
- **Per element.** Two `<img>` of one source keep separate states in cc;
  each view here has its own player too.

The schedule is `AnimationSchedule` and `AnimationClock` in Swift and
`schedule`/`animationClock` in `image-glue.js`, whole milliseconds so the
sums are exact.

## 2. Apple

- **Decode.** `RasterImage.decode` keeps the first frame exactly as before
  (the poster, charged to the raster ledger) and, for a GIF or WebP with
  more than one frame, a `RasterAnimation`: frame count, schedule, plan and
  the source's file (spooled remote bytes stay alive with it). Nothing else
  is decoded until the image shows.
- **Frames, at the display size.** One serial queue decodes frames in order
  at the poster's plan (the view's size at its `object-fit`). A GIF goes
  through ImageIO's thumbnail at index, which composes each frame over the
  last cheaply in order (1.7 ms for a 480-px frame; 40 ms at random). A
  WebP is composed here from its `ANMF` frames (RFC 9649 §2.7: offset,
  blend or not, dispose to transparent), each frame's bitstream decoded
  alone: ImageIO composes a WebP's frame *i* from frame 0 every time
  (98 ms for frame 99 of a 100-frame 480-px animation, even through
  `CGAnimateImageAtURLWithBlock`), which cannot keep up. The composing
  canvas is the image's own size, at most 4096 × 4096.
- **Cache.** A player keeps every frame when all of them fit in 4 MiB, else
  the one showing and the next, decoded ahead of its time. Frames beyond
  the poster are not in the raster ledger (§4).
- **Clock and visibility.** One main-queue timer, set for the earliest frame
  change among players on screen (`VideoVisibilityHost.fraction`, the rule
  video uses). Scroll, layout, window and batch notices re-evaluate. A player
  off screen, or whose view let the image go (a recycled row), costs
  nothing; its view shows its last frame until it is seen again.
- **Presentation.** On iOS a frame swaps the image layer's `contents`, or
  redraws where `draw(_:)` paints (a tint, a radius the layer cannot say);
  macOS redraws.
- **Agent's clock.** Nothing runs on its own. Each batch and each `clock`
  shows the frame the clock says, decoded there and then, so a screenshot is
  exact. `clock` first waits, up to 3 s, for images on screen to land: an
  animation starts on the clock it lands at. `state.raster.animated` counts
  players, frames cached and decoded.

## 3. Web

Outside agent mode `<img>` plays itself. In agent mode (`?agent=1`) the
browser's own playback cannot be seeked, so `image-glue.js` holds each GIF or
WebP `<img>`: Chrome's decoder (`ImageDecoder`) gives the frames and their
durations, and the element shows the frame §1 says for the agent's clock,
swapped as a PNG blob. `clock` and the driver's `screenshot` wait until every
held image shows its frame.

## 4. Not built

- **Linux** decodes PNG only (`host/linux/src/image`); a GIF or WebP does not
  load there at all, animated or not. Declared unsupported in the parity smoke.
- Animated PNG, AVIF and HEICS paint their first frame (Chrome plays them).
- The frames a player holds are bounded (4 MiB, or two frames) but are not
  charged to the session's raster budget, and do not lower their resolution
  under pressure the way a poster decode does.
- `clock settle` does not wait for a finite animation to end; an image is
  host I/O, not the runner's motion.
- WebP composition decodes each frame's bitstream through ImageIO; a lossy
  frame with alpha goes through a synthesized `VP8X` header.
