## 1. Decisions

### D1 — a wide window adapts by itself

1. **A root tab bar is a sidebar on a wide window, where the author left placement unsaid.** On the
   web at a desktop width, on an iPad in a regular width, and on the Mac, a root `tablist` whose tabs
   have `aria-controls` is presented as a sidebar: the tabs as rows with their symbols, the selected one
   highlighted, and the panels beside it. A phone keeps the tab bar. An authored arrangement (a rail the
   app lays out, a `tablist` placed in the content) wins. LLP 1038 D12 is amended: the rail is the
   platform's default when unsaid, and the app's choice when written.
2. **A list and its detail split on a wide window with nothing new to write.** When a stack's root
   route holds a list and a row pushes its detail (a non-modal push from that row), a wide window shows
   the root and the top side by side, as `UISplitViewController` does, and a compact width keeps the
   pushed stack. On iOS the split is UIKit's, which decides when it collapses (LLP 1127). A pushed form or a modal route stays a sheet. `navigationSplit` opts out (`"none"`) or sets
   the list's width. It stays LLP 1038 D12's "two columns over one value": the router is unchanged, and
   the host places the rows it already knows. Navigation state, history, modals and retained screens
   survive a resize. The `list-detail` pattern drops its `when wide` branch.
3. **`check` advises on a phone layout at a desktop width**: a bottom tab bar stretched across the
   window, or a list that only pushes where a split would fit.

### D2 — iOS controls without tells

- **A row's swipe and its press don't race:** a fast horizontal swipe reveals the swipe actions or
  performs a full swipe, never the row's press (grocery-list).
- **An inset-grouped list's background reaches the bottom edge**, under the home indicator, in both
  schemes (chat-rooms).
- **A segmented control in a grouped row fits the row** (unit-converter): it is laid out within the
  cell's content width, shrinking its segments as UIKit's does.
- **A footer's or a text's `\n` is a line break** on iOS, as on the web with `white-space: pre-line`
  where the author wrote it (unit-converter).
- **A grouped row's checkmark is the nearest accent** (polls).

### D3 — a closed sheet leaves its background live

A sheet closed by Cancel, by its action, or by a swipe never leaves the screen beneath it inert on the
web or blank on iOS. Reproduced from chat-rooms' log; a lane is on it.

### D4 — warnings for the slips graders see

- **`lower-button-unlabelled`:** a `button` with no text, no `aria-label`, and no labelled image is
  blank to a person and to VoiceOver.
- **`lower-dialog-role`:** `role="dialog"` or `"alertdialog"` on an element that isn't a `dialog`
  gets none of a dialog's behaviour (Escape, focus, the iOS sheet). The repair is the `dialog` element,
  or a route presented modally. `navigation-stack` stops recommending the role for a modal laid out
  by hand and shows one complete `dialog` idiom: open, Save, Cancel, Escape.
- **A count reads right:** a template that puts a number before a plural noun (`${n} cards`) warns
  that it reads "1 cards", and the repair chooses the noun by the number.

### D5 — answers in one short lookup, and a choice is not a tab

1. **`contract vocab` answers for the concepts builders search for**, with the whole repair in the
   answer and no second read: scrolling to a place, loading before data, a count's plural, a choice
   of one value, a modal (`dialog`), the date and the day. Several names in one call answer together.
   Builders who don't find a recipe today go to `contract-grammar` (2,000 lines).
2. **Choosing one value is a radio group, drawn as the platform's segmented control.** A
   `tablist` switches panels. A value chosen from two to five (a goal preset, a persona) is a
   `radiogroup` whose options are radios: UIKit's `UISegmentedControl`, AppKit's segmented control, and
   on the web a segmented radio group with its semantics and arrow keys. The docs that send a choice to
   `tablist` change with it.

### D6 — fakes don't crash the web runtime

The web runtime survives a page whose timers and clock are faked (Playwright's `clock.install`):
water-tracker threw "Cannot read properties of undefined (reading get)" and drew nothing.

### D7 — one command verifies

`bun exact.mjs verify [web|ios]` builds, runs `app.test.contract`, and runs `check` in both schemes
at a phone width and a desktop width (the platform's sizes on iOS). It prints one compact report:
what passed, each finding, and where its screenshots went. Those are for milestones; nothing needs
to be read back. `test` and `check` stay; `verify` is one turn where a builder now takes three to five
(test, drive, check, screenshot). The docs say a clean `verify` is done.

*Amended 2026-10-16* (the euxy port, friction item 12: a faithful port's brand palette and small keys
were 43 findings it could not clear). app.json `verify.accept` lists findings that are the app's design,
each by `check`'s finding kind and a node's testId (or the testId of the control whose label it is; a
non-empty prefix with one trailing `*` matches a prefix, and a bare `*` is refused), with the reason. `verify` prints those under "accepted", each once with
its reason, and does not count them; an entry that took no finding is named. A finding on a node with
no testId cannot be accepted. The agent's own `check` still reports every finding.

### D8 — development storage is isolated

- A development app's storage is its own. contacts read another app's `contacts.json`, written under
  the same default app id (`com.example.contacts-exact2`), and spent five minutes on it. `exact new`
  writes an app id unique to the app, and the web host keys development storage by the app's
  directory as well as its id.
- A drive's `--storage` name isolates everything the app persists on the web (water-tracker's goal from
  a test run appeared in a fresh named store).

### D9 — status and unavailable actions in the starters

event-rsvp lost on "Full" and "Cancelled" read only in text, with Going still enabled when full.
The `rest` and `list-detail` patterns show a status badge (an `exact:content` part if it recurs) and an
action disabled with its reason when it can't be taken. The `dashboard` pattern composes two columns
on a wide window.
