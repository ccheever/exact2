**Doc lookups are the difference: 11 to 15 a build, against 1 or 2.** Each is a step, and each step re-sends the
whole context (≈30k tokens late in a build), so twelve lookups cost more than any byte we cut. They are small
and many: unit-converter asked `contract vocab` about `persist select input screen`, then `parseNumber
formatNumber round pow abs tablist option`, then `split contains concat filter select change`, then `at get nth
resource`, read three recipes, and grepped the docs, the apps and the host's Swift for a `tablist`, a `select`
and the ± key. The builder is asking, one call at a time, what the React builder already knows: which
functions exist, what a tag takes, what a part's call looks like. (Muse writes Exact2 in half SwiftUI's edits;
its gap is the same lookups.)

The rest of round 12: doc complaints in the diaries rose from 5 to 12; three are regressions of ours (the
accent Badge's tint is flagged invisible on a card, trivia, ten minutes; the chat bubble's `AccentColorText`
still fails at 4.5:1 on iOS; `help`'s `--help all` form is refused), and four are gaps (no `expect` for a
disabled control; `request` cannot expect a refusal; a part's vocab entry shows no call; `min=-1e300` must be
quoted). The losses are mostly the builders' own: state lost on reload, validation, a float shown raw.

## D1. One read for the language, instead of twelve lookups

`bun exact.mjs contract vocab` with no name prints the card, at most 8 KB, generated from the schema and the
vocab tables so it cannot drift: every function with its signature and meaning; the tags an app uses with
the attributes each takes; each `exact:content` part as a working call; every test step; the agent
operations; and the rules builders grep for: what an event hands its action (a field's text, a checkbox's
bool), `persist`'s limits, a negative `min` giving the decimal pad its ± key, a `select`'s `option`s, a
`tablist`'s tabs. start-here, still the one required read, ends by saying: run `contract vocab` once for the
whole card, then write; `contract vocab <name>` for the rare name the card leaves out. `AGENTS.md` says the
same. The recipes stay for composition a line cannot carry (a timer, money as cents, a server).

Measure: lookups made after the card is read (R12: GPT 11–15 lookups a build, Muse 14), fresh and total
tokens against React and SwiftUI.

## D2. Our regressions

- The accent `Badge` (LLP 1116.007 D3.10: the accent at 16% under the label): looked at first. If its capsule
  does not show on a card (trivia's builder: not in either scheme), the Badge's fill changes; `check` stays.
- The chat bubble (authored text in `AccentColorText` on the accent) failed `check` on the web at 4.2:1.
  Authored text keeps 4.5:1: LLP 1116.007's 3:1 exemption for `AccentColorText` (`db706103f`) is withdrawn,
  and the chat-thread recipe's bubble is chosen to pass in both schemes.
- `help` names forms that work: `bun exact.mjs <verb> --help all`, and a bare `--help all` prints help.

## D3. Gaps a build hit

- **A disabled control in a test.** `expect disabled "id"` and `expect enabled "id"`; and a `tap` on a disabled
  control does the same on every host (today it fails on iOS and passes on the web): it fails the step, saying
  the control is disabled.
- **A refusal through the server.** `request POST "/items" {...} as bob expect 409` passes when the server
  answers that status (today any non-2xx fails the step).
- **A part's vocab entry is a call.** `contract vocab EmptyState` shows a working call, not only the props
  (habit-tracker missed the `symbol:` prefix).
- **A negative literal.** `min=-1e300`, `min=-40` are numbers, as `min=40` is (today a negative must be quoted).

## D4. The bake-off measures where the gap is (a change to the process)

Expo is beaten on every count over 48 pairs (43-3-2; tokens 0.49×, minutes 0.53×, all three in 41). From round
13 its eight specs are built against SwiftUI instead, on the same iOS platform, so a round is 16 SwiftUI pairs
and 8 React: twice the signal where Exact2 is even on quality and behind on tokens, and Exact2's own arm
compares round over round unchanged (same spec, same platform). Expo returns every third round (round 15) as a
regression check. Round 13 is read by stack, never pooled across the change: the original eight SwiftUI
pairs as their own cohort, the eight moved ones beside them, and every result by builder too. Each round
reports `stepmix.py` beside the ratios.

## D5. A miswiring the compiler can see is a warning

The compiler warns where it can see what a build will refuse or ignore at run time: a `swipeTrailing` that
names a node outside its `scroll` (grocery-list spent seven minutes on it), a `navigationKey` written as a
literal on a route `each` draws, which stops every push (chat-rooms, eight minutes), and others the round-12
diaries name. `verify` shows a run-time refusal at the top of its report, not in the log.

## D6. A screen survives a reload

`verify` reloads the app on the screen each test ends on (its route and its storage) and compares what it
shows with what it showed before: a screen that reloads into another state ("0 of 0", defaults where saved
values were, a draft gone) is a finding naming the route. The patterns' screens pass it; the recipes say what
a reload keeps (the route's location, and storage) and what it does not (a state that is neither).

## Not in scope

The builders' own bugs; the web's neutral look (ruled, not yet switched on: the round after it lands measures
it); the bare `date +%H:%M` steps (the prompt asks for real timestamps; Exact2's builders take more of them in
longer builds, and fewer steps take fewer).

## Round 13

The new design (D4): 16 SwiftUI pairs (the eight Expo specs move to SwiftUI) and 8 React. Read it on doc
lookups per build (D1), tokens and grades against React and SwiftUI, and the regressions (D2).

## As built (2026-10-12)

Four lanes, merged on `bake/1116.010-integration` with origin/main; lane log `~/bakeoff/lanes-1116.010.md`.

- **D1.** `bun exact.mjs contract vocab` with no name prints the card, 7.8 KB: the 50 functions as `call ->
  result`, the tags an app uses with their attributes and events, the nine parts as calls, the test steps

  host; `request … as <persona> expect <status>`. Each part's vocab entry ends with a call that compiles. A
