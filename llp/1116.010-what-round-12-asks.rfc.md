**Doc lookups are the difference: 11 to 15 a build, against 1 or 2.** Each is a step, and each step re-sends the
whole context (≈30k tokens late in a build), so twelve lookups cost more than any byte we cut. They are small
and many: unit-converter asked `contract vocab` about `persist select input screen`, then `parseNumber
formatNumber round pow abs tablist option`, then `split contains concat filter select change`, then `at get nth
resource`, read three recipes, and grepped the docs, the apps and the host's Swift for a `tablist`, a `select`
and the ± key. The builder is asking, one call at a time, what the React builder already knows: which
functions exist, what a tag takes, what a part's call looks like. (Muse writes Exact2 in half SwiftUI's edits;
its gap is the same lookups.)

The rest of round 12: doc complaints in the diaries rose from 5 to 12; three are regressions of ours (the
accent Badge's tint is flagged invisible on a card, trivia, ten minutes; the chat bubble's `AccentColorText`
still fails at 4.5:1 on iOS; `help`'s `--help all` form is refused), and four are gaps (no `expect` for a
disabled control; `request` cannot expect a refusal; a part's vocab entry shows no call; `min=-1e300` must be
quoted). The losses are mostly the builders' own: state lost on reload, validation, a float shown raw.

## D1. One card for the language

A generated `docs/card.md`, at most 8 KB, is the second and last read before code: every function with its
signature and a one-line meaning (`parseNumber(s): option<number>`, `formatNumber(n, digits)`, `at(xs, i)`, `split`,
`contains`, `round`, …); the tags an app uses with the attributes each takes (`input type value input change
inputmode min max step`, `select value change` with `option`, `tablist`/`tab`/`tabpanel`, `list listStyle`,
`section`/`header`/`footer`, `scroll`, `image`, `link href`, `dialog`, `progress`, `routes` and the router's
verbs); every `exact:content` part as a call (`EmptyState(symbol="symbol:tray", title=…, message=…)`); every test
step; the agent operations. It is generated from the schema and the vocab tables, so it cannot drift, and a
test holds its size. start-here says: read this page, then `card.md`; then write. `contract vocab <name>` stays for
the rare thing the card leaves out.

Measure: doc lookups per build (R12: GPT 11–15, Muse 14), tokens against React and SwiftUI.

## D2. Our regressions

- The accent `Badge` (LLP 1116.007 D3.10: the accent at 16% under the label) is not "a card that does not
  show": `check` judges a badge by its label, which is legible.
- On iOS the observation names `AccentColorText` (UIKit draws it as white), so the 3:1 rule LLP 1116.007 added
  for the platform's ink reaches it, as on the web.
- `help` names forms that work: `bun exact.mjs <verb> --help all`, never a bare `--help all`.

## D3. Gaps a build hit

- **A disabled control in a test.** `expect disabled "id"` and `expect enabled "id"`; and a `tap` on a disabled
  control does the same on every host (today it fails on iOS and passes on the web): it fails the step, saying
  the control is disabled.
- **A refusal through the server.** `request POST "/items" {...} as bob expect 409` passes when the server
  answers that status (today any non-2xx fails the step).
- **A part's vocab entry is a call.** `contract vocab EmptyState` shows a working call, not only the props
  (habit-tracker missed the `symbol:` prefix).
- **A negative literal.** `min=-1e300`, `min=-40` are numbers, as `min=40` is (today a negative must be quoted).

## D4. The bake-off measures where the gap is (a change to the process)

Expo is beaten on every count over 48 pairs (43-3-2; tokens 0.49×, minutes 0.53×, all three in 41). From round
13 its eight specs are built against SwiftUI instead, on the same iOS platform, so a round is 16 SwiftUI pairs
and 8 React: twice the signal where Exact2 is even on quality and behind on tokens, and Exact2's own arm
compares round over round unchanged (same spec, same platform). Expo returns every third round (round 15) as a
regression check. Each round reports `stepmix.py` beside the ratios.

## Not in scope

The builders' own bugs; the web's neutral look (ruled, not yet switched on: the round after it lands measures
it); the bare `date +%H:%M` steps (the prompt asks for real timestamps; Exact2's builders take more of them in
longer builds, and fewer steps take fewer).
