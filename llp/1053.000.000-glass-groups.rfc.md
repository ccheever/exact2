## Summary

On iOS 26 and macOS 26, Liquid Glass elements near each other merge into one shape when they share a container: UIKit's `UIGlassContainerEffect`, AppKit's `NSGlassEffectContainerView`, SwiftUI's `GlassEffectContainer`, each with a `spacing` at which shapes begin to merge. Exact draws glass (`backgroundMaterial="glass"` → `UIGlassEffect` / `NSGlassEffectView`) but has no container, so two Exact glass buttons side by side never merge. James asked for "the concept of a glass group" and noted it makes no sense on Android.

This adds one prop, `glassGroup=<spacing>`, on any element. On Apple it makes the element's subtree one glass container; everywhere else it draws nothing and changes no layout. Measured on iOS 26.5, iOS 27.0 and macOS 27.0 (§2), a container also changes what an ancestor's opacity, clipping and paint order do to the glass inside it. The host keeps CSS's opacity and clipping by isolating a glass node whose path to its group fades or clips (D4). Paint order inside a group is declared, because nothing restores it without giving up the merge (D5).

## 1. What exists

`backgroundMaterial` is the host-policy material prop (LLP 1053.000 D3). Its `glass` and `glass-clear` rows draw `UIGlassEffect(style:)` on iOS 26 (`Backdrop.swift`, `materialEffect`) and `NSGlassEffectView` on macOS 26 (`NodeViewMac.updateMaterial`), with the node's children in the effect's content view (`NodeView.container`), `isInteractive` for an enabled pressable, and the node's radius as the glass's corners. The web draws the table's approximation (blur, saturate, tint, rim); Linux the blur and tint. Messages uses glass for its compose, header, composer and panel controls.

The platform container (iOS 26 `UIGlassContainerEffect` on a `UIVisualEffectView`, `spacing`; macOS 26 `NSGlassEffectContainerView`, `contentView`, `spacing`) renders the glass elements under it "in one combined view, behind the visual effect view's contentView" (UIKit's header) or "elevates the z-order of descendants of contentView … merges descendants … within the proximity specified in spacing … processes similar glass effect views as a batch" (AppKit's header). SwiftUI adds `glassEffectID`/`glassEffectUnion`/`glassEffectTransition`, which have no UIKit or AppKit counterpart.

## 3. Design

**D1. One prop, `glassGroup`, whose value is the spacing.** `glassGroup=12` on any element makes its subtree one glass group whose glass begins to merge 12 points apart; `glassGroup=0` groups without merging (the platforms still batch the glass and join only touching shapes, row 4). It is a prop (`kind: float`, beside `backgroundMaterial` in `schema.json`), not a style: CSS has nothing like it, and a host-policy name does not pretend to be CSS (LLP 1053.000 D3). It is layout-neutral on every host: it makes no containing block (the Apple container fills the node's box at its origin), adds no box, and the web, Linux and any future Android host ignore it, so their pixels are unchanged and only Apple's glass merges. Below iOS 26 and macOS 26 it does nothing. Contract refuses a literal that is negative or not finite; a bound value is clamped at 0.

**D2. The platform's container, as the node's group view.** On Apple the node gets one more view: iOS a `UIVisualEffectView(effect: UIGlassContainerEffect)` with `spacing`, macOS an `NSGlassEffectContainerView` whose `contentView` is a flipped plain view. It is inserted as the first subview of the node's `container` (so inside a scroll view, clip box or canvas overlay that `container` names), and the node's children move into its content view. `container` returns that content view, so later child ops land there, and hit testing walks its children as it walks glass's content today (`NodeViewIOS.hitTest`, `GlassBackground.hitTest`). A spacing change sets `spacing`; clearing the prop moves the children back and removes the view, as clearing a material does. A pooled view clears it on reuse (LLP 1068 §4.1, `NodePoolIOS` beside the material reset).

**D3. Membership is the platform's: a glass node belongs to its nearest group.** Glass merges only with glass of the same innermost group (row 5). Glass overlapping glass, or inside another glass's content, fuses into one shape (row 6); that is what merging means and is not refused.

**D4. The host keeps CSS opacity and clipping by isolating, not by declaring.** Inside a group the platform ignores the opacity and clipping of every view between the group and the glass (rows 8–11), so a fading glass button would draw at full opacity and glass in a scrolled list would draw past the list. A nested container below the fading or clipping view restores both (row 14). So: a glass node is *isolated* while any node on its path to its group — itself included, the group excluded — has opacity below 1, clips (`overflow` other than `visible`, a scroll container, a clip box), or has a `clip-path`. An isolated glass node's material view sits inside its own container (`spacing` 0): its opacity and clipping are CSS's, and it merges with nothing. It rejoins when the path is clear again.
- iOS keeps a wrapper `UIVisualEffectView` around a grouped glass node's material view and switches its effect between `nil` (joined) and a container (isolated), which needs no reparenting (row 15). The wrapper is made when the glass node's material view is made inside a group, or when a group is set above it; a fade then never moves views.
- macOS has no effect-less container: isolating moves the material view into a fresh `NSGlassEffectContainerView`, rejoining moves it back. The window's first responder is saved before the move and restored after if it was inside.
- The check runs only in a presenter that has a group (`glassGroups` non-empty): on an `opacity` present op (`PresenterIOS`/`PresenterMac`), on a style that changes `overflow` or `clip-path`, and when a group or glass node is made or moved. Each re-evaluates the glass nodes under the changed node by walking up to their group; an app without a group pays one set-emptiness test per opacity op.
- The cost is visible and correct: while a grouped glass button fades, its merge with its neighbours is gone, then returns at opacity 1 (a translucent glass cannot share an opaque neighbour's shape).

*Why isolate the glass node, not partition at the fading or clipping node?* A nested container at the clipping node would let glass under one clip still merge among itself, but it means reparenting that node's children — scroll and collection rows (LLP 1068), focused editors — on every fade. Isolating at the glass node touches only the glass's own views and is the conservative reading: a group merges only glass it can draw correctly.

**D5. Paint order inside a group is declared.** The platform draws all of a group's glass in one layer, beneath the group's other content on iOS and above it on macOS (row 7), whatever the CSS order. The glass's own content (its children, in its content view) still draws on it. Nothing restores CSS order short of isolating the glass, which gives up the merge, so it is declared in LLP 1001: a group should hold glass and the content on it, not other painted boxes that overlap that glass. The agent reports each group's glass so an overlap can be found (D7).

**D6. One refusal.** Contract refuses `glassGroup` and `backgroundMaterial` on the same element: a node has one effect view, and a glass that is its own group would fuse with every glass inside it (row 6). The message names the fix: put the group on the parent. (A bound `backgroundMaterial` that resolves on a group node at run time draws the material and logs once that the group is ignored.)

**D7. The agent sees it.** iOS and macOS `state`/`layout <node>` report, for a group node, `native.glassGroup: {spacing, drawn}` (`drawn`: `UIGlassContainerEffect`, `NSGlassEffectContainerView`, or `none` below 26); for a grouped glass node, `native.glassGroupOf: "#<group id>"` and `isolated: "opacity" | "clip"` when D4 isolated it. The web and Linux report the prop as they report any prop, and `drawn: none`.

**D8. Docs.** LLP 1001 §"Platform background materials": "Glass grouping is not implemented" becomes a paragraph stating D1–D5 with the measured deviations (rows 5–7, D4's isolation). This LLP is the record.

## 4. Build

1. `kernel/tables/schema.json`: prop 216 `glassGroup`, `float`. Regenerate through `kernel/build.rs` (never edit generated code).
2. Contract (`contract/lower`): the attribute (`tags.rs`, beside `backgroundMaterial`); the value check (`values.rs`); D6's refusal; tests in `contract/cli/tests/it/styles.rs` beside the material tests.
3. Apple, `host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/GlassGroup.swift` (new, shared iOS/macOS with `#if`, like `Backdrop.swift`): the group view (D2), the wrapper and isolation (D4), the agent fields (D7). `NodeViewIOS.swift` is 1,437 lines and `NodeViewMac.swift` 1,470 of the 1,500 cap, so they gain only call sites: `container`, the hit-test case, `applyProps`/`applyStyle` calls, the pool reset. The presenters' `opacity` case calls the D4 check.
4. Web and Linux: nothing drawn. The web writes no attribute for it (`element.rs`).
5. Tests: XCTests on both platforms (the group view and its spacing; children in its content view; clearing restores them; isolation on opacity < 1 and on clipping, and rejoining; a pooled view resets; the agent fields). A fixture, `scripts/fixtures/glass-group.contract`, with §2's rows 1, 2, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11, driven on the iOS simulator (26.x and 27) and macOS with `scripts/agent.mjs … screenshot`, compared with `~/bench/glassgroup/`.
6. A real consumer: Messages' composer row (the Add button and the composer pill) in a group, driven with the agent (type, add, send) on iOS, and once by hand for the press response of interactive glass and a focused composer while its ancestor fades.
7. The five checks, plus the iOS and macOS builds (Swift edits compile in neither the checks nor each other).

## 5. Questions for review

1. D4: isolate at the glass node (the plan), partition at the fading or clipping node, or declare and refuse (no isolation)?
2. D4: is "opacity below 1, overflow other than visible, a scroll container, a clip box, `clip-path`" the right set of path conditions? What else between a group and its glass would the platform skip (masks, filters, `mix-blend-mode`, a canvas overlay)?
3. D5: is declaring paint order enough, or should the host or the agent flag a painted box that overlaps grouped glass?
4. D1: is a float prop the right shape, or should the value accept CSS lengths (`12px`)?
5. Anything in the plan that would break an existing app, the pool (LLP 1068), navigation's moved views, modals' deferred geometry, or the canvas overlay?
