## Summary

On iOS 26 and macOS 26, Liquid Glass elements near each other merge into one shape when they share a container: UIKit's `UIGlassContainerEffect`, AppKit's `NSGlassEffectContainerView`, SwiftUI's `GlassEffectContainer`, each with a `spacing` at which shapes begin to merge. Exact draws glass (`backgroundMaterial="glass"` → `UIGlassEffect` / `NSGlassEffectView`) but has no container, so two Exact glass buttons side by side never merge. James asked for "the concept of a glass group" and noted it makes no sense on Android.

This adds one prop, `glassGroup=<spacing>`. On Apple it makes the element's mounted subtree one glass container; everywhere else it draws nothing and changes no layout. Measured on iOS 26.5, iOS 27.0 and macOS 27.0 (§2), a container ignores the opacity and clipping of the views between it and its glass, so the host isolates a glass whose path to its group fades or clips (D4): CSS's opacity and clipping hold, and that glass merges with nothing until the path is clear. Paint order inside a group is declared (D5). A group on a scroll container or a canvas, and grouping across navigation's controller views, are not offered (D6, D3).

## 1. What exists

`backgroundMaterial` is the host-policy material prop (LLP 1053.000 D3). Its `glass` and `glass-clear` rows draw `UIGlassEffect(style:)` on iOS 26 (`Backdrop.swift`, `materialEffect`) and `NSGlassEffectView` on macOS 26 (`NodeViewMac.updateMaterial`), with the node's children in the effect's content view (`NodeView.container`), `isInteractive` for an enabled pressable, and the node's radius as the glass's corners. `backdrop-filter` builds the same material view (`materialRequest`, `Backdrop.swift:125`). The web draws the table's approximation (blur, saturate, tint, rim); Linux the blur and tint. Messages uses glass for its compose, header, composer and panel controls.

The platform container (iOS 26 `UIGlassContainerEffect` on a `UIVisualEffectView`, `spacing`; macOS 26 `NSGlassEffectContainerView`, `contentView`, `spacing`) renders the glass elements under it "in one combined view, behind the visual effect view's contentView" (UIKit's header) or "elevates the z-order of descendants of contentView … merges descendants … within the proximity specified in spacing … processes similar glass effect views as a batch" (AppKit's header). SwiftUI adds `glassEffectID`/`glassEffectUnion`/`glassEffectTransition`, which have no UIKit or AppKit counterpart and are not offered.

A scratch app per platform (UIKit and AppKit, one file each), glass views over saturated stripes, compared with and without a container. ## 3. Design

**D1. One prop, `glassGroup`, whose value is the spacing in points.** `glassGroup=12` on an element makes its mounted subtree one glass group whose glass begins to merge 12 points apart. `glassGroup=0` is still a group: the platforms batch its glass and join only touching shapes (row 4). Absent (or cleared) is no group. It is a prop (`kind: float`, prop 216, beside `backgroundMaterial` in `schema.json`), not a style: CSS has nothing like it, a host-policy name does not pretend to be CSS (LLP 1053.000 D3), and float props are already bare numbers in points (`scrollLeft`, `estimatedItemHeight`). It is layout-neutral on every host: it adds no box and makes no containing block (not added to `contains_absolute` or `positioned`, `tags.rs:70`; the Apple group view fills the node at its origin and neither clips nor masks, so an absolute child that sticks out still paints). Below iOS 26 and macOS 26 it draws nothing.
- *Range.* Contract refuses a literal outside 0 … 10,000 (`numeric_literal` handles a negative literal). At run time the Apple host clamps a bound value into that range and, for a value that is not finite (the bridge narrows floats to `f32`, so a huge `f64` arrives as infinity), draws no group and logs once. The agent reports the spacing applied (D7).
- *Web.* The web writes the attribute as it does `backgroundMaterial` (`element.rs`, `glassGroup`), so a bound value compiles on the JS target, whose binding needs the prop's DOM name (`host/web-js/src/emit.rs:1151`, `style.rs:405`). No rule reads it; nothing is drawn. Linux reads nothing.

**D2. The platform's container, as the node's group view, always innermost.** On Apple a group node gets one view: iOS a `GlassGroupView` (`UIVisualEffectView` with `UIGlassContainerEffect`, `spacing`), macOS a `GlassGroupView` (`NSGlassEffectContainerView` whose `contentView` is a flipped plain view). Its place is fixed: the *base container* is what `container` names today (`scroll ?? overlay ?? glass content ?? clipBox ?? self`); the group view is the first subview of the base container, filling it (autoresizing, so a modal's deferred geometry replay sizes it too); the node's children and flat leaves are in its content view; `container` returns that content view when the group view exists. Every host path that moves a node's children moves the group view instead when there is one: scroll creation and removal (`NodeViewIOS.swift:1095/1102`, `NodeViewMac.swift:1054/1061`), the clip box (`BoxShadow.swift:140/144`), and the material rebuild (both `updateMaterial`s). A group is never on a scroll container or a canvas (D6), so the group view is never a scroll's content and never the canvas overlay's direct child.
- *Flat leaves.* Installing or removing a group view marks the node's flat leaves for placement (`FlatLeaves` gains a public `containerChanged(id)`; `flush()` re-homes them into the new container's layer). The material rebuild calls it too, which it did not.
- *Hit testing.* The group view is never a hit target: its `hitTest` tests its content view's subviews without a bounds check (visible overflow stays hittable) and returns nil when none is hit, so empty group space, and the bridge between merged shapes, fall through to the group node as before. It adds no gesture.
- *Focus.* One helper performs every view move in this LLP: it records the window's first responder (on macOS the field editor's owning `NSTextField` too, as `AgentMac` reads it), moves, and restores focus and selection if the move dropped it. A move happens only when a group or a slot is installed or removed (D2, D4) or, on macOS, when isolation changes; never per frame.
- *The pool.* `NodePoolIOS.children` accepts the group view and the iOS glass slot (D4) as the node's own views. Parking drops both, children and material back in the node, as it drops the material (LLP 1068 §4.1); the next row's props make them again.

**D3. Membership is the mounted view tree.** A glass node (a `glass` or `glass-clear` material) belongs to the nearest group view above it in the view tree, found by walking superviews. Glass merges only with glass of the same innermost group (row 5). Glass overlapping glass, or inside another glass's content, fuses into one shape (row 6); that is what merging means and is not refused. Navigation's routes live in controller views (`NavigationIOS.swift`), so a group above a navigation stack does not reach route content: put groups inside a route. A leaving view keeps its membership while it draws.

**D4. The host keeps CSS opacity and clipping by isolating the glass, not by declaring.** Inside a group the platform ignores the opacity and clipping of every view between the group and the glass (rows 8–11), so a fading glass button would draw at full opacity and glass in a scrolled list would draw past the list. A nested container below the fading or clipping view restores both (row 14). So a glass node is *isolated* while its *path* is not clear, and an isolated glass sits in its own container (`spacing` 0): its opacity and clipping are CSS's, and it merges with nothing. It rejoins when the path clears; while a grouped glass button fades, its merge with its neighbours is gone (a translucent glass cannot share an opaque neighbour's shape).
- *The path* is every view from the glass node's view up to, not including, the group view: nodes, scroll views, clip boxes, canvas overlays. It is not clear when a view on it has `alpha` below 1 (the glass node's own included), a layer `mask` (`clip-path`, a layout transition's stand-in mask; the glass node's own included), or clips its bounds (`clipsToBounds`/`masksToBounds`, which covers `overflow: hidden`, a dormant scroll, a scroll view, macOS's backdrop clipping; the glass node's own excluded, since its bounds hold its glass). A canvas overlay held at alpha 0 after capture is on it. `isHidden` and transforms are honoured by the platform (row 12) and do not isolate.
- *Opacity a host can read.* A box's opacity animation is lowered to Core Animation (LLP 1055 D7) and never reaches the host as a value. The Apple Rust host samples it instead (`Engine::set_node_sampled`, as exits already are, `presence.rs:127`) for a node inside a group: then every opacity on a grouped path arrives as a `present` op and `alpha` is the truth. A node that leaves every group goes back to lowering.
- *iOS* keeps a *slot* around a grouped glass node's material view: a `UIVisualEffectView` whose effect is `nil` while joined and a `UIGlassContainerEffect` (spacing 0) while isolated, switched with no reparenting (row 15). The slot is installed when the glass node is first found in a group (normally the batch that mounts it, before it can hold focus) and stays until the material goes or the view parks. The batch's material reorder sends the slot, not the material inside it, to the back (`PresenterIOS.swift:802`); the hit test's glass case reaches the material through it.
- *macOS* has no effect-less container: isolating moves the glass view into a fresh `NSGlassEffectContainerView` in the node, rejoining moves it back, through the focus helper. `appliedMaterial` looks through it.
- *When.* One reconciliation at the end of a batch, after flat leaves are placed and before the transaction commits, when the presenter has a group and the batch changed anything that can change a path (a `present` opacity, a style, props, children, a material, a group). It walks the registered glass views (a weak table, so a leaving view is in it while it draws) to their group and switches those whose answer changed. An app without a group pays one emptiness test per batch.

*Why isolate the glass node, not partition at the fading or clipping node?* Both reviewers agreed. A nested container at the clipping node would let glass under one clip still merge among itself, but it means reparenting that node's children — scroll and collection rows (LLP 1068), focused editors — on every fade. Isolating at the glass node touches only the glass's own views.

**D5. Paint order inside a group is declared.** The platform draws all of a group's glass in one layer, beneath the group's other content on iOS and above it on macOS (row 7), whatever the CSS order; the glass's own content still draws on it. Nothing restores CSS order short of isolating the glass, which gives up the merge, so it is declared in LLP 1001: a group should hold glass and the content on it, not other painted boxes that overlap that glass. Hits follow views, not the merged paint: where painted controls overlap inside a group, the CSS order decides the hit while the platform decides the paint. No host or agent warning; D7's fields are how a driver finds an overlap. A box with a CSS `filter` already draws its subtree without materials on Apple (`BoxFilter.swift`, LLP 1053.000 §3); grouping does not change that.

**D6. Three refusals, and what wins at run time.** Contract refuses, with the fix in the message:
- `glassGroup` beside `backgroundMaterial` or `backdrop-filter` on one element, literal or bound: the group would own the subtree's glass while the node's own material sits inside it, fused (row 6). *Put the group on the parent.*
- `glassGroup` on an element that scrolls by its tag or by a literal `overflow` of `scroll`: a scroll's content is sized and owned by the scroll and collection machinery (`fitScroll`, `NodePool.list(holding:)`). *Put the group on a child inside the scroll.*
- `glassGroup` on a `canvas`: a canvas enumerates its overlay's direct children to capture and place them (`GpuIOS.swift:341`). *Put the group on a child of the canvas.*

At run time, what Contract cannot see (a class's `backdrop-filter`, a bound `overflow` that becomes `scroll`, a producer below Contract) resolves without a crash: a material view, a scroll or a canvas overlay on the node wins, the group view is not installed (or is removed), and the host logs once; the group returns when the conflict clears.

**D7. The agent sees it.** `layout <node>` on iOS and macOS reports, beside `native.material`: for a group node, `native.glassGroup: {spacing, drawn}` where `drawn` is `UIGlassContainerEffect`, `NSGlassEffectContainerView`, or `none` with a `reason` (`os`, `material`, `scroll`, `canvas`, `spacing`); for a grouped glass node, `native.glassGroupOf: "#<group id>"` and, while isolated, `native.isolated: [reasons]` (`opacity`, `mask`, `clip`). The web and Linux report the prop as they report any prop.

**D8. Docs.** LLP 1001 §"Platform background materials": "Glass grouping is not implemented" becomes a paragraph stating D1–D6 with the measured deviations. This LLP is the record; `QUEUE.md`'s line is deleted when it lands.

## 4. Build

1. `kernel/tables/schema.json`: prop 216 `glassGroup`, `float`, with the `@ref`. Regenerate through `kernel/build.rs`.
2. Contract (`contract/lower`): the attribute (`tags.rs`), the range (`values.rs`), D6's refusals; tests in `contract/cli/tests/it/styles.rs` beside the material tests, including an absolute child under a static group (no containing block).
3. Web: `element.rs`'s arm. The JS target compiles a literal and a bound `glassGroup`.
4. Apple Rust host: sample opacity animations on nodes inside a group (D4), with a `box_motion_tests.rs` case (inside: sampled; outside: lowered; the group cleared: lowered again).
5. Apple Swift: `GlassGroup.swift` (shared, `#if` per platform): the group views, install/remove, the focus-preserving move, the iOS slot and the macOS isolation container, the reconciliation, the spacing parse, the agent fields. Call sites only in `NodeViewIOS.swift` (1,437 of 1,500 lines) and `NodeViewMac.swift` (1,470): `container`, the hit-test case, the child-move predicates, the material insertion point; stored fields in `NodeExtras` on iOS and on the class on macOS. `BoxShadow.swift`, `NodePoolIOS.swift`, `FlatLeavesIOS.swift`, `PresenterIOS.swift`/`PresenterMac.swift` (the reconciliation call, the material reorder), both agents.
6. XCTests (structural, both platforms): the group view and its spacing; children and flat leaves in its content and back when cleared; it stays innermost through a clip box appearing and going; the slot's effect switches on alpha below 1, an ancestor clip and a mask, and back; a pooled row parks and reuses; hits through empty group space reach the group node, an overflowing child is hittable; a focused text field survives a group added and removed and, on macOS, an isolation and a rejoin.
7. Pixels: `scripts/fixtures/glass-group.contract` with rows 1, 3, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11 and a container inside a scroll, driven with `scripts/agent.mjs` on the iOS simulator (26.x and 27) and macOS (`screenshot … window`, since the default capture draws no materials), compared by eye with §2.
8. A real consumer: Messages' composer row (the Add button and the composer pill) in a group, driven on iOS and macOS and on the web's default build; by hand once on iOS for the press response of interactive glass and a focused composer while its ancestor fades.
9. The five checks, plus the iOS and macOS builds (the checks compile no Swift).

## 5. Owed after landing

Interactive glass's press response measured on a device; a fade over a populated group measured for cost; grouping across navigation's controller views, if an app asks; partitioning at a clipping node, if glass under one clip should merge among itself.

## 6. Review fold (r1 → r2)
