# LLP 1075.003.000.000: The price of a hooked node

**Type:** Research
**Status:** Record, 2026-10-02 (not in `llp/current/`)
**Systems:** native modules (the `element` hook); the UIKit presenter (the node pool, flat leaves); the Extra Heavy feed benchmark
**Author:** Claude (Opus 5.5) for Charlie Cheever
**Date:** 2026-10-02
**Related:** [1075.003.000](1075.003.000-hooks-on-every-host.rfc.md) §3.3 (what a hooked node gives up), §3.4 (what it costs), §7.4–7.5 (the summary of this record); [1068](1068-recycling-heavy-views-in-list-rows.rfc.md) §6 (flat leaves, the node pool); [1050.000](1050.000-choosing-the-fill-tradeoff.rfc.md) (row reuse); `~/bench/xheavy/SPEC.md` (the feed); `~/bench/PROGRAM.md` (the series rules)

## Summary

A node marked `hook="word"` hands its view to the app's native code, and on
iOS it leaves two of Exact's optimizations: it is never drawn as a flat leaf,
and a list row that holds one is never reused (1075.003.000 §3.3). Charlie
asked that the cost be made clear. This record measures it on the Extra Heavy
feed, with every row's avatar hooked and the hook empty, on an iPhone 13 Pro
Max and an M1 iPad Pro.

- **The price.** A fling's CPU rises about 16% on both devices (+96 to +107
  ms/s). The iPhone loses about 5 fps (115.4 → 110.4) and first drops under
  110 fps at 12,000 pt/s instead of beyond 24,000. The iPad loses about 2.5
  fps. Peak memory falls 26–41 MB.
- **It is all the lost row reuse.** The same hooked build with the pool's
  hook condition removed runs as fast as the unhooked one, within noise. The
  call into the module, made at every row's mount, costs nothing measurable.
- **Nothing hooked costs nothing.** The build with the hook machinery and no
  hooked node runs as fast as its base commit, within noise, on every metric
  of both devices.
- **What followed.** A hook may now say it undoes what it adds
  (`element.reusable`, as UIKit's `prepareForReuse` asks of a cell), and its
  rows are reused again. Measured the same way, a reusable hook in every row
  costs nothing: iPhone 115.3 → 115.0 fps, CPU 573 → 583 ms/s; iPad 117.6 →
  117.6 fps, CPU 619 → 617 (§8).

## 1. Questions

1. What does a hooked node in every row of a heavy list cost a fling?
2. Is that the call into the app's module, or the optimizations the node
   gives up?
3. Does a build that has the hook machinery cost anything when nothing is
   hooked?

## 2. What was measured

### 2.1 The feed and the hooked node

The Extra Heavy feed (`~/bench/xheavy/SPEC.md`): 3,000 rows of 19 kinds (GPU
shader, canvas, SVG, video, maps, Markdown, ten fonts, carousels, GIF, WebP
and Lottie, glass, live counters, web views, two nested virtualized lists).
Every row has a header with a 36 pt round avatar, an `image`.

- **The hooked node** is that avatar: `image r.avatar hook="avatar" …` (the
  app's `app.contract`, line 427), with `"hooks": ["avatar"]` in `app.json`.
- **The hook is empty.** The app's module (`NativeMap.swift`, which already
  serves `native-map`) does not override `element`, so the module's default
  runs. Every row's mount still calls the module through its table: the C
  call, the JSON of the node's fields, an `ExactElement`.
- **An image is never a flat leaf** (only a plain box is eligible), so this
  measures the lost row reuse and the call, not the lost flat leaf (§5).
- On the simulator the hooked build journals `hook: connected`, `hook element
  avatar: a view, not a flat leaf; its row is not reused (LLP 1075.003.000)`,
  `hook element avatar is in a row of list feed: …` and one `built` per row,
  as built.

### 2.2 The builds

One copy of the app (`~/bench/xheavy/exact-hook`) built at the native-control
lane, and the unchanged app (`~/bench/xheavy/exact`) at the lane's base.
Each bundle is re-signed with the probe and stamped; every result file carries
its stamp, checked for all 360 files.

The "hooked, reuse allowed" build hands a hooked node's view to the next row
of its shape. That is wrong for an app whose hook changes the view; with an
empty hook it isolates the call from the reuse.

### 2.3 The devices and the harness

- iPhone 13 Pro Max, iOS 17.6.1 (21G101); M1 iPad Pro 12.9" (5th generation),
  iPadOS 27.2 beta (24B5089g). Both at 120 Hz.
- The program's interleaved series (`~/bench/xheavy/series.sh`): 19 kinds;
  three rounds; the build order rotated each round (PROGRAM rule 4: about 19
  ms/s of CPU comes from position alone); the device lock taken per build, the
  build installed under it, other apps terminated first (`devclean.sh`); ten
  scenarios per build per round (fling at fixed speeds, live fling, rest,
  ladder, jump, cold start, layer-rendered fling, the nested lists' fling and
  kept position); medians over rounds (`summarize.py`).
- **Series A** (two builds: unhooked, hooked): 09:52–10:45, both devices in
  parallel. **Series B** (all four): 10:58–12:40.
- Thermal state stayed nominal throughout, except "fair" on the iPhone during
  series B's `before` and `unhooked` runs and some of its `hooked, reuse
  allowed` runs. The three still agree, and the `hooked` runs, the slow ones,
  were all at nominal, so the state did not make the hook look worse.
- The iPad journaled one Jetsam event (11:49:48): a system agent
  (`nsattributedstringagent`) over its own limit while no benchmark app ran.
  No run was partial or retried.

## 4. Reading

- **Nothing hooked costs nothing (question 3).** `before` and `unhooked`
  agree on every row: fps within 0.1, CPU within 4 (iPhone) and 10 (iPad)
  ms/s, main within 1, late frames, busy, rest, nested lists. The cold start
  and peak memory differences (194 vs 187 ms on the iPhone, 346 vs 317 MB on
  the iPad) are within the spread of the three builds whose flings agree
  (the iPad's peaks: 346, 317, 336 MB), and go in no one direction. By
  construction the machinery adds a dictionary lookup or two a node
  (1075.003.000 §7.1), and this agrees.
- **The call costs nothing measurable (question 2).** With reuse allowed, the
  hooked build is the unhooked one within noise (CPU +4 and +6 ms/s, main +5
  and +3, busy +0.07 and +0.03 ms/frame, fps −0.2 and −0.1), though every
  row's mount calls the module, makes its JSON and an `ExactElement`.
- **The price is the lost reuse (question 1).** About 96–107 ms/s of CPU, all
  of it from the pool. A parked row keeps its views in the list and the next
  row of its shape takes them (`NodePoolIOS.swift`). Without that, every
  row a fling builds makes each view, moves it into the window and later walks
  it out again, which the pool's header names as most of a row's cost in
  UIKit. (LLP 1068 §6.2 measured the pool's own take and retire at 34 ms/s,
  16 once flat leaves left it.) Most of the added CPU is off the main thread
  (iPhone +98 ms/s in all, +18 on main; iPad +96, +29); which threads take
  it was not traced.
- **Memory falls for the same reason.** With no parked rows waiting, peak
  memory is 26–41 MB lower and the memory at the end 25–49 MB lower.
- **The iPhone feels it at speed.** Hooked, its fling holds 115–118 fps at
  1k pt/s, 107–112 from 3k to 12k, and 104–105 at 24k; it first drops under
  110 at 12k in every round, where the unhooked build held to −24k or beyond.
  The iPad, with more headroom, loses up to 5.5 fps from 3k to 24k and first
  drops under 110 at 24k in two rounds of three (48k unhooked).
- **The two series agree** within 9 ms/s of CPU and 0.5 fps, an hour apart,
  with different build orders.

## 5. What this does not show

- **The lost flat leaf.** The hooked node is an `image`, which is never flat.
  A hooked plain box in every row would also lose its flat leaf. LLP 1068
  §6.2 bounds that: making every eligible leaf of this feed flat took the
  iPhone's main thread 405 → 390 ms/s and the iPad's 411 → 392, so one box a
  row is a small part of it.
- **A hook that works.** The hook here is empty. What a real hook does runs on
  the main thread at each row's mount, on top of this (1075.003.000 §3.4).
- **macOS and the web.** Neither has a node pool or flat leaves, so a hooked
  node gives up nothing there but the call (1075.003.000 §3.3), and the call
  is the part this record finds free on iOS. Not measured on those hosts.
- **Other lists.** One heavy feed. A light list's rows are cheaper to make,
  so losing reuse costs less in absolute terms, and the pool's share of each
  row may differ.
- **Three rounds.** Medians of three, on two devices, one of them on a beta
  OS.

## 6. What follows (built: §8)

The pool is where the whole price lies, and it can be had back. Today a
hooked row is never parked because a parked view would carry what the app's
hook did to it into another row. A reuse moment would let the app undo that:

1. **The row parks.** Each hooked node's hook hears `elementEnded`, with its
   view still in the row, and undoes what it added: removes its interactions,
   gestures, subviews and sublayers. The handle ends.
2. **Another node takes the row.** Its hook hears `element` with `isNew`, a
   new handle and the same view, and sets the view up again.
3. **A hook that cannot undo** keeps today's behavior. A per-word opt-out in
   `app.json`, or an `ExactElement` flag the hook sets, would keep its rows
   out of the pool.

That is UIKit's own contract for a reused cell (`prepareForReuse`). The cost
moves to the app's undo, which an app that only adds an interaction pays
cheaply. The measurement above says what it would save: the whole price. It
is in QUEUE under 1075.003.000.

## 8. A reusable hook, measured (2026-10-02)

Charlie (on whether to build §6): "I think it might be pretty common. If the
opt-in version isn't too hard to build, let's give it a shot." Built as
1075.003.000 §7.6, commit abfcdb75e:

- a hook sets `element.reusable = true` when `elementEnded` undoes all it
  added to the view; the `element` call returns it, the view carries the
  mark, the node pool reuses a row whose hooked nodes are all reusable, and a
  reused view's mark is cleared for the next node's hook to decide;
- the pool's own checks still refuse a view with interactions or gesture
  recognizers left on it, so a hook that forgets costs the reuse, not
  another row;
- every hooked node a batch destroys now ends at the batch's start: the
  fixture's smoke found that a row's root is destroyed before its children
  and the pool decides at the root, so before this the dots' hooks had not
  yet taken their recognizers back when the pool looked, and no row parked.

- **A reusable hook costs nothing measurable.** It is the unhooked build
  within noise on both devices: iPhone fps −0.3, CPU +10, main +8 ms/s, late
  frames +0.2; iPad fps 0, CPU −2, main +1. The nested lists (busy 2.93–2.96
  and 3.09–3.11 ms/frame), kept positions (12/12) and rest are the same in
  all three.
- **The plain hook's price reproduced a third time:** iPhone −5.1 fps, +116
  ms/s CPU, late frames 2.8 → 5.5; iPad −2.5 fps, +98 ms/s.
- **Thermal:** the iPhone ran warmer in this series than in §2's: "fair" in
  the main flings and "serious" in the jump, cold start and layer-rendered
  fling, in the same pattern for all three builds. The iPad stayed nominal.
  The iPad journaled the same Jetsam event as before (14:05:06,
  `nsattributedstringagent` over its own limit).
- **Not measured:** a hook that adds and removes something real. Its add and
  remove are the app's work, on the main thread, at each row's mount and
  retirement; the fixture's dot (a long-press recognizer added and taken
  back) shows the contract works (`smoke.mjs ios`: rows are reused only with
  `data-reuse`), not what such work costs.
- **Found by the fixture, not fixed:** a virtualized list's live rows did not
  re-read a binding to their component's state (`data-reuse=(reuse ? …)`): the
  toggle changed one node, its own label; rows built afterwards carried the new
  value (QUEUE).

So the advice in the hook's documentation changes: hook what you need, and in
a list's rows make the hook reusable. Then a hook in every row costs what
the hook itself does, and nothing for being one.
