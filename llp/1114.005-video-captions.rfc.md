## 1. Summary

Bluesky's captions don't reach a player as a sidecar file. The poster attaches
WebVTT files to the record. The video service writes them into the HLS
playlist as subtitle renditions, and the player picks them up from there. The
clone already plays that playlist with `controls`. On Apple, AVKit's own
controls list HLS subtitle renditions, and Safari's do too, so captions may
already work there. Chrome's native HLS skips subtitle renditions, so Chrome
very likely plays the video without them.

This RFC proposes:

1. **Verify and observe the stream's own captions** (stage 1, small). Drive
   each host, then add the text tracks and the cues showing to
   `state.media`, so an agent can see them. Where an engine doesn't show
   them (Chrome, by its source), Q1 decides: an HLS adapter loaded on demand,
   or captions only on the engines that have them.
2. **HTML's `<track>`** for a sidecar WebVTT file next to a progressive video
   (stage 2, deferred). The browser renders it on the web. Elsewhere Exact
   parses the WebVTT and draws the cue text over the video in the system
   caption style. The clone doesn't need this. It moves a `rules/DEFERRED.md`
   line, so it waits for a ruling.
3. **Picking a `.vtt` file** so the composer can attach captions (D5, small).
   The source calls this clone work, but the picker refuses `text/vtt` today.

## 2. What Bluesky does

- **The record.** `app.bsky.embed.video` has `captions`: up to 20 entries of
  `{lang, file}`, where `file` is a `text/vtt` blob of at most 20 KB
  (`lexicons/app/bsky/embed/video.json`).
- **The view has no captions.** `app.bsky.embed.video#view` carries `cid`,
  `playlist`, `thumbnail`, `alt`, `aspectRatio` and `presentation`. A client
  gets captions only through the HLS playlist.
- **Playing them.** Bluesky's web player
  (`src/components/Post/Embed/VideoEmbed/VideoEmbedInner/VideoEmbedInnerWeb.tsx`)
  imports hls.js on demand and plays through it whenever `Hls.isSupported()`
  and MSE plays H.264 and AAC, Safari included. It never hands the playlist
  to the browser's native HLS. hls.js's `SUBTITLE_TRACKS_UPDATED` tells it the
  playlist has subtitle tracks. Its CC button sets `hls.subtitleTrack` to 0 or
  -1, and it repositions the cues in `video.textTracks` above its controls.
  So Bluesky's captions on Chrome come from hls.js, not from Chrome. The
  source's "`<track>` … mapped to AVPlayer's legible media selection"
  describes a sidecar design that Bluesky doesn't use.
- **Attaching them.** The composer's captions dialog picks a `.vtt` file per
  language and uploads each as a blob before the post.

## 3. What exact2 has today

- **`video`** is a replaced leaf whose `src` is HTTP(S), a bundled asset or
  an `app:/` file (`contract/lower/src/tags.rs:161-166`; LLP 1042 §3). The
  kernel gives it no children (`kernel/src/node.rs:56-68`). Contract has no
  `track` or `source` tag. `rules/DEFERRED.md:498` keeps both out "until a
  consumer asks".
- **Apple** plays it in one `AVPlayer`, behind AVKit when `controls` is set
  and an `AVPlayerLayer` otherwise (`host/apple/videoarm/VideoArm.swift`;
  LLP 1042 §2). The arm has no media-selection code: no `legible` group, no
  `select(_:in:)`, no `AVPlayerItemLegibleOutput`. Its only time observer is
  the 0.25 s periodic one behind `timeupdate` (`VideoArm.swift:142`).
  - Whatever AVKit offers for an HLS item's subtitle renditions, it offers on
    its own: its subtitle menu, and automatic selection from the system's
    caption setting (`AVPlayer.appliesMediaSelectionCriteriaAutomatically`,
    true by default).
  - LLP 1042 §2 says so: "native HLS and alternate tracks are delegated to
    AVKit".
- **The web** plays a plain `<video>` (`host/web/media-glue.js`) and touches
  no `textTracks`. No glue loads an HLS library.
  - Safari plays HLS natively and offers its subtitle renditions in its own
    controls.
  - Desktop Chrome turned on native HLS in 141–142. Its parser skips subtitle
    and closed-caption renditions (`multivariant_playlist.cc`, Chromium main
    on 2026-10-08), so its `textTracks` will most likely stay empty for
    Bluesky's playlists.
  - Firefox plays no HLS, so the clone's videos don't play there at all.
- **`state.media`** reports time, duration, size, readiness and the renderer,
  but no tracks (`VideoArm.swift:183-194`).
- **Linux and Windows** play no video (LLP 1042 §5). The terminal refuses
  `video` ("a terminal plays no media", `contract/cli/src/terminal.rs:62`).
- **The picker's `accept`** names `image/*`, `video/*`, their subtypes, and
  the types a `file_handlers` entry declares (LLP 1069.002 D1).
  - `text/vtt` is none of these.
  - It isn't in `DOCUMENT_UTIS` (`scripts/app.mjs:609-625`), so a
    `file_handlers` entry naming it is refused. Declaring it would also make
    the app a `.vtt` viewer in Finder.

## 4. What the web does

- **[`<track>`](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/media.html#the-track-element)**,
  a child of `<video>`, with `kind` (`subtitles`, `captions`, `descriptions`,
  `chapters`, `metadata`), `src`, `srclang`, `label` and `default`. The
  browser fetches the file (under CORS when the video has `crossorigin`),
  parses [WebVTT](https://www.w3.org/TR/webvtt1/), and renders the cues of a
  showing track over the video. Its native controls offer a captions menu,
  and authors style the cues with `::cue`.
- **[Text tracks](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/media.html#timed-text-tracks).**
  `video.textTracks` lists every track: those from `<track>` elements, those
  the media resource carries ([in-band tracks](https://dev.w3.org/html5/html-sourcing-inband-tracks/),
  such as HLS subtitle renditions where the browser handles them), and those
  a script adds (hls.js adds its renditions this way). Each has `kind`,
  `label`, `language`, `mode` (`disabled`, `hidden`, `showing`) and
  `activeCues`. Choosing a track is script only: setting `mode`. No attribute
  chooses an in-band track.

## 5. Design

### D1 — The stream's captions are the engine's; verify, then rule the gaps (stage 1)

For Bluesky the captions are in the stream, and LLP 1042 already gives a
stream's alternate tracks to the engine. So the first step is a drive, not
code:
- play a captioned Bluesky video in the clone on iOS, macOS and tvOS, with
  `controls`;
- open AVKit's subtitle menu;
- turn on "Closed Captions + SDH" in the system settings and check that the
  captions show without a tap;
- repeat on Safari and Chrome.

Apple and Safari failures get an issue under this LLP. Chrome is expected to
fail by its source, and a failure there isn't a bug to file but a missing
capability. Q1 rules what follows:
- **(a) An HLS adapter loaded on demand.** The web glue loads an hls.js-style
  library as a separate artifact, after first paint, for an HLS `src` on an
  engine that can't show its subtitle renditions. The library adds the
  renditions to `video.textTracks`, so D2 reads them as it reads Safari's,
  and native controls list them. It also makes the clone's videos play on
  Firefox. It is the path Bluesky's own web player takes.
- **(b) Captions only where the engine has them:** Apple and Safari, stated
  in LLP 1042 §2 and in the clone's notes. Chrome plays the video uncaptioned.

`<track>` is no answer to either: it can't load an HLS subtitle rendition.

On Apple, an `AVPlayerLayer` video (no `controls`) shows the selected legible
track but has no way to choose one. The system's setting still chooses
automatically. An app that wants its own CC button needs D3.

### D2 — Text tracks in `state.media` (stage 1)

Each `video` in `state.media` gains:
- `textTracks`: `[{kind, label, language, mode}]`;
- `activeCues`: the text of the cues currently showing.

Sources:
- **Web:** `video.textTracks` and each track's `activeCues`, whether the
  engine or Q1 (a)'s adapter made them.
- **Apple:** the item's `.legible` `AVMediaSelectionGroup`, and
  `currentMediaSelection` for which one shows. An `AVPlayerItemLegibleOutput`
  attached only while a drive reads `state` gives the cue text. Stage 2's
  sidecar tracks come from Exact's own parser.
- **Linux:** an empty list, since it plays nothing.

An agent then checks captions with `clock +N real` and `state`, and sees them
with `screenshot`. An empty `textTracks` on a captioned stream is how a drive
shows Chrome's gap. About 100 lines.

### D3 — Choosing a track without native controls (when an app asks)

HTML chooses a track only from script, by setting `mode` on an entry of
`video.textTracks`. The tracks an app here would choose among are mostly
in-band, and an in-band track has no element.

**Options:**
- **(a)** Native controls only. This is today's behaviour.
- **(b)** A choice on the `video`: which text track shows, by language, or
  none. It projects the `textTracks` list's modes, as `paused` projects a DOM
  property (LLP 1042 §3). The web sets `mode` on the matching entry; Apple
  selects in the `.legible` group, or turns on stage 2's overlay for a sidecar
  track. One mechanism covers in-band and sidecar tracks.
- **(c)** A host command naming the video and a language. That is a method
  HTML doesn't have.

r1's `track … mode="showing"` is dropped: it reaches sidecar tracks only.

**Recommended: (a) now, and (b) when an app without native controls asks.**
Its name and value vocabulary are ruled then. The clone uses `controls`, so it
doesn't ask.

### D4 — `<track>` for a sidecar WebVTT file (stage 2, deferred)

`video src="clip.mp4"` with children `track kind="captions" src="en.vtt"
srclang="en" label="English" default=true`.

- **Lowering.** `track` children lower to one prop on the `video` node: the
  list as JSON, the way `metadata=` is lowered for its fields
  (`contract/lower/src/media.rs`). The kernel holds no children for a replaced
  element and gains no node type.
  - `kind` admits `subtitles` and `captions`. `descriptions`, `chapters` and
    `metadata` are refused, each naming why: they need speech, UI or script
    events that Exact doesn't have.
  - `src` takes `video`'s sources. A file over 1 MiB is refused (Bluesky's
    limit is 20 KB).
  - At most one `default` per video, as HTML's rule for the captions and
    subtitles kinds.
- **Web.** The glue puts real `<track>` elements in the `<video>`. The browser
  fetches, parses and renders them, and `::cue` styling follows. A
  cross-origin `src` needs `crossorigin` on the video and CORS headers from
  its server, as on any page. The browser stays the only full cue renderer.
- **Apple (iOS, macOS, tvOS).** The arm draws the cue text itself.
  - **What doesn't work:**
    - AVFoundation offers a legible selection only for tracks in the asset,
      or for an HLS playlist's subtitle renditions.
    - `AVMutableComposition` can't make a subtitle media-selection group from
      a VTT file. An Apple engineer said so on the developer forums
      ([thread 45060](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/45060)).
    - Rewriting the manifest through an `AVAssetResourceLoaderDelegate` helps
      only when the video is already HLS. A progressive MP4 isn't a valid HLS
      segment.
    - `AVPlayerItemLegibleOutput` reads in-band tracks only.
  - **What it does:**
    - The host loads the file as it loads `src`.
    - A small Rust WebVTT parser, shared with Linux's `state.media`, reads
      cue timing, identifiers and text with the `b`, `i`, `u`, `c`, `v` and
      `lang` spans. Cue settings (`line`, `position`, `size`, `align`),
      regions, ruby and inner timestamps are ignored, and the agent's `state`
      lists what a track used and was ignored.
    - The arm draws the active cues at the default place, centred near the
      bottom, in a layer above the picture: over the `AVPlayerLayer` inline,
      and in AVKit's `contentOverlayView` when AVKit shows the video (full
      screen included).
    - Styling is the user's caption style (`MACaptionAppearance`,
      MediaAccessibility), as the system's own captions are.
    - A boundary time observer at each cue's start and end shows and hides
      cues, and it is re-armed on a seek or a rate change.
  - Matching the browser's cue placement is not the bar. AVKit's own captions
    for in-band tracks already follow Apple's renderer and the user's style,
    not the browser's layout.
  - AVKit's subtitle menu doesn't list a sidecar track. That limitation is
    stated. `default` and D3 (b), once ruled, control it.
- **Linux, Windows.** No playback. `state.media` lists the tracks.
- **Terminal.** Refused, as `video` is.

### D5 — Picking a `.vtt` file (with stage 1)

The composer needs `input type=file accept="text/vtt,.vtt"`. Today that is
refused (§3).

**Options:**
- **(a)** Admit `text/vtt` in `accept` beside images and video, as a picker
  type with no `file_handlers` entry. iOS uses
  `UIDocumentPickerViewController` with the type from the `.vtt` extension,
  macOS uses `NSOpenPanel`, and the web uses its own input. The file arrives
  as `app:/tmp/picked/<n>.vtt` (LLP 1069.002 D4).
- **(b)** Let `file_handlers` declare it. Rejected: it registers the app as a
  `.vtt` viewer, which is a side effect the clone doesn't want.

**Recommended: (a).** It is one line in `rules/DEFERRED.md`'s picker entry (`:175`)
("still no picker for any file") and one type in the bake's check. The upload
itself is clone work: `com.atproto.repo.uploadBlob` with
`exactBodyFrom: path` and `content-type: text/vtt`.

### D6 — The grant

None. A track's `src` loads as the video's `src` does: a view's source, not a
data module's `fetch`. Q1 (a)'s adapter fetches the playlist and segments as
the browser would for the same `src`. Picking a file needs no grant (LLP
1069.002 D4). Uploading the file is `net.fetch` to the PDS, which the clone
already has.

### D7 — The agent's view

- **Stage 1.** The drive opens a captioned post and taps play. It runs `clock
  +3000 real`, then reads `state.media.<id>.textTracks` and `activeCues`, and
  takes a screenshot. This runs on iOS, macOS, Safari and Chrome; with Q1 (a),
  Chrome and Firefox go through the adapter.
- **Stage 2.** `apps/video-player` gains a short `.vtt` beside `motion.mp4`.
  The drive checks the cue text at two times on the web and Apple, and checks
  `state.media` on Linux. Screenshots show the cue is drawn; they aren't
  compared for its place.
- **D5.** `type @t captions.vtt` answers the picker natively, and `tap
  <input> drop captions.vtt` answers it on the web.

## 6. Questions for Charlie

1. **Must captions work in Chrome, and by what?** Chrome's native HLS skips
   subtitle renditions (§3), so stage 1's Chrome drive will most likely
   fail. Astra: yes, Chrome is in the clone's intended coverage; admit an
   adapter loaded on demand after first paint, costed separately, or approve
   narrower coverage explicitly. Grok: no question until the drive fails, and
   then the follow-up is in-band selection and display on that host, not
   `<track>`. Both agree that `<track>` doesn't answer it. *Recommended: rule
   it now, conditional on the drive: (a), the adapter (D1).* It is the path
   Bluesky's own player takes, it is the separate artifact CLAUDE.md asks for
   optional capability, and it also makes the clone's videos play on Firefox.
   Choose (b) if a third-party player library on the web is a cost you won't
   take for this.
2. **Move `track` off `rules/DEFERRED.md`?** LLP 1098's entry keeps `source`
   and `track` children out "until a consumer asks". *Recommended: not yet*,
   and both reviewers agree. Bluesky's captions are in the stream (D1), so the
   clone isn't a sidecar consumer. Move `track` when an app plays a
   progressive video with a sidecar file. If you want it now, it needs a take
   or your waiver.
3. **Admit `text/vtt` in the picker's `accept` (D5 a)?** *Recommended: yes*,
   and both reviewers agree. It is the one thing the clone can't do without
   Exact2.

## 7. What the clone does after

- **Showing captions:** nothing on Apple and Safari, if D1's drive passes.
  Its `video src=post.video.playlist controls=true` already gets AVKit's
  menu. On Chrome and Firefox, nothing after Q1 (a); after (b), they play
  uncaptioned. This was read from the clone's deploy copy of 2026-09-27;
  check the current one. If the drive fails on Apple or Safari, the fix is
  Exact2's, under D1.
- **Attaching captions, after D5:** the composer's captions dialog. One
  picker per language, each file checked against 20 KB from `Picked.size`,
  uploaded with `uploadBlob`, and placed in the record's `captions` with its
  `lang`. S–M, as the source says.
- **Verification:** a drive posts a video with an English VTT to a stub, then
  once live from a test account with your OK. It reads the record back and
  checks `captions`. After the service has transcoded the video, it plays the
  post and reads `state.media.textTracks`.

## 9. Not in scope

- Choosing a text track from Contract, until an app without native controls
  asks (D3).
- `descriptions`, `chapters` and `metadata` tracks; `cuechange` events;
  `addTextTrack()`.
- Cue settings, regions and ruby on native hosts. Styling cues beyond the
  user's system caption style.
- Audio-track selection.
- Playing HLS in Firefox, except as Q1 (a) gives it.
- Making captions: speech to text, or a caption editor.
