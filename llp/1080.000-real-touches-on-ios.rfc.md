## Summary

On iOS the agent's finger is not a finger:
- `tap` hit-tests the window and calls the presenter (`delivery: activation`,
  or `host-activation` for native controls; `AgentIOS.swift:408-568`).
- A contact's phases are `unsupported` on a phone.
- On a simulator, contact phases depend on `host/apple/pointer.swift`
  posting a Mac mouse into the simulator's window (§1).

UIKit's own touch path has never been under the driver: HID delivery,
hit-testing, gesture recognizers, delayed touches and real timestamps. The
`_delayTouchesForEvent:inPhase:` nil-insertion crash (LLP 1012) passed every
agent drive. A person found it on their first touch.

This RFC adds a **touch runner**: an XCTest UI-test bundle, started with the
session, that takes the driver's JSON lines over the outbound token bridge
the phone carrier already uses. It injects touches with **public
`XCUICoordinate`**:
- The touches are real and come from HID.
- It works on a simulator and on a phone.
- It needs no Accessibility permission, no visible window and no unlocked Mac.

Under `--touch platform` a `tap` becomes a real touch at a point the host
resolved. The reply says `delivery: platform` only once the app's window
logged that touch. **Held contacts stay `unsupported`** until a stage-0 probe
shows that one touch can stay down across separately acknowledged requests.
Only XCTest's private event synthesis could do that, and it is admitted only
if the probe passes.

## 1. What exists (checked on this Mac, 2026-10-03)

- **Xcode 27.0 (27A266a).** UI automation is its own framework,
  `Platforms/iPhone{Simulator,OS}.platform/Developer/Library/Frameworks/XCUIAutomation.framework`.
  `Developer/Library/Xcode/Agents/XCTRunner.app` exists for both platforms.
- **Public API.** In `XCUICoordinate.h`, `tap`, `pressForDuration:` and
  `pressForDuration:thenDragToCoordinate:[withVelocity:thenHoldForDuration:]`
  are each one whole gesture: the press and the lift both happen inside the
  call, and the call waits for the app to go idle. `XCUIDevice.orientation`
  is a `UIDeviceOrientation` (`XCUIDevice.h:55`), not an interface
  orientation.
- **Private synthesis.** `XCUIAutomation` exports `XCSynthesizedEventRecord`
  and `XCPointerEventPath` (`nm -gU`). Its strings include:
  - `initWithName:interfaceOrientation:` and `addPointerEventPath:`;
  - `initForTouchAtPoint:offset:`, `pressDownAtOffset:`,
    `moveToPoint:atOffset:` and `liftUpAtOffset:`;
  - `eventSynthesizer` and `synthesizeEvent:completion:`.

  No selector names a continuation or a persistent touch identity. Every
  path begins with `initFor…AtPoint` and is a new pointer. Whether a path
  with no lift stays down after its record completes is unknown (P3).
- **The `.xctestrun` contract** (`man 5 xcodebuild.xctestrun`).
  - `UITargetAppPath` is mandatory for UI tests (`:109`), and with it
    xcodebuild installs the target.
  - `UseDestinationArtifacts` (`:222`) installs nothing. It replaces those
    paths with `TestHostBundleIdentifier`,
    `TestBundleDestinationRelativePath` and `UITargetAppBundleIdentifier`.
  - `TestingEnvironmentVariables` reaches the runner.
- **The desktop pointer, as it is today.**
  - The window is found by bundle identifier and title (`pointer.swift`).
  - For Device Hub, the driver captures the simulator's framebuffer with
    `simctl io screenshot`, captures the window, and image-matches the two
    (`locateScreen`, `scripts/png.mjs:13`; `agent.mjs:683-697`). The older
    hover calibration (`agent.mjs:698-707`) remains for Simulator.app.
  - The helper binary is rebuilt by mtime (`agent.mjs:723-729`).
  - Verified limit: on this Mac the smoke's step 4c prints `unsupported`,
    because the terminal has no Accessibility permission (`QUEUE.md`,
    2026-10-02).
  - Historical, not re-verified: the 2026-09-30 hover failure under Device
    Hub, and a locked screen at a 2026-10-02 landing.
  - I found no record of the shipped helper completing a held contact.
- **Other HID paths.** SimulatorKit is now in `Contents/SharedFrameworks`.
  `axe` uses its Indigo HID path. Per `QUEUE.md`, axe starts neither the edge
  pop nor a `UITabBar` selection. idb is not installed.
- **Prior XCTest evidence.** LLP 1035.003 §3, "Candidate 2" (2026-09-10):
  public `XCUICoordinate` presses drove Messages' row tap, interactive Back
  cancel and complete, and sheet dismiss and resist, while the driver read
  the agent socket. Its `/tmp` evidence is gone, and it ran under a launch
  whose presentation was substituted by agent mode (D7).
- **Agent-mode substitutions** test `agentMode`, not timing:
  - the tab bar is hidden (`NavigationTabsIOS.swift:103,109`);
  - navigation bars are suppressed (`NavigationBarIOS.swift:245,521,557`);
  - popovers are painted (`MenusIOS.swift:109,289,301,336`);
  - a calibration hover recognizer is added (`PresenterIOS.swift:136`).
- **Clocks.** Agent timing sets `session.clock = 0` at launch
  (`ExactIOS/main.swift:188`). Platform timing leaves it `nil`, so `now()`
  reads wall time (`Session.swift:550`) until a `clock` operation assigns it
  (`Agent.clock`).
- **Line counts** (cap 1,500): `host/apple/build.mjs` 1,498,
  `scripts/smoke.mjs` 1,499, `scripts/agent.mjs` 1,481,
  `host/apple/touch.mjs` 85.

## 3. Decisions

### D1 — The runner, and its launch contract

- **Source.** `host/apple/touches.swift`: one `XCTestCase` with one test,
  `testDrive`.
  - It connects outward with `EXACT_TOUCH_CONNECT` and `EXACT_TOUCH_TOKEN`,
    sends the `{ready, token}` hello, and answers lines until the driver
    hangs up.
  - Its target is `XCUIApplication(bundleIdentifier:)`. It never calls
    `launch()`, `activate()` or `terminate()`.
- **Build.** `host/apple/touches.mjs` exports `touchRunner({destination,
  udid})`, imports `profile`, `identity` and `phoneBridge` from `build.mjs`,
  and adds nothing to `build.mjs`. It:
  - compiles the source into `ExactTouches.xctest` (`xcrun --sdk <sdk>
    swiftc -bundle`, linking XCTest and XCUIAutomation);
  - copies `XCTRunner.app` to `ExactTouches-Runner.app` (bundle id
    `com.exact.touches.xctrunner`) with the bundle in `PlugIns/`;
  - embeds the frameworks;
  - signs ad hoc for a simulator, or with the team profile and identity for
    a phone;
  - installs the runner (`simctl install` or `devicectl device install
    app`).
- **Cache key.** Destination (`iphonesimulator`｜`iphoneos`), arch, SDK
  version (`xcrun --sdk <sdk> --show-sdk-version`), Xcode build, and the
  source's SHA-256. Signing is checked separately on every phone start:
  profile expiry, that the profile covers the phone, and the identity's
  SHA-1. A failed check re-signs; it never rebuilds.
- **`.xctestrun`** (FormatVersion 2, one configuration, one target):

  ```
  TestHostBundleIdentifier          com.exact.touches.xctrunner
  TestBundleDestinationRelativePath __TESTHOST__/PlugIns/ExactTouches.xctest
  UITargetAppBundleIdentifier       <the app's id from app.json>
  UseDestinationArtifacts           true
  IsUITestBundle                    true
  TestingEnvironmentVariables       { EXACT_TOUCH_CONNECT: <ip:port>, EXACT_TOUCH_TOKEN: <hex> }
  ```

  Started as `xcodebuild test-without-building -xctestrun <file>
  -destination id=<udid>`. The PID is recorded (RULES §Fleet).
- **Order (eager).** Under `--touch platform`, `open()` does this:
  1. starts the runner and waits for its hello (30 s bound);
  2. then launches the app by the existing path;
  3. then checks with the runner that the app is `runningForeground`.

  The runner's own launch can take the foreground, so it must come first.
  It is never started lazily under a running app. P1 is the proof.

### D2 — Ownership, foreground and teardown

- **One runner per device.** `touches.mjs` holds the lock
  `$TMPDIR/exact-touches-<udid>.lock` (the driver's PID; a stale lock with a
  dead PID is taken over). A second session on the same device refuses:
  `the device's touches are held by PID n`.
- **Foreground, before every injection.** The runner checks
  `XCUIApplication(bundleIdentifier:).state == .runningForeground`. The
  host's aim (D4) checks that the app is `.active`, that the target's scene
  is `.foregroundActive`, and that the target's window is the key window. A
  failure refuses, by name, before anything is injected.
- **Teardown.** On close, timeout, an error, or a hang-up from either side:
  1. If a contact is held (stage 2 only), the driver sends `up` and waits
     for it to be acknowledged, at most 1 s.
  2. The runner also lifts whatever it holds on EOF, before `testDrive`
     returns.
  3. Then the driver waits for xcodebuild, at most 3 s, and kills only its
     recorded PID.

  Public taps hold nothing between requests, so for stage 1 this path is
  only "end the test".

### D3 — Held contacts: unsupported until proven

With option B, every gesture is complete within one call, so phases on iOS
reply:
`{"phase":…, "delivery":"unsupported", "reason":"no held contact across requests on iOS (LLP 1080.000 P3)"}`.
The exceptions are unchanged: `recognizedPan` on a `pan` node, and a
canvas's recognized contact (`CanvasInputIOS.swift:120-160`). Both still
reply `recognized`.

**P3, the held-contact probe** (stage 0, simulator and phone), with the SPI
as WebDriverAgent's class-dumped headers declare it. These signatures are
not verified against Xcode 27 beyond the selector names in §1:

```
XCSynthesizedEventRecord  -initWithName:(NSString*) interfaceOrientation:(UIInterfaceOrientation)
                          -addPointerEventPath:(XCPointerEventPath*)
XCPointerEventPath        -initForTouchAtPoint:(CGPoint) offset:(double seconds)
                          -moveToPoint:(CGPoint) atOffset:(double)   -liftUpAtOffset:(double)
XCUIDevice                -eventSynthesizer → <synthesizeEvent:(record) completion:(void(^)(BOOL, NSError*))>
```

The probe has three steps:
1. Record 1 presses and never lifts. When its completion arrives, the
   runner acknowledges `down`.
2. The driver then runs `layout`, `state` and `screenshot`, and sends
   `move`, `hold`, a reversing `move` and `up`. Each is its own record,
   acknowledged before the next request.
3. The app's dispatch log (D5) is the evidence.

**Exit:**
- every `move` and the `up` belong to the touch identity that began at
  `down`;
- there is no `ended` or `cancelled` dispatch before `up`;
- all three reads answered while the touch was down;
- all of this on both destinations.

**Pass:** stage 2 builds phases on the SPI, in the runner only. **Fail:**
phases stay `unsupported` on iOS, the SPI is never used, and the record
says so. No contact is ever replayed from a script.

### D4 — Aim: a side-effect-free resolver, and orientation

`{"op":"tap","id":V,"aim":{"x":X,"y":Y}}` is a private carrier message. The
point is in the viewport space and is optional, defaulting to the visible
centre. `at <x> <y>` stays an offset from the box (`agent.mjs:1079`). The
host runs the aim **before** every branch that mutates: `canvasTap`,
`menus.agentTap`, `menus.activate`, `controls`, `segments` and
`swipeActions`. The aim changes nothing.

It returns one of three things:

```
{"aim":{"seq":S,"session":"main","incarnation":I,"orientation":"portrait"|"portraitUpsideDown"|"landscapeLeft"|"landscapeRight",
        "screen":{"w","h","x","y"},                    // the viewport's origin in the scene's coordinate space, points
        "point":[px,py],                               // viewport point + origin
        "node":V}}                                     // an Exact view will be under it, by window.hitTest
{"aim":{…same tags…, "native":{"kind":"tabBarButton"|"navigationBarButton"|"segment"|"swipeAction"|"alertAction"|"menuItem","label":L,"index":k}}}
{"error":"tap #V: <why>"}                              // off the viewport, obscured, hidden by placement, not foreground, rotated
```

- **Native items are named, never placed.** A tab adopted by a
  `UITabBarController`, a projected segment (`SegmentsIOS.swift:205,339`
  hides the authored views), a revealed swipe action, a confirmation's
  `UIAlertAction` (no public geometry, `AgentIOS.swift:283`) and a native
  menu item are all resolved by the host to `native` with UIKit's own
  label and index. The runner finds the element with an XCUIElement query
  (`app.tabBars.buttons.element(boundBy:)`, `app.alerts.buttons[L]`, …)
  and taps its own frame. A hidden authored box is never a coordinate.
- **Orientation.**
  - `layout.screen` gains `orientation`, the owning
    `UIWindowScene.effectiveGeometry.interfaceOrientation`.
  - The point is in that scene's interface-oriented coordinate space, which
    is the space `XCUIApplication`'s coordinates use. P4 checks that claim
    in all four orientations. The runner taps
    `app.coordinate(withNormalizedOffset: .zero).withOffset(point)`.
  - Before injecting, the driver re-reads `layout.screen` and refuses if
    `orientation`, the origin or the size differ from the aim's: `the
    screen rotated or moved since aim`.
  - Device orientation is never used. A locked app reports its interface
    orientation whatever the device's is.
  - Each pane of the sample host reports its own scene and origin.

### D5 — The dispatch log: what the window dispatched, and nothing more

- **Where it records.** ExactKit gains `ExactWindow: UIWindow`, used by both
  mains (`ExactIOS/main.swift:231`, `ExactHostIOS/main.swift:222`). Its
  `sendEvent` calls `super.sendEvent`, then records each touch in the event,
  under the agent only.
- **What each entry holds:**
  - `seq`, monotonic per process;
  - `touch`, a small integer assigned the first time each `UITouch` object
    is seen;
  - `phase`: `began`, `moved` or `ended`. `moved` is one entry per event,
    carrying the last point. `stationary` is not recorded;
  - `t`, the touch's `timestamp` in seconds;
  - `at`, the window point;
  - `session` and `incarnation` of the `NodeView` under `touch.view`, and
    its node id, or `null` with the view's class.
- **Bounds.** A ring of 256 entries. Older entries are dropped and counted.
- **Reading it.** `{"op":"tap","log":S}` returns the entries after `seq` S,
  at most 32 per reply, with `next`, `dropped` and `truncated`. It answers
  immediately and never waits, because requests run serially on the main
  run loop (`Agent.swift:101`), which is also what dispatches touches.
- **The barrier.** The driver takes `seq` S from the aim before injecting.
  After the runner acknowledges, it polls the log every 16 ms for at most
  1 s.
  - A `tap` is matched when exactly one new touch identity shows `began` and
    then `ended`.
  - No such touch: error `no touch reached the app's window within 1 s;
    the runner finished at <wall>`.
  - Two or more new identities: error `ambiguous: N touches after seq S`,
    with the entries listed.
  - The ring dropped past S: error `log truncated past seq S`.
  - The touch landed in another session or incarnation than the aim's:
    error, naming where it landed.

  The reply says `delivery: platform` only on a match.
- **What it does not claim.** Recognizer cancellation reaches views through
  `touchesCancelled`, not through the window, and delayed touches separate
  dispatch from responder delivery. So the log reports **window dispatch
  only**. It has no `cancelled` and no "handled". Whether a press activated
  something, or a recognizer won, is asserted from `state` and `tree`, as
  today. It is never inferred from the injected path.

### D6 — Clocks

- **Agent timing** (the default): the runner's clock is 0 at launch and
  moves only by `clock`. Touch recognition runs on UIKit's real time: the
  touch timestamps and UIKit's delayed-touch and long-press timers. A press
  that UIKit delivers commits at the seeked clock.
- **Platform timing**: the clock reads wall time until the first `clock`
  operation assigns it. UIKit's transitions animate. *Corrected by §11:* the
  host's `now()` does, but the runner's clock, which Contract's `now()` and
  timers read, need not: a dispatch passes the host's time to
  `Host::dispatch_at`, which keeps it as the host's (and motion's) time,
  while the runner's own clock moves only when the runner advances; a motion
  `tick` moves the host's time, not the runner's. Agent mode never advances
  the runner on the frame pump (`Session.swift`, `!agentMode`), so without
  `clock` it stays put. Observed on the Signal clone under `--timing platform` with no `clock` op:
  the journal stamps `pointerdown` and `pointerup` 1.5 s apart both `t=0`,
  and a 1.5 s hold sends no voice note. *And by §12:* the first `clock`
  starts at the host's wall time, not the runner's 0. I make no claim about
  how an interactive transition finishes in either mode. G1 and G3 run in
  each mode separately, and each reports its own results.
- **Replies.** A reply keeps `observed.wall`, a scalar in ms. The injection
  gets its own field, `injected: {start, end}`, in Unix ms from the runner.

### D7 — Native presentation under `--touch platform`

`--touch platform` launches with `EXACT_AGENT_PRESENTATION=native`, and
ExactKit adds `ExactEnv.agentPaints = agentMode && presentation != "native"`.
The presentation branches move from `agentMode` to `agentPaints`. These are
the tab bar, navigation bars and popovers in §1, and the calibration
recognizer is deleted outright (D9). With `native`:
- UIKit's `UITabBar`, navigation bars and popovers show, exactly as in a
  person's app;
- `tree` still lists the authored nodes, and `layout` marks adopted ones
  `native`;
- `tap` on them aims at the native element (D4).

**G3 must name the container and control it touched**, from the log entry's
view class and the aim's `native`. Until stage 3, any case that needs a
native container answers:
`unsupported: native presentation lands at stage 3`.

### D8 — Wire, capability, precedence, bounds

**Session opt-in.** `open({touch: 'platform'})`, CLI `--touch platform`.
Without it, iOS input is exactly as today. With it, a runner that fails to
start fails `open()`, naming xcodebuild's last 20 lines. The driver never
falls back to activation.

**Capability** (`open().input` on iOS, under `--touch platform`):

```
{"contact": <P3 passed>, "hold": <P3 passed>, "runner": "xctest-coordinate",
 "delivery": {"press": "platform", "down|move|hold|up": "unsupported" | "platform", "cancel": "unsupported",
              "contextmenu|dblclick|hover|pinch": "recognized", "wheel": "activation"}}
```

**Runner wire** (driver ↔ runner; private):

```
→ {"op":"tap","point":[x,y]}                         → {"done":true,"injected":{"start":ms,"end":ms}}
→ {"op":"tap","native":{"kind":K,"label":L,"index":k}} → the same, or {"error":"no <K> labelled L"}
→ {"op":"foreground"}                                → {"state":"runningForeground"|…}
(stage 2, only if P3 passed) {"op":"down","point"} {"op":"move","path":[[x,y,ms],…]} {"op":"up"}
(§11) {"op":"drag","point":[x,y],"to":[x,y],"press":s,"velocity":pt/s,"hold":s} → the same as tap
```

**Bounds:**
- a `move` path holds at most 240 samples and lasts at most 10 s;
- `hold` lasts at most 10 s;
- a runner reply is at most 4 KB;
- the log is bounded as in D5;
- past a bound the request is refused, never clipped.

**A `tap` reply** under `--touch platform`:

```
{"tapped":V,"at":[x,y],"delivery":"platform","carrier":"ios-sim"|"ios-device","mode":"agent"|"platform",
 "landed":{"session":"main","incarnation":I,"node":N|null,"view":"UITabBarButton"?},
```

**Precedence for a phase.** `recognizedPan`, then a canvas's recognized
contact, then the runner (stage 2 only), otherwise `unsupported`. **For a

used under `--touch platform`.

`tags` is. They are not in the CLI grammar or the library, so they are not
operations (DEFERRED `:491`).

**The form.** `tap <target> drag <dx> <dy> [from <x> <y>] [press <ms>]
[over <ms>] [hold <ms>] [during "<op>" …]`; in the library
`s.tap(target, {drag: {dx, dy, from, press, over, hold, during}})`.
- One gesture: the finger presses for `press` ms (default 0), drags in one
  straight line by (`dx`, `dy`) points over `over` ms (default 250), holds
  `hold` ms (default 0), and lifts. A zero delta is a still press for
  `press + hold`.
- `from` is an offset from the target's box, as `down at <x> <y>` is (not
  §10's viewport point), and defaults to its middle. D4's aim resolves it,
  occlusion refusal included; an edge start names a target whose box reaches
  the edge (the app's root), never an exemption.
- Bounds (`DRAG_BOUNDS`, `touches.mjs`): `press` and `hold` ≤ 10 s, `over`
  ≤ 2 s so the moves fit D5's 256-entry ring, the whole gesture ≤ 10 s; every
  value finite and non-negative, `from` two finite numbers; a delta needs
  `over > 0`; the start and the end must lie in the viewport. Past a bound the request is refused, never clipped.
- `during` is the last option: quoted ops, nothing else. They are reads and
  the clock only (`tree`, `layout`, `state`, `logs`, `screenshot` but not a
  filmed one, `clock`);
  any other op, a `perf` that could drive one included, is refused, and the
  session refuses `tap`, `type`, `prefer`, `pointer` and ticket answers while
  they run (a library thunk included). They run after the press and the move,
  inside the hold (since 2026-10-04, so a screenshot during a drag shows it
  moved), so `during` needs `hold > 0`, the time they run in, and is refused
  without one: under the runner the lift is scripted, and the ops would find
  the finger lifted. Under the runner each is bracketed by the log: the ops
  start only after the touch's `began` is logged and the press and the move
  have elapsed, and the reply is an error if an `ended` is logged before the
  last op finishes ("lengthen hold"); "lifted" means the one new touch's `ended`,
  and two new touches are the barrier's ambiguity error. Every read and op
  races the gesture, one op at a time: past its end and a second's grace no
  further op starts, the app's carrier is stopped (the runner has already
  finished), and the reply, a transport failure with no diagnostic read,
  says the ops outlasted it. Without the runner the carrier's phases press,
  move, run the ops, hold and lift, each op bounded by the gesture's 10 s. Whatever fails, the gesture is let finish first, and
  the runner's error wins. "While down" is the window's view: requests and
  touch dispatch share the main run loop, so an op that finished before a
  read that found no `ended` finished before the window dispatched the
  lift. The replies come back in `during`.

**Under `--touch platform`** (`realTap` with `drag`, `touches.mjs`):
- The aim, the second aim, the foreground check and the barrier are a
  `tap`'s (D2, D4, D5). The runner's new `drag` op calls
  `press(forDuration:thenDragTo:withVelocity:thenHoldForDuration:)`, or
  `press(forDuration:)` for a zero delta.
- `delivery: platform` only when the log shows exactly one new touch, begun
  on the node the aim hit-tested at the aimed window point and lifted at that
  point plus the delta, each within 1 pt or 5% of the distance, with at least
  one `moved` for a delta. The reply adds
  `touch.travel`, `touch.lastMove`, `drag` and `during`.
- Where the code is: `host/apple/touches.swift` (the runner's `drag`),
  `host/apple/touches.mjs` (`realTap` with `drag`, `DRAG_BOUNDS`),
  `scripts/agent-drag.mjs` (validation, the viewport, the route, the
  composite), `NavigationIOS.swift` (the fix below).
- **Velocity is points per second.** The header calls it pixels per second;
  on a 3x simulator a drag at `v` covers `v` points a second (asked 400 ms,
  measured 400 ms from `began` to `ended`; scaled by 3 it took a third of
  that). The driver passes distance over `over`.

**Elsewhere** the driver composes the carrier's own phases: `down` (at
`from`), the `during` ops, `hold press`, `move by dx dy over`, `hold`, `up`.
The reply carries the `down`'s delivery: `platform` on the web (CDP
touches) and macOS (the held mouse), `presenter` on Linux. On iOS without
the runner only the recognized contacts hold (a `pan` node's pan, a
canvas's contact), and they reply `recognized`; anything else replies
`unsupported`, naming `--touch platform`. On every path the start and the
end must lie in the viewport (under the runner checked again against the
start and viewport the host's aim resolved), each phase's reply is checked,
a failure or refusal while the contact is down cancels it (else lifts it;
a `down` that threw is released at the carrier; if neither is confirmed the
contact stays recorded and the error says so),
and no input is accepted while `during` runs.

## 12. Platform timing's first `clock` (fix, 2026-10-04)

Reviewed by Astra and Grok (`llp/reviews/code-2026-10-04-first-clock.{astra,grok}.md`).
Found by the Signal clone's agent on build 13: under `--timing platform`,
`tap bubble-… drag 160 0 from 30 20 over 400` replied `delivery: platform`
with the asked travel, and `replyId` stayed empty. Reproduced on clone
76f0d88 against exact2 main, then traced with an instrumented build: the
swipe's `holdBegin` was refused with `hold: ClockWentBackwards`, so no
`swiperight` was ever sent. Agent timing, and platform timing with no
`clock` op before the swipe, both worked.

**Cause.** Until the first `clock` op, platform timing leaves
`session.clock` nil, so the host's `now()` is the wall's, and motion and
holds run on it. `Agent.clock` started every seek from `session.clock ?? 0`,
the runner's 0, where the driver's numbers start. The first `clock settle`
or `clock +N` therefore set the host's clock back to a few hundred
milliseconds while the motion engine already stood seconds later, and every
hold begun after it was refused. This was not the drag form and not the
clone: any swipe in such a session, scripted or by hand on the simulator,
failed the same way, as would other holds begun on the host's clock (a
height drag). A reorder does not: it clamps to the host's and the engine's
time. Only a target behind the motion engine's time set it back; a seek
past it did not.

**Fix.** `Agent.clock` (shared by iOS and macOS):
- Under platform timing, the first `clock` op takes the clock over: before
  anything waits, `session.clock` is set to the host's `now()` (the wall),
  or the runner's clock if that is ahead (no known path does that; it is
  the safe floor), and the seek
  starts there. The runner catches up from wherever it stood,
  firing its timers at their own times, but the host's clock is never set
  behind that floor meanwhile (`advanceStepped`'s `floor`), so a hold
  delivered during the catch-up is not refused either.
- A long catch-up that passes the runner's timer-fire limit is progress, and
  the seek goes on from there within its bound.
- `to` stays absolute: behind the taken clock it is refused ("cannot go
  backwards") and the clock stays at the wall. A new private field,
  `{"op":"clock","take":true}`, is the takeover alone: it moves nothing and
  replies where the clock stands (under agent timing, the clock it already
  has). It is not a new operation and not in the CLI, as `tags` is not.
- The driver (`s.clock`) sends `take` before every `clock` but `settle` on
  the Apple hosts, whatever the timing (the host knows its own), to the
  session it addresses, and counts a relative or stepped seek from the clock
  that reply reports. Each session of the sample host is taken over on its
  own. `settle` needs no count, and the host takes over within it, so one
  `clock settle` keeps one 20 s bound.
- Agent timing is unchanged: its clock is 0 from launch, and `take` only
  reports it.

A consequence, stated: the first successful seek after the takeover catches
the runner up to at least the takeover time (the wall), so a duration
measured across it (the clone's voice note, started at runner time 0) counts
the time since launch.

**Proof.** `AgentClockIOSTests` boots the fixture, puts the motion engine at
the wall (a `tick` at `now()`, as a running animation leaves it), and then:
`take` lands at or past the wall, a hold begun after it is accepted, a later
`take` is a no-op, and a target behind the clock is refused; a runner ahead
of the wall (the host's clock cleared by hand) is the floor; a first
`{"to":100}` leaves the clock at the wall and the next hold is accepted (by
reading, the old code set the clock to 100, behind the engine; the test was
not run against it). On the clone under
platform timing, `clock settle` now lands at about 3,473 ms instead of 0,
and the swipe after it sets `replyId` and logs `swiperight`; `clock +600`,
`clock +60000` (stepped) and `clock settle` as the first op each count from
the wall, and the reply works after each.
