## Summary

Three modules of LLP 1133.000's wave 3, in one RFC because they meet the
same two questions and share a consumer's permission:

- **`@exact/health`** reads the person's health store: HealthKit on iOS
  and iPadOS, Health Connect on Android. It answers daily totals and
  workouts in tier 1 and any of the platform's types by its own identifier
  in tier 2.
- **`@exact/pedometer`** counts steps: `CMPedometer` on Apple, the step
  counter sensor on Android.
- **`@exact/altimeter`** reads air pressure and the change in height:
  `CMAltimeter` on Apple, the pressure sensor on Android.

The web has none of the three, and no web specification defines them, so
each tier 1 is a declared deviation in the web's style. Macs, Linux and
tvOS are absent for stated reasons, and Windows' pedometer and altimeter
are `later` (D21).

```
use healthStatus, requestHealthAuthorization, healthStatistics, healthWorkouts,
  HealthStatus, HealthStatistics, HealthWorkoutPage from "@exact/health"

resource health = healthStatus(read=["steps", "workouts"]) as shape HealthStatus
resource steps = healthStatistics(type="steps", start=monthStart, end=monthEnd,
  interval="P1D") as shape HealthStatistics else empty()
resource workouts = healthWorkouts(start=dayStart, end=dayStart + 86400000)
  as shape HealthWorkoutPage else empty()

action showActivity
  send health = requestHealthAuthorization(read=["steps", "workouts"])
```

```
use pedometer, Pedometer from "@exact/pedometer"
use altimeter, Altimeter from "@exact/altimeter"

resource walked = pedometer(start=walkStart) as shape Pedometer else empty()
resource climbed = altimeter(interval=2000) as shape Altimeter else empty()
```

Two questions decide most of the design:

1. **HealthKit hides whether reading was denied.** "To help prevent
   possible leaks of sensitive health information, your app cannot
   determine whether or not a user has granted permission to read data. If
   you are not given permission, it simply appears as if there is no data
   of the requested type" ([`authorizationStatus(for:)`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/authorizationstatus(for:))).
   Since iOS 27 the person may also grant only "a recent limited window of
   data rather than their full history", and the app can identify that
   window but still "can't distinguish between full access and denied
   access" ([Authorizing access](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/authorizing-access-to-health-data)).
   Contacts, calendar and photos settled that a state which changes what a
   granted read answers is a fact every caller sees (LLP 1133.018 D5, LLP
   1133.019 D6). Here the platform withholds the fact itself. The answer
   (D5) is to say exactly what is known: a type's `read` is `unknown` once
   it has been asked on Apple, every answer carries `since`, the earliest
   moment it can see, and nothing ever presents an empty read as a denial
   or a denial as an empty read.
2. **The pedometer and the altimeter are one decision on Apple, and two
   on Android.** iOS asks for "Motion & Fitness" once for `CMPedometer`,
   `CMAltimeter` and the rest, under one `NSMotionUsageDescription`.
   Android asks `ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION` for steps and nothing for pressure.
   One permission name shared by two interfaces, present on the targets
   that ask it, keeps the runner's queue to one prompt where the platform
   shows one (D15).

No app in the repository needs any of the three today; the one known
consumer, the Brooks port's Shoe Finder, is outside it (LLP 1069.008.000).
So the RFC proposes two small, real features (§12): the Calendar example
shows the person's daily steps and workouts beside their calendars, and
Fieldnotes shows a walk's steps and climb while it lasts.

Every drive reads fixtures. No drive reads this Mac's or any person's
health, steps or altitude (D23).

## 0. What is new here

1. **A permission per item of a list.** HealthKit authorizes "each data
   type" ([Authorizing access](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/authorizing-access-to-health-data)),
   and Health Connect has one runtime permission per record type
   (`android.permission.health.READ_STEPS`,
   [`HealthPermissions`](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/health/connect/HealthPermissions)).
   Every earlier module has one permission state. Here the request names
   its types, reads never prompt, and the runner keeps a state per type
   (D5, D6, D19 item 1).
2. **A read whose denial is hidden.** The summary's first question; D5.
3. **A read whose history is cut.** Apple's limited window, Health
   Connect's "30 days prior to the time permission was granted" without
   `READ_HEALTH_DATA_HISTORY`
   ([get started](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/develop/get-started)),
   `CMPedometer`'s "only the past seven days"
   ([`queryPedometerData`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmpedometer/querypedometerdata(from:to:withhandler:))),
   and Android's step counter, which counts only "since the last reboot
   while activated" ([`Sensor`](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/hardware/Sensor#TYPE_STEP_COUNTER)).
   Each answer names the moment its data starts (`since`), so "no steps on
   the 3rd" and "cannot see the 3rd" never look alike (D2, D12, D13).
4. **Data a store's terms forbid passing on.** HealthKit's terms say "you
   must not disclose any information gained through HealthKit to a third
   party without express permission from the user"
   ([Protecting user privacy](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy)),
   App Review 5.1.3(i) extends that to "Motion and Fitness" data, and
   Google Play prohibits "sharing health data with third parties without
   explicit, informed user consent"
   ([Health Connect policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12991134)).
   A tool invocation's result goes to an agent exact2 cannot vouch for. So
   tools and wakes read none of the three modules (D7, D15; §16 question 1).
5. **One platform decision behind two modules.** D15.

## Part I — `@exact/health`

## 2. Tier 1

### D1 — The names, a declared deviation

The web has no health API; the W3C has never chartered one. The names
follow the web's style (a status, a request, sources that read) and the
platforms' shared model: typed samples in a store, read as statistics
over intervals and as workouts.

| Name | Kind | Apple | Android | Contract | Data code |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `healthStatus(read=)` | live source; a fact | `isHealthDataAvailable()`, `getRequestStatusForAuthorization`, `getEarliestAuthorizedSampleDate` | `getSdkStatus`, `getGrantedPermissions` | `resource s = healthStatus(read=[…])` | `await healthStatus({read})` |
| `requestHealthAuthorization(read=)` | send; a request | `requestAuthorization(toShare: [], read:)` | `PermissionController.createRequestPermissionResultContract()` through `startActivityForResult` | `send s = requestHealthAuthorization(read=[…])` | `await requestHealthAuthorization({read})` |
| `healthStatistics(type=, start=, end=, interval=)` | live source; a fact | `HKStatisticsCollectionQuery`, `cumulativeSum` | `aggregateGroupByPeriod` (calendar intervals) or `aggregateGroupByDuration` (hours) | `resource r = healthStatistics(…)` | `await healthStatistics({…})` |
| `healthWorkouts(start=, end=, limit=, after=)` | live source; a fact; a page | `HKSampleQuery` over `HKWorkoutType` | `readRecords(ExerciseSessionRecord)` | `resource w = healthWorkouts(…)` | `await healthWorkouts({…})` |

**The types** are a closed tier 1 list, each mapped to both platforms;
every other type is tier 2 (D10):

| `type` | Unit | Apple | Health Connect | Android permission |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `steps` | count | `HKQuantityTypeIdentifier.stepCount` | `StepsRecord`, `COUNT_TOTAL` | `READ_STEPS` |
| `distance` | metres | `.distanceWalkingRunning` | `DistanceRecord`, `DISTANCE_TOTAL` | `READ_DISTANCE` |
| `activeEnergy` | kilocalories | `.activeEnergyBurned` | `ActiveCaloriesBurnedRecord`, `ACTIVE_CALORIES_TOTAL` | `READ_ACTIVE_CALORIES_BURNED` |
| `workouts` | — | `HKWorkoutType` | `ExerciseSessionRecord` | `READ_EXERCISE` |

`distance` differs by platform, declared: Apple's identifier is walking
and running alone (cycling and swimming are their own types), while
Health Connect's `DistanceRecord` is any activity's. A daily total read
on both therefore differs for a person who cycles. The unit is the one
both Health apps show; HealthKit's `HKUnit.kilocalorie()` and Health
Connect's `Energy.inKilocalories` are the same quantity.

**`read`** is a literal list of these types, refused at bake when computed
(a new check, D19 item 4), because the Android manifest and the Play
Console declaration name each permission the app can ask (D4). The same
list is the request's and the status's, so an app writes it once.

**`interval`** is an ISO 8601 duration from a fixed set: `PT1H`, `P1D`,
`P1W`, `P1M`. Days, weeks and months are calendar intervals in the
session's time zone (`exactTime().timeZone`), anchored at `start`, so a
day across a daylight-saving change is 23 or 25 hours, as the Health apps
show it. An answer holds at most 400 buckets (a year of days and a
margin); more is refused at bake when literal and fails `RangeError`
when computed.

**A day on Android is the day where it was lived, declared.** Health
Connect's `aggregateGroupByPeriod` takes `LocalDateTime` bounds and
counts each record on its own local day, by the zone offset it was
recorded with ([`TimeRangeFilter`](https://developer.android.com/reference/kotlin/androidx/health/connect/client/time/TimeRangeFilter));
instant bounds are not accepted for periods ([aggregate](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/develop/aggregate-data)).
The module passes the session zone's local wall times for `start` and
`end`, so for a person who stayed in one zone the buckets are the Apple
ones. A person who walked in San Francisco and reads the month in New
York sees an evening's steps on the day they were walked on Android, and
on the New York day on Apple. That is each platform's own Health app's
answer, so the module does not compute instant buckets on Android (one
`aggregate` call per bucket, up to 400, against Health Connect's rate
limit). `PT1H` uses `aggregateGroupByDuration` with instant bounds on
both.

**Errors** are `DOMException` names, as LLP 1133.006 D8 item 2 answers
them: `NotSupportedError` (no store on the target, or Health Connect not
installed), `NotAllowedError` (a type not granted where the platform
says so, D5; a caller the origin rule refuses, `message`
`person-required`, D7; the app in the background), `NotReadableError`
(the store is locked, HealthKit's
[`errorDatabaseInaccessible`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkerror/code/errordatabaseinaccessible);
Health Connect's rate limit), `InvalidStateError` (a stale cursor,
`cursor-stale`, as LLP 1133.018 D6).

### D2 — Values

| Shape | Fields |
|---|---|
| `HealthStatus` | `available`; `state` (D5); `types`, one `HealthTypeStatus` per type asked; `apple`, `android` (D10) |
| `HealthTypeStatus` | `type`; `read` (`granted`, `prompt`, `denied`, `unknown`); `since` (`option<number>`, D5); `complete` (bool, below) |
| `HealthStatistics` | `type`; `unit` (`count`, `m`, `kcal`); `since`; `complete`; `buckets`, each `{start, end, value}` with `value` an `option<number>` |
| `HealthWorkoutPage` | `workouts`; `next` (`option<string>`, the key cursor of LLP 1133.018 D6); `since`; `complete` |
| `HealthWorkout` | `id`; `activity` (below); `start`, `end` (ms since the epoch); `duration` (s, the platform's active time); `distance` (m) and `energy` (kcal), each `option`; `source` (`option<string>`, the writing app's name); `apple`, `android` |

**`value` is `none` where the module saw no sample, and 0 only where the
platform summed samples to 0.** Health Connect's buckets are sparse ("a
bucket is not included in the list if it doesn't contain any data",
[aggregate](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/develop/aggregate-data));
`HKStatistics.sumQuantity()` is nil for an interval with no sample. The
module fills the missing buckets with `none`, so every answer has one
bucket per interval. `none` says nothing was visible, never that nothing
happened.

**Two facts say how much of the store an answer saw** (LLP 1133.018 D5's
rule: a state that changes what a granted read answers is beside the
state, for every caller):

- **`since`** is a moment before which the answer saw nothing, or `none`
  when the module knows of no such moment. A bucket that ends before
  `since` has `value` `none`; one that spans it is the sum from `since`.
- **`complete`** is true only where the platform has told the module that
  the read is granted and reaches back to `since` (or, with `since`
  `none`, to the store's start): Android's granted types. It is false
  wherever the platform withholds that: every Apple read, since HealthKit
  never says a read is granted (D5).

A bucket that ends before `since` is unseen on every platform, whatever
`complete` says. At or after `since`, `complete: true` with `value: none`
is a day with no samples, and `complete: false` with `value: none` is a
day this app could not see anything for, which may be a quiet day, a denial, or data outside a
window the platform did not name. The module never turns the second into
the first. D5 says what sets each.

**Totals are the platform's own aggregation, never a sum of samples.**
HealthKit's statistics "automatically merge the data from all of your
data sources before performing the calculations"
([`HKStatistics`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkstatistics)),
and Health Connect's aggregate "accounts for any duplicate data and keeps
only the data from the app with the highest priority" for activity
types (aggregate, above), whose read guide says "for cumulative types
like `StepsRecord`, use `aggregate()` instead of `readRecords()` to avoid
double counting". An iPhone and a Watch that both counted a walk are one
walk, as the Health app shows it.

**`activity`** is a shared word where the two platforms agree, and
`other` elsewhere, with the platform's raw value always in tier 2:

| `activity` | Apple `HKWorkoutActivityType` | Health Connect `ExerciseSessionRecord` |
|---|---|---|
| `walking` | `.walking` | `EXERCISE_TYPE_WALKING` |
| `running` | `.running` | `EXERCISE_TYPE_RUNNING`, `EXERCISE_TYPE_RUNNING_TREADMILL` |
| `hiking` | `.hiking` | `EXERCISE_TYPE_HIKING` |
| `cycling` | `.cycling` | `EXERCISE_TYPE_BIKING`, `EXERCISE_TYPE_BIKING_STATIONARY` |
| `swimming` | `.swimming` | `EXERCISE_TYPE_SWIMMING_POOL`, `EXERCISE_TYPE_SWIMMING_OPEN_WATER` |
| `other` | every other case | every other type |

A workout's `distance` and `energy` are Apple's per-workout statistics
(`HKWorkout.statistics(for:)`) and, on Android, an aggregate of
`DistanceRecord` and `ActiveCaloriesBurnedRecord` over the session's
interval from the session's own data origin. Each is `none` unless its
type is in the granted `read` list on Android; on Apple stage 2 records
whether reading a workout's statistics needs the type's own read.

## 3. Permission

### D4 — Declare: the existing grant, with an Android column

The app writes `device.health-read purpose.<key>`, the line LLP
1069.008.000 built. This RFC adds what Android derives from it:

| Target | Derived |
|---|---|
| iOS, iPadOS | as built: `NSHealthShareUsageDescription`, its companion key, `com.apple.developer.healthkit`, the profile check |
| macOS | as built: the two usage keys, no entitlement; the module answers absent (D21) |
| Android | one `android.permission.health.READ_*` per type in the app's literal `read` lists (D1's table, and D10's identifiers); `READ_HEALTH_DATA_HISTORY` when the app writes `android.readHealthDataHistory=true`; the manifest fragment below |
| web, Linux, Windows, tvOS | nothing |

**The manifest fragment.** Health Connect shows its permission screen only
for an app that declares an activity handling
`androidx.health.ACTION_SHOW_PERMISSIONS_RATIONALE` (Android 13 and
lower) and an `activity-alias` with `android.intent.action.VIEW_PERMISSION_USAGE`
in `android.intent.category.HEALTH_PERMISSIONS`, guarded by
`android.permission.START_VIEW_PERMISSION_USAGE` (Android 14 and later),
and "the activity must display the same privacy policy you provide for
your app in the Google Play Console" (get started, above). On Android 13
and lower the app also lists `com.google.android.apps.healthdata` in
`<queries>`. The Android implementation's declaration carries this
fragment, merged into the app's manifest only when the app uses the
module (D19 item 3). Its activity opens the app's privacy policy.

**`privacy_policy`.** HealthKit requires "a privacy policy for any app that
uses the HealthKit framework" (Protecting user privacy), and Health
Connect's screen links to one. `app.json` gains `privacy_policy`, an
`https:` URL. An app granting `device.health-read` without it is refused
at bake for Android (`grant-privacy-policy`), since the permission screen
cannot work without it, and warned for iOS. It is one field, and the App
Store's and Play's own listings ask for the same URL.

The rows keep `keeps_no_answers` (D9). Writing stays out (§14): the
request asks no share types, so `device.health-write` derives nothing new
here.

### D5 — Read: availability, a state per type, and what is known

`healthStatus(read=[…])` answers:

- `available`: Apple's `isHealthDataAvailable()`, which is true "on iOS,
  watchOS, and visionOS … [and] iPads running iPadOS 17 or later", and on
  a Mac "return[s] `false`"
  ([`isHealthDataAvailable()`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkhealthstore/ishealthdataavailable()));
  Android's `getSdkStatus` equal to `SDK_AVAILABLE`. On Android 13 and
  lower Health Connect is an app from Google Play, and Android 14 makes it
  "part of the Android Framework" (get started, above); a provider that
  needs installing or updating reads `available` false with
  `android.sdkStatus` saying which (D10).
- `types`: per type, `read` and `since`.
- `state`: what `requestHealthAuthorization` with the same list would do
  now, in LLP 1133.002 D8's words, total over every case: `prompt` if it
  would show the platform's screen for any data type or for history;
  otherwise `unknown` if any data type's `read` is `unknown`; otherwise
  `denied` if every data type is denied; otherwise `granted`, which then
  means at least one data type reads and `types` says which. History's
  denial (below) stays out of `state`; its own `read` shows it.
  It is the app's summary; admission reads each type's own permission
  (D19 item 1). An app reads `types` for what each read will answer.

**Apple.** HealthKit tells the app two things about reading, and the
module answers both and no more:

| What HealthKit says | `read` | `since` | `complete` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `getRequestStatusForAuthorization(toShare: [], read: [type])` is `shouldRequest`: never asked | `prompt` | `none` | false |
| `unnecessary`: asked, and the person answered | `unknown` | the date `getEarliestAuthorizedSampleDate(for:)` returns for the type (iOS 27), else `none` | false |
| `unknown`: the status could not be determined | `unknown` | `none` | false |

`since` `none` on Apple therefore means only that HealthKit named no
window; with `complete` false it promises nothing about what came before.

`unknown` is LLP 1133.002 D8's word for exactly this: "the platform will
not say (… HealthKit hides read authorization by design)". After asking,
HealthKit returns "only samples that your app successfully saved" for a
denied type, and limited is "the only authorization state your app can
positively identify". So an Apple read of an asked type always proceeds,
and an empty answer means "nothing this app may see", which the app must
word as such (the recipe and §12 say how). The module never guesses: it
does not read an empty answer as a denial or a full one as a grant.

`since` on Apple is Apple's limited window, and the module passes it to
every query as the predicate's start, as Apple's guide does ("pass the
provided `startDate` into your data-store query"). HealthKit "evaluates
the boundary against a sample's end date", so a sum from `since` may
include a sample that began before it; the bucket is counted as the
platform counts it.

**Android.** Health Connect's grants are readable
(`getGrantedPermissions`), so `read` is never `unknown` there:

| Health Connect | `read` |
|---|---|
| the type's permission granted | `granted` |
| never asked, or cancelled once | `prompt` (`android.shouldShowRationale` per LLP 1133.002 D8) |
| cancelled twice: "your app should present the user with a screen" of its own ([permissions UI](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/health-connect/ui/permissions)), since the request shows nothing more | `denied`, with `android.permanentlyDenied` true |
| revoked in Health Connect's settings | `prompt`, by LLP 1133.002 D8's history rule |

The history is LLP 1133.008 D8 item 7's one Android permission history,
keyed per permission. `complete` is true for a granted type, from
`since`. Without `READ_HEALTH_DATA_HISTORY`, "all applications can read
data … for up to 30 days prior to when any permission was first granted"
([read data](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/health-connect/develop/read-data)),
and the platform resets that moment when every grant is revoked and given
again, which can happen while the app is closed and leave the granted set
looking unchanged. The one window that holds whatever happened is the
last 30 days: the first grant was at or before now, so the platform's
limit is at or before 30 days before now. So without history, `since` is
30 days before the answer's now, and reads start there; the module
records no grant time. With `READ_HEALTH_DATA_HISTORY` granted, `since`
is `none`. An app that shows more than a month asks history (D10), which
is what Health Connect's guide has an app do.

**History is its own permission.** `READ_HEALTH_DATA_HISTORY` is a
separate runtime permission (read data, above), so it is a key of its own,
`history`, beside the types (D19 item 1), on Android only: it appears in
`types` when the app writes `android.readHealthDataHistory=true`, has its
own `read` state, and makes a request ask it whenever it is `prompt`, even
if every type is granted. An app that adds history to a later version
asks it with the same request.

**Changes.** On Apple, HealthKit's authorization changes only through the
app's request or the person's Settings; the module reads the status again
at each return to the foreground. On Android it reads the grants at each
resume (`resumed()`), as LLP 1133.002 D8 does. Either change is announced
on the `health` topic (D8), so every reader is asked again.

### D6 — Ask: one request names the types; reads never prompt

**The request is the only call that prompts.** `requestHealthAuthorization(read=)`
asks for the listed types and answers `HealthStatus` for them. A source
never prompts, so a reader asked again by a store change, a foreground
return or a re-render never opens a sheet (the rule LLP 1133.018 D5 gives
calendar's raise). A source read of a type whose `read` is `prompt` fails
`NotAllowedError` with `message` `requestHealthAuthorization`, and is
asked again when the state changes; this holds on Apple too, where
HealthKit would answer an unasked type with nothing and give the app no
reason why.

- **Apple:** `requestAuthorization(toShare: [], read: types)`. HealthKit
  shows its sheet "only for types not yet asked"; the person toggles each
  type, then (iOS 27) chooses a recent window or the full history. The
  completion says the request was handled, not what was granted; the
  module answers the status afterwards.
- **Android:** the contract's intent through the context's
  `startActivityForResult`, with the types' permissions and, when
  written, `READ_HEALTH_DATA_HISTORY`, in one request. The result names
  what was granted; the module records each outcome in the history.
- By state: while `prompt` it asks; otherwise it answers the status at
  once, since neither platform will show anything (Apple's
  `unnecessary`; Android's twice-cancelled types).
- **One request at a time:** LLP 1133.002 D9's queue. Two requests for
  overlapping lists queued together share the decision for the types they
  share.

**Explain and recover** (LLP 1133.002 D11). Neither platform's screen
shows more than the app's purpose (Apple's `NSHealthShareUsageDescription`)
and its privacy policy (Android's link), so the app explains in its own
words beside the control that asks. Recovery: on Apple the person changes
access in Settings › Health › Data Access & Devices or in the Health app,
which "a person can change … at any time" (Authorizing access); the app
names the path, and `openURL("app-settings:")` opens the app's own
settings page. On Android the tier 2 send `openHealthConnectSettings()`
opens Health Connect's screen for the app (D10), where the person grants
what they declined twice.

### D7 — Who may call: the person, through the app

| Origin | `requestHealthAuthorization` | `healthStatistics`, `healthWorkouts` | `healthStatus` |
|---|---|---|---|
| a person's input; the app's own code in a foreground session | asks while `prompt` (D6) | proceeds | answers |
| a tool invocation (LLP 1111) | `NotAllowedError`, `person-required`, in every state | `NotAllowedError`, `person-required`, in every state | answers |
| a background wake; a headless session | `person-required` | `person-required` | answers |
| the app in the background | `NotAllowedError` | `NotAllowedError` | answers |

**Reading is the app's** (`"origin": "app"`, `"foreground": true`, LLP
1133.009 D10 item 8). This departs from geolocation, contacts and photos,
where a tool reads what the app may read (LLP 1133.002 D12). The reason
is in the platforms' terms, not exact2's taste: a tool's result goes to
an agent, which may be another company's model, and HealthKit's terms
forbid disclosing "any information gained through HealthKit to a third
party without express permission from the user … even with permission,
you can only share information to a third party if they also provide a
health or fitness service to the user" (Protecting user privacy); Google
Play prohibits "transferring or selling user health or fitness data to
third parties" and sharing it "without explicit, informed user consent"
(Health Connect policy). exact2 cannot know what a tool's caller is, so
it cannot meet either test, and an app that ships the module must. LLP
1133.002 D12's delegation, when it exists, is where a person grants an
agent this read explicitly; until then no tool reads it (§16 question 1).

The status is not health data: it says which types the app asked for,
and answers every caller.

**What the origin rule reaches, and what it does not.** `"origin": "app"`
refuses a tool's call of these names. It cannot stop an app from handing
a tool a value it already read for the person: a standing resource a
tool returns (LLP 1111 D2, D4) or data code's own copy. Two rules cover
what exact2 can see:

1. **The bake refuses a tool whose result reads, through the plan, a
   resource that depends on a source declared `"person": true`**
   (`tool-reads-person-data`): every Health, pedometer and altimeter
   source declares it (D18, D19 item 6). The plan's dependencies are
   static, so the check is exact for Contract.
2. **Data code is the app's.** A data module that reads Health and returns
   a derived value from a data source a tool calls passes no module call
   the runner can refuse, so the runner does not try. The tool's call into
   data code carries the tool's origin, so a Health read that data code
   makes for it fails `person-required`; a value data code cached earlier
   is the app's to keep from tools, and the recipe says so. That is the
   terms' own division: the platform holds the app, not its framework, to
   them.

A wake cannot read in any case: HealthKit's store is encrypted while the
device is locked, so "your app may not be able to read data from the
store when it runs in the background" (Protecting user privacy), and
Health Connect reads in the background only with
`READ_HEALTH_DATA_IN_BACKGROUND` (read data, above). Background delivery
waits for a consumer (§14).

## 4. The store

### D8 — Bounded answers, off the main thread, and a topic

LLP 1133.018 D6 and D7, applied:

- **Bounded.** Statistics answers have at most 400 buckets of three
  numbers; workout pages at most 500 workouts. A page's `next` is a key
  cursor over `(start, id)`, so a page asked again after a change resumes
  after the same workout; one whose workout is gone fails
  `InvalidStateError`, `cursor-stale`, and the caller starts again.
- **Off the main thread.** HealthKit's queries run on HealthKit's own
  queue and answer through a handler; Health Connect's calls are
  suspending functions, which the implementation runs on a background
  dispatcher. Each replies on the main thread, as `later` allows.
- **Transit, not state.** The runner journals a reply's name, arguments
  and size, never its values (LLP 1133.018 D6).
- **A topic, `health`.** On Apple, one `HKObserverQuery` per read type
  while that type has a reader, which "alerts you when the store saves or
  removes matching data" ([`HKObserverQuery`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkobserverquery)),
  announced coalesced as LLP 1133.018 D7 coalesces a store's
  notifications. Health Connect sends no notification, so on Android an
  answer whose range includes the session's now names a `stale` time 60
  seconds on (LLP 1133.015 D7), and the runner asks it again then;
  an answer wholly in the past is asked again only on the topic. On both,
  each return to the foreground announces once.

### D9 — An app granted Health keeps no answers

LLP 1069.008.000 D7's rule stands as built, for its own reason: an app
that grants `device.health-read` or `device.health-write` keeps no
answers across launches, so no Health value is written outside the store
by the framework. The Calendar example's data is a Rust crate, which
keeps no answers in any case, so it loses nothing (LLP 1133.018 D8,
"What it costs").

**What the app stores is the app's.** App Review 5.1.3(ii) says apps "may
not store personal health information in iCloud"; Google Play's policy
limits what an app does with health data. The module stores nothing and
keeps nothing; a value the app saves (a note, a backup, a server row) is
the app's decision under those terms, as every other use of the data is.
The recipe names both rules beside the call that saves. §12's consumers
save nothing of Health; Fieldnotes shows a walk's figures and saves none
(§16 question 2).

## Part II — `@exact/pedometer` and `@exact/altimeter`

## 6. Tier 1

### D12 — Pedometer: steps since a moment

No web specification counts steps. The names are a deviation in the
Generic Sensor's style (a status, a request and a reading), and they
name what both platforms have: a count of the person's steps since a
moment.

| Name | Kind | Apple | Android |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pedometerStatus()` | live source; a fact; `sync` on Apple | `isStepCountingAvailable()`, `authorizationStatus()` | the Recording API's client, else the default `TYPE_STEP_COUNTER` sensor; `ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION` |
| `requestPedometerPermission()` | send; a request | a query of the last second, which prompts while `notDetermined` | `Activity.requestPermissions` |
| `pedometer(start=, interval=)` | live source, polled; a fact | `startUpdates(from: start)` | the Recording API's `TYPE_STEP_COUNT_DELTA`, with `TYPE_STEP_COUNTER` between reads |

**`Pedometer`**: `steps`; `since` (the moment `steps` counts from);
`timestamp`; `apple` (`distance` m, `floorsAscended`, `floorsDescended`,
`currentPace` s/m, `currentCadence` steps/s, `averageActivePace`, each
`option`, from `CMPedometerData`).

**`since` is where the platforms differ, and it is visible.**

- **Apple** answers "the cumulative data starting at the specified
  `start` date and ending at the current time"
  ([`startUpdates`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmpedometer/startupdates(from:withhandler:))),
  from a cache the system keeps whether or not the app runs, with "only
  the past seven days" available to queries. The reader group's
  accumulation starts once, at its first ask, from the later of `start`
  and seven days before that moment, and that boundary is fixed for the
  group's life: `since` is the `startDate` of the `CMPedometerData` that
  answers, which is the same boundary in every answer, and the count
  grows from it. Steps taken while the app was suspended or closed are
  counted. A walk longer than a week keeps counting from its boundary;
  stage 1 records what Core Motion reports for a start older than seven
  days, and if it moves `startDate`, `since` follows it and the answer
  says so.
- **Android** counts steps for an app that is not running only through
  Google Play services' Recording API on mobile, which records "steps,
  similar to a pedometer" on the device, "in a battery-efficient way",
  with no account, and makes "data since the latest subscription - for up
  to 10 days" readable ([Recording API](https://developer.android.com/health-and-fitness/guides/recording-api)).
  The sensor alone cannot: the step counter "does not count steps if it
  is not activated" ([`Sensor`](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/hardware/Sensor#TYPE_STEP_COUNTER)),
  and Android's sensor service stops delivering to, and may deactivate
  the sensor for, an app whose process is idle ([`SensorService`](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/native/+/master/services/sensorservice/SensorService.cpp)), so a count kept from the
  sensor's own value can silently miss a pocketed walk with no falling
  counter to say so. So the implementation subscribes to
  `TYPE_STEP_COUNT_DELTA` at the first granted request (`LocalRecordingClient.subscribe`),
  and each answer is the Recording API's total from the reader group's
  boundary to now (`readData` aggregated into one bucket), plus the step
  counter's increase since that read while the app is in the foreground,
  so the live line moves step by step. `since` is the latest of `start`,
  the subscription's start and the oldest moment the Recording API still
  keeps: it holds data "for up to 10 days", even under a live
  subscription ([`LocalRecordingClient`](https://developers.google.com/android/reference/com/google/android/gms/fitness/LocalRecordingClient)).
  So a group older than ten days counts its last ten days, and `since`
  moves with them, as Apple's does when `startDate` moves; no read is
  issued from before the retained window, so retention never ends the
  poll with an error. A subscription the person's Play services dropped
  is renewed and moves `since` too. Without Play services at
  `LocalRecordingClient.LOCAL_RECORDING_CLIENT_MIN_VERSION_CODE`, the
  implementation counts from the step counter alone, and every hidden
  stretch starts a new zero at the return, so `since` moves and the
  answer never claims steps it could not have seen; `android.source`
  says which (`recording`, `sensor`). The pedometer's Android
  implementation adds `com.google.android.gms:play-services-fitness` to
  the locked graph (LLP 1133.008 D8 item 5) at a pinned version, with its
  merged manifest inventoried before the lock, as LLP 1133.015 does for a
  Play library, since R8 strips no library's `<uses-permission>` or
  components. Both paths ask the same `ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION`.

`interval` is the poll's (LLP 1133.009 D10 item 7): the runner asks again
every `interval` (default 1,000 ms, at least 250 ms) while a reader reads.
A count is cumulative, so the latest answer holds everything, and no
batch is needed: coalescing loses nothing (LLP 1133 D6 rule 7 is about
values that do not accumulate).

**Background.** Apple suspends delivery ("when the app is suspended, the
delivery of updates stops temporarily"), and the next answer after the
return is cumulative again, so nothing is missed. Android's reference
says the opposite of what motion does: "do NOT unregister for this sensor,
so that it keeps counting steps in the background even when the AP is in
suspend mode and report the aggregate count when the AP is awake". That
advice holds for the sensor service's suspend, not for an idle app, so
the Android implementation does not rely on it: the Recording API holds
the count in the background (above), and the first answer after the
return reads it. A person on a test phone (LLP 1133.020) records that a
pocketed walk with the app in the background reads its steps at the
return on the Recording path, and moves `since` on the sensor path.

**A reader is not foreground-gated; only a request is.** The readers are
`"origin": "app"` and not `"foreground": true`, as motion's sensor
sources are not (LLP 1133.013 D8; only its `startSensorInput` is). While
the page is hidden a poll that comes due answers what the module holds:
the last cumulative count, which the next answer after the return
supersedes. A foreground-gated reader would fail its first hidden poll,
and the runner stops polling a source whose answer was an error until a
reader asks anew (`runner/src/runner/modules/poll.rs`), so the walk's
line would stay empty after the return. The requests stay
`"foreground": true`.

`requestPedometerPermission` answers `PedometerStatus`
(`available`, `state`, and `apple`'s `isDistanceAvailable`,
`isFloorCountingAvailable`, `isPaceAvailable`, `isCadenceAvailable`).
`CMPedometer` has no request call; the first query prompts. So the
request issues `queryPedometerData(from: now − 1 s, to: now)`, and its
handler's `CMErrorMotionActivityNotAuthorized` (`CoreMotion/CMError.h`)
or data settles it, before the module reads `authorizationStatus()`
again. A reader never starts updates while the state is `prompt`: it
fails `NotAllowedError`, `message` `requestPedometerPermission`, as
motion's readers do on WebKit (LLP 1133.013 D11).

### D13 — Altimeter: pressure, and a change of height

No browser ships a barometer, and no W3C specification defines one: the
Device APIs Working Group's 2012 "Atmospheric Pressure Events"
([commit](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-dap-commits/2012Sep/0005.html))
never left its unofficial draft, and the Generic Sensor family
([Generic Sensor API](https://www.w3.org/TR/generic-sensor/)) has no
pressure sensor. The names are a declared deviation shaped as that family
shapes a sensor, so they read beside `@exact/motion`'s: one quantity per
name, `frequency`, a `timestamp`, and the batch of LLP 1133.013 D5.

| Name | Kind | Apple | Android | Windows (D21) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `altimeterStatus()` | live source; a fact; `sync` on Apple | `isRelativeAltitudeAvailable()`, `authorizationStatus()` | the default `TYPE_PRESSURE` sensor | `Barometer.GetDefault()` |
| `requestAltimeterPermission()` | send; a request | relative updates started and stopped at the first callback | answers the status: Android asks nothing | — |
| `barometer(frequency=, interval=, history=)` | live source, polled, sampled; a fact | `CMAltitudeData.pressure` (kPa) × 10 | `TYPE_PRESSURE` (hPa) | `StationPressureInHectopascals` |
| `altimeter(frequency=, interval=, history=)` | live source, polled, sampled; a fact | `CMAltitudeData.relativeAltitude`, re-zeroed per reader group | `SensorManager.getAltitude` differences | `AltitudeChangeInMeters` |

**Values:** `Barometer` is `pressure` in hectopascals and `timestamp`,
with the batch fields (`sequence`, `samples`, `dropped`, `interrupted`)
of LLP 1133.013 D1. `Altimeter` is `relativeAltitude` in metres since the
reader group's zero, `since` (when that zero was taken), `timestamp`, and
the batch fields.

**Hectopascals,** not pascals: Android's pressure is "hPa or mbar"
([Environment sensors](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/sensors/sensors_environment)),
Windows' is `StationPressureInHectopascals`, weather reports use hPa, and
Apple's kPa is ten of them. The Generic Sensor family uses prefixed SI
units where they are the field's own (`Magnetometer` is µT).

**A relative altitude is relative to its reader group.** Apple's first
update "will be established as the reference altitude and have relative
altitude 0" (`CMAltimeter.h`), and one `CMAltimeter` stream serves every
reader in the process (LLP 1133.013 D4's one acquisition). So each reader
group's zero is its own first sample, and its readings are the stream's
less that sample. On Android, height comes from pressure alone:
`getAltitude(PRESSURE_STANDARD_ATMOSPHERE, p)` gives "good results"
for differences "if you don't know the altitude as sea level"
([`getAltitude`](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/hardware/SensorManager#getAltitude(float,%20float))),
so a group's `relativeAltitude` is that of the latest sample less that of
its first. Weather moves pressure as well as height, about 8 m per
hectopascal near sea level, so Android's relative altitude drifts over
hours; Apple's fuses more than pressure and is documented for a hike
("a hiking app could use this object to track the user's elevation change
over the course of a hike", [`CMAltimeter`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmaltimeter)).
The difference is declared, and the recipe says that an hour's walk is
what either does well.

**Continuity across a gap.** Android delivers no continuous-mode sensor
events in the background (LLP 1133.013 D8), so the implementation
unregisters there, re-registers on return, and keeps the group's first
pressure as its zero: height stays relative to the same zero across the
gap, by construction. On Apple the module does not stop updates in the
background; the system suspends their delivery. If the next update after
a return has a new reference (a person records whether Core Motion keeps
it, below), the implementation bridges the gap by the change in pressure across
it, with the same formula as Android, and the answer reads
`interrupted`. So a group's `relativeAltitude` is continuous for its
life on both, and `since` changes only when a new group begins. A poll
while the page is hidden answers an empty batch with `interrupted` set,
as motion's hidden stretch does (LLP 1133.013 D8), never an error, by the
rule D12 gives the pedometer. Whether Core Motion keeps the reference
across a suspension is recorded by a person on LLP 1133.020's test
iPhone: a simulator has no barometer.

**Rates.** Apple's updates come "at regular intervals", every few seconds
(`CMAltimeter.h`), whatever the reader asks; `frequency` is inert there,
a declared deviation. On Android `frequency` is the sampling period's
hint, default `SENSOR_DELAY_NORMAL`, as the environment sensors' guide
registers it. `interval` defaults to 1,000 ms (at least 250), and
`history` to 0, at most 256.

`requestAltimeterPermission` on Apple starts relative updates while
`notDetermined`, which shows the prompt, and stops them at the first
callback; the handler's `CMErrorMotionActivityNotAuthorized` or a sample
settles it.

### D14 — Unsaid defaults

| Unsaid | Apple | Android |
|---|---|---|
| `start` on `pedometer` | the reader's first ask | the same |

## 7. Permission

### D15 — One decision on Apple, two lines of Android

**Declare.** Both modules use the line apps already write for motion:
`device.motion purpose.<key>`. On Apple one key carries one text, and
"Motion & Fitness" is one decision per app: `CMPedometer` and
`CMAltimeter` each say "the usage description appears in the prompt that
the user must accept the first time the system asks the user to access
motion data" ([`CMPedometer`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmpedometer),
[`CMAltimeter`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coremotion/cmaltimeter)),
and both report the shared `CMAuthorizationStatus`
(`CoreMotion/CMAuthorization.h`). Two lines would write one key with two
texts. The row derives by use (LLP 1133.013 D11's rule, which motion
applies to its headphones):

| Target | Derived, when the app uses the names |
|---|---|
| iOS | `NSMotionUsageDescription`, for any pedometer or altimeter name (and motion's `headphoneMotion`). Without it "your app crashes when you call this API" |
| Android | `android.permission.ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION` for a pedometer name; nothing for the altimeter, which needs no permission |
| macOS, web, Linux, Windows, tvOS | nothing new |

**Read.** Each module answers its own status (LLP 1133 D7):

| Platform | `state` |
|---|---|
| Apple `notDetermined` | `prompt` |
| Apple `authorized` | `granted` |
| Apple `denied`; `restricted` | `denied` (`restricted` is `apple.authorizationStatus`) |
| Android `ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION`, pedometer | LLP 1133.002 D8's Android rows |
| Android, altimeter | `granted` |
| a target without the sensor | `available` false, `state` `unknown` |

**One permission, two interfaces.** Both declarations name the permission
`motion-fitness`. The runner keys its decision queue (LLP 1133.002 D9) and
its tickets by that name, so on Apple a pedometer request and an
altimeter request made together prompt once and settle both, and
`--permission motion-fitness=granted` sets both. The altimeter declares
the permission on Apple targets only; on Android its status reads
`granted` and its request answers at once, while the pedometer's still
asks `ACTIVITY_RECOGNITION` under the same name (D19 item 2). Motion's
`apple.headphoneMotion` reads the same Apple decision and joins the name
when it is built (§13).

**Who may call.** Each reader and request is `"origin": "app"`, and
each request `"foreground": true` (D12): a tool's or a wake's call fails
`person-required` in every state, and the sources declare `"person":
true` (D7's bake rule). Two reasons. Starting a sensor is the app's, as LLP 1133.013
D11 rules for motion. And App Review 5.1.3(i) names "Motion and Fitness"
data with HealthKit's: it may not be disclosed to third parties but to
improve health management, so the reason D7 gives for Health holds here.

**Recovery:** Settings › Privacy & Security › Motion & Fitness on iOS,
`openURL("app-settings:")` for the app's page; on Android the app's
permission page, by the same URL.

### D19 — Seam additions

Each general, each proved by the out-of-tree fixture before a package
uses it (LLP 1133.001 D8):

1. **Permissions chosen by an argument's values, asked together.**
   Camera's `permissionBy` picks a call's permission by which argument it
   gives (LLP 1133.008 D8, `runner/src/runner/modules/admit.rs`
   `permission_of`). Health extends it in four ways, each general:
   (a) **by value:** `"permissionBy": {"type": "health.{}"}` makes
   `healthStatistics(type="steps")` ask `health.steps`; (b) **by list:**
   `"permissionEach"` makes a request ask the permission of every listed
   value, and of every flag it sets, in one ticket, since the platform
   shows one sheet, where `ask_next` today asks one permission after
   another; (c) **a typed answer to a permission ticket:** `tap @t allow`
   grants every permission the ticket asks, `tap @t deny` denies them,
   `type @t steps,workouts` grants those and denies the rest, and
   `steps@2026-10-01` grants with a window from that date; `cancel` leaves
   them `prompt`; (d) **a per-target record:** `"answered": {"ios": "unknown"}` makes
   the admission word on an iOS target only `prompt` or `unknown`: a
   ticket answered either way stores `unknown`, `denied` is not writable
   there (`prefer` included), and settling to `unknown` releases the held
   request and forwards it as settling to `granted` does today
   (`permits.rs`, `set_permission_asked`), so admission never refuses an
   asked Apple type and never holds one. Beside the word the runner keeps
   the fixture facts the person's answer set, per permission: `shared`
   (whether the person allowed it) and `since` (a window's start). Every
   `tell_preferred` sends them with the state, `{"state", "shared",
   "since"}` under `permissions`, and a later state does not clear them; a
   drive sets them with `prefer permission.health.steps shared=false` or
   `since=2026-10-01`. The implementation, its own substitute, applies
   them to every read: a type not shared answers as HealthKit answers it,
   with only the app's own samples (none), and a window becomes `since`.
   On Android targets the states are `granted` and `denied`, as today, and
   the facts are not used. `--permission health=granted` sets every
   permission named `health.*`; on an iOS target it, and `prefer
   permission.health.steps granted`, store `unknown` with `shared` true,
   so an Apple `read` never reads `granted` or `denied`. A permission without these declarations
   is today's.

   The permission of a statistics call is that of the type it actually
   queries: a tier 2 `apple.quantityType` or `android.recordType` beats
   `type` (D10), so it picks the permission too. The bake derives a
   `health.<identifier>` permission for every literal tier 2 identifier
   the app writes, and `read` accepts the same literals, so a heart-rate
   read is admitted only on a heart-rate grant and fails
   `NotAllowedError`, `requestHealthAuthorization`, while it is `prompt`.
   `healthStatus` is forwarded to the implementation, which answers it
   from the states and facts it was told.
2. **One permission across interfaces, and per target.** Two interfaces
   that declare the same permission `name` share its decision, queue and
   tickets; a declaration's `targets` limits the permission to the
   targets listed, and elsewhere the name has no permission. Each
   interface names its own status source (`pedometerStatus`,
   `altimeterStatus`), and the runner writes the shared state into the
   `field` of each, so one allow makes both read `granted`. The bake
   refuses two declarations of one name whose state words differ (one
   with `answered`, one without).
3. **An Android implementation's manifest fragment** (`"manifest"` in its
   `exact.json`): activities, activity aliases and `<queries>` entries,
   merged into the app's manifest when the app uses the package, as
   `maven` adds libraries. Its activity may read the app's
   `privacy_policy` (D4).
4. **Derivation by literal argument and by name** in the grant table:
   Android permissions from the values of a literal list (D4's `READ_*`)
   and of a literal flag (`readHealthDataHistory`), and a usage key or
   permission from the names an app uses (D15). It generalizes
   `android_writes` (LLP 1133.018 D11) and motion's policy by use; a
   computed list where a literal one is declared is refused at bake
   (a new check).
5. **`privacy_policy` in `app.json`** and the bake's refusal (D4).
6. **`"person": true` on a source,** and the bake's refusal of a tool
   whose result reads, through the plan, a resource that depends on one
   (`tool-reads-person-data`, D7). A tool's `returns`, `fails when` and
   `available when` are expressions over the root's resources (LLP 1111
   D2), so the bake knows each one's dependencies; a `person` value in
   `fails when` or `available when` leaks as surely as in `returns`.

Each is apparatus under `rules/RULES.md` and lands with this ruling.

## 16. Rulings (Charlie, 2026-10-25: "Accept all (Recommended)")

Each question below was ruled as recommended; question 8 as Charlie
worded it.

1. **Tools and wakes read none of the three (D7, D15).** *Proposed: yes
   (75%).* HealthKit's terms, App Review 5.1.3 and Play's policy forbid
   passing this data to a third party without the person's express
   consent, and exact2 cannot tell what a tool's caller is. The
   alternative is LLP 1133.002 D12's rule, under which a tool reads what
   the app may read; it would make every app that ships the module
   answer for its agents to Apple and Google.
2. **Fieldnotes shows a walk and stores none of it (§12, D9).** *Proposed:
   yes (65%).* The framework keeps none of these values: module sources
   are not kept unless declared `"kept"` (LLP 1133.015 D4), none here is,
   and an app granted Health keeps no answers at all. A Stamp that wrote
   the walk into a note would put Motion and Fitness data (App Review
   5.1.3) into Fieldnotes' notes and backups, which iCloud Backup copies
   ([Optimizing your app's data for iCloud Backup](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/optimizing-your-app-s-data-for-icloud-backup)),
   and would need Fieldnotes to decide how such notes are stored. The
   alternative is that Stamp, with that storage decision made for
   Fieldnotes; it is an app's decision, not the framework's.
3. **One line, `device.motion`, for Motion & Fitness (D15).** *Proposed:
   yes (70%).* iOS has one key and one decision. The alternative, a
   `device.motion-fitness` line, would write the same key as motion's
   headphones with a second text.
4. **The consumers: the Calendar's Show my activity and Fieldnotes' walk
   (§12).** *Proposed: yes (65%).* Neither app needs them today; both
   features are what a hand-built app of the kind offers. The
   alternative is to build the modules against their fixtures alone.
5. **Windows `later` for the pedometer and the altimeter (D21).**
   *Proposed: yes (70%).* The API exists, so the parent's D8 says
   `later`, not `absent`; an app sees the same absence either way. The
   alternative is the first Windows package implementation, in Rust over
   `Windows.Devices.Sensors`, which needs LLP 1133.001's desktop Rust
   builder first and could be driven on the NucBox only as an absence.
6. **Health Connect through the Jetpack client (D18).** *Proposed: yes
   (70%)*, for Android 12 and 13. The alternative is the framework's
   `HealthConnectManager` on Android 14 and later only, with no Maven
   library.
7. **`privacy_policy` in `app.json`, required for Health on Android (D4).**
   *Proposed: yes (75%).*
8. **`rules/DEFERRED.md`:** an "Expanded" record admitting the three
   modules by these names on iOS and Android, the web, macOS, Linux and
   tvOS a visible absence and Windows `later`, with §14 as its "Still
   out"; and
   motion's record no longer keeps "steps, floors, altitude, pressure"
   out. Take: none offered; a waiver, or Charlie names one. Ruled "Waive
   both (Recommended)": an Expanded record with no take.
9. **D19's six seam additions** as apparatus admitted with this ruling.
   *Proposed: yes.*
