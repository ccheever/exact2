## 0. Round 1, and what r2 changed

Both reviewers backed the ranged body, part-count progress and the 64 MiB
cap. Both questioned stage 3, which r2 no longer proposes for Exact2.

- **Astra C1 (MAJOR), taken.** A long request doesn't cancel a running
  encoder. `Work::Later` hands its owner only a `Reply`
  (`runner/src/request.rs:613-619`), the executor's handoff passes no abort
  (`host/apple/src/executor_core.rs:764-769`), and `forget` lets a begun
  module turn run on (`:380-385`). An abandoned transcode could finish and
  commit over a newer one. r1's "for free" was wrong. Real cancellation and
  a revoked right to publish are now requirements on any framework
  transcode (D4).
- **Astra, Q1 (T3 has a web seam), taken.** LLP 1067 D5 gives an app a page
  module with long calls and device topics, so the clone's own module isn't
  Apple-only by construction. Q1 now asks whether to prove the transcode in
  the clone first (D4, §6).
- **Grok C1 (MAJOR), taken.** A fixed `video/mp4` result is one the web
  can't honestly produce. Bluesky's own web compressor tries `avc`, `hevc`,
  `vp9`, then `vp8`, writes WebM for the last two, and drops the audio when
  no codec for that container can encode it (`compress.web.ts`, checked
  2026-10-08). LLP 1042 already says codecs are engine capabilities. Also
  true: a file the web writes, as `compressImage` does, is a byte record that
  `requestBody` reads whole (`storage-fs.js:394`, `:419-420`), so ranged parts
  of it would copy it each time. A3 is withdrawn. T3 is the recommendation
  now (D4). The point that `exact-native:` is the app module's URL is moot
  with A3 gone.
- **Grok C2 (MAJOR), taken in part.** The `startUpload` lexicon gives
  `partSizeBytes` no maximum, and `planParts.ts` uses whatever comes back.
  Four buffered parts of tens of MiB is D1's jetsam shape, and the transport's
  pool of four sessions (`darwin_http.mm:364-386`) makes a fifth request wait.
  Taken: the clone budgets parts in flight and logs the value the service
  returns (D1, §7). Not taken: a change to Exact2. The real value can't be
  observed without a signed-in `startUpload`, and streaming the range natively
  is D1's lift, done when a service needs it.
- **Grok C3 (MAJOR), taken.** P1's one slot per name loses three of four
  parts in flight, and a host announcing `progress:video` would share the
  app module's topic names. The P1 sketch is deleted. D3 keeps P0 and states
  only what a later byte progress must be.

## 1. Summary

Bluesky uploads a video in parts: it asks the video service for a job, sends
the file in parts of a size the service names, four at a time, and finishes the
job. Before that it shrinks a large video to about 1080p at 3 Mb/s. Exact2 can
send a whole app file as a request body (`exactBodyFrom`, at most 64 MiB), but
not a part of one. It reports no upload progress, and it has no video
transcode.

This RFC proposes:

1. **A byte range of a file as a body.** `exactBodyFrom` takes `{path, start,
   end}` as well as a path, sliced as `Blob.slice` slices. With it the clone
   uploads any video the service accepts. The 64 MiB cap stays, and the clone
   budgets how many parts it keeps in flight.
2. **Progress from counted parts.** The clone's progress bar counts the parts
   that have answered. Exact2 adds nothing.
3. **No framework transcode now.** On Apple the clone transcodes in its own
   Swift module (LLP 1067), which needs no ruling. On the web it uploads the
   original, as Bluesky's web client does without WebCodecs. D4 says what a
   later `compressVideo` in Exact2 would have to meet.

Stage 1 is small and unblocks the feature. Nothing here reopens LLP 1069.002.

## 2. What Bluesky does

The source's account is older than Bluesky's current client. As of
2026-10-08 (`bluesky-social/social-app` main, and the `app.bsky.video`
lexicons in `bluesky-social/atproto`):

- **Limits.** `VIDEO_MAX_SIZE` is 300 MB (ISO megabytes) and
  `VIDEO_MAX_DURATION_MS` is 10 minutes (`src/lib/constants.ts`). The
  `app.bsky.embed.video` record says "up to 300 MB (formerly 100 MB)". The
  source's 100 MB and 3 minutes are the old limits. Accepted types are
  `video/mp4`, `video/mpeg`, `video/webm`, `video/quicktime` and `image/gif`.
- **Compression.** `@bsky.app/video-compressor`, not `react-native-compressor`
  (`src/lib/media/video/compress.ts`). Target 3,000,000 b/s, longer side at
  most 1920, at most 30 fps, codec `auto`. An accepted type under 25 MiB is
  passed through unchanged. On the web (`compress.web.ts`) it is Mediabunny
  over WebCodecs. It tries the video codecs `avc`, `hevc`, `vp9`, `vp8` in
  that order, writes MP4 for the first two and WebM for the last two, and
  pairs AAC with MP4 and Opus or Vorbis with WebM, dropping the audio when
  none encodes. It passes the file through when WebCodecs is missing, the
  file is small, or compression throws.
- **Upload.** Multipart, not one POST (`src/lib/media/video/multipart/`):
  1. A service-auth token from the PDS (`com.atproto.server.getServiceAuth`,
     `lxm` `com.atproto.repo.uploadBlob`), cached and refreshed a minute
     before it expires.
  2. `app.bsky.video.startUpload` with `sizeBytes`, `mimeType` and the
     advisory `durationMs`, `width`, `height`. The answer names `jobId`,
     `partSizeBytes`, `partCount` and `expiresAt`. The lexicon puts no bound
     on `partSizeBytes`.
  3. `app.bsky.video.uploadPart?jobId=…&partNumber=…` per part, as
     `application/octet-stream`. "Content-Length must match [the part's
     expected size] exactly." Parts are idempotent. Four upload at once
     (`MULTIPART_CONCURRENCY`), each with 5 attempts and a 120 s timeout. The
     client reads each part as a byte range of the file (`readChunk.ts`:
     expo-file-system's `File`, `offset`, `readBytes`; the web slices the
     `Blob`). Parts must be at least 5 MiB except the last (R2 and S3's
     minimum, per `multipart/constants.ts`); that is a floor, not the size.
  4. `app.bsky.video.finishUpload`, then `getUploadStatus` to recover, and
     `getJobStatus` until the transcode is done. `abortUpload` on cancel.
  5. The post embeds `app.bsky.embed.video`: the blob, `aspectRatio`, `alt`,
     and `captions` (up to 20 WebVTT files of at most 20 KB each; LLP
     1114.005).
- **Progress.** Per part, from `XMLHttpRequest`'s `upload` `progress` event
  (`uploadPart.ts`), summed over parts.
- The single-POST `app.bsky.video.uploadVideo` the source describes is still
  in the lexicons, beside the multipart five.

## 3. What exact2 has today

- **Picking a video.** `input type=file accept="video/*"` gives
  `app:/tmp/picked/<n>.<ext>` with `type`, `size`, `width`, `height` and
  `duration` (LLP 1069.002 D3). On iOS an `accept` without `video/*` asks
  PHPicker for `.compatible` video, which is H.264 at the source's size
  (`host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/Picker.swift:236`). Nothing makes it smaller.
- **`exactBodyFrom`.** `fetch(url, {exactBodyFrom: "app:/…"})` sends a whole
  app file, under `fs.read`, at most 64 MiB (`runner/src/request.rs:119-120`;
  `docs/reference.md:381`). The source is right that it exists.
  - **Natively** the worker reads the whole file into the request on a reader
    thread before sending (`host/apple/src/executor_body.rs:10-14`). It
    checks `stat` against the cap first (`:204`). At most four readers run at
    once (`:86`). Linux uses the same file. The transport then copies the
    bytes again: Darwin into an `NSData` `HTTPBody`
    (`darwin_http.mm:347`), and rustls/ureq from the slice
    (`rustls_http.rs:469`). Windows refuses it.
  - **On the web** a picked entry is the browser's own `File`, so the body is
    a `Blob` the browser streams from disk. A file the app wrote is a byte
    record, which IndexedDB reads whole before it is wrapped
    (`host/web/storage-fs.js:407-427`). The cap is the same constant, checked
    before the `Blob` is made. Both carriers (`http-body.js:185-202`,
    `ts-fetch.js:15-35`) send it with `fetch`.
- **No byte range.** There is no part of a file as a body, and no ranged
  `readFile` (`vendor/ibex/crates/ibex2/src/bindings/storage.d.ts:14`). A
  clone could read the whole file and slice it in JavaScript today, but that
  is what `exactBodyFrom` exists to avoid (a 2 MB photo already broke the
  100 ms step; LLP 1108 D6).
- **No progress.** No transport reports bytes sent. The Darwin delegate
  implements no `didSendBodyData` (`darwin_http.mm`). LLP 1069's survey says
  so: "No upload/download tasks with progress"
  (`llp/1069-expo-sdk-survey.research.md:166`).
- **Cancelling.** `fetch` takes `signal`, and a replaced answer's fetch
  rejects `Aborted` natively and in the web's wasm realm. On the JS target the
  fetch runs to completion instead (`docs/reference.md:517-521`).
- **Long work.** `native.later` is a long call carried as a request, outside
  the 100 ms step, forgotten when a newer answer replaces it (LLP 1067 D1).
  Forgetting drops the reply; it does not stop the work. The handoff gives
  the module only a reply (`runner/src/request.rs:613-619`,
  `host/apple/src/executor_core.rs:764-769`), and a begun module turn runs on
  (`:380-385`). An app's Swift module works in the app's own roots
  (`host/apple/Sources/ExactKit/NativeModule.swift:531-541`), so it can read
  `app:/tmp/picked` and write `app:/tmp`. The web's page module answers long
  calls too (LLP 1067 D5).
- **`compressImage`** is the model for a transcode call
  (`data/src/image.rs:1-8`; LLP 1069.002 A1). It does not fit a video as
  built. It runs as an Ibex fs op with a 20 s trial budget and a 30 s waiter
  (A1.5), and it has no progress and no cancel (A1.8). What it does have is a
  right to write: a call that has been given up on writes nothing (A1.5).
- **Concurrency.** The Darwin transport keeps a pool of four sessions, and a
  fifth exchange waits for one to come back (`darwin_http.mm:364-386`). The
  executor has four file readers. Parallel parts need the independent lane
  (`exactIndependentHttp`), since an ordered source's fetches wait in one
  FIFO (`docs/reference.md:391-394`).

## 4. What the web does

- **A part of a file:** [`Blob.slice(start, end)`](https://w3c.github.io/FileAPI/#dfn-slice),
  then `fetch(url, {method: "POST", body: part})`. The browser reads the slice
  from disk and sends a `Content-Length`. Node has the same shape for a path:
  [`fs.openAsBlob(path)`](https://nodejs.org/api/fs.html#fsopenasblobpath-options).
- **Upload progress:** only `XMLHttpRequest`'s
  [`upload`](https://xhr.spec.whatwg.org/#the-upload-attribute) object, whose
  `progress` events are [`ProgressEvent`](https://xhr.spec.whatwg.org/#interface-progressevent)s
  with `loaded`, `total` and `lengthComputable`. `fetch` has no upload
  progress. A [streamed request body](https://fetch.spec.whatwg.org/#dom-requestinit-duplex)
  (`duplex: "half"`) lets a page count bytes as the stream is pulled, but only
  Chromium ships it, only over HTTP/2 or later, and it sends no
  `Content-Length`, which `uploadPart` refuses.
- **Transcoding:** [WebCodecs](https://www.w3.org/TR/webcodecs/) decodes and
  encodes frames. It neither reads nor writes a container, so a page brings
  its own demuxer and muxer (Bluesky's is Mediabunny). Which codecs encode
  differs by browser, which is why Bluesky's ladder ends in WebM and may drop
  audio (§2). [`MediaRecorder`](https://www.w3.org/TR/mediastream-recording/)
  records a stream in real time. A 3-minute video takes 3 minutes, and the
  container and codec vary by browser.
- **Cancelling:** `AbortSignal`, for `fetch` and XHR alike.

## 5. Design

### D1 — The 64 MiB cap stays; the clone budgets parts in flight

The source asks for bodies over 64 MiB. Bluesky's multipart upload doesn't
need them: each part is `partSizeBytes`, which the service chooses. The clone
checks that `partSizeBytes` is at most 64 MiB and refuses the upload if not.

Raising the cap natively would mean streaming the body from the file, because
today's reader holds the whole body in memory and the transport copies it
(§3). A 300 MB body would cost about 600 MB at peak, enough for iOS to end the
app. What raising it would take, for the record:

- Darwin: `uploadTask(with:fromFile:)` or an `HTTPBodyStream`.
- ureq: a reader body.
- The web: nothing, since its body is already a file-backed `Blob`.

**Parts in flight.** The same arithmetic applies to parts. Each part in
flight is read whole and copied once more, so four parts cost about eight
times `partSizeBytes` natively at peak. At 5 MiB that is 40 MiB; at 64 MiB it
is 512 MiB, the case above. The 5 MiB figure is S3's floor, and the service's
actual value isn't known. So the clone keeps at most
`min(3, floor(64 MiB / partSizeBytes))` parts in flight, and at least one.
That bounds the peak near 128 MiB. Three, not four, leaves the transport's
fourth session for the token refresh and `abortUpload`, which would otherwise
wait behind the parts. The clone logs `partSizeBytes` on its first live upload
(§7). If the service's parts are large enough that one at a time is too slow,
the fix is streaming the range natively (the list above), not a larger cap.

**Recommended:** keep 64 MiB. Lift it, with streaming, when an app must send
one body that large or a service's parts make buffering too costly.

### D2 — A byte range of a file as a body (stage 1)

**Options:**

- **R1 — `exactBodyFrom: {path, start, end}`.** TypeScript adds the object
  form beside the string. Rust adds `Request::body_from_range(path, start,
  end)`. `start` and `end` follow `Blob.slice`: `end` is exclusive, a
  negative value counts from the end, and both clamp to the file's size.
  - Natively the reader opens the file through the same handles, seeks, and
    reads `end - start` bytes, at most 64 MiB, in its 1 MiB chunks (the
    `AppDirectories::open_file` path, `executor_body.rs`).
  - On the web, `requestBody` returns `blob.slice(start, end, "")`. It
    makes no copy for a picked file.
  - Grants, refusals, the deadline and abort are `exactBodyFrom`'s, unchanged.
  - About 150 lines with tests.
- **R2 — `storage.fs.openAsBlob(path)` and `blob.slice()`,** with a `Blob`
  body. This is the web's and Node's own shape. It needs LLP 1108 D5's native
  `Blob` (slice 3, about 1,100 lines, behind an RFC that is NOT READY), plus a
  file-backed `Blob` in the host store.
- **R3 — read the whole file in JavaScript and slice it.** This works today
  but defeats `exactBodyFrom`. A 60 MB video crosses the bridge, and the
  100 ms step breaks.

**Recommended: R1 now.** It extends a call that just landed, on every
executor that has `exactBodyFrom`. If R2 lands later, R1 goes ("delete; don't
deprecate"). Its slice semantics are R2's, so the migration is mechanical.

### D3 — Progress: count parts (stage 2)

The clone's own state machine knows how many parts have answered. With 5 MiB
parts, a 60 MB video moves the bar in twelve steps. Exact2 does nothing.

If an app later needs byte progress (one large body, or a link where a part
takes a minute), it gets its own RFC. That design must sum the bytes of all
an upload's requests in flight, not keep one request's. It must also be owned
and named by the host, not announced on the app module's device topics (LLP
1016.002). On the web the only source is XHR's `upload` events (§4).

### D4 — No framework transcode now; the clone's own module on Apple

**Options:**

- **T1 — `storage.fs.compressVideo` in Exact2,** a sibling of
  `compressImage` (r1's Amendment A3 to LLP 1069.002). Not now, for three
  reasons:
  - Its result type was `video/mp4`, H.264 and AAC. The web can't promise
    that (§2, §4), so the web, the parity oracle, would answer
    `unsupported` for good.
  - A long request doesn't cancel. An abandoned encode would run for minutes
    and could commit over a newer one (§3, "Long work").
  - A web output written as bytes would be read whole for every part (§3).
- **T2 — transcode at pick,** as `UIImagePickerController.videoExportPreset`
  does. Rejected: LLP 1069.002 D6 rules out transforms at pick, PHPicker has
  no preset, and the web can't.
- **T3 — the clone's own Swift module** (LLP 1067). Recommended now. It needs
  no ruling, its output contract is the clone's, and the module already
  works in the app's roots (§3). The clone calls it when the file is over
  25 MiB or isn't an accepted type, as Bluesky does.

**T3 on Apple, for the clone's lane.** `AVAssetReader` to `AVAssetWriter`:
H.264 High at `AVVideoAverageBitRateKey`, the longer side scaled to 1920,
frames dropped above 30 fps, the track's `preferredTransform` kept, AAC
audio, `shouldOptimizeForNetworkUse` so the `moov` box comes first. HDR (HLG
or Dolby Vision from an iPhone) is converted to SDR BT.709; that must be
verified on a device. `AVAssetExportSession` was considered and rejected: its
presets don't set a bitrate. The module cancels itself, because Exact2 won't:
the clone's `later` takes a `cancel` request naming the job, which the
encoder's loop checks between samples. Each job writes to its own path,
named by the pick, so a stale job can't overwrite a newer one's output, and
the clone deletes what it abandons.

**T3 on the web.** The clone uploads the original. Its page module (LLP 1067
D5) could carry Mediabunny later. Not checked here: whether the page module
can read a picked `File` and store its output as a `Blob` entry rather than
bytes.

**What a later `compressVideo` in Exact2 must meet.** Once T3 has run in the
clone, an RFC can propose a framework call. It needs four things:

1. **An honest result.** It returns the container and codecs the host
   actually wrote (`video/mp4` or `video/webm`, with or without audio), or
   `unsupported`. Then a WebCodecs implementation on the web can meet the
   same signature.
2. **Real cancellation.** The request's abort and the session's retirement
   reach the encoder. A cancelled or superseded call loses its right to
   publish `to`, as `compressImage`'s right to write does (LLP 1069.002
   A1.5). Writing beside `to` and renaming prevents a partial file, but not a
   stale complete one.
3. **Output that slices.** On the web, the output is stored as a `Blob`
   entry (`putBlob`), so a ranged part doesn't read it whole.
4. **The shape T3 proved,** so the framework call isn't designed twice.

### D5 — Cancelling an upload

The clone cancels by sending `abortUpload` and by abandoning the answer.
Natively and in the web's wasm realm, the parts in flight reject `Aborted`. On
the JS target they run to completion (§3), and the service's abort makes them
harmless (`UploadAborted`). That difference is the JS target's existing one
and is not widened here.

### D6 — Grants

No new grant:
- `fs.read app:/tmp/picked` for the parts;
- `fs.read app:/tmp` for a T3 output, which the module writes in the app's
  roots;
- `net.fetch https://video.bsky.app` and the PDS.

For a PDS the clone learns at sign-in, see LLP 1114.009.

### D7 — The agent's view

- **Picking.** `type @t <file>` answers the picker natively, and `tap
  <input> drop <file>` answers it on the web (LLP 1069.002 D9, LLP 1069.007
  D4). The fixture is `apps/video-player/assets/motion.mp4` (10 s).
- **Parts.** A drive uploads to a stub of the five `app.bsky.video`
  procedures that checks each part's `Content-Length` and bytes. The stub is
  the clone's, not Exact2's, and it can name a large `partSizeBytes` so the
  drive checks the in-flight budget. `fail fetch <prefix> times 1` fails one
  part to exercise its retry.
- **Transcode (T3).** The clone's drive checks the module's result, plays the
  output in a `video`, reads `state.media`'s `duration` and `videoWidth`,
  cancels a job, and checks that no file was left and none appears later.
  This runs on macOS and the iOS simulator. On the web the drive expects the
  original to be uploaded.

## 6. Questions for Charlie

1. **Amendment A3 (`compressVideo` in Exact2): admit now?** *Recommended: no,
   changed from r1.* Grok says no: the fixed MP4 contract can't be met by the
   web, so the oracle would never run it, and T3 covers Apple. Astra says the
   decision should be split: approve the capability, but choose its placement
   on reuse, file access and lifecycle cost, with T3 a real alternative and
   stage 3 conditional on cancellation. Both lead to the same place: T3 in
   the clone now, and a framework call later only by its own RFC, against D4's
   four requirements. What you'd accept by ruling this: on the web, the clone
   uploads originals, and a minute of 4K from an iPhone is roughly 170 MB
   (Apple's own Camera settings figures, approximate), so clips past about
   1.75 minutes of 4K are refused there.
2. **A range now (R1), or wait for `Blob` (R2)?** *Recommended: R1 now*, to
   be replaced by R2 if LLP 1108 D5 lands. Both reviewers agree.
3. **Progress: count parts (P0)?** *Recommended: yes, and no byte-progress
   sketch on the record* (Grok C3). A later one gets its own RFC under D3's
   two constraints.
4. **Keep the 64 MiB cap?** *Recommended: keep it (D1)*, with the clone's
   in-flight budget. Both reviewers agree.

## 7. What the clone does after

**After stage 1** (about a day, as the source estimates):
- the composer's video button (`accept="video/mp4,video/quicktime,video/webm"`);
- a preview `video` on the picked path;
- a 10-minute and 300 MB check from `Picked`;
- `getUploadLimits`, then the service-auth token;
- `startUpload`, then the parts with `exactBodyFrom: {path, start, end}` on
  the independent lane, at most `min(3, floor(64 MiB / partSizeBytes))` at
  once (D1);
- `finishUpload`, with `getUploadStatus` recovery;
- `getJobStatus` polled from a Contract `task` (a data module has no timers;
  LLP 1027.000);
- the post with `app.bsky.embed.video` (blob, `aspectRatio` from `Picked`, and
  `alt`);
- a part-count progress bar;
- cancel through `abortUpload`.

**Verified** first by a drive on the web and the iOS simulator against the
stub (D7). Then one live post from a test account, with Charlie's OK, which
the drive deletes. That run records the `partSizeBytes` `video.bsky.app`
returns, in the clone's notes and in this LLP.

**With T3** (the clone's Swift module, D4): the clone transcodes on Apple when
the file is over 25 MiB or isn't an accepted type, and uploads the result. It
is verified on macOS, the simulator and a physical iPhone, with a 4K HDR clip
among the fixtures the drive picks.

## 9. Not in scope

- A framework video transcode (D4), until its own RFC.
- Byte-level upload progress (D3), until its own RFC.
- Background uploads that survive the app being suspended (a background
  `URLSession`).
- Resuming an upload across launches.
- Download progress and `exactSaveTo` (LLP 1108 slice 2).
- Recording video (the camera module, `rules/DEFERRED.md`).
- Trimming or editing.
- Uploading captions, which is a small `uploadBlob`. Picking a `.vtt` file is
  LLP 1114.005 D5.
- Raising the 64 MiB cap (D1).
