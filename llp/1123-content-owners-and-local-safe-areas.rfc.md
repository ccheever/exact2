## Summary

A web page has one viewport, and its safe area is the root's. A native app is made of screens and view controllers, each with its own size, safe area and keyboard overlap.

exact2 keeps the web's model for the document: one viewport per Exact session (`exactViewport()`, `vw`/`vh`, media facts, `env()`), never changed by a sheet. `env()` is the root view's, as CSS Env 1 makes it the document's. Below that it adds the native model. Every native container that hosts Exact content is a **content owner**: a route, a sheet or full-screen presentation, a tab panel, or an app-owned container. Its root is given a host block (LLP 1115.002 D8.2), the containing block its content is laid out in, and the chrome the author did not write (bars, a side strip such as the iPhone Duo's, a sheet's edges, an embedder's reserved space) is cleared automatically and added to authored padding, as SwiftUI adds the safe area to `padding()`. `-exact-safe-area` turns the clearance off per edge, as SwiftUI's `ignoresSafeArea(edges:)` does.

Inside an owner the platform, not the browser, defines the values a layout needs: UIKit on iOS, Exact's own sheets and containers on the web. The host sets them on the owner's content root as inherited custom properties, each in the owner's own coordinates: the safe area (`--exact-safe-area-inset-*`, 0 on an edge Exact clears, the owner's inset on an edge the author takes) and the fold's segments (`--exact-viewport-segment-*`), as UIKit reports them for a view in that place, and the screen's layout regions (`--exact-<region>-inset-*`), its controller's regions translated into that place. Logic that must branch on an owner's size reads a fenced `container:` fact.

The rule is the same at every level and on every host: **the host lays out around its own chrome; what an author reads is what is left, in the box the author writes in.** Mobile Safari does exactly this on the Duo (§"Evidence"), and UIKit does it for every view (§"Evidence: UIKit per view").

## Motivation

- **A sheet resizes the document.** While a sheet is up, `ExactViewIOS.fit` measures the viewport from the sheet (`presenter.modals.coordinateView`), and `ModalHost.present` moves `presenter.viewport` into the sheet's controller. The whole document is laid out at the sheet's size: on the open iPhone Duo, a two-column screen under a 653-point sheet becomes one column, and its layout changes again when the sheet closes.
- **Root fitting follows presented chrome and bar visibility.** `NavigationHost.wantsWholeView` counts every navigation controller that shows its bar, presented ones included, so a sheet that shows a bar changes how the document behind it is fitted, and hiding a header changes it too. Only the window's size, which the viewport units read, takes the root's containers alone (`rootWantsWholeView`, LLP 1075.003 §9.11).
- **Clearance exists only for navigation routes, and only at rest.** `NavigationBarIOS.reportCovers` measures a route's bars after a transition settles. A sheet, a tab panel or an embedder's container has no clearance, and a pushed route slides in without it.
- **Authors cannot express local insets.** Nothing names "this sheet's safe area" or "this screen's size". Apps use `exactViewport().width`, which is wrong inside a sheet, and `env(safe-area-inset-*)`, which is the window's, not the sheet's or the route's under its bar.
- **A document value is wrong once an owner starts elsewhere.** `env(viewport-segment-*)` is in the window's coordinates, but a route under a bar or beside the web's sidebar starts elsewhere. On the Duo folded like a laptop, under a 134-point bar, duo-lab's Fold screen sized its upper pane by `env(viewport-segment-height 0 0)` and the pane ran from 134 to 589.7, across the hinge at 455.5 to 495.5 (2026-10-24); `QUEUE.md` records the same fault for a column on a laptop fold and for panes beside the web's sidebar.

## Evidence: Mobile Safari on the iPhone Duo (simulator, iOS 27.1, 2026-10-08)

A probe page printed `innerWidth`, `env(safe-area-inset-*)` resolved through CSS, `window.viewport.segments` and `navigator.devicePosture`. Two static copies of it differed only in the viewport `<meta>`, one with `viewport-fit=cover` and one with `auto`, and every reading below was the same under both.

| Pose | `innerWidth` | Right inset | Left inset | Bottom inset (`cover` and `auto`) | Segments, posture |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cover screen (466 × 678, 84-point trailing strip) | **382** | 0 | 0 | 0 | not exposed |
| Unfolded (951 × 669) | 951 | 0 | 0 | 34 | not exposed |

On the cover screen, Safari sizes the page beside the bar strip and puts its own controls (back, bookmarks, new tab, tabs) and the clock in it. The page sees no side inset with either `viewport-fit` value. Safari treats the strip as host chrome: it lays out around it and reports to the page only what is left. Unfolded, Safari lets the page reach the bottom of the screen under either value and reports the home indicator's 34 points as `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`, `auto` included: Safari's `auto` does not fit the bottom edge. Exact's `auto` does (D1). Neither `window.viewport.segments` nor `navigator.devicePosture` exists in this Safari. That is this LLP's rule, observed in the platform's own browser.

## Evidence: UIKit per view (simulators, iOS 27.0 and 27.1, 2026-10-24)

A probe app (a plain UIKit app, no Exact) recorded, for a controller's root view and for a subview pinned to that view's `safeAreaLayoutGuide`, the view's frame in the window, its `safeAreaInsets`, its layout regions (`edgeInsets(for:)`) and its division regions (`reservedRegions(kind: .division)`). Insets are top, right, bottom, left, in points.

|---|---|---|
| iPhone 18 Pro, route, large title | 0, 0, 402 × 874; 168, 0, 34, 0 | 0, 168, 402 × 672; 0, 0, 0, 0 |
| iPhone 18 Pro, route in a tab | 0, 0, 402 × 874; 168, 0, 83, 0 | 0, 168, 402 × 623; 0, 0, 0, 0 |
| iPhone 18 Pro, page sheet | 0, 62, 402 × 812; 70, 0, 34, 0 | 0, 132, 402 × 708; 0, 0, 0, 0 |

| iPad Pro 11-inch, form sheet | 127, 275, 580 × 660; 64, 0, 0, 0 | 127, 339, 580 × 596; 0, 0, 0, 0 |
| iPhone Duo, open, route in a tab | 0, 0, 951 × 669; 82, 84, 34, 0 | 0, 82, 867 × 553; 0, 0, 0, 0 |
| iPhone Duo, closed, page sheet | 8, 8, 450 × 670; 74, 76, 34, 0 | 8, 82, 374 × 562; 0, 0, 0, 0 |

- **The safe area is per view.** A controller's root view spans under its bars and reports them; a view inside the safe area reports 0 on every edge. A floating form sheet has no bottom inset; an edge-attached sheet has the home indicator's, and so does a sheet at the medium detent, which UIKit draws scaled by 386/402, 8 points in from the screen's sides and bottom: its frames in the window are scaled, while its insets are in its own points. After an animated presentation that sheet reports a bottom inset of 24.67 until its next layout pass.
- **Regions are per view.** The Duo's route root reports margins 82, 84, 34, 20 and readable content 82, 84, 34, 52: each region's inset from that view's own edges, never less than its safe area.
- **Fold regions are per view.** Folded like a laptop (the window's division at y 455.5 to 495.5) under a 134-point bar, the route root reports the division at y 455.5 and the subview inside the safe area at y 321.5: the same region in the subview's coordinates. Folded like a book, a page sheet moved onto the left segment (x 8 to 467.3) reports the division at x 447.5, 12 points inside its right edge.
- **The window's safe area includes the Duo's side strip** (right 84 open and closed), so the document's `env()` already carries it.

## Apple's APIs and the HIG, and how Exact maps each

Exact follows CSS where CSS has a word, follows UIKit where only UIKit does, and declares each deviation. The tables say, per API, what it does and what Exact does with it: **follows**, **deviates** (with the reason), or **defers** (until a consumer). iPhone Duo rows are iOS 27.1 SDK evidence.

### A view's size and traits

### The HIG

| Page | What it asks | Exact |
|---|---|---|
| [Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout) | Adapt the composition to the available space and text size; keep important content inside the safe area; let backgrounds and scrolling surfaces extend to the edges; respect margins. | Follows: owners adapt by allocation; content clears chrome by default; the owner's background propagates to its whole allocation; margins are the author's padding (D3). |
| [Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets) | A scoped task; detents, a grabber for resizable sheets; form and page sheets on iPad. | Follows: `navigationPresentation="modal"`, `navigationDetent`, `navigationSizing` (D7). |
| [Modality](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/modality) | Use modality for a focused task; make dismissal obvious; keep the task simple. | Follows: a modal owner's background is inert; `closedby` governs dismissal (LLP 1007 §10 addendum, "Modal routes", 1008 §9); focus enters the owner and returns on close (D2). |
| [Virtual keyboards](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/virtual-keyboards) | Keep the focused field visible; use the keyboard layout guide; reveal a hidden insertion point by scrolling just enough. | Follows: `keyboardAvoidance="auto"` reveals the focused editor through its nearest scroller and adds only the residual docked overlap (D7). It does not shrink every container. |
| [Designing for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo) | Preserve state across poses; adapt panes and margins; keep interactive content off the fold band; avoid disruptive rearrangement. | Follows for the document (LLP 1078) and for each owner (D9); the fold band belongs to no segment. Pane policy is the app's (`docs/recipes/foldables.md`), or the platform's through an arrangement (LLP 1123.001, proposed). |
| [Multitasking](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/multitasking) | Work at any size; do not assume full screen. | Follows: every size reaches Exact as a real resize; nothing reads the screen's bounds. |

## Design

### D1 — One viewport per session

`exactViewport()` (width, height, media preferences, `devicePosture`, segments), `vw`/`vh` and the other viewport units, and `env(safe-area-inset-*)` and `env(viewport-segment-*)` are the Exact session's document facts. They are measured from the session's own view after root fitting, under the first root's `viewport-fit` and `interactive-widget`.

- **Root fitting** has three rules. They depend on the root's `viewport-fit` and on whether the primary screen is a native container (a navigation stack or tab container projected from the root's routes). They never depend on whether a bar is visible at the moment, and never on a presentation.
  - `viewport-fit="cover"`: the document covers the session view. `env(safe-area-inset-*)` is the view's safe-area insets.
  - `viewport-fit="auto"` with no native primary container: the document is fitted inside the view's safe area on all four edges, so no obstruction overlaps it and `env()` is 0 (LLP 1008 §9). Safari's `auto` fits less: unfolded on the iPhone Duo it leaves the bottom edge under the home indicator and reports it (§"Evidence"). Exact's web host reports what the browser reports (C38).
  - `viewport-fit="auto"` with a native primary container: the document covers the session view, so the container's bars reach under the status bar. `env()` is 0, as under the second rule; covering the view while `env()` is 0 is a native deviation, declared in LLP 1001. Authored content is inside owners, which clear (D3). No gesture scrolls the document as a page then; its routes scroll their own content. On iOS the host mounts it outside the viewport's scroll view, because a scroll view above a container changes where UIKit places its bars: inside one, an active bar search field stays in its column behind the keys (iOS 27.1, measured). The viewport stays the document's coordinate space (`DocumentMountIOS.swift`).
  - The fitting reads one rule, "a native primary container exists", for the viewport and the window alike (`NavigationHost.wantsWholeView` and `rootWantsWholeView` become one). Hiding a primary route's header and its bar does not change the fitting or `exactViewport()`.
- No presentation, route or controller changes these facts. `viewport-fit` or `interactive-widget` written inside an owner's content is ignored: only the first root's props are policy (`PresenterIOS.viewportFit`).
- A sheet is like a modal `<dialog>` in the top layer: the same document, laid out in its own box, with the background inert. It is never an iframe with its own viewport.
- The unit is the session. Two embedded sessions can have different viewports, and a session embedded in a sheet takes that sheet's size as its document size.
- `svh`, `lvh` and `dvh` are admitted (LLP 1001); a native window has no retractable browser chrome, so its small, large and dynamic viewports coincide. On the web `exactViewport().height` follows `innerHeight` and `vh` the large viewport, as in browsers. An owner's own size is D12's container units, not a viewport unit.

### D2 — Content owners and their host blocks

A content owner is any of:
- a route (`navigationKey`) in a native navigation stack;
- a presented route (`navigationPresentation="modal" | "fullscreen"`);
- a tab panel;
- an app-owned container admitted through LLP 1075.003 §3.6, such as a pane an arrangement gives Exact, or a split-view column an app hosts Exact content in;
- on the web, a wrapper the web host's emulated chrome (`nav-chrome.js`) puts around a route or sheet.

Popovers and other anchored boxes (`popovertarget`, `position-area`, menus, choosers) are top-layer boxes of the document, not owners.

The session root is not an owner. Ordinary boxes are never owners.

**Chrome every route shows belongs inside each route.** A header or footer the routes share is written inside each route's owner, as `apps/realworld` composes its `Header` and `Footer`. A box written before the native stack is beneath every owner, so a route that fills the root covers it and takes its touches; Exact adds no stacking exception for it.

**A retained route that rejoins the stack** below the selected route, without appearing, keeps a real block: when its controller has a loaded view with nonempty bounds, the host restores that block without claiming a placement, and an offscreen route that kept its block retains it. Only a first route still in document layout can stay live beside a Mac detail pane (`head::inactive`); a covered native owner is inactive. Its regression removes and reintroduces the owners through normal projection (an authored temporary route before the retained stack), not by editing UIKit's stack directly, which projection would restore.

**Nested owners clear once.** Owners nest: a route inside a tab, a sheet over either. Only an owner whose content is authored clears. A container whose whole content is another owner, such as a tab panel holding a navigation stack, contributes no clearance of its own. UIKit already includes the outer chrome (the tab bar) in the inner owner's `safeAreaInsets`, so the inner owner clears it, once. A tab panel whose content is authored directly is itself the owner that clears.

**An owner is a host block.** LLP 1115.002 D8.2 gives the kernel one way to lay a subtree out in a box that is not the window: the host gives the subtree's root a **containing block**, a width and a height, each the size or a maximum. The root is then out of its parent's layout and laid out as a root is, in the block; its frames are in the block's own space; the block's used size is published with the layout that changed it; viewport units, `env()` and `rem` stay the window's. A pane on the Mac (LLP 1115.002 D4) and a sheet on the Mac use it. An owner's root is given a block the same way, on every host with native containers:

| Owner | Block | As in |
|---|---|---|
| A route in a stack, a tab panel, an app's pane, a full-screen presentation | `(is, is)`: its controller's view bounds | a split's pane |
| A sheet with detents | `(is, is)`: the sheet's view bounds at the current detent | a pane, resized at every detent step |
| A sheet whose content decides its height (`navigationSizing="fitted"`, the `fit-content` detent, D7) | `(is, at most)`: the sheet's width, its maximum height | LLP 1115.002 D8.2's form sheet |

The block is the owner's untransformed bounds; in what follows, the owner's **allocation** is its block's used size. A transform (the presenting screen scaled behind a page sheet) is never a resize.

**Where an owner is shown.** The kernel never reads where an owner is; the runner needs it for public geometry, since a node's frame is in its block's space. There are two kinds of owner here, and they differ in stacking.
- **A presentation** (a sheet, a full-screen presentation) is above the document, as a modal `dialog` is in the top layer (CSS Position 4). Its root is reported as an open top-layer box with the dialogs and popovers, back to front, by its block's rectangle in the session view's space (`exact_top_layer`, `Scrolled::top_layer_frames`): `client_rect` maps its subtree through it, `elementFromPoint` tests it before the page's roots, and it is not walked again under its authored parent. The host also says where in that list the frontmost presentation begins, as it decides what UIKit presents and what its dimming makes inert (`inertBehind`); the runner keeps that box and those above it live to point reads and selectors and the rest inert, and reads no presentation attribute of its own.
- **An ordinary owner** (a route in a stack, a tab panel, a split's pane) keeps its place among its siblings: a toast written after the routes is above them, in UIKit and to the runner alike. Its host reports a **placement**, the block's rectangle where it shows it, in the local coordinates of an ancestor that holds the block in the host's hierarchy as in the document: the ancestor's border box before its own transform. That is the root's parent when the host shows the block in it; UIKit's tab container holds a tab's route beside its panel, so on iOS it is the routes' owner, which holds the native container, and the rectangle is UIKit's conversion into the owner's view. That conversion crosses only UIKit's containers, which neither turn nor skew what they hold, so the rectangle is exact, where one converted into a turned panel would be its bounding box. The runner keeps the root in its ordinary walk and steps from the root through the placement instead of the root's frame, straight to that ancestor, leaving out the boxes between, which move nothing the host shows, as the host's own hierarchy does: `client_rect` maps the subtree through it, the transform and scrolling of that ancestor and of every box above it applied once, and `elementFromPoint` reaches the root in its tree and rank order and tests its subtree there. The placement rides the block's own entry in `exact_host_covers` and is sent when it changes. Only the runner reads it; it is never a layout input. A block with no placement is mapped at its block's origin.

In both, the root sits in its block by its own frame (its margins, a width narrower than the block), and a rectangle of another size than the block's used size is a scale: the host's own transform (a sheet scaled at a lower detent), never the difference between the block and the root. A route under another route of its stack, or under a modal one, is inactive to the runner's walk (`head::inactive`), so hit testing passes it, as UIKit does. The agent's coordinates, taps and screenshots follow the same mapping.

On iOS, an unpresented route under native chrome has a document viewport only under `resizes-visual`. Its placement identifies that scrollport and carries its offset in block points, including zero: the root and its descendants move while the allocation's clip stays fixed. Ordinary routes use authored scrollers. Their placements, like fixed panes', have no content offset. This scrolling changes no session viewport facts or layout allocation. A kernel-implied popover block supplies layout space and adds no clip.

**What an owner's block carries beyond D8.2.** Two facts the kernel lays out with, published in the same commit as the block:

- **Local insets** (D3, D9): the owner view's `safeAreaInsets`.
- **The primary scroller** (D3's executor), when the owner names one.

One host-to-kernel entry per owner carries the block and these facts, beside `exact_insets` and `exact_segments`, never size and insets in separate calls; the hosts already send blocks through `exact_host_covers` (kinds 4 and 5), and the owner's facts ride the same door.

- **When the host publishes it.** At `viewIsAppearing`, before the owner is first visible; after the owner's layout (`viewDidLayoutSubviews`); and on safe-area, trait, keyboard and hinge changes. That includes every step of a detent drag and every frame of a window resize. A predicted size from `viewWillTransition(to:with:)` is never combined with current insets.
- **Retirement is separate from publication.** A transition's outcome decides retirement and router state (LLP 1035.001 D2). When a dismissal or pop is cancelled, the surviving owner is sampled again. An owner detached mid-transition keeps its last committed block for its children; a new child under it lays out against that. A block taken back returns the root to its parent's layout (LLP 1115.002 D8.2 item 10).
- **Mounting.** The host moves the owner root's node view into the container's view, as `BlockHostMac` does for a pane, and the document's viewport never moves: a sheet mounts only its own navigation and content. `width: 100%` on the root of a 653-point sheet is 653; `100vw` is the document's width and may overflow.

**Two ancestries are kept apart.**
- *Logical ancestry* (the Contract tree) carries inheritance, state, custom properties and document order. A block changes none of it (LLP 1115.002 D8.2, "What does not change").
- *Rendering ancestry* carries containing blocks, scrolling, clipping, sticky lookup, fragmentation, text flow, auto-height measurement, publication, public geometry (`client_rect`, `elementFromPoint`), agent coordinates, screenshots and hit testing. A host block already cuts it for layout, flow, sticky lookup, child order and publication; placement extends the cut to public geometry and hit testing.

A presented owner escapes its logical ancestors' clipping, scrolling and transforms, as top-layer content does (CSS Position 4 §top-layer). An ancestor's `display: none` still suppresses it (D8.2 item 11). Stacking order selects the active owner: hit testing reaches the topmost owner at a point, focus enters the owner on top, and the background is inert while a modal owner is on top (HTML modal dialogs). Within an owner, document order and the platform's own focus behavior stand unchanged.

### D3 — Automatic local clearance, added to authored padding

On every host with native chrome, an owner's content is cleared of the chrome the author did not write. The required clearance per edge, `local`, is the owner's own safe-area inset: UIKit's `safeAreaInsets` for the owner's view, which already includes bars, a side strip, a sheet's edges, `additionalSafeAreaInsets` and tvOS overscan, as it falls on the owner's block. Where a policy ends the block before the view's edge (`resizes-content` at the keys, D7), `local` on that edge is the part of the inset the block still overlaps, so a block that ends at the keys above the home indicator has no bottom clearance, as content laid out above both the keyboard and the safe area rests on the keyboard alone (the keyboard layout guide). The session root gets none: as on the web, `viewport-fit` decides whether the document is fitted to the safe area or covers the screen, and the author pads with `env()`.

**Formula, per owner and edge:**

```
clearance     = local, or 0 on an edge D4 turns off
content inset = clearance + authored        (the "Used" column below)
```

Clearance never depends on what the author wrote. Authored padding adds to it, as SwiftUI's `padding()` adds to the safe area and as a UIKit view pinned to its controller's safe-area guide keeps its own margins inside it. The authored root is laid out in the cleared area, so the values it reads say so (D9): on an edge Exact clears, `var(--exact-safe-area-inset-<edge>)` is 0, as UIKit reports 0 for a view inside the safe area, and padding that reads it adds nothing. An author who wants the edge's value in a padding takes the edge (D4) and pads it. In the table the inset is 34; "off" is an edge D4 turns off.

| Authored on the edge | `local` | D4 | Used (content inset) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 34 | on | 34 |
| 16 | 34 | on | 50 |
| `var(--exact-safe-area-inset-…)` = 0 | 34 | on | 34 |
| `max(16px, var(--exact-safe-area-inset-…))` = 16 | 34 | on | 50 |
| 0 | 34 | off | 0 |
| `var(--exact-safe-area-inset-…)` = 34 | 34 | off | 34 |
| `max(16px, var(--exact-safe-area-inset-…))` = 34 | 34 | off | 34 |
| `max(16px, var(--exact-safe-area-inset-…))` = 16 | 0 | off | 16 |
| `calc(var(--exact-safe-area-inset-…) + 8px)` = 42 | 34 | off | 42 |

Nested owners: a route inside a tab panel above an 83-point tab bar has `local` bottom 83, which already includes the tab bar; the tab panel contributes nothing; used 83, not 166.

`env()` inside an owner is the document's (D1). Under a root that covers the screen (`viewport-fit="cover"`), `padding-top: env(safe-area-inset-top)` on a header inside a route adds the status bar to a clearance that already holds it. Lowering warns about `env(safe-area-inset-*)`, `env(-exact-<region>-inset-*)` and `env(viewport-segment-*)` written inside an owner (`lower-env-in-owner`) and names the owner's custom property (D9).

**What reaches under the clearance.** The wrapper's padding always clears the content root, so a box that does not scroll and holds no scroller that reaches the edge, in flow or absolutely positioned, is laid out clear of the chrome, as a UIKit view pinned to the safe-area guide is, and it follows the clearance when that changes: when a large title collapses, UIKit's safe area shrinks from the expanded bar to the collapsed one and a view pinned to it moves up with it (168 to 116 on an iPhone 18 Pro, §"Evidence: UIKit per view"). UIKit lets two kinds of view reach under the chrome while their content stays clear, and Exact lets the same two:
- **A scroller reaches an edge** of the owner's allocation when its side lies on the authored root's content-box side and the authored root's border box reaches the content root's side with no border on that side, or, nested, when its side lies on the content-box side of a scroller that reached that edge, with no box between that clips (`overflow` other than `visible`) or has a border on that side. A centred root narrower than the content root, a root with a margin or a border on that side, or a scroller narrowed by `max-width`, beside a pane, or below or above another box reaches nothing on that side, so the readable column goes inside the scroller (C34), and an in-flow control above it, a pane beside it or a composer below it is cleared by the wrapper (C33a). A scroller that reaches an edge is laid out with its border box extended to the allocation's edge on that side, as a UIKit scroll view pinned to its controller's edges is. The boxes between the authored root and that scroller extend with it on that side, the authored root too, so a pane that holds a route's bottom scroller reaches under a tab bar with its background, as UIKit's split arrangement extends its lower view (iPhone Duo simulator, iOS 27.1, turned: the lower view of a `UIArrangementViewController` under a tab bar ended at the window's bottom with a bottom safe area of 83 points, its table inset by 83). Each lays its in-flow children out in its extended box, which extends on that side alone, so an upper pane starts below the top bar. That is a deliberate difference from UIKit, whose arrangement divides its whole bounds, the top bar's area included: turned, UIKit's split lies at 475.67 of 951 points, where Duo Lab's two panes meet at 542.67. Panes that divide the window as UIKit's do are an arrangement (LLP 1123.001). An absolutely positioned box whose containing block is a box of the chain is placed as though that box had not extended on that side, so an inset from that side is measured from above the chrome, as a view pinned to the lower view's safe-area guide stays above the tab bar while one pinned to the view's own bottom does not; a percentage inset follows the extended box. The content root does not extend, so the owner's values and container units (D9, D12) still describe it, while a percentage on a box of the chain follows its extended containing block. The distance it extended (the clearance, the root's padding and any ancestor scroller's padding on that side) is applied by axis. On an axis it scrolls, it is the scroller's content and indicator inset, never its padding: the content is laid out in the extended box less the insets it rests at with the bars expanded, so content that fills the scroller ends clear of the chrome (LLP 1116.000 D1.2) and a collapsing large title, which changes only the live inset, changes no layout but that of lengths the author ties to the owner's live size (container units, D12, which follow the content root as it grows); the content rests clear and scrolls under the chrome. On the other axis it is padding inside the scroller: the content is laid out that much narrower and stays clear, while the scroller's clip and background reach under. This is UIKit's automatic adjustment, which insets only the axes a scroll view scrolls (under the Duo's side strip a vertical scroller pinned to its controller's edges reported adjusted insets 82, 0, 34, 0, and a horizontal one 0, 84, 0, 0), and a carousel inside a vertical list reaches under a split view's list column as Landmarks' carousels do (the detail's carousel rested at 320 and scrolled under the list). When a large title collapses only the inset follows the bar: the scroller's box and its content keep their layout (UIKit's scroll view kept its 874-point frame while its adjusted top inset went from 168 to 116), and the offset origin stays the expanded inset, as `scrollTop` 0 says below. On an edge D4 turns off the clearance is 0, and a scroller that reaches that edge extends by the padding alone.
- **A background extension** (D11) reaches an edge by the same rule and extends its border box to the allocation's edge on each side it reaches; its content keeps the place and size it was laid out at, and the platform fills the extension.

On iOS a scroller's executor is UIKit's own adjustment (`.automatic`), reconciled to the extension. UIKit adds to `adjustedContentInset` the scroll view's own overlap with its safe area ([`safeAreaInsets`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiview/safeareainsets), [`adjustedContentInset`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiscrollview/adjustedcontentinset)); Exact reads that contribution (`adjustedContentInset` less `contentInset`, capped at the scroller's overlap with its superview's safe area: a refreshing `UIRefreshControl` adds its room to the scroll view's own safe area, and so to the contribution, but not to its superview's, and writes no `contentInset` as it begins or ends; iOS 27.0 and 27.1, measured with a pull) and sets the scroller's own `contentInset` on each edge of an axis it scrolls to the difference between the extension and the contribution, positive or negative, and on each other edge to the contribution's negation, since there the extension is padding. Worked, top edge, `local` 106, `padding-top: 16` on the root: the scroller extends 122 points up; it overlaps the safe area by 106, so UIKit contributes 106 and Exact adds 16; when the title collapses to a 54-point bar UIKit contributes 54 and Exact still adds 16. On an edge D4 turns off, UIKit still contributes its overlap and Exact adds the negation, so a refreshing control keeps its room there. The primary scroller (`navigationScroll`, or the existing hook binding) is the one UIKit's bar tracks (`setContentScrollView(_:for:)`): its large title collapses natively (LLP 1035.001 D9), and Exact normalizes scroll offsets so CSS `scrollTop` 0 rests at the expanded inset. Stage 2 verifies authored padding, an opted-out edge, content shorter than the scroller, a large title collapsing and expanding with percentage-sized content in the list (unmoved), a `50cqh` box in it (following the content root) and a stationary box beside it, a centred narrow root and a bordered root (no reach), a carousel under the Duo's side strip and under a split view's list column, and a carousel inside a vertical list.
**A lifted header** (one a bar replaces, `HostCover::Whole`) is `display: none`: its padding does not clear. The transfer of its `env()` padding to the route's top cover (`header_inset`, `kernel/src/kernel/cover.rs`) goes, because `local` is the whole of the owner's top safe area. A route whose lifted header carried no `env()` padding changes from a bar-height cover to the full local top (44 to 106 in `kernel/tests/it/cover.rs`); the test changes with it.

**The wrapper and box sizing.** Clearance is applied inside the owner's block, outside the authored root. The kernel lays the root out in a **wrapper** of two boxes in place of the block's own root box: an outer box the allocation's size, positioned, and an inner box inset in it by the clearance on each edge, which is the root's containing block whether the root is in flow or absolutely positioned. LLP 1115.002 D8.2's rules for the root (a `static` root is `relative`; under a maximum it is sized as a modal `dialog`) apply in the inner box. The authored root's own box model is unchanged: it sits in a containing block of the allocation minus the clearance. Example: a 653-point sheet with a 20-point right clearance gives a 633-point containing block.
- A root with `width: auto` is 633 wide.
- A `box-sizing: border-box` root with `width: 100%` and `padding: 16` is 633 wide.
- A content-box root with `width: 100%` and `padding: 16` is 665 wide and overflows, exactly as it would in any CSS containing block 633 points wide.

The side covers that pad a route today (`HostCover::Edges` right and left from `NavigationBarIOS.reportCovers`, laid out by `kernel/src/kernel/cover.rs`) go: a side's clearance is the block's. Borders, min and max sizes, and absolute descendants anchored to the root behave as in any 633-point containing block.

**Backgrounds extend.** The wrapper paints the authored root's computed `background-color` across the whole allocation, clearance included, as LLP 1008 §9 paints the first root's background behind the status bar. This is Exact's chosen subset of CSS's root-background propagation (CSS Backgrounds 3 §2.11.1), which also propagates background images; Exact propagates the colour only, a subset LLP 1001 declares (stage 2). Content clears the chrome; the surface does not. A background image or gradient stays in the root's own box. Artwork under the chrome is written by turning the edge's clearance off and padding the foreground: `-exact-safe-area="none"` (or the edges concerned) on the root, the artwork in the root, and `padding-top="var(--exact-safe-area-inset-top)"` on the content box beside it (D4, D9). The root itself cannot reach under the clearance otherwise: a block's root is positioned (a `static` one becomes `relative`, LLP 1115.002 D8.2), so it is the containing block of its absolutely positioned descendants (CSS 2 §10.1), and they stay inside the inner box.

**Per host:**
- **iOS, iPadOS, tvOS.** As above.
- **macOS.** Two containers host Exact content as host blocks today: a split's detail pane (`BlockHostMac`, `SplitMac`, LLP 1115.002 D4) and a modal route's sheet (`RouteSheetMac`, D7.4). They become owners with `NSView.safeAreaInsets` in stage 3, the pane reporting a placement and the sheet a top-layer box (D2). Routes in a window's own navigation are not projected yet (LLP 1075.003 §3.11); a window becomes an owner when they are, clearing its title bar and toolbar under a full-size content view. HTML dialog lifting (`DialogsMac.swift`) is a separate top-layer mechanism and follows D2's rendering ancestry.
- **Web.** The root gets no automatic clearance. The web host's emulated chrome (its sheets and bars, `nav-chrome.js`) is a set of owners whose `local` is that chrome and the browser's `env(safe-area-inset-*)` on each edge the owner reaches, so a sheet's footer clears the home indicator in Safari as it does natively.
- **Linux, Windows.** No native chrome around content: `local` is zero everywhere. The rules apply unchanged, so an app behaves the same.
- **Terminal.** Not applicable: no safe areas.

### D4 — `-exact-safe-area`: the per-edge opt-out

```
-exact-safe-area: auto | none | [ top || right || bottom || left ]
```

- An `-exact-` CSS property (LLP 1081), declared as a deviation in LLP 1001: CSS has no word for it.
- It applies only to an owner's authored root, is not inherited, and defaults to `auto`. On any other node it is refused at lowering (`lower-attr-value`), since it would create no owner.
- `none` turns off Exact's local clearance on every edge. An edge list turns it off on exactly the listed edges and keeps it on the others, as SwiftUI's `ignoresSafeArea(edges:)` lists the edges it ignores. Authored padding is untouched. The background still extends (D3).
- On an edge it turns off, the owner's `var(--exact-safe-area-inset-<edge>)` is the owner's inset, the value to pad with (D9); on the others it stays 0.
- Every literal a value can be (a conditional's branches, a template with no `${}`) is checked at lowering. A computed value that is none of these is `auto`, journalled once per route.
- A route with `navigationBar="transparent"` has its top turned off as by `top`: its content starts at the top edge, under the clear bar.
- The SwiftUI counterpart is `ignoresSafeArea(.container, edges:)` on content.
- It does not apply to the session root, which has no automatic clearance: the root's choice is `viewport-fit`. It changes clearance, never allocation: `edgesForExtendedLayout` is not its counterpart.

```contract
column navigationKey="gallery" -exact-safe-area="top" background-color="#000"
  image "hero.jpg" width="100%"
```

### D5 — `calc()`, `min()`, `max()` and `clamp()`, and the local inset as a term

LLP 1001 §2 admits CSS Values 4 §10's math functions (`kernel/src/style/calc/`): `calc()`, `min()`, `max()` and `clamp()`, nested, with px and the absolute units, percentages, viewport lengths, `env(safe-area-inset-*)` and `env(viewport-segment-*)` (with or without a fallback) as values, `+` and `-` between lengths and `*` and `/` by a number, within §10.8's 32 values and 32 levels, simplified to an interned tree (§10.10) and range-clamped for the property (§10.12). A tree with a percentage is evaluated at layout against its basis; the rest resolve when the environment does. It is wire kind 24 (`style/calc/wire.rs`), and the web hosts write it back as CSS text.

This LLP adds an owner's values (D9) as leaves of that tree: `var(--exact-safe-area-inset-<edge>)`, `var(--exact-<region>-inset-<edge>)` and `var(--exact-viewport-segment-<field>-<x>-<y>)`, standalone or in any position a length takes. A segment's leaf may carry a fallback, as `env(viewport-segment-*)` may; the others take none. They are the only custom properties a length reads. Each resolves per node, against the values that node inherits, so a tree that also holds a percentage is handed to layout with them already in it as points. On the wire each is a leaf of its own: tag 40, then four bytes naming the value (its family, safe area, region or segment, then its edge, region and edge, or field and segment; `style/calc/wire.rs`).

### D6 — Reading local values in logic: a fenced `container:` fact

Most adaptation needs nothing: percentages, flex and grid resolve against the allocation, and a parent can pass a prop. For logic that must branch on a container's size or local insets:

```contract
shape ContainerGeometry
  available: bool
  generation: number
  width: number
  height: number
  safeTop: number
  safeRight: number
  safeBottom: number
  safeLeft: number

resource detail = exactSurface("container:detail") as shape ContainerGeometry
derive wideDetail = detail.available and detail.width - detail.safeLeft - detail.safeRight >= 600
```

- `container:<navigationKey>` names a live owner. A duplicate live name is refused.
- The values are the owner's allocation with no keyboard, before padding; the safe fields are `local` (D3) as it falls on that allocation. The host reads it from sources no keyboard changes: a sheet's width at its selected detent's resting height as UIKit resolves the detent (`UISheetPresentationController.Detent.resolvedValue(in:)`, given the sheet's maximum with no keyboard), which UIKit's growth of a medium sheet for the keyboard does not change; a route inside a sheet (its first, or one pushed in it), which fills the sheet's height and so grows by as much, at its bounds less that growth, the sheet's height less its resting height; and a route outside every sheet at its controller's bounds (Exact's own block trim for `resizes-content` is not applied to them). An owner installed while a keyboard shows reports the same. A keyboard never changes the fact, so an editor that a branch mounts, focuses or removes cannot feed back through it. An owner that fills only part of a sheet (an arrangement's pane, whose fact LLP 1123.001 D4 defers) has no such source yet: its fact waits until its keyboard-free size is resolved through its containing owners. Its qualification adds a medium sheet that grows for the keyboard, a route pushed inside it, and each installed with the keyboard up.
- `available` is false before installation, after disposal, and while the owner is retained but inactive. `generation` changes per incarnation, and stale callbacks are dropped.
- No size-class field: numbers only, as LLP 1039 D1 rules. A native hook reads traits.
- **Feedback fence.** What the fact reports must never depend on a branch that reads it.
  - **Content-sized owners are refused:** `navigationSizing="fitted"`, or a detent resolved from content.
  - **Independence.** The owner's reported allocation and insets must not depend, by any path, on a branch that reads the fact. The paths are:
    - the presentation: `navigationPresentation`, `navigationSizing`, `navigationDetent`;
    - the chrome Exact projects from authored content, which `local` includes: whether a header is shown (the bar), the heading's level (large or inline title, which changes `safeTop`), the tablist, and any attribute projected into a bar or tab item.
  - **Scope: every owner.** A read may feed no owner's chrome, presentation, scroll, reveal or focus, not only its own owner's and its ancestors', whose bars and tablist contribute to its safe area: two owners live side by side (a split's columns) can each read the other's fact and drive the other's title, a cycle no single owner's scope sees (A large when B is large, B inline when A is large, and around). The fact is for branching content, never chrome.
  - **Indirect paths count.** A read can reach chrome directly, through a `when` or a `derive`. It can also reach it through an action: the action reads the committed fact and stores state, and that state drives `aria-level`, a bar, a tab or a presentation attribute in a later batch. The cycle `safeTop` 106 → inline title → 54 → large title → 106 is refused whichever path it takes.
  - **Commands count too.** An action that reads the fact can change chrome with no state and no attribute: a `scrollIntoView` or a scroll of the primary scroller collapses or expands a large title, and a focus that reveals an editor scrolls it. The cycle `safeTop` 168 → reveal deep in the list → 116 → reveal the first item → 168 is refused as the attribute paths are.
  - **At bake:** any static path from a read of `container:<key>` (in a `derive`, a `when`, or an action that writes state or issues a command) to an attribute that feeds any owner's projected chrome or presentation, or to a scroll, a reveal or a focus in any owner, is refused with `bake-container-feedback`, whichever owner the effect is in. A path the bake cannot rule out is refused. The qualification adds an action that reads the fact and calls `scrollIntoView` without storing state, and two live sibling owners whose heading levels each read the other's fact.
  - **At runtime:** nothing is held. The bake refuses every path from a read to the chrome or presentation it could feed, so a change the runtime sees did not come from a read, and the fact publishes it as it publishes any other change: a heading that an unrelated action switches from large to inline is reported as the new `safeTop`. A host-side cause the bake cannot see (a native hatch changing a bar) is not a Contract path and is not fenced; if a consumer shows one forming a cycle, the fact waits for a design that covers it rather than freezing on any change.
- **Prerequisites.** LLP 1039 D5 admits the fact; the bake check that limits `exactSurface` names to canvas surfaces admits `container:`. It lands only with a consumer.
- **Fold segments.** CSS reads an owner's segments as D9's custom properties. This record gains `segments: list<ContainerSegment>` (`index`, `x`, `y`, `width`, `height`, D9's rectangles with no keyboard, relative to the content root) when logic must branch on them; until then `exactViewport()`'s counts are the document's.

### D7 — Presentation attributes

Exact implements these on every host, so they are first-class Contract attributes next to `navigationPresentation` and `navigationDetent`. That follows the pattern of `keyboardDismissMode`, `closedby` and `retainFocus`. `data-*` remains the app's own words for its own hooks (LLP 1075.003 §3.3). This is a narrow admission by the owner; `DEFERRED.md`'s broader restriction on native property names in Contract stays, and `kernel/tables/schema.json` is the authority for both rows.

**`navigationSizing="automatic | form | page | fitted"`** on a presented route. It maps to UIKit's form and page sheets and SwiftUI's `presentationSizing`; the web gets bounded recipes in the page module.
- The default is `page`, today's page sheet. Moving to `automatic` (form on iOS 18 and later) is a separate, visible change (Q1).
- `fitted` refuses explicit detents. It measures the owner's content at its final usable width, the sheet's width less the side clearance (D3), with no top or bottom clearance and no keyboard, within a maximum bound (the sheet's `maximumDetentValue` on iOS), then adds the top and bottom clearance once. The width is the final one because side clearance changes wrapping, and so the height: measured at the full width, a line that wraps once the side clearance is taken would be clipped. Percentage and fill children cannot determine their own enclosing fitted size.

**`keyboardAvoidance="auto | none"`** on an owner, next to `keyboardDismissMode`.
- `auto`, the default, converts the keyboard's occupancy into the owner's coordinates and intersects it with the owner's allocation, after clearance and after any resize the platform already applied to that allocation (a medium sheet UIKit grows for the keyboard). The dismissed guide's safe-area baseline is excluded.
- A docked keyboard's residual overlap becomes the bottom content inset of the owner's primary scroller when the focused editor is inside that scroller and the scroller reaches the bottom edge (D3). Otherwise it becomes bottom padding on the wrapper: a composer below the scroller is lifted above the keyboard, and the scroller above it shortens. Reach is judged against the layout before this accommodation, after any resize the platform or the policy made, so the padding cannot change the choice that added it. In both cases the focused editor is scrolled into view by its nearest scrolling ancestor.
- A floating or split keyboard is tracked as a rectangle: the caret is revealed, and no bottom padding is added.
- Nested owners consume the overlap once, innermost first.
- `none` turns off Exact's extra accommodation. The root's keyboard policy (`interactive-widget`) and the platform's own behavior stay: on a native host `resizes-content`, iOS's default (LLP 1008 §9) or written, shortens the editing owner's block, never the document or the container that holds it (C29, stage 5); on an ordinary page, which has no owner, it shrinks the document's viewport, as in a browser. `auto`'s accommodation takes only what that policy left of the keyboard's overlap.
- `env(keyboard-inset-*)` is not added (Q3).

Two peer panes that the platform places by the container's shape and the fold are an arrangement: LLP 1123.001.

### D8 — Readable width and margins

Plain CSS: `max-width: 42rem; margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto`. UIKit's `readableContentGuide` documents no fixed width, so a number such as 672 is the app's choice. Default padding stays zero on every host. No fact or element is added.

### D9 — An owner's values in CSS: `--exact-*` custom properties

Inside an owner the platform, not the browser, defines the values a layout reads: UIKit on iOS, Exact's own sheets and containers on the web. The host sets them as inherited custom properties on the owner's content root, the box the authored root is laid out in (D3's inner box). The safe area and the segments are what UIKit reports for a view there; the regions are the owner controller's, translated there (§"Evidence: UIKit per view"). They are Ionic's `--ion-safe-area-*` in Exact's spelling (LLP 1081), extended to the platform's other per-view values.

| Property | In an owner | UIKit, for a view at the content root |
|---|---|---|
| `--exact-safe-area-inset-<edge>` | 0 on an edge Exact clears; on an edge D4 turns off, the part of the owner's `local` inset the content root still reaches | `safeAreaInsets` |
| `--exact-<region>-inset-<edge>`, D10's five regions | the region's inset at the owner's controller root view, measured from the content root's edge, never below 0 | the controller root view's region, translated into the content root's coordinates |
| `--exact-viewport-segment-<field>-<x>-<y>`, the six fields of CSS Env 1 (`top`, `right`, `bottom`, `left`, `width`, `height`), `x` and `y` each 0 or 1 | segment `x, y` of the content root: its box divided by the active divisions that cross it, in its own coordinates, margins included as LLP 1078 D5 counts them; set only when the content root has two segments or more, each with positive width and height, as CSS Env 1 §2.3 defines a viewport's ("only defined when there are at least two such segments") | `reservedRegions(kind: .division)`, which UIKit reports in the queried view's coordinates |

- **The content root as laid out.** Every value is measured from the content root's rectangle after clearance and after the keyboard: wrapper padding or a policy that ends the block at the keys moves its bottom edge up, so a content root that ends above the bottom safe area has a bottom inset of 0 there, and its regions and segments are measured from the same rectangle.
- **Regions.** The regions are the controller's, not a subview's own: UIKit gives a plain subview 8-point margins (0 on the Duo's panels), and a subview that preserves its superview's margins still reports its own readable width (20 where the Duo's controller reports 52), where the screen's margins and readable width are its controller root view's. Measured from the content root they are that view's insets less the clearance. Worked, the Duo open, a route in a tab: readable content at the controller root is 82, 84, 34, 52 and the clearance 82, 84, 34, 0, so the owner's `--exact-readable-content-inset-*` is 0, 0, 0, 52.
- **Segments.** Worked, the Duo folded like a laptop under a 134-point bar (the window's division at 455.5 to 495.5): the route's content root starts at 134, so segment `0, 0` is top 0, height 321.5, and segment `0, 1` starts at 361.5. A page sheet that UIKit moves onto the left segment of a book fold reaches 12 points into the band, so its content root lies on one segment, and so it has no segment properties at all, as a viewport with one segment has none. Twenty-four properties name the segments of a grid up to 2 × 2, the largest a device or browser reports; a larger document grid has no owner properties past index 1. At every owner's wrapper and every top-layer root the host sets all twenty-four: a segment that exists to its length, every other to the guaranteed-invalid value (`initial`), so a nested owner never inherits an ancestor's segments and `var(--exact-viewport-segment-left-1-0, 0px)` falls back as `env(viewport-segment-left 1 0, 0px)` does. Segments place boxes that do not scroll; inside an extended scroller (D3) they are still the content root's, as scrolling content does not move with a fold (`docs/recipes/foldables.md`).
- **At the session root** the values describe the area the document covers, measured from the document's own edges: when the root covers the session view (D1's first and third rules), the view's safe-area insets, its controller's regions and the document's segments; when it is fitted inside the safe area, a safe area of 0, each region less the safe area, and the document's segments. Under `cover` they equal `env()`. Under `auto` with a native primary container they are the view's real insets and regions while `env()` is 0 and D10's regions are measured from the safe area: a box outside every owner (a toast, an overlay) pads with `var()` and stays clear. This is a deliberate difference from `env()`, declared in LLP 1001.
- **A content-sized owner.** A sheet that fits its content (`navigationSizing="fitted"`, a detent resolved from content) takes its segment properties from its maximum allocation, the box it is measured within (D7), not from the size its content gives it, an exception to the rule above. Segments change in steps (two become one as a sheet shrinks off the band), so content that read them as laid out could shrink the sheet onto one segment, lose the property, take its fallback and grow it back, without end. Its safe area and regions stay as laid out.
- **An owner on one segment.** An owner can lie on one segment of a document with two: a split's detail column on a book fold, a page sheet moved onto one segment. It then has none of the segment properties, while logic that branches on the document's counts (`exactViewport()`; a local count waits for D6's segment record) sees two. So a length that reads an owner segment gives it a fallback, which takes over there as `env()`'s does: with `width="var(--exact-viewport-segment-width-0-0, 50%)"` and the second pane's start from `var(--exact-viewport-segment-left-1-0, 50%)`, two panes lie in halves. With one band, which every foldable that reports segments has, an owner has one segment or the document's two along the same axis, so the document's count and these fallbacks agree. A 2 × 2 document grid (admitted, as an agent's `prefer segments 2x2`) can give an owner two segments along the other axis, which the document's count does not tell; logic for it reads the owner's own counts (D6's segment record, with its consumer) or uses an arrangement (LLP 1123.001), which reads the owner's grid itself. Stage 2 qualifies a one-segment owner inside a two-segment document, none of its twenty-four properties set, and 1 × 2 and 2 × 1 owners in a 2 × 2 document.
- **Top-layer boxes** (a `dialog`, a popover) are the document's (D2): their roots take the session root's values, not those of the owner they are written in, so a dialog opened from a sheet reads the window's inset, as its box is the window's. Their container units keep the owner's container (D12).
- **The host sets them on the wrapper** (D3), on every target. For these properties the wrapper is the authored root's parent in inheritance: an authored root inherits from its wrapper, and the wrapper from the owner's logical parent. On the web the wrapper is a real ancestor element. In the native kernel, an inheritance walk that reaches an owner's authored root takes the owner's host values next, and only then continues to the logical parent. They change with every coherent commit (D2), transitions included; on an edge Exact clears the safe area stays 0 while a large title collapses, and on an edge D4 turns off it follows the bar, as UIKit's does.
- **They inherit** to every descendant as computed values (CSS Variables 1), so a box deep in a sheet reads the sheet's values without any per-element setting.
- **Authors may override** the safe-area and region properties on any node, as attributes spelled exactly as the properties: `--exact-safe-area-inset-bottom="0px"`. The segment properties are the host's alone: an authored declaration of one is refused at lowering (`lower-host-property`), so they need no override semantics. Descendants then inherit the authored value. The value is any length the kernel admits; a relative length (`em`, `rem`) computes at the declaring node and the computed length is what inherits, as a registered `<length>` does. A declaration that is invalid at computed-value time (a cycle through `var()`, a value outside the syntax) behaves as `unset`, per CSS Variables 1 §3.1 and the Properties and Values API: the node inherits its parent's computed value, so an invalid override on an owner's authored root reads the wrapper's host value.
- **Registration.** The safe-area and region properties are registered as `@property { syntax: "<length>"; inherits: true; initial-value: 0px; }`, as `--exact-accent` is registered (LLP 1069.011) and as `index.html` registers `--exact-press-factor`. The segment properties are not registered, so one the host sets to `initial` takes `var()`'s fallback.
- **In a length,** each is a term beside `env()` in `calc()`, `min()`, `max()` and `clamp()` (D5), and alone. The native kernel resolves it per subtree from the nearest ancestor-or-self computed value, as it resolves `var(--exact-<role>)` (`kernel/src/style/roles.rs`); a change to an owner's committed values re-derives and dirties exactly the nodes whose style reads them, as `set_env` does for `env()`. The web host writes the properties on each owner's wrapper and on the root element (`--exact-safe-area-inset-top: env(safe-area-inset-top)`, the segments from `env(viewport-segment-*)` where they exist) and passes authored `var()` text through for the browser to resolve. The JS target does the same.
- **On Linux, Windows and the terminal** the safe-area and region values are `0px` and no segment is set.
- **Lowering.** The rows are the first Contract attributes whose names begin with `--`. The Contract lexer admits only the `-webkit-`, `-apple-` and `-exact-` prefixes today, so stage 2 extends its attribute-name rule (and the Lean syntax if it constrains names). `kernel/tables/schema.json` declares the rows, and the native kernel and both web targets write the same canonical CSS text for them.

### D10 — The layout regions: `env(-exact-<region>-inset-<edge>)`

An app gets the same layout regions a UIKit or AppKit screen gets, as `env()` lengths beside `safe-area-inset-*`, so spacing that follows the platform is written once and changes with the device: the margins a controller's root view has, the readable width, and each of these and the safe area kept clear of rounded display or window corners ([`UIView.LayoutRegion`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiview/layoutregion), iOS and tvOS 26; [`NSView.LayoutRegion`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nsview/layoutregion), macOS 26). They are the root view's, as `env()` is (D1); an owner's are D9's custom properties.

| Name | UIKit | AppKit |
|---|---|---|
| `-exact-safe-area-corners-inset-<edge>` | `.safeArea(cornerAdaptation:)` | `.safeArea(cornerAdaptation:)` |
| `-exact-layout-margins-inset-<edge>` | `.margins()` | `.margins()` |
| `-exact-layout-margins-corners-inset-<edge>` | `.margins(cornerAdaptation:)` | `.margins(cornerAdaptation:)` |
| `-exact-readable-content-inset-<edge>` | `.readableContent()` | the margins |
| `-exact-readable-content-corners-inset-<edge>` | `.readableContent(cornerAdaptation:)` | the margins, corner-adapted |

- **What a value is.** The distance at that edge from the layout viewport to the region, as the platform lays it out, and never less than zero. By default a region lies inside the safe area (UIKit's [`insetsLayoutMarginsFromSafeArea`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uiview/insetslayoutmarginsfromsafearea)); where an app turns that off, its margins can lie outside it, and the value says so. Under `viewport-fit="auto"` a region is measured from the safe area, where `env(safe-area-inset-*)` is zero, so a margin is what lies beyond it; that holds too where a native primary container takes the whole view (D1).
- **Corners.** A `-corners-` value takes left and right from the region's horizontal corner adaptation and top and bottom from its vertical one. Measured on UIKit, each axis moves only its own edges (the iPhone Duo's inner panel: the safe area's left 0 becomes 16 horizontally, its top 0 becomes 16 vertically, and neither moves the other), so the combined set loses nothing.
- **Which view.** UIKit's margins depend on the view: a controller's root view takes the system minimum (16 or 20 points), a plain subview 8. The session's view is usually a subview, so the iOS host reads the regions of the root view of the nearest controller that holds it. The Mac reads the session's view.
- **The source.** On iOS and tvOS 26 and macOS 26 every value comes from the layout-region API, whose readable content can differ from the older `readableContentGuide` (on the Duo's inner panel the region's left is 52 points where the guide's is 20); earlier systems use `layoutMarginsGuide` and `readableContentGuide`, with no corner adaptation.
- **Other hosts.** A host that reports no regions resolves each to its safe-area inset: the web and JS targets write `env(safe-area-inset-<edge>)` for the browser, inside a calculation too, and Linux, Windows, Android and the terminal take the kernel's fallback. This deviates from the platforms that have margins and is declared in LLP 1001 §2. The agent's `env` facts name all twenty values on every host.
- **Wire.** A dimension of kind 25 carries the offset, the region and the edge, and a calculation tree's `env()` term 16 + region × 4 + edge (`kernel/tables/schema.json`). A change re-derives exactly the nodes that read the environment, as the safe-area insets do.
- **Deferred calculations.** A length the Apple host resolves against a basis it learns later (`exact_calc_create`) carries the regions in its environment snapshot.

### D11 — `-exact-background-extension`: artwork that extends under the chrome

```
-exact-background-extension: none | auto
```

- On a box, `auto` extends it past each edge of its owner's content box that its border box touches (D3, "What reaches under the clearance"), by the clearance and the root's padding on that edge. The box's content keeps the place and size it was laid out at, so an image keeps its composition in the cleared area, and the platform fills the extension from the content's edges, mirrored and blurred, as Landmarks' header image does under a sidebar and an inspector.
- **iOS, iPadOS and tvOS 26:** the host wraps the box's view in a [`UIBackgroundExtensionView`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/uibackgroundextensionview) with `automaticallyPlacesContentView` off and the content view at the laid-out frame. **macOS 26:** `NSBackgroundExtensionView`. Earlier systems, the web, Linux and Windows have no such effect: the extension is the box's own background colour, a deviation declared in LLP 1001.
- The SwiftUI counterpart is [`backgroundExtensionEffect()`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/backgroundextensioneffect()). It is not inherited and defaults to `none`.
- An image that should reach above the top safe area, as Landmarks' does, also takes the top edge with D4; stretching it as the list is pulled down is the app's own behaviour, from the scroll offset.

### D12 — An owner's size: container units

Viewport units stay the document's (D1), as CSS makes them the viewport's. An owner's size is the size of its content root, the box the author writes in, and CSS's word for an ancestor box's size is a container unit: `cqw`, `cqh`, `cqi`, `cqb`, `cqmin` and `cqmax`, each 1% of a dimension of the nearest ancestor query container ([CSS Conditional 5, container relative lengths](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-conditional-5/#container-lengths)).

- **Every owner's content root is a size container,** as if it said `container-type: size`: its size never depends on its content, because the host gives the allocation and the clearance is the platform's. `100cqh` anywhere in a route is the route's content height, `100cqw` its width, at any depth, where `100%` needs every box between to have a definite height. Under `-exact-safe-area="none"` the content root reaches the allocation, and `100cqh` is the controller view's own height, UIKit's `view.bounds.height`.
- **Outside every owner** no container exists, and the units resolve as CSS says, to the small viewport's (`svh`, `svw`), which is the document's.
- **Through the top layer.** The nearest container is found up the tree, a `dialog`'s or a popover's root included: a box in a dialog opened from a route resolves `50cqw` against the route, as CSS keeps a modal dialog's parent query container (the web platform test `css/css-conditional/container-queries/top-layer-dialog.html`). D9's reset at a top-layer root resets the custom properties, not the container.
- **A content-sized owner** (`navigationSizing="fitted"`, a detent resolved from content) is not a container, as its size depends on its content: its units resolve against the next owner out, or the viewport, as D6's fence refuses the same owners.
- **No `@container` query.** A length relative to a box whose size cannot depend on its content creates no loop; a branch on it would, so LLP 1039 D5's exclusion of container queries stands for queries.
- They are lengths of every row that admits viewport units, and terms in D5's calculations. Each resolves at layout against its owner's content box, as a viewport unit resolves against the viewport; a change of an owner's size or clearance re-lays out the nodes that read them.
- **The web** writes the units as CSS and gives each owner's wrapper `container-type: size`; the browser resolves them.

## Staged plan

Each stage builds on LLP 1115.002 D8.2's host block, which the kernel and the Mac already have.

1. **Ownership on iOS, on host blocks.**
   - Each presented owner's root is reported to the runner as an open top-layer box, and each ordinary owner's root as a placement that keeps its place among its siblings (D2), so `client_rect`, `elementFromPoint` and the agent's coordinates and screenshots map through where it is shown. Placements are sent when they change, with the block.
   - The root fits from its own chrome only (`fit` from the session view, under D1's one rule).
   - A sheet and a full-screen presentation mount their route's node view in the presented controller's view and give it a block, as `BlockHostMac` does for a pane; the document's viewport stays in the session view.
   - Routes in the primary stack and tab panels are owners too: each route's root gets a block its controller's view bounds.
   - A docked keyboard ends the blocks of the top presentation's routes, where the focused editor is, at the top of the sheet's keyboard layout guide, which UIKit lays out as it grows the sheet, which follows no undocked keyboard, and which leaves out the bottom safe area; the route's own scroller reveals the editor once the keys settle, and the sheet's bar stays where it is. This reproduces what the document viewport moved into the sheet did, a containing-block resize, without moving the navigation. D7 replaces it with the owner's accommodation.
   - Session-owned mount membership replaces viewport-descendant checks for keyboard ownership (`hasNativeKeyboardEditor`), the keyboard toolbar and native editors.
   - Modal input and accessibility gating, focus entry and restoration, and cancellation preservation move with the mount. A presented route's logical ancestors still hear its touches (pointer observers, a long press's `contextmenu`, a double tap's `dblclick`) through recognizers on the mount, and the runner's point reads treat what lies behind a presentation as inert.
   - Proof: a wide document with a narrow sheet.
     - The background's width, units and facts stay the document's through presentation, detent drags, interactive dismissal and its cancellation.
     - Sheet percentages are the sheet's.
     - Typing, VoiceOver and coordinate taps work in the sheet, throughout; structural reads agree at settled geometry and report a native animation's destination while it runs (above, "Native UIKit transition tests").
2. **Local clearance and an owner's values (D3, D4, D9, D11, D12)** in the block: the wrapper and its clearance, scrollers and background extensions that reach under it, the lifted header, background propagation, `-exact-safe-area`, `-exact-background-extension`, D9's custom properties (safe area, regions, segments) and their leaves (D5), D12's container units, and `lower-env-in-owner`. Side covers go.
3. **Other hosts.**
   - macOS: the pane and the route sheet become owners with `NSView.safeAreaInsets`, and windows with route projection (§3.11). A `dialog`'s sheet stays a top-layer box: it takes the document's values (D9) and no owner clearance.

6. **`navigationSizing` (D7) and the `container:` fact (D6),** each with a consumer.
