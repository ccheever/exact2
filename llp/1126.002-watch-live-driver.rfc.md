## 1. Summary

A whole exact2 app runs live on Apple Watch: its watch entry is its own live plan, run by the Rust runner and kernel, as on the other Apple platforms, and drawn by SwiftUI at the kernel's frames. The second spike builds the ruled shape and runs it on a Series 10:

- the entry is a file and a root the manifest names, compiled at bake as a whole live plan, refused at bake where the driver cannot draw it, and booted from its own bytes without the update store;
- the driver's tree is its own type over the snapshot driver's leaves, so a widget's archived view does not change;
- the router's routes are a `NavigationStack`: a push slides in, the edge swipe and the bar's Back go back by iOS's rule; `haptic()` reaches WatchKit;
- on the watch, a release build's first frame is 367 ms after exec (median), against 172 ms for an empty SwiftUI watch app. Exact's own share misses the 100 ms budget; §2 says where it goes and §4 how stage 1 meets it.

## 3. Design

### D1 — The live tree is its own type over the snapshot driver's leaves

**Proposed (built):** `LiveNodeView` in `ExactSnapshot` draws a live node with the snapshot driver's leaves (text, `time`, image, icon, progress) and its box modifiers (clip, path clip, semantics), and adds what a live session has: a press, a scroller, the platform's control and field, a navigation stack. `SnapshotNodeView`, the view WidgetKit archives, is main's again: no `Button`, `ScrollView` or `TextField` enters its type. Stage 1's done-line includes the snapshot Swift tests and an archived-widget check, which the six checks and the iOS smoke do not compile.

### D2 — One snapshot per turn, with a kill line

The driver publishes the session's whole snapshot after each turn; SwiftUI keeps each node's identity by its id, but every view that reads the snapshot runs its body again. On the watch the first body costs 103–130 ms (§2), most of Exact's share of the startup. **Proposed:** keep the whole-snapshot model provisional. Stage 1 measures SwiftUI's time per turn on the watch, and if the first body or a 1 Hz tick's body exceeds its budget (§4), the driver changes to LLP 1126 D10's model per node before it is done.

### D3 — `HeadlessSession` is a live session

**Proposed (built):** one session type. `HeadlessSession` keeps what a live host needs from each batch, and has public calls for what iOS's `ExactSession` does through `Runtime`:

| It keeps or does | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| a node's `handlers` and its scroll `content` size | the driver lays a scroller's children over its content, not its box |
| the timer deadline; `advance(at:)` | from every batch that is not busy |
| a turn's `command` ops; `takeCommands()` | `haptic` is the first the watch runs; `preventDefault` and `stopPropagation` stay the runner's |
| `onWake` | a reply's wake comes on the executor's thread; the session posts it to the main queue, where the driver refreshes |
| `insets`, `scrolled`, `fieldEvent`, `controlTitle`, `back(root:route:at:)` | `back` is iOS's `NavigationRules.back` (D5) |
| `agent(_:)` | a development build's only (`EXACT_INSPECT`, now set on ExactCore too), as the widget extension links the same session |

These are `public` because `EXACT_CORE_FRAMEWORK` makes ExactCore a binary target (`host/apple/Package.swift`), across which `package` access does not reach.

A scroller's offset is posted to the runner and never waited on; a Crown tick runs no turn and publishes no snapshot. By the code, not yet by a test: a reply that arrives while the app is inactive still refreshes the snapshot, while timers do not run (D6); and a wake after the driver is gone finds no model (the driver holds itself weakly) and does nothing. Stage 1 adds those tests, and `scroll` and `scrollend` as their own dispatches.

**The field.** The wire of a field's events moved to ExactCore (`FieldWire`), which ExactKit's UIKit and AppKit fields now call, so the watch links no UIKit field code. The watch's input sheet (dictation, Scribble, the keyboard) returns the whole text on submit: the driver sends one `input` (kind 54) `insertReplacementText` with the caret at the end. A dismissed sheet sends nothing. The app's writes to `value` reach the field as its prop.

**Control measurement (not built).** iOS answers `exact_set_button_measure` with a real `UIButton`'s fitted size, provisional on a miss and measured on main; the headless session registers no measurer, so a native button keeps the kernel's default size (the spike's station rows). Stage 1 answers it on the watch the same way, with SwiftUI's `ImageRenderer` on the main thread and a provisional answer until then.

### D4 — The watch entry: its own file, its own live plan

**Proposed (built):** `app.json` names the entry beside `widgets` and `live_activities`:

```json
"watch": { "source": "watch.contract", "component": "CaltrainWatch" }
```

`source` is a Contract file beside `app.contract`; `component` is its first component, which roots the plan, as a terminal entry is its own file (LLP 1101 D2). A root may own resources and tasks only as a file's first component, so the watch's root cannot live in `app.contract` beside the phone's. It is not a widget: no families, preview props or refresh; its key `watch` is unique with the widgets' and activities' kinds.

At bake, `compile_watch` checks the watch's admission (D9) and compiles the file as a whole live plan with the main plan's checks; `compile_entries` writes it as `plans/watch.plan`, keyed `watch` in the envelope's plans map, beside the widgets' and activities' entries and not in `widgets.json`. The watch app boots it from its bytes (`exact_boot_plan`), which wires the app's data module, I/O and executor as `exact_boot` does and never opens or selects from the update store. The app's build script reruns when the watch source changes.

The watch plan's sources are the app's data module's, answered by name; Caltrain's watch file declares its own shapes for them. **Proposed for stage 1 (not built):** `watch.mjs` omits every widget entry of an app with `app.ts`; that is not the watch entry's rule. An app with `app.ts` builds its watch entry when the entry's plan reaches no TypeScript source, and is refused at bake when it does, until LLP 1126.001 §5 Q3's gate passes. Until that check is built, `watch.mjs` omits the watch entry of any app with `app.ts`, saying so.

### D5 — The platform's leaves

**Proposed (built unless noted):**

- **Back and routes.** A node whose children carry `navigationKey` is a navigation root, drawn as a `NavigationStack`: the first route is the base, the routes up to the active one are pushed. A push slides in and shows the system's Back; when SwiftUI pops (the bar's Back, the edge swipe), the driver goes back by iOS's rule: press the control the root's `navigationBack` names, else deliver the location beneath to the root's `navigate`, else the runner's own back of the router. The next tree sets the path. Caltrain's watch screen pushes its station list on its router. r1's chevron for an Escape control is gone: Back is there whenever a route is pushed, as LLP 1115 asks.
- **A press** is a plain `Button` over the node, with no haptic of its own: `haptic(kind)` is how an author asks for one, which the watch plays with `WKInterfaceDevice` (`selection` and `impact`, `-light`, `-medium`, `-soft` as `click`; `impact-heavy` and `-rigid` as `start`; `success`; `warning` as `retry`; `error` as `failure`; a kind it has no type for is logged). Whether a hand-built SwiftUI button plays one is checked against one on a watch in stage 1.
- **A native button** draws its face's title from `exact_press_face`, as a capsule. Stage 1's done-line: the watch's bordered button and, for a vertical list of presses, a `List`, each judged against a hand-built SwiftUI watch screen on watchOS 26. Of the face, the watch draws the title; its symbol, image and subtitle are stage 1's with the measurement (D3).
- **A field** is a `TextField` over the system's input sheet (D3).
- **A scroller** is a `ScrollView`, which the Digital Crown scrolls.
- **The screen.** Stage 1 lays the root out on the whole screen and gives the safe area to the session (`insets`), so the content reaches the bottom and `env(safe-area-inset-*)` is the watch's.
- **Type and the reader's settings.** The kernel uses iOS's metric column on the watch (`host/apple/src/scale.rs`) and the root size scales iOS's Dynamic Type ramp. Stage 1 qualifies unsaid text sizes against watchOS's text styles, and passes Bold Text, contrast and reduced motion to the session as iOS does; the spike passes scale, colour scheme and text size only.

### D6 — Lifecycle

**Proposed (built):** the driver arms one timer at the runner's deadline. When `scenePhase` leaves `active` (a lowered wrist, Always On), timers slow to once a minute (the amendment below); on return the driver advances once. Logging the phase, the dimmed state and each deadline, an active run fires one tick per deadline (§2). Between those minutes a `time` or `progress` node is drawn by SwiftUI from its instant, as in a widget, so Caltrain's countdown (now a `time` node at the train's real instant) stays right. The `time` node's box is laid out at the runner's text and SwiftUI draws the live countdown in it, so its width can differ from what is shown; stage 1 measures a live `time` at the format's widest value, as LLP 1126 D6 does for widgets.

**Amendment, decided (Charlie, 2026-10-23, PR #881; it supersedes §0 Q2's "no ticking at a reduced rate" for the inactive state):** while the scene is inactive, the driver runs due timers at most once a minute. In the background it runs none. On return to active it advances once. Charlie: "That matches the once-a-minute update Apple gives an Always On face, at the cost you measured."

- **Why.** An app's state can depend on time, not only what a `time` node draws: Caltrain's boards drop a departed train only when its tick runs. An inactive app stays on screen while it is frontmost, "usually two minutes", then goes to the background and is suspended; while it shows, the system reduces its update frequency, and a `TimelineView`'s cadence drops to seconds or minutes ([Designing your app for the Always On state](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/watchos-apps/designing-your-app-for-the-always-on-state)). Once a minute is the minutes cadence. Caltrain keeps its 1 s tick while the wrist is raised, as the watch's Timer app does.
- **Cost, measured on the Series 10** (development build, 2026-10-20). The wrist down for two minutes ran one timer turn (7.45 ms), 84 s after the scene went inactive: watchOS delays an inactive app's timer past the driver's tolerance. The app went to the background 121 s after it went inactive. The process used 0.34 s of CPU in those 121 s, against 12–17% of a core while the main screen was active.

Stage 1a builds it (`LiveModel.inactiveInterval`).

### D7 — Architectures: arm64 at watchOS 26

Rust's `aarch64-apple-watchos` sets watchOS 26 as its minimum. Apple: "Apple Watch Series 9 and later, and Apple Watch Ultra 2 now use the arm64 architecture on watchOS 26" ([WWDC25, What's new in watchOS 26](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/334/)).

**Proposed (ruled):** arm64 only, watchOS 26 and later. That runs on Series 9, 10, 11 and 12 and Ultra 2, 3 and 4; SE 3 is in the same class as Series 9 and later in Xcode's device traits (`WatchOS.platform/usr/standalone/device_traits.db`, `arm64e`), which a device should confirm. It leaves out the watches watchOS 26 supports on arm64_32: Series 6, 7 and 8, SE (2nd generation) and Ultra (1st generation). watchOS 27 supports only Series 9 and later, Ultra 2 and later, and SE 3 ([Apple Watch User Guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/guide/watch/apd2054d0d5b/watchos)), so on watchOS 27 no supported watch is left out.

arm64_32: LLP 1126.001 WP17 booted `ExactCore` there (`1a33d0d6a`); no live plan has run there, and `host/apple/link.mjs` refuses a 32-bit Mach-O. The Swift side still names watchOS 10 (`Package.swift`, `watch.mjs`'s simulator path, `widgets.mjs`); stage 3 moves them to 26, and its device build replaces the spike's.

### D8 — Development and the agent

On the simulator, a development build listens on the Unix socket `EXACT_AGENT_SOCKET` names, as the iOS simulator does, and answers JSON lines: the runner's `tree`, `state`, `logs` and `tags`; `tap` and `input` by testId, which go through the same session calls as a person's tap and submit; and `frames`, the driver's own snapshot. Every check of §2 on the simulator drove it, and a screenshot after each change showed it in the drawn app (`xcrun simctl io <watch> screenshot`). On a device there is no socket ([TN3135](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3135-low-level-networking-on-watchos)); the spikes read the app's lines from standard output.

**Proposed:** a `watchos` host in `scripts/agent.mjs` over this socket, in stage 2. It is apparatus, so it needs a yes (§6, question 1). A device's agent is later work: an outbound URLSession bridge, as the phone's token bridge is.

### D9 — What the watch refuses, at bake

**Proposed (built):** a `watch` admission column in `kernel/tables/schema.json`, as `snapshot` and `terminal` are columns. It admits the rows the snapshot column admits, which the driver draws with the same leaves, and `navigationKey`, `navigationBack` and `placeholder`, which only a live session uses. `contract/cli/src/watch.rs` refuses, with the edit to make: a tag the driver does not draw (it draws the snapshot driver's tags and `scroll`, `button`, `input`), a handler it does not deliver (it delivers `press`, `input` and `navigate`; `scroll` waits for its dispatch), a horizontal scroller, more than one `tab`, a surface or media, a prop or style row outside the column, and a `calc()` or `env()` length. The rows of a named style an element applies (`class`) are checked as its own are. The driver paints with the snapshot driver's leaves, so their value limits are its own (`contract/cli/src/snapshot/values.rs`: one border for four sides, sRGB colours, the gradients the leaf draws), checked on literal values, and an image is the app's (`app:/`, bundle-relative or `symbol:`), never the network's. Stage 1 adds a runtime log line, only for what the bake admitted and the driver still cannot place.

### D10 — Packaging: a companion

**Proposed (ruled):** on the simulator and on a device, the watch app ships inside the iPhone app, with its complications, and runs independently of it (`WKRunsIndependentlyOfCompanionApp`). The spike's device build installs the watch app alone (`WKWatchOnly`, no App Group, no extension) only so that the team's development profile signs it.

Under LLP 1126 D8 the watch process pins a generation and never opens the update store. **Proposed:** the watch plan is part of the iPhone app's bundle and its updates: the phone's update owner writes each generation's `plans/watch.plan` with the others, and the watch app reads the generation the phone last made current, pinned for its whole run. `bootPlan` opens no store, which the spike shows; the pinning is stage 3's, with the device packaging.

## 5. Alternatives

- **The UIKit presenter.** watchOS has no UIKit views (LLP 1126 §1).
- **A SwiftUI view per Contract element, laid out by SwiftUI.** A second layout engine would disagree with the kernel, which is the web's layout everywhere else (LLP 1115).
- **The watch screen as a branch of the app's plan on the viewport's width.** r1's answer, refused (§0): a small window elsewhere would show it, and the whole phone plan would ship to the watch.
