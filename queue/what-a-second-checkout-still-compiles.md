**What a second checkout still compiles** (2026-10-04; LLP 1036.000 §7, §10, §11). The host's two Rust modules and the registry crates under an app are now kept for the machine (`~/.cache/exact/apple-modules`, `~/.cache/exact/apple-crates`): a second checkout's first build is 78 → 58 s on an M4 and 38 crates, all of them path crates or standing on one. What is left, in the order it costs: the kernel (33 s in one rustc front end, the chain every build waits on); `wgpu-core` and `wgpu`, registry crates rebuilt in every checkout because the vendored `wgpu-hal` under them is a path crate (20 s of the GPU crate's build; a patched crate consumed as a registry or git source would be kept like the rest); ExactKit (150 CPU-seconds, beside the bake, and not what the Rust waits for: at a lower priority the build is the same, 57.7–58.9 s against 57.3–58.6). The kernel alone is 12.6 s on an idle eighteen-core M5, 10 s of it rustc and 7 of those LLVM over its sixteen codegen units, so it is the cores and not the front end. sccache was tried on the Linux host and is not it (95 → 79 s): what it cannot share is the same path crates.
