**iOS's `line-height: normal` is not Chrome's, and no rule makes it so** (2026-09-26, same lane; Chrome is the oracle). Chrome keeps a face's ascent, descent and gap as whole pixels, rounded; macOS now does the same (TextParityMacTests). iOS's SF has other vertical metrics than the Mac's (15.23 + 3.86 at 16 px against 15.47 + 3.38), so Chrome's rule on iOS faces gives 16 at 14 px, not the 17 the scroll fixture's cross-host 652 needs; iOS keeps CoreText's sum rounded up once per paragraph, which is Chrome's height for one line at 12-15 px and 1-2 px taller at 11, 16 and 17 (a 16 px paragraph's lines are 19.09 apart, Chrome's 18 at 1x, 18.67 at 3x). Matching Chrome on iOS would take the Mac's SF metrics as constants, or a fixture that does not pin the Mac's font. For the record, Safari: iOS WebKit rounds each metric up (20 at 16 px, baseline 16; 17 at 13; 22 at 17), macOS WebKit to the nearest (as Chrome); Safari's underline sits `max(1, ceil(size/32))` below the baseline, `size/16` thick; Chrome's and CoreText's follow the font. Chrome renders `ui-monospace` in Times (unsupported).
