**Text pixels on iOS without app-side bitmaps: measured, not built** (2026-09-27, perf/crypto-mem, M1 iPad Pro, 240 crypto-row labels drawn each way by a scratch probe). No public path draws a label's glyphs in the render server: `UILabel` and `CATextLayer` paint into in-process backing stores the size of the label (footprint +3.8 and +5.4 MB, against 1.9 MB of malloc for exact2's ink-cropped CGImages); both tint a private alpha image (`CATintedImage`) for single-colour text. Glyph paths in `CAShapeLayer`s are rasterized by the render server but hold 1.8 MB of paths and differ from CoreText (mean 4.5/255). An alpha-only (A8) raster is 3.5x smaller (2.56 -> 0.73 MB of pixels) and within one level of the BGRA one, but Core Animation shows A8 `contents` as white, so any other colour needs a `mask` layer, and 960 moving masked labels cost the render server 3.3x the CPU of plain contents (+115 against +35 samples/s over idle, all-process Time Profiler): not built. What would pay without a mask: white text (the crypto list's letter and change badge, about 0.4 MB at rest) as plain A8 contents, which matched the BGRA pixels exactly; it leans on undocumented Core Animation behaviour.
