# Outside, together

A field guide for our six-scene launch. Imagined places, quiet light and a little room to wander. Make the sequence feel like one unhurried breath.

## 01 · Find the opening

Start at **North shore**. Give the water a moment before the title arrives. Keep the curve of the beach and the headland in conversation; neither needs to fill the frame.

Check the whole photograph at Fit, then study the shoreline at 2×. The crop should make the scene easier to read, not busier.

## 02 · Shape the sequence

Move from cool water to warm dunes. Let **Alpine water** slow the pace before **Winter ridge** lifts it again. Try the greens of **Glasshouse** on either side of the snow: one order feels like arrival, the other like release.

Use **Pick up**, **Earlier** or **Later**, then **Place** to try an order. Choose Cancel to keep the sequence as it was.

Save **Last light** for the close. Let the warmth linger after the title has gone.

## 03 · Keep the crew in the room

Keep framing, sequence and sound decisions together in the crew notes. Name the scene, describe the change and give a reason. “Glasshouse before the ridge gives the snow more space” is more useful than “try another order.”

- **Maya:** agree the opening and the final sequence.
- **Jules:** check the crops and keep a clear point of interest.
- **Ari:** find the sound that carries across each cut.
- **Noor:** gather the decisions for the handoff.

## 04 · Listen between the pictures

Begin with the wind bed. Keep it soft enough to leave the coast in the foreground, then let it carry us into the dunes. Every new photograph does not need a new sound.

Leave a little silence at the close. The last image should have time to become a memory.

## 05 · Review the handoff

- Agree the opening crop and closing frame.
- Check all six photographs at Fit and 2×.
- Confirm the sequence with the director.
- Mark the sound transitions that need attention.
- Leave one clear crew note with the final decisions and open questions.

## Closing note

Keep the small details that make a place feel lived in: a path into the dunes, a reflection on the lake, a patch of light inside the glasshouse. Those are the reasons to look again.
